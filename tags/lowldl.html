U-shaped mortality curves for cholesterol, BMI, A1C and BP reflect reverse causality

PCSK9 inhibitors lower circulating cholesterol only, not brain cholesterol

Mar 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 24 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Louis states that PCSK9 inhibitors lower cholesterol only in circulation and not in the brain, because circulating cholesterol cannot cross the blood-brain barrier and the brain produces its own. He frames this as basic physiology countering claims about brain harm from lower LDL.

Tissues make their own cholesterol: even very low LDL-C does not impair hormone production

Sep 17, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 11 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that the blood holds only a small fraction of the body's cholesterol and that tissues, including the brain, mostly synthesize what they need. The steroid hormone demand supplied by blood is only tens of milligrams a day, easily met at very low LDL-C. He cites FOURIER and ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, in which LDL-C below 15-20 mg/dL did not impair adrenal or gonadal hormone production or vitamin D.

Low LDL in cancer patients reflects reverse causality: tumours scavenge LDL

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 12 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that rapidly dividing tumour cells take up circulating LDL, so LDL falls in people with cancer and observational data show low LDL with higher cancer mortality. The shared 2026 Science Advances study found aggressive breast cancers with TP53 mutations depend on cholesterol, and blocking PI5P4K enzymes trapped cholesterol and disrupted mTOR signalling.

Blood carries only 2-5% of body cholesterol; cells and the brain make their own

May 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Shared breakdown of why blood LDL cholesterol is not a measure of what cells need. The body holds roughly 120-150 g of cholesterol but only 3-7 g is in blood, and the brain makes all of its own. One cited model suggests only about 50-90 mg/dL total blood cholesterol is needed to keep transport functional, versus a US average near 200.

The post explains a chart from a European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement showing that genetic variants affecting LDL through different pathways (LDLR, PCSK9, APOB, HMGCR, NPC1L1, SORT1, ABCG5/8) change coronary risk in proportion to the LDL change. Louis explains a 2022 Chinese cohort of about 36,500 people showing the commonly observed U-shaped relationship between ApoB and all-cause mortality disappears once comorbidities, bilirubin and malnutrition are corrected for. FOURIER stroke analysis: LDL below 40 mg/dL cut major events ~31% and recurrent stroke 27%

Cholesterol as a master regulator of blood stem cells, driving inflammatory hematopoiesis

Review of preclinical evidence that high LDL pulls hematopoietic stem cells out of dormancy, causing proliferation, oxidative stress, telomere shortening, impaired repopulation and lasting epigenetic changes that program blood and immune cells toward chronic inflammation.
