Cooking with extra virgin olive oil is fine: Lyon, PREDIMED and CORDIOPREV cut events

Aug 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

The post says three long RCTs (Lyon Heart Study, PREDIMED, CORDIOPREV) used extra virgin olive oil for nearly all cooking and all reduced cardiovascular events and deaths. The only caution is to stay below the oil's smoke point; extra light olive oil has smoke points up to 450F.

Refined olive oils have smoke points of 410-446 F, high enough for deep frying

Dec 15, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 15 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis says it is a myth that olive oil cannot be used for high-heat cooking: some (light/refined) olive oils have smoke points of 220-230 C, above typical deep-frying temperatures of 350-375 F, and the flavor is almost fully removed so they suit baking. He names the product he uses in an image.

Paleolithic humans were omnivores who relied heavily on cooked starchy tubers

May 09, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 13 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis rebuts the idea that paleolithic humans ate keto or carnivore diets, pointing to evidence of cooked starch use and Richard Wrangham's 'Catching Fire' hypothesis that cooked tubers and roots, a reliable glucose source, helped drive brain growth. Starchy underground foods were available even when meat and fish were not.
