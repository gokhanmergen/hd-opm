Very low-carb diets rarely push A1C below 5 because of physiological insulin resistance

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues that in people who already have low A1C, severe carb restriction makes muscles temporarily insulin resistant to spare glucose for heart and brain, so blood sugar can rise rather than fall. This 'adaptive glucose sparing' or 'pseudo-diabetes' also explains why people on long-term keto often fail glucose tolerance tests. Personal observation and physiology reasoning rather than a cited trial.

Rapamycin for 8 weeks improved diastolic and endothelial function in six older men

Oct 31, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 2 Aging & longevity

Summarises a proof-of-concept open-label pilot in six healthy men aged 70-76 taking rapamycin 1 mg daily for 8 weeks. It was safe and well tolerated; systolic function was unchanged but cardiac MRI showed better diastolic function (peak filling rate p=0.014) and endothelial function improved (p=0.020). Very small and uncontrolled; larger placebo-controlled trials are needed.

Rapamycin did not slow atherosclerosis in marmosets, and why stents use it anyway

Apr 18, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis cites Adam Salmon's marmoset work showing rapamycin had no clear effect on aging cardiovascular function, with plaque developing at the same rate as controls. He considers it unwise to rely on rapamycin to prevent heart disease. He also explains rapamycin on stents works by blocking scar tissue (neointimal hyperplasia), not plaque growth.

Rapamycin on stents blocks scar-tissue restenosis, not plaque; it failed in marmosets

Dec 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Louis explains that rapamycin is used on drug-eluting stents because it inhibits vascular smooth muscle overproliferation (neointimal scar tissue), which is what usually causes restenosis, not because it prevents plaque. He distinguishes plaque from scar tissue and notes Adam Salmon's group found rapamycin did not inhibit plaque growth in marmosets.
