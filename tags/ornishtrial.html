Esselstyn's plant-based diet is supported only by case series, not high-level evidence

Nov 11, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 23 · 💬 22 ↩ 2 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis argues the Esselstyn diet is probably healthy and likely lowers cardiovascular risk, but that claims for it are overhyped. Esselstyn's published work consists of case reports and case series, which sit low on the evidence hierarchy, so the strong evidence for plant-based diets in general should be allowed to speak for itself.

Ornish trial: a plant-based lifestyle cut events but did not cure heart disease

May 31, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 30 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis reposts details of Dean Ornish's small RCT of a whole-food plant-based no-oil diet plus lifestyle changes. Five-year events were lower than in controls, but even adherent patients had heart attacks, procedures and deaths, so it is not a cure.

Ornish trial: lifestyle program cut cardiac events, but plant-based diet was not a cure

Jun 24, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 20 ↩ 13 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

In Ornish's small 1998 randomized trial, patients on the diet and lifestyle program had significantly fewer cardiac events over 5 years than controls (25 events in 28 patients vs 45 in 20 controls). Even among 28 who adhered, there were still 2 heart attacks, 8 angioplasties, 2 bypasses, 23 hospitalizations and 2 deaths, so the diet was not a cure.

Ornish trial's 'plaque reversal' may be x-ray angiography measurement error

Dec 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 12 ↩ 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains criticisms of the small 1986-1992 Ornish trial: it used x-ray angiography, which outlines the blood-flow lumen rather than measuring plaque, so the small reported reversal may largely be measurement error. He contrasts this with modern CT angiography and IVUS, and notes the trial's tiny size as another major weakness.

A normal angiogram can hide large plaque burden because of Glagov remodeling

Dec 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains that angiography is a 'luminogram' that misses plaque inside the artery wall because early plaque causes outward (Glagov) remodeling that preserves the lumen. Modern plaque-progression studies use IVUS or CT angiography instead, and he cites this as a flaw of Dean Ornish's small 1990s trial.
