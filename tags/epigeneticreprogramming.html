Vitamin C may reprogram epigenetics in skin cells, a lab finding on skin aging

Jul 26, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 13 ↩ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis links vitamin C's role in lab stem cell reprogramming to a Journal of Investigative Dermatology study suggesting it may restore more youthful gene expression in skin. It is early mechanistic evidence unrelated to heart disease.

First epigenetic reprogramming trial in the eye; cardiovascular use is speculative

Jun 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 26 · 💬 8 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis comments on the start of a clinical trial using Yamanaka-factor epigenetic reprogramming to restore youthful cell states in the eye. The eye is the testing site because it is isolated from the body by the blood-ocular barrier, limiting cancer risk. He says if it works and is safe, cardiovascular regeneration could follow, which is speculation.

Epigenetic regulation in atherosclerosis: reversible changes as a therapeutic target

Aug 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis shares a 2026 Nature Reviews Cardiology review on how epigenetic modifications guide gene programs in endothelial, smooth muscle and immune cells in atherosclerosis. He notes epigenetic age can be reversed in cells and to a limited degree in animals, but it is still early.

Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.
