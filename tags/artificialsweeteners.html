High blood xylitol linked to 57% higher MACE risk in observational cohorts (ESC 2026)

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A study presented at ESC Congress 2026 analyzed 17,710 participants from CLSA and EPIC-Norfolk. Highest-quartile xylitol levels were associated with 57% higher MACE risk over six years in CLSA and 18% higher over 30 years in EPIC-Norfolk, with a dose-response pattern. It is observational and cannot show xylitol causes events.

Meta-analyses: swapping sugary drinks for low-calorie sweetened ones does not harm weight

Aug 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 0 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

A quoted rebuttal to Peter Attia's claim that diet soda swaps would not aid weight loss cites a systematic review of 17 RCTs where replacing sugar-sweetened beverages with low/no-calorie sweetened beverages reduced weight, BMI, body fat and liver fat. A cohort meta-analysis found no cardiometabolic harm from these drinks used as a substitution.
