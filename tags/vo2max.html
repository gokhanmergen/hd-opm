Norwegian 4x4 intervals: Rhonda Patrick on reversing heart aging and raising VO2 max

Oct 04, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 27 · 💬 29 Exercise & fitness

Summarises a Rhonda Patrick video: moderate exercise is ineffective for many, so vigorous HIIT such as the Norwegian 4x4 (four 4-minute efforts at about 80-90% max heart rate with 4-minute recovery) improves VO2 max. She cites a two-year study that reversed about 20 years of cardiac aging in sedentary 50-year-olds, and exercise reducing glycation-driven heart stiffness (Asif et al., PNAS 2000).

Master@Heart: higher wearable-measured training load linked to more coronary plaque

Mar 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 27 ↩ 3 Exercise & fitness

Louis argues intense exercise, especially high intensity plus high volume, carries risks as well as benefits, citing a Circulation 2026 Master@Heart analysis of wearable training load and coronary atherosclerosis in middle-aged and older athletes versus active controls. He notes the net benefit of exercise remains large but the plaque signal can no longer be ignored.

Two-year high-intensity exercise reversed cardiac aging by up to 20 years in an RCT

Feb 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 30 · 💬 19 Exercise & fitness

A shared summary of a 2-year randomized trial in sedentary middle-aged adults combining HIIT, moderate endurance and strength sessions. It improved VO2 max, baroreflex sensitivity and heart elasticity versus a stretching/balance control, though in healthy participants only.

Mendelian randomization finds no causal effect of VO2-max on longevity or diabetes

Sep 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 24 Exercise & fitness

Louis argues that a 2024 two-sample Mendelian randomization study found no association between genetically predicted cardiorespiratory fitness and longevity or diabetes. He interprets the observational VO2-max/mortality link as reverse causality or confounding.

VO2max as a causal longevity metric is doubtful; Mendelian randomization shows no effect

Dec 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 6 ↩ 7 Exercise & fitness

Louis endorses Chris Masterjohn's article arguing that most studies link treadmill performance, not measured VO2max, to lifespan, and that treadmill tests are influenced by many other factors and by unhealthy volunteers. Louis adds that Mendelian randomization shows no forward causal effect of VO2max on longevity but evidence of causality in the reverse direction.

Aging causes VO2max to decline, not the reverse

Nov 01, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 8 ↩ 4 Exercise & fitness

Louis argues that aging drives VO2max decline regardless of activity habits, and that Mendelian randomization suggests the association comes mainly from reverse causality. He cites a longitudinal Circulation study showing the decline accelerates in each successive decade.

Mendelian randomization: high VO2max is a health proxy, not a causal driver of longevity

May 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Exercise & fitness

Louis shares a 2-sample Mendelian randomization of about 70,000 people: genetic predisposition to high VO2max did not predict longevity, but genetic predisposition to low body fat, high muscle mass and high activity did. The same logic applies to grip strength.

VO2 max is a ratio, and chasing it can mislead about heart health

Jul 19, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 1 Exercise & fitness

Louis agrees with a critique of influencers who treat VO2 max as the key heart-health metric. VO2 max is oxygen consumption divided by body weight, so it can rise through weight loss without real fitness gain. He also notes AHA and ACC statements recognizing high-volume intense exercise can have downsides.
