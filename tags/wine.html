PREDIMED follow-up: mild-to-moderate wine linked to 50% lower CV risk, not proven causal

Jun 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 37 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis describes a PREDIMED follow-up that tracked wine intake via a urinary tartaric acid biomarker. Mild-to-moderate wine consumption over a 9-year follow-up was associated with lower heart attack, stroke, heart failure and CV mortality, but he stresses causation is unproven and alcohol's role is actively debated.

CORDIOPREV: Mediterranean diet shrank carotid plaque and cut recurrent events about 25%

Mar 28, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 13 ↩ 27 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis summarizes CORDIOPREV, an RCT of 1002 coronary patients comparing a Mediterranean diet rich in extra virgin olive oil with a low-fat diet. The Mediterranean diet reduced carotid intima-media thickness at 5 and 7 years while the low-fat diet showed no change, and also lowered carotid plaque height. He also cites roughly 25% fewer recurrent major cardiovascular events over 7 years, with benefits seen despite statin treatment and mean LDL-C below 90.

Meta-analysis of 4.3 million: moderate wine not linked to higher overall cancer risk

Jan 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 17 ↩ 3 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis shares Dr. Brian Lawenda's post on a Frontiers in Nutrition meta-analysis of 73 studies, covering over 4.3 million participants, that found moderate wine consumption (up to one drink/day for women, two for men) was not associated with increased overall cancer risk and might lower some cancer types. The post questions treating wine like beer and spirits.

Harvard: 100+ cohorts show 25-40% lower CVD risk with light-to-moderate alcohol

Dec 03, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 22 ↩ 7 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis quotes Harvard's Nutrition Source: more than 100 prospective studies show light-to-moderate drinking is linked to a 25-40% lower risk of heart attack, ischemic stroke, peripheral vascular disease, sudden cardiac death and CVD death, and Harvard epidemiologists consider it almost certainly causal. Above 4 drinks a day, risks of hypertension, arrhythmia, stroke, heart attack and death rise. An NIH-funded alcohol trial was stopped. This view is contested and observational.

Polyphenol-rich diets in 3,100 TwinsUK adults linked to better BP, lipids and CVD risk

Dec 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 30 · 💬 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis quotes a BMC Medicine study of more than 3,100 TwinsUK adults followed over a decade. Diets rich in tea, coffee, berries, nuts and whole grains were associated with healthier blood pressure and cholesterol and lower CVD risk scores, and urinary polyphenol metabolites showed the same pattern.

UNATI: 10,000-person randomized trial of moderate red wine vs reducing alcohol

Aug 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 3 ↩ 1 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

UNATI, led by Martinez-Gonzalez, will randomize about 10,000 drinkers (men 50-70, women 55-75, 3-40 drinks per week) to reduce alcohol or drink moderately (under 7/week women, under 14/week men, red wine with meals) for about 4 years. Outcomes include mortality, cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and dementia.

New 5-year RCT from the PREDIMED lead investigator will test red wine versus CVD

Dec 12, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 3 ↩ 7 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis describes a funded randomized trial at the University of Navarra led by PREDIMED's principal investigator, which will randomize physicians aged 50-75 to moderate red wine or an alcohol-free alternative for five years, with cancer and all-cause mortality as secondary endpoints. He reviews PREDIMED's finding of 30% lower CVD with a Mediterranean diet including wine, and that the Mediterranean diet loses up to 23.5% of its protective effect without wine, per Martinez-Gonzalez. Results are not yet available.

Habitual red wine drinkers had higher CAC but fewer cardiac events in a Brazilian cohort

Mar 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Dr. Daniel Chong summarizes a 2018 observational study of 200 red wine drinkers and 154 abstainers: drinkers had higher CAC yet MACE of 0.2 vs 1.5 per 100 person-years, and 79% lower MACE after adjustment. Limitations include male participants and residual confounding.

Meta-analysis of 76 studies finds no link between wine drinking and cancer risk

Dec 03, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 ↩ 3 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis highlights a Frontiers in Nutrition systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 epidemiological studies, which found no association between wine consumption and any cancer and a protective trend for pancreatic, skin, lung, brain and overall cancer. He cautions that observational data cannot prove wine is harmless, and advises staying within guidelines of at most 2 drinks a day for men and 1 for women.

CORDIOPREV: Mediterranean diet reversed carotid plaque and cut recurrent events ~25%

Apr 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis summarizes the 2021 CORDIOPREV RCT in patients with prior heart attack or stroke on standard medical care: a Mediterranean diet rich in olive oil, nuts and fish reduced carotid thickness at 5 and 7 years, unlike a low-fat diet, and reduced maximum plaque height. Recurrent major cardiovascular events were roughly 25% lower, even though participants were already on statins and other drugs.

AHA: up to 1-2 drinks a day shows no risk to possible benefit; heavy drinking is harmful

Nov 06, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 1 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis argues alcohol and CVD claims should rely on evidence, citing decades of prospective studies and the AHA's statement that low intake (no more than 1-2 drinks daily) carries no risk to possible benefit, while 3 or more drinks daily is consistently worse. He notes a randomized red wine trial is underway.

Mendelian randomization is a poor tool for studying alcohol and CVD

Nov 06, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 2 ↩ 1 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis argues that Mendelian randomization is poorly suited to foods and drinks because no gene forces someone to drink, and variants like ALDH2 affect behavior and are ethnically clustered. He cites a critique using alcohol and cardiovascular disease as the example.
