Age raises CVD death risk about 19.5x from 65 to 85, far above diabetes or smoking

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 20 ↩ 5 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

Louis compares hazard ratios from the SCORE O.P. risk model for people over 65. A hazard ratio of 1.16 per year of age compounds to about 19.5x higher CVD death risk between 65 and 85, far larger than diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure or LDL. The numbers used are for women; men are similar. (The post also writes this as 19,500%, which is a mislabel of 19.5x.)

Gly-Low glycation-lowering cocktail extended lifespan in mice

Nov 30, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 12 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

A Buck Institute study in Cell Reports tested a five-ingredient mix (nicotinamide, thiamine, pyridoxamine, alpha lipoic acid, piperine) that lowers glycation. In mice it extended lifespan, reduced appetite and body fat and improved insulin sensitivity.

REACT in NEJM: 57% of adults have atherosclerosis and age is the biggest risk factor

Aug 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 4 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis shares the REACT study in NEJM of more than 16,000 adults aged 18-70 without known CVD, imaged by vascular ultrasound and CT. About 57% had plaque, rising from 1 in 13 in their 20s to about 9 in 10 in their 60s. Men developed silent disease 5-10 years earlier than women. He concludes age is the biggest risk factor.

Glycation and AGEs in cardiovascular aging: Kapahi on methylglyoxal and the GLYLO suppleme

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis says he underestimated glycation and AGEs in aging after Pankaj Kapahi's work at the Buck Institute. Kapahi's post explains methylglyoxal as a reactive glycolysis byproduct that drives AGEs and aging, and describes GLYLO, a combination of lipoic acid, nicotinamide, piperine, pyridoxine and thiamine that lowered methylglyoxal and AGEs and extended mouse lifespan even when started late. Louis says Kapahi plans a paper showing it reduces arterial stiffening. Evidence is preclinical and the product is commercial.

JAPAN-ACS sub-study: statins lowered AGEs, which were linked to plaque progression

Sep 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Statins

A sub-study of the JAPAN-ACS trial found high baseline advanced glycation end products (AGEs) were independently associated with plaque progression, and statin therapy significantly lowered AGE levels. Louis notes the trial produced the largest plaque regression, about -17%, in a drug trial.
