Reliable medical knowledge does not require RCTs: smoking and trans fats as examples

Jan 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 How to read research

Louis argues that long RCTs are often unethical or impractical for diseases that take decades to develop, and that no human RCT showed smoking causes lung cancer or trans fats cause heart disease. Instead, causality is established by combining high-quality prospective cohorts, short-term biomarker RCTs and animal mechanistic studies, applying Bradford-Hill criteria, as with the Framingham Heart Study. He says much nutrition knowledge was built this way, so dismissing it for lacking long RCTs is mistaken.

US labels can show 0 g trans fat when under 0.5 g per serving; check for hydrogenated oils

Dec 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 19 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis explains that the USA lets manufacturers list 0 g trans fat when a serving has under 0.5 g, which can add up over several servings, so the ingredient list ('partially hydrogenated') is the reliable check. He notes natural ruminant trans fat in dairy and beef does not appear to share the harms. A WHO figure in the share says each 2% of energy from trans fat raises heart disease risk 25% and all-cause death 14%.

Processed meat has no safe intake level in Nature Medicine burden-of-proof review

Apr 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 11 Meat, eggs & dairy

A shared summary of the IHME Burden of Proof analysis of 70+ studies finding a dose-response between processed meat and health risks with no apparent safe threshold. Louis clarifies that processed meat means chemically transformed by salting, curing, smoking or fermenting, including 'uncured' celery-powder products.

Dietary fat and mortality meta-analysis: polyunsaturated favorable, trans fat harmful

Dec 01, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Summarizes a meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (over 1 million participants) on dietary fat and mortality. Higher total, monounsaturated and especially polyunsaturated fat were associated with lower mortality, trans fat with higher mortality, and saturated fat showed a non-linear pattern with excess risk up to about 11% of energy.

Fat type beats amount: PUFA tied to lower mortality, trans and saturated fat to higher

Aug 24, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 10 · 💬 8 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A 2020 Clinical Nutrition meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (1,013,273 participants, 195,515 deaths) found polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fats associated with lower mortality, and saturated and trans fats with higher. Gokhan cautions that data guide rather than prescribe.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.

Harvard: unsaturated fats protect, trans fats harm; PREDIMED backs olive oil and nuts

Sep 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis quotes Harvard's Nutrition Source page correcting myths about dietary fat. It says polyunsaturated fats from vegetable oils, nuts and seeds help ward off type 2 diabetes, trans fats do the opposite, and the PREDIMED Mediterranean diet with olive oil or nuts reduced cardiovascular events.

French fries, but not other potato dishes, linked to higher diabetes risk

Aug 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis reports that Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study analysis found french fries raised diabetes risk over 30 years, apparently due to trans fats, while boiled or baked potatoes did not versus the standard diet. Versus whole grains, all white potatoes were linked to higher risk.

Harvard review: fat not clearly linked to cancer; red and processed meat raise colon risk

Dec 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 2 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis quotes the Harvard Nutrition Source summary on dietary fat and cancer. Total fat percentage and fat types show no clear association with cancer, and the Women's Health Initiative low-fat trial did not reduce breast or colon cancer. Red and processed meat do increase colon cancer risk, and animal fat in young women was linked to higher breast cancer risk in Nurses' Health Study II. He notes a later Harvard study updates this.

Over 100 randomized trials show polyunsaturated seed-oil fats improve insulin sensitivity

Nov 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis states that over 100 human randomized trials in aggregate show polyunsaturated fats improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control. The shared FoundMyFitness post adds that these omega-6 fats can lower LDL and have not been shown to raise inflammation in trials, but repeatedly reheated oil forms harmful oxidation products.

Dairy's natural trans fats may lack the risks of industrial trans fats, review suggests

A shared article reports that milk, butter, yogurt and cheese contain small amounts of naturally occurring trans fats, and that a large review suggests these do not carry the cardiovascular risks tied to industrial trans fats. No numbers or review details are given in the post.
