Lp(a) carries LDL cholesterol, so LDL-C on a lab report includes Lp(a) cholesterol

Jun 14, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 49 ↩ 3 Lp(a)

Louis explains with a boats-and-cargo analogy that Lp(a) particles carry LDL cholesterol just as LDL particles do, so LDL-C counts cholesterol from both. In people with very high Lp(a), a significant part of reported LDL-C actually comes from Lp(a). He adds that Lp(a) seems dangerous partly because it deposits LDL cholesterol into artery walls.

Lp(a) carries LDL cholesterol, so lab LDL-C includes cholesterol from Lp(a) particles

Oct 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 37 · 💬 23 Lp(a)

Louis explains that Lp(a) particles are 'boats' carrying the same LDL cholesterol 'cargo' as LDL particles, so a lab LDL-C value includes cholesterol carried by Lp(a); in people with very high Lp(a), a significant portion of LDL-C comes from Lp(a). Lp(a)'s sticky apo(a) protein deposits cholesterol in artery walls and is prothrombotic.

ApoB is the 'sign' on atherogenic particles; LDL and Lp(a) carry LDL cholesterol

Jun 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 10 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis extends a boat-and-cargo analogy: LDL, VLDL and Lp(a) particles carry an ApoB tag that makes them atherogenic, and an ApoB test counts the total number of such particles. LDL and Lp(a) particles carry LDL cholesterol, while VLDL carries triglyceride, so LDL-C on a lab report includes cholesterol carried by Lp(a).

High Lp(a) risk offset by about 19 mg/dL lower lifelong LDL-C in UK Biobank analysis

Oct 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 6 Lp(a)

Louis shares a TCTMD report on a Ference analysis of 445,765 UK Biobank participants using genetic (natural randomization) methods. Lifelong high Lp(a) was associated with a projected 37% higher risk of major coronary events, completely offset by lower lifetime LDL-C; the later LDL lowering starts, the larger the reduction required.

Very low LDL-C cancels much of the risk from high Lp(a), possibly explaining HORIZON

Sep 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 3 Lp(a)

Louis explains that Lp(a) carries LDL cholesterol, so lowering LDL-C reduces the cholesterol available to deposit in arteries even from Lp(a). He speculates this may help explain why the HORIZON Lp(a) trial failed: once LDL-C is low enough, there may be too little to cause major damage.
