Nattokinase and PAI-1: how it may work and why PAI-1 matters

Dec 03, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 14 · 💬 16 ↩ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Notes that nattokinase appears to act by inhibiting PAI-1, a marker linking thrombosis, inflammation, metabolic disorder and aging. People who genetically make less PAI-1 live about 7 years longer, and PAI-1 inhibition helped fatty liver and dyslipidemia in preclinical work.

Olive oil may reduce clotting by raising apoA-IV, which limits platelet aggregation

Jul 02, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 10 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A Life Extension article describes mechanistic studies in which apoA-IV, a protective HDL component, reduces platelet aggregation and thrombosis, and monounsaturated fats such as extra virgin olive oil raise apoA-IV, especially after meals. This is mechanistic and partly animal evidence.

Aging stem cells open a direct platelet pathway that raises thrombosis risk (Cell 2024)

Jun 09, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 12 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Shares the abstract of a Cell paper showing that with age, hematopoietic stem cells increasingly make platelets through a direct route that bypasses the usual progenitor cascade. The platelets from this pathway are more prone to thrombosis and cause higher platelet counts. It offers a possible cellular explanation for why clotting-related cardiovascular disease rises so steeply in older people.

Louis shares a summary of a Life Metabolism study in 160 coronary artery disease participants plus human blood and mice. Intermittent fasting appeared to inhibit platelet activation and clot formation through increased gut microbial production of indole-3-propionic acid.

Louis shares a 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis finding that people with post-COVID syndrome have an increased risk of thrombosis. This raises risk of heart attack, stroke, DVT and pulmonary embolism.

Lp(a) makes clots form more easily and resist breakdown via OxPL and apo(a)

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Lp(a)

Louis shares a summary of an ATVB commentary by Rafael Zubiran explaining why high Lp(a) may be worse than LDL. Its oxidized phospholipids activate monocytes to make tissue factor, and apo(a) resembles plasminogen and competes with clot breakdown, producing denser, harder-to-dissolve clots. Louis notes statins, especially atorvastatin, may neutralize OxPL and aspirin lowers clotting risk.

How Lp(a) captures and carries oxidized phospholipids through its apo(a) tail

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Lp(a)

A detailed explainer on how Lp(a) is the main carrier of oxidized phospholipids (OxPL) in the blood, which may be largely responsible for the damage Lp(a) causes. Lp(a) is an LDL-like particle with apoB-100 plus an apo(a) tail made of kringle domains, and a lysine binding site in kringle IV type 10 binds OxPL.
