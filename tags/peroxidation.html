Pomegranate juice and heart health: CIMT, blood pressure and ACE evidence reviewed

Jan 15, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 15 · 💬 16 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A review of pomegranate juice and cardiovascular disease credits its polyphenols (ellagitannins, punicalagins, anthocyanins). It cites a study where juice for up to a year reduced carotid intima-media thickness by up to 35% in carotid stenosis patients while controls rose 10%, and a meta-analysis of 8 RCTs showing lower blood pressure.

Monkey study links iron buildup ('ferro-aging') to organ decline; vitamin C may protect

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 19 ↩ 2 Aging & longevity

A Cell Metabolism paper describes chronic age-related iron buildup in liver, heart, lung, brain and muscle driving ACSL4-mediated lipid peroxidation and senescence in a 40-month monkey study. Louis suggests keeping ferritin toward the low-normal end if no iron deficiency, with vitamin C possibly protective.

Super-oxidized fish oil fed for 7 weeks caused no harm in human trial

Sep 17, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 7 Supplements & vitamins

The poster shares a Bill Harris clip arguing oxidized fish oil is likely not harmful. In the trial described, researchers deliberately super-oxidized fish oil and gave it to participants for 7 weeks, finding no difference in oxidation, peroxidation, inflammation, glucose, lipids or liver enzymes versus non-oxidized fish oil.

Vitamin C reduced iron-driven 'ferro-aging' in monkeys by blocking ACSL4 lipid damage

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 11 Aging & longevity

Louis shares an article summarizing a monkey study in which vitamin C was the most effective of 100 ferroptosis-related molecules screened at reducing lipid damage from ACSL4. He notes the article's headline is overstated.

Human trials: omega-6 and oxidized fish oil do not raise oxidation or inflammation markers

Sep 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 4 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

The post states that human randomized trials have repeatedly shown omega-6 fats do not cause oxidation or lipid peroxidation, and that oxidized fish oil does not raise inflammation, oxidation or peroxidation markers or worsen cardiometabolic or liver health. It shares a saturated fat versus omega-6 breakdown by Dr Alan Flanagan.
