Rapid heartbeat is most often AFib, but coronary blockage is another common cause

Mar 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 13 Heart rhythm & heart failure

Louis shares Mayo Clinic and Cleveland Clinic information that atrial fibrillation is the most common type of tachycardia and that ischemic heart disease from atherosclerotic blockages is the most common cause of ventricular tachycardia. He advises seeing a cardiologist promptly if these symptoms occur.

Mayo Clinic study: skin generates senescent cells that may age the heart and other organs

Oct 23, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 2 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a Mayo Clinic study reported by Neuroscience News suggesting skin makes senescent cells that travel to other tissues, including the cardiovascular system, and accelerate aging. He adds that most circulating CRP originates in skin and advises caring for your skin to lower inflammation. The linked article is framed around brain aging, and the CRP claim is Louis's own.

Mayo Clinic: over half of heart attacks in women under 65 had nontraditional causes

Sep 28, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 1 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

A Mayo Clinic study reports many heart attacks in people under 65, especially women, stem from causes other than clogged arteries, such as spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) and embolism. It challenges assumptions about how heart attacks occur in younger people.

Mayo Clinic: senescent cells drive inflammation in severe fatty liver disease (MASH)

Dec 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares a Mayo Clinic report that senescent 'zombie' cells trigger inflammation that accelerates MASH, and speculates that senolytic drugs or supplements could be tested. The post is a news summary of mechanistic research with no trial data.

Chelation therapy for heart disease: TACT showed some signal, TACT2 did not confirm it

Mar 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Louis quotes Mayo Clinic on EDTA chelation. The TACT trial found no support for routine use but a possible benefit in people with diabetes and prior heart attack; the follow-up TACT2 in that group did not show lower risk of heart attack, stroke or related events.

Targeting senescent-cell inflammation (senomorphics) may beat killing the cells

Jul 31, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 Aging & longevity

Louis says senescent cells cause multiple diseases including CVD and argues that shutting off their inflammation may be better than senolytics. Mayo researchers found a mitochondrial pathway driving this inflammation.
