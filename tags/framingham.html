Reliable medical knowledge does not require RCTs: smoking and trans fats as examples

Jan 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 How to read research

Louis argues that long RCTs are often unethical or impractical for diseases that take decades to develop, and that no human RCT showed smoking causes lung cancer or trans fats cause heart disease. Instead, causality is established by combining high-quality prospective cohorts, short-term biomarker RCTs and animal mechanistic studies, applying Bradford-Hill criteria, as with the Framingham Heart Study. He says much nutrition knowledge was built this way, so dismissing it for lacking long RCTs is mistaken.

Higher midlife vitamin D linked to lower brain tau 16 years later in Framingham

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Brain health & dementia

A Framingham Heart Study analysis followed 793 adults for 16 years, starting at average age 39. Vitamin D was measured in 2002-2005 and brains were scanned in 2016-2019, and higher midlife vitamin D was linked to lower tau tangles but not to amyloid. The post notes vitamin D acts like a steroid hormone with receptors in memory regions.

MIND diet: 20% less grey matter loss per 3-point score rise in Framingham MRI study

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 1 Brain health & dementia

In 1,647 Framingham Offspring participants followed about 12 years with repeated MRI scans, closer adherence to the MIND diet (Mediterranean-DASH hybrid) was linked to slower grey matter loss. Each 3-point rise in MIND score matched 20% less age-related grey matter decline, equal to about 2.5 years of slower brain aging. This is an observational association.
