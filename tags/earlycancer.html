Cancer is a disease of aging; young-onset colorectal rise is small in absolute terms

Dec 30, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 11 ↩ 1 Cancer

Louis agrees with Dr. Andrea Love that cancer is many diseases whose main common factor is age, so prevention is best served by slowing aging via diet, exercise and lifestyle. The shared post says the chance of being diagnosed with colorectal cancer under 50 is 0.3%, and the recent increase is small in absolute terms, though still worth lowering.

JAMA Patient Page on risk factors for early-onset gastrointestinal cancers

Oct 01, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Cancer

Shares a JAMA Patient Page on early-onset gastrointestinal cancer, which is the fastest-rising early-onset cancer in the US. It covers risk factors, symptoms, management, prognosis and prevention, with a focus on colon cancer in people under 50.

FIT screening starting at age 40-49 reduces colorectal cancer incidence and mortality

Jun 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Cancer

Louis shares a JAMA finding that starting fecal immunochemical test screening for colorectal cancer at ages 40 to 49 leads to reductions in colorectal cancer mortality and incidence. No numbers are given.

Early-onset cancer incidence doubled since 1992 but mortality is stable: overdiagnosis

Nov 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Cancer

Louis shares a JAMA Internal Medicine analysis finding that rising cancer rates in people under 50 largely reflect more diagnostic scrutiny, because deaths have not risen. Colon and endometrial cancers are exceptions, with small genuine increases in death rates of under 1 per 100,000.

Adenoma detection rates support lowering colorectal cancer screening age to 45

Jun 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Cancer

A shared JAMA post from ASCO 2025 notes that adenoma detection rates were similar in people aged 45-49 and 50-54, with slightly lower prevalence in the younger group, supporting screening from age 45.
