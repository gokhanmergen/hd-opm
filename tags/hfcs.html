Cane-sugar Coke is no healthier: sucrose splits into glucose and fructose just like HFCS

Jul 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 18 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

In response to news that Coca-Cola would use cane sugar in US cola, Louis explains that sucrose is exactly 50% glucose and 50% fructose and is split by sucrase almost immediately, much like HFCS. He warns members not to think it makes soda healthier.

Cane sugar and high-fructose corn syrup are both roughly 50% glucose and 50% fructose

Jul 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 2 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

The shared explainer notes that sucrose is one glucose bonded to one fructose, and HFCS is also about half glucose and half fructose, so the body handles them almost the same. It concludes that swapping HFCS for cane sugar in sweetened drinks does not change health impact meaningfully.

Switching Coca-Cola to cane sugar is no healthier; avoid sugary soda either way

Jul 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 1 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

A shared opinion argues that pushing Coca-Cola to use cane sugar rather than corn syrup encourages unnecessary sugar consumption and is no healthier. It is a short opinion without citations.

25 RCTs: swapping corn syrup for cane sugar changes few cardiometabolic markers

Jul 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis shares a video describing an analysis of 25 randomized controlled trials that found replacing high-fructose corn syrup with cane sugar did not significantly change most cardiometabolic health markers. Little additional detail is provided.
