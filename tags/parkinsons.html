APOE4 drives cholesterol buildup in brain astrocytes, promoting toxic protein deposits

Oct 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 4 Brain health & dementia

Mount Sinai researchers using human stem-cell-derived miBrain tissue found APOE4 causes cholesterol to accumulate in astrocytes, impairing lysosomal clearance of alpha-synuclein and promoting amyloid and alpha-synuclein deposits. A second study showed APOE4 damages brain blood vessels through mechanisms unrelated to cholesterol. The poster reads this as cholesterol being potentially causal in APOE4 carriers.

Why smokers have 40-60% lower Parkinson's risk: low-dose carbon monoxide hypothesis

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 6 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

The post explains that prospective studies show smokers have a much lower Parkinson's risk and that newer research points to very low-dose carbon monoxide rather than nicotine, possibly via HO-1 induction. He stresses there is no safe way to inhale CO and smoking is harmful, and suggests dietary strategies that trigger HO-1 instead.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.

Lewy body dementia is the second most common dementia, on a spectrum with Parkinson's

Louis explains that Lewy body dementia is often misdiagnosed as Alzheimer's, is closely related to Parkinson's, and is marked by alpha-synuclein clumping in the brain. The post was prompted by Ted Turner's death and has little cardiovascular relevance.

Bone injury may speed brain healing: tibia loading helped mice and pigs after brain injury

Oct 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

The poster notes that brain injury speeds bone healing and that new animal work shows the reverse: mild bone stress improves brain recovery. He speculates this could partly explain why heavy lifting and bone-stressing cardio are neuroprotective. The Nature Neuroscience study was in mice and pigs.

Shin bone loading may speed brain injury recovery via PIEZO1 in mice and pigs

Oct 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience paper found that compressing the tibia for a few minutes a day after traumatic brain injury improved movement and memory in mice and pigs. Bone cells sensed the force through the PIEZO1 sensor, and turning it off removed the benefit. The poster calls it the most interesting bone study he has read in a long time.

Nicotinamide riboside failed in phase 3 early Parkinson's trial, symptoms modestly worse

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a JAMA-reported phase 3 trial of oral nicotinamide riboside (NR), a popular NAD+ booster, in early Parkinson's disease. It did not improve outcomes versus placebo at 52 weeks and patients on NR had modestly worse MDS-UPDRS scores. Louis says it shows the supplement may have downsides in some conditions.

PD-STAT: simvastatin did not slow Parkinson's progression in a 235-person randomized trial

Mar 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Statins

Louis explains that the 2-year PD-STAT trial found simvastatin neither slowed nor accelerated Parkinson's disease progression versus placebo in 235 people with moderate disease at hospitals in England. Simvastatin was chosen partly because lipophilic statins cross the blood-brain barrier more easily.

Caffeinated coffee linked to 37% lower Parkinson's and 34% lower Alzheimer's risk

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A shared summary says that in one large study unsweetened caffeinated coffee was linked to a 37% lower risk of Parkinson's disease and a 34% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias. Caffeine blocks adenosine A2A receptors that regulate dopamine signaling in movement circuits. The evidence described is observational, with some animal mechanistic work.

Niacin (nicotinic acid) showed promise in a small Parkinson's trial, likely via brain NAD

Louis highlights a small 2021 human trial of niacin in Parkinson's disease, noting it is the form of vitamin B3 once used to lower cholesterol and that benefit may work through raising NAD in the brain. Nicotinamide riboside has similar small-trial results, including a 2023 high-dose safety trial.
