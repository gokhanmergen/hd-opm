Testosterone for anti-aging lacks proven longevity benefit; high levels tied to deaths

Mar 23, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 34 ↩ 1 Hormones, women's & men's health

Louis warns that the only proven benefit of testosterone therapy is treating clinically diagnosed hypogonadism and no credible study shows longevity benefit. He cites data from 3,690 men aged 70-92 showing an inverse U-shaped relation between testosterone and mortality, with risk rising above an optimal level, plus animal studies showing androgens shorten and castration extends lifespan. The animal evidence and sex-difference argument are suggestive, not proof in humans.

Tissues make their own cholesterol: even very low LDL-C does not impair hormone production

Sep 17, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 11 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that the blood holds only a small fraction of the body's cholesterol and that tissues, including the brain, mostly synthesize what they need. The steroid hormone demand supplied by blood is only tens of milligrams a day, easily met at very low LDL-C. He cites FOURIER and ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, in which LDL-C below 15-20 mg/dL did not impair adrenal or gonadal hormone production or vitamin D.

Blood carries only 2-5% of body cholesterol; cells and the brain make their own

May 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Shared breakdown of why blood LDL cholesterol is not a measure of what cells need. The body holds roughly 120-150 g of cholesterol but only 3-7 g is in blood, and the brain makes all of its own. One cited model suggests only about 50-90 mg/dL total blood cholesterol is needed to keep transport functional, versus a US average near 200.

Why the LMHR phenotype happens: starvation-like physiology in lean keto dieters

Jan 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 11 ↩ 9 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis proposes that the lean mass hyper-responder (LMHR) pattern, with extremely high LDL-C on keto/carnivore diets, reflects an ancient starvation response described in calorie-restriction literature. With near-zero body fat and no carbohydrate, the body acts as if starving, raising circulating LDL to deliver fat as fuel, lowering leptin, LDL receptors and thyroid and sex hormones. He links a trial showing fasting raised LDL and ApoB in healthy non-obese people, and cautions that overdoing restriction is the danger. This is a hypothesis, not proof of harm or safety.
