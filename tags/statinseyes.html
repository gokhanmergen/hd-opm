Statin use linked to 15% lower risk of open-angle glaucoma at 5 years

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Statins

A Medscape report says statin use was associated with a 15% lower risk of developing open-angle glaucoma at 5 years and a substantially reduced need for laser or surgery in patients who already had it. Associations were somewhat weaker at 10 years and seen with atorvastatin and rosuvastatin. Observational data.

Statin users had 15% lower glaucoma risk and needed fewer eye procedures

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Statins

Louis shares a report from the British Journal of Ophthalmology: among over 200,000 statin users and matched controls, statin users were 15% less likely to develop open-angle glaucoma over 5 years and 10% less over a decade. Among nearly 280,000 glaucoma patients, statin users needed fewer laser treatments and surgeries. The mechanism is unclear and the study is observational.

Statin use linked to lower open-angle glaucoma risk in a large UC San Diego database study

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Statins

A retrospective TriNetX cohort study reported in the British Journal of Ophthalmology found statin use was associated with lower open-angle glaucoma incidence and less need for treatment escalation. It is observational, so it shows association and not proof of protection.
