MESA: high Lp(a) raised CVD risk only when hsCRP was 2 mg/L or higher

Jul 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 12 ↩ 4 Lp(a)

In MESA (mean follow-up 13.6 years, 684 CVD events), high Lp(a) conferred no significant risk when hsCRP was below 2 mg/L. With hsCRP of at least 2, Lp(a) of 50-99.9 mg/dL had HR 1.36 and 100 or more had HR 2.09. Participants were lower-risk, averaged age 62, so applicability to others is unclear.

Top versus bottom quintile hazard ratios were 1.70 for hs-CRP, 1.36 for LDL and 1.33 for Lp(a), each independent, with the combination predicting best. The authors argue prevention should extend beyond 10-year risk estimates.

Meta-analysis of 26 RCTs: statins lowered hs-CRP by about 1 mg/L in patients with CVD

A 2022 meta-analysis pooled randomized trials and found statins significantly reduce hs-CRP and CRP in patients with cardiovascular disease, in both high-intensity and lower-intensity regimens, with treatment over 10 weeks. For someone with hs-CRP of 2 mg/L this is about a 50% drop.

What CRP and hs-CRP actually measure, and why a single reading is unreliable

Oct 27, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 5 Inflammation & CRP

Louis clarifies that CRP and hs-CRP are the same marker, with hs-CRP simply more sensitive for the low levels relevant to heart disease. Both reflect inflammation anywhere in the body, spike temporarily with infections, vaccination, injury, surgery or intense exercise, and fluctuate day to day, so the aim is to capture the quiet background level and not rely on one test.
