Low-carb high-fat diet linked to higher LDL-C, ApoB and 2.18-fold MACE risk at 11.8 years

May 04, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 24 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis quotes a JACC: Advances cohort analysis comparing low-carbohydrate high-fat (LCHF) eaters with standard-diet controls. LCHF had significantly higher LDL-C and ApoB, more severe hypercholesterolemia, and more major adverse cardiovascular events at 11.8 years; the association remained after adjustment. Very low-carb diets rarely push A1C below 5 because of physiological insulin resistance

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues that in people who already have low A1C, severe carb restriction makes muscles temporarily insulin resistant to spare glucose for heart and brain, so blood sugar can rise rather than fall. This 'adaptive glucose sparing' or 'pseudo-diabetes' also explains why people on long-term keto often fail glucose tolerance tests. Personal observation and physiology reasoning rather than a cited trial.

No long-lived population ate a carbohydrate-free diet; long-term effects unknown

Jun 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 4 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues that every long-lived population studied includes carbohydrate from vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains or fermented dairy, and that the few carbohydrate-free-eating groups, such as certain Eskimo populations, were not notably long-lived. He says low-carb diets may help short-term but long-term heart-disease effects are speculative, so he suggests including low-GI carbohydrates.

Low-carb diets lose their diabetes advantage by 12 months, trials and meta-analyses show

Nov 25, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 8 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Argues low-carb gives better remission and HbA1c at 6 months, but the edge fades. A 2021 BMJ meta-analysis (23 RCTs) found benefit at 6 months diminishing by 12; Silverii 2020 (37 RCTs, 3,301 patients) found HbA1c differences vanishing and reversing by 24 months; a 2-year calorie-matched RCT showed identical weight loss. Concludes the benefit comes from adherence, not metabolic magic.

CARDIA: low-carb diets in young adults tied to more coronary calcium if animal-based

Dec 01, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 3 · 💬 7 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Posts the abstract of a CARDIA analysis of 2,226 participants (mean age 40.4) followed 8.3 years. Higher carbohydrate intake was inversely associated with CAC progression (HR 0.731); an animal-based low-carb score predicted higher progression (HR 1.456) but a plant-based score did not (HR 1.016). 26.5% had CAC progression.

High-carb diet raised insulin yet people ate 689 fewer calories a day in an NIH ward study

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 0 · 💬 3 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis shares a post arguing that normal insulin spikes from healthy carbohydrate do not drive hunger or overeating. In a controlled NIH study, 20 adults lived in a research facility on a high-carb low-fat diet (about 75% carbohydrate) and a ketogenic diet (about 10% carbohydrate) for two weeks each. Despite higher glucose and insulin, they ate an average of 689 fewer calories per day on the high-carb diet, contradicting the carbohydrate-insulin model's prediction, although the diets differed in other ways.
