Statin muscle side effects may stem from HMG-CoA reductase gene variants

May 03, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 27 · 💬 24 ↩ 3 Statins

Louis shares an Atlantic article on a proposed mechanism for statin-related muscle symptoms: people at higher risk likely carry variants in genes coding for HMG-CoA reductase, which statins then further inhibit. Extreme versions of the same gene defects cause a very rare limb girdle muscular dystrophy. Louis calls it a definitive answer; the article is journalism rather than a primary paper.

What to try for statin muscle pain: lower dose, other statins, non-statins; beware nocebo

Feb 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 18 Statins

Louis explains that statin muscle pain is often genetic, linked to HMG-CoA reductase gene variants, so CoQ10 usually fails though it is harmless. Practical steps include lowering the dose, switching statins (pitavastatin or pravastatin often tolerated) or using non-statin drugs, while recognizing that much reported muscle pain may be nocebo.

Elevated creatine kinase on a statin means muscle symptoms are real, not nocebo

Apr 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 28 Statins

Louis explains that significantly elevated creatine kinase on a statin, after ruling out exercise, infection, trauma or hypothyroidism, indicates a genuine statin effect that is uncommon and more serious than ordinary myalgia.

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.

Statin muscle toxicity overstated: rhabdomyolysis ~1 in 100,000; sarcopenia use studied

Dec 16, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 14 ↩ 2 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle at normal doses in most people. Statin myopathy occurs in genetically susceptible people, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Low-dose statins and metformin are being studied for sarcopenia via hormetic mitochondrial effects.

Serious statin muscle side effects (myopathy) are rare; benign aches are common

Jun 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 5 Statins

Louis notes that a large new study confirms benign muscle aches are fairly common but serious statin-related muscle problems are rare. The shared Duke post says concern about side effects exceeds the evidence and points to the PREVENTABLE trial of statins in older adults without cardiovascular disease.
