Adding ezetimibe to a statin lowers CRP by an extra 10% in pooled trials

Nov 09, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 9 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Pooled analysis of placebo-controlled ezetimibe trials found ezetimibe alone lowered CRP numerically more than placebo (6%, not significant), while added to statin therapy it gave a significant extra 10% CRP reduction. Gokhan lists related studies on ezetimibe's anti-inflammatory effects.

Statin tendon problems are possible but uncommon; consider other causes and simvastatin

Jun 03, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 8 Statins

Louis shares cardiologist Paul Thompson's answer on whether statins cause tendon rupture: it is theoretically possible but quite uncommon, and high cholesterol itself can cause tendinopathy (consider familial hypercholesterolemia in young athletes with Achilles tendinitis). Louis adds that if someone suspects a statin-related tendon issue, switching to simvastatin might be worth discussing since some evidence suggests it may protect tendons.

Two RCTs found statins neither helped nor harmed Alzheimer's disease progression

Jun 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 5 ↩ 3 Statins

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as a treatment for established Alzheimer's: simvastatin in 406 people with normal lipids over 18 months, and atorvastatin 80 mg (LEADe) in people already on donepezil over 72 weeks. Both were neutral on cognition and safe.

Which statin to choose: atorvastatin, rosuvastatin and other options by patient type

A shared infographic summarizes how statins work and how the choice varies by patient: atorvastatin after MI/ACS and usable in CKD, rosuvastatin as the most potent with few interactions. General reference without study data.

Two RCTs of statins in Alzheimer's disease were neutral: no benefit, no harm

Aug 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Statins

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as treatment for existing Alzheimer's disease, one with simvastatin and one with atorvastatin. Neither slowed progression, and neither worsened it. This concerns treating established AD, not preventing it.
