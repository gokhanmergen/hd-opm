DISCO-CT: DASH diet plus statins reversed ~40 mm3 of soft plaque in a year on CT

Aug 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 38 · 💬 21 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

In the DISCO-CT randomized trial, a DASH-style diet with statins reduced non-calcified plaque volume by 39.5 mm3 (adjusted to one year from 66 weeks), the largest dietary regression on CT angiography to date. An at-risk person typically grows about 5 mm3 per year, so this equals roughly 8 years of progression. DASH fat was ~30% of calories, mostly unsaturated.

Sabatini on diet and mTOR: limit amino acids and insulin; ~18 h fasting may inhibit mTORC1

Louis shares notes on a David Sabatini interview about how diet affects mTOR and autophagy. Sabatini suggests low amino acid or low carbohydrate diets lower mTOR, favors a higher-carbohydrate diet of low-glycemic carbs, and thinks mTORC1 senses total daily insulin. He guesses about 18 hours of fasting inhibits mTORC1, while stressing nobody has all the answers.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.

Mediterranean-like macronutrient mix lowered estimated biological age in 4 weeks

Sep 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Aging & longevity

A University of Sydney study in 104 adults aged 65-75 found three diet groups reduced estimated biological age after four weeks, with the strongest evidence for an omnivorous diet of 28-29% fat, 14% protein and 53% carbohydrate. Biological age was estimated from 20 biomarkers including cholesterol, insulin and C-reactive protein.

Harvard review: fat not clearly linked to cancer; red and processed meat raise colon risk

Dec 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 2 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis quotes the Harvard Nutrition Source summary on dietary fat and cancer. Total fat percentage and fat types show no clear association with cancer, and the Women's Health Initiative low-fat trial did not reduce breast or colon cancer. Red and processed meat do increase colon cancer risk, and animal fat in young women was linked to higher breast cancer risk in Nurses' Health Study II. He notes a later Harvard study updates this.
