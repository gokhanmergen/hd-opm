MESA: high Lp(a) raised CVD risk only when hsCRP was 2 mg/L or higher

Jul 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 12 ↩ 4 Lp(a)

In MESA (mean follow-up 13.6 years, 684 CVD events), high Lp(a) conferred no significant risk when hsCRP was below 2 mg/L. With hsCRP of at least 2, Lp(a) of 50-99.9 mg/dL had HR 1.36 and 100 or more had HR 2.09. Participants were lower-risk, averaged age 62, so applicability to others is unclear.

In MESA, high Lp(a) mattered mainly when CAC was 100 or higher

Jun 01, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 15 ↩ 5 Lp(a)

Louis shares a new MESA analysis suggesting that elevated Lp(a) raises 10-year heart attack and stroke risk mainly in people with CAC of 100 or more. With CAC between 1 and 99, high versus low Lp(a) mattered less, and a zero CAC appeared to 'trump' high Lp(a) over a decade.

MESA: high Lp(a) raised ASCVD risk 2.34-fold only in people with large waist-to-hip ratio

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 5 ↩ 4 Lp(a)

Louis summarizes a MESA cohort analysis in Progress in Cardiovascular Diseases showing waist-to-hip ratio (a visceral-fat and liver-fat proxy) modifies the ASCVD risk of Lp(a), while BMI did not. Lp(a) of 50 mg/dL or more with high WHR carried the excess risk, which mostly vanished when WHR was low. He stresses this is a prospective cohort, so it cannot prove causality, but suggests losing visceral fat is important with high Lp(a).

Urinary metals linked to coronary calcium comparable to smoking and diabetes in MESA

Sep 18, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 8 · 💬 5 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

A JACC study in 6,418 MESA participants followed over 10 years found higher urinary levels of non-essential (cadmium, tungsten, uranium) and essential (cobalt, copper, zinc) metals corresponded to more coronary artery calcification, with associations similar in size to traditional risk factors like smoking and diabetes.

MESA: high Lp(a) mostly matters when CAC is 100 or above

Jul 18, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

A repost of a MESA analysis suggesting that people with high Lp(a), CAC under 100 and hsCRP under 2 mg/L have 10-year risk not much above those with normal Lp(a). The highest risk is with elevated Lp(a) and CAC of 100 or more. Louis notes this is a cohort study, not an RCT.
