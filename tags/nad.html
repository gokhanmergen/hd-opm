Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.

Blood NAD+ is a useless aging biomarker: stable across seven cohorts (Nature Metabolism)

May 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

Louis argues that Charles Brenner is vindicated by a Nature Metabolism study quantifying NAD+ in seven human cohorts by mass spectrometry. Whole-blood NAD+ stayed stable with age and lifestyle and changed only with nicotinamide riboside supplementation, so blood NAD+ tests do not reflect tissue levels.

Vitamin B3 (nicotinamide) shows early promise for protecting retinal cells in glaucoma

A shared summary says replenishing NAD+ with vitamin B3 may help retinal ganglion cells keep their energy supply and function longer in glaucoma. A 2020 randomized clinical trial of 57 people with glaucoma is cited as human evidence. Evidence is early and not specific to heart health.

Whole-blood NAD+ does not change with age or lifestyle, so it is a poor aging marker

May 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis, echoing Charles Brenner, shares a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that human whole-blood NAD+ is stable across age and not altered by exercise or lifestyle interventions. Nicotinamide riboside raised NAD+, but that does not prove clinical or longevity benefit; tissue NAD matters.

Nicotinic acid (vitamin B3) may prevent or treat glaucoma, possibly via NAD

Louis notes growing evidence that nicotinic acid, the niacin form used to lower cholesterol, may prevent or treat glaucoma, a disease with no effective treatment that causes irreversible blindness. Nicotinamide riboside failed in phase 3 early Parkinson's trial, symptoms modestly worse

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a JAMA-reported phase 3 trial of oral nicotinamide riboside (NR), a popular NAD+ booster, in early Parkinson's disease. It did not improve outcomes versus placebo at 52 weeks and patients on NR had modestly worse MDS-UPDRS scores. Louis says it shows the supplement may have downsides in some conditions.
