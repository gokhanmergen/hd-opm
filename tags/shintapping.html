Bone injury may speed brain healing: tibia loading helped mice and pigs after brain injury

Oct 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

The poster notes that brain injury speeds bone healing and that new animal work shows the reverse: mild bone stress improves brain recovery. He speculates this could partly explain why heavy lifting and bone-stressing cardio are neuroprotective. The Nature Neuroscience study was in mice and pigs.

Shin bone loading may speed brain injury recovery via PIEZO1 in mice and pigs

Oct 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience paper found that compressing the tibia for a few minutes a day after traumatic brain injury improved movement and memory in mice and pigs. Bone cells sensed the force through the PIEZO1 sensor, and turning it off removed the benefit. The poster calls it the most interesting bone study he has read in a long time.

Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.
