Dr Brad Stanfield: 'normal' systolic BP near 140 raises dementia and CVD risk

Feb 09, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 12 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

Summary of a Dr Brad Stanfield video linking high blood pressure to dementia. Citing SPRINT and a follow-up study, it says lowering systolic pressure below 120 reduces heart attacks, strokes, death and dementia, and recommends salt under 1500 mg/day, the DASH diet, exercise, weight control and medication when needed.

A video summary of the CORDIOPREV study comparing a Mediterranean diet with a low-fat diet in coronary heart disease patients. The Mediterranean arm showed less plaque buildup, mostly in the first five years, and fewer heart attacks and strokes.

Optimal diets vary: Mediterranean first, with individualized low-carb or low-fat options

Jun 30, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 14 · 💬 2 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Gokhan says no scientist can tell you the single best diet. The Mediterranean diet has the strongest evidence for average people, while those with insulin resistance may try low-carb or low-fat vegan diets. Dietary saturated fat can raise cholesterol sharply in some people, and monitoring weight, blood markers and blood pressure matters more than the specific diet.

Olive oil raised GLP-1 and lowered post-meal glucose versus butter in type 1 diabetes

Aug 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Olive oil, like all fats, raises GLP-1, and the quoted crossover data suggest it does so more than butter, helping people feel full sooner. After a high-glycemic meal in people with type 1 diabetes, glucose exposure was lower with EVOO than with butter or a low-fat meal, plausibly via slower gastric emptying and glucose-lipid competition.

Harvard cohorts: diet quality matters more than carb-versus-fat ratio for coronary disease

Feb 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Karl Nadolsky summarizes a JACC analysis of about 200,000 US adults followed up to 30+ years in the Nurses' Health Studies and HPFS. Both low-carb and low-fat diets looked beneficial when plant-based or healthy, and worse when animal-based or processed.
