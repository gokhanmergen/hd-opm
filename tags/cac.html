Calcified plaque is a marker, the goal should be reversing soft plaque

Aug 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 44 · 💬 51 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis argues a high CAC score predicts risk mainly because calcified plaque reflects the amount of soft plaque, which is the dangerous part. He says to focus on reversing or stabilizing soft plaque rather than lowering CAC, and notes randomized outcome trials are needed.

25 statin trials: about 40% fewer heart attacks and strokes per 75 mg/dL LDL drop

Mar 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 37 · 💬 35 Statins

Louis shares a graph of all 25 statin RCTs up to 2016 (excluding those under 6 months or under 50 events), showing about 40% lower MACE per 75 mg/dL LDL reduction, with larger effects in the 7 primary prevention trials. In his own comments he argues calcified plaque is the stable endpoint of soft plaque, and that statins and exercise help by preventing new soft plaque, regressing it and stabilizing the remainder through calcification.

A zero CAC score does not rule out blockages: 54% of 23,759 symptomatic patients had CAC 0

Jan 01, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 37 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis challenges the myth that a CAC of zero means no risk. In the Western Denmark Heart Registry of 23,759 symptomatic patients undergoing CT angiography, 54% had CAC zero, and half of people with obstructive blockages have zero CAC. Soft plaque can grow without calcification, so CAC shows late-stage disease and is poor for early detection.

High CAC score is not a death sentence: weight loss, LDL below 70, diet and exercise

Louis tells group members with high CAC that the risk is high but manageable: about 30 lb weight loss clears liver and pancreas fat and can reverse soft plaque, LDL below 70 mg/dL often halts or reverses plaque, a Mediterranean diet has the most evidence, and exercise lowers risk at the same CAC.

Vitamin K2 activates matrix Gla protein to limit arterial calcification, review says

Sep 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 21 Supplements & vitamins
