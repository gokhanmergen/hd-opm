Taurine deficiency may drive aging; Columbia study spans worms, mice, monkeys and humans

Mar 01, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 23 · 💬 28 ↩ 4 Aging & longevity

Louis highlights a 2023 Columbia University study on taurine and aging in worms, mice, monkeys and humans, noting taurine also benefits the cardiovascular system. He stresses that age is the biggest causal risk factor for heart disease and taurine might be an anti-aging intervention, with emphasis on 'might', and that it seems reasonably safe.

Monkey study links iron buildup ('ferro-aging') to organ decline; vitamin C may protect

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 19 ↩ 2 Aging & longevity

A Cell Metabolism paper describes chronic age-related iron buildup in liver, heart, lung, brain and muscle driving ACSL4-mediated lipid peroxidation and senescence in a 40-month monkey study. Louis suggests keeping ferritin toward the low-normal end if no iron deficiency, with vitamin C possibly protective.

40-month Cell study: metformin slowed aging clocks in male monkeys, brain age down ~6 yrs

Sep 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis reposts the Cell paper abstract on a 40-month trial of metformin in adult male cynomolgus monkeys. Metformin slowed pan-tissue aging clocks, notably about a 6-year regression in brain aging, preserved brain structure and improved cognition, partly through Nrf2 activation in neurons. Only males were studied.

Metformin slowed brain aging in monkeys, with neurons resembling those 6 years younger

Sep 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 11 ↩ 6 Aging & longevity

Louis flags a Cell study, covered by Nature, in which male monkeys on daily metformin for over three years showed slower age-related brain decline, enhanced cognition and preserved liver function. Their neuronal activity resembled monkeys about six years younger (around 18 human years). The study was small, and human benefit is unproven.

Mouse taurine doses of 500-1000 mg/kg scale to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg human

Oct 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 7 Aging & longevity

Louis explains that the 2023 Columbia taurine study used 500-1000 mg/kg in mice, and that allometric scaling for faster mouse metabolism converts this to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg person. He suggests that is the range to test in human trials, since mouse mg/kg doses cannot be applied directly to humans.

Monkey metformin dose of 20 mg/kg scales to only about 450 mg in a 70 kg human

Sep 13, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 8 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis calculates that the cynomolgus monkey aging study used 20 mg/kg metformin daily for 40 months. Using the FDA allometric scaling factor of 3.1, this is about 6.45 mg/kg, or roughly 450 mg for a 70 kg person, about a quarter of the 2 g typical diabetic dose.

Taurine is not a reliable aging biomarker, says new multi-species study

Jun 07, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

A shared summary of a large longitudinal study across humans (26-100 years), monkeys and mice finds taurine levels do not consistently fall with age, often rise (especially in women), and vary more between individuals than with age. The post notes that taurine, like NAD+ boosters and resveratrol, shows promise in animals but human evidence is limited.
