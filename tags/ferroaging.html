Should you take iron for low-normal ferritin? Probably not without symptoms or a cause

Mar 21, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 24 ↩ 2 Risk assessment & lab tests

Louis discusses low-normal ferritin with no anemia or symptoms: see a doctor to find sources of iron loss, treat true iron deficiency or anemia, but in otherwise healthy asymptomatic people he thinks extra iron is generally a bad idea. He notes iron deficiency can promote clotting and reduce oxygen delivery.

Monkey study links iron buildup ('ferro-aging') to organ decline; vitamin C may protect

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 19 ↩ 2 Aging & longevity

A Cell Metabolism paper describes chronic age-related iron buildup in liver, heart, lung, brain and muscle driving ACSL4-mediated lipid peroxidation and senescence in a 40-month monkey study. Louis suggests keeping ferritin toward the low-normal end if no iron deficiency, with vitamin C possibly protective.

Vitamin C reduced iron-driven 'ferro-aging' in monkeys by blocking ACSL4 lipid damage

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 11 Aging & longevity

Louis shares an article summarizing a monkey study in which vitamin C was the most effective of 100 ferroptosis-related molecules screened at reducing lipid damage from ACSL4. He notes the article's headline is overstated.
