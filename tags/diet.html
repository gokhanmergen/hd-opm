Full-fat yogurt lowered ApoB while low-fat yogurt plus butter raised it in a crossover RCT

Jun 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 15 ↩ 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis summarizes a randomized crossover trial matching saturated fat between full-fat yogurt and low-fat yogurt with added butter. Despite equal saturated fat, the butter arm raised ApoB while full-fat yogurt lowered it, which the study attributes to the milk fat globule membrane (MFGM) that is intact in yogurt but lost in butter. He suggests this may partly explain why yogurt looks better than other dairy in epidemiology.

High protein intake linked to higher cardiac risk in adults over 55

Nov 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Protein

Louis shares a 2026 cohort study finding that high protein intake may raise CVD risk in older adults and notes it matches 30-year Harvard cohort data on animal protein. He recommends mostly plant protein with some seafood and yogurt.

Number needed to treat explained: short prevention trials understate long-term benefit

Oct 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 8 How to read research

Louis explains NNT (1 divided by absolute risk reduction) and why it looks poor in short prevention trials, where heart attack risk is only 2-3% over a few years. He argues that diet, exercise and statins taken for decades could cut lifetime risk from about 20% to 10%, an NNT of about 10, and that NNTs from short trials shouldn't mislead.

Higher adipose linoleic acid linked to lower CVD mortality, countering seed-oil claims

Oct 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared critique argues that influencers dismiss research showing higher adipose-tissue linoleic acid (omega-6) is associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. It says the contradicting evidence was lower quality and excluded from the meta-analysis for not meeting inclusion criteria.

Harvard cohorts: diet quality matters more than carb-versus-fat ratio for coronary disease

Feb 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Karl Nadolsky summarizes a JACC analysis of about 200,000 US adults followed up to 30+ years in the Nurses' Health Studies and HPFS. Both low-carb and low-fat diets looked beneficial when plant-based or healthy, and worse when animal-based or processed.

A 10% weight loss cut liver fat 33% and raised insulin sensitivity 40%, beating exercise

May 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares Karl Nadolsky's summary of a study showing 10% weight loss lowered liver fat by about 33% and intramuscular fat by about 13%, and improved insulin sensitivity by about 40%. Exercise alone improved fitness and insulin sensitivity but did not significantly reduce liver or muscle fat.
