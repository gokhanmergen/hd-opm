Willett: refined starch and sugar (~40% of US calories) drive heart disease and diabetes

Jan 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 31 ↩ 26 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis argues that mainstream nutrition science now sees refined sugar and refined starch, possibly including white potatoes, as direct causes of heart disease and diabetes, citing Harvard's Walter Willett in a ZOE interview. He notes about 40% of US calories come from refined sugar/starch and white potatoes, and says this should be replaced with healthier carbohydrate or healthy fat and protein sources. The post is opinion backed by an expert interview, not a specific study.

Harvard trial tests whether plain white potatoes damage endothelial function

Jan 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 25 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Louis describes a randomized feeding trial at Harvard Medical School and MGH asking if plain white potatoes impair endothelial function and promote arterial inflammation. The trial follows cohort findings linking potato consumption to hypertension and diabetes.

Unhealthy plant-based diets raise CHD risk, healthy ones lower it, in Harvard cohorts

Dec 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis stresses that not all plant-based diets are healthy. In the Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study, a plant-based index rich in healthy plant foods was linked to substantially lower CHD risk while one emphasizing sugary drinks, refined grains, potatoes and sweets was linked to higher risk, worse than the standard diet baseline.

Vegans with insulin resistance should target liver fat; white potatoes are not health food

Sep 19, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 17 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis advises vegans with insulin resistance, high blood sugar or high triglycerides that the main cause is excess visceral and liver fat, even in lean people. He also cautions against white potatoes, citing a Harvard trial testing whether they harm arterial endothelium, and prefers sweet potatoes.

White potatoes: short-term endothelial decline (MODERATE) and more hypertension in cohorts

Apr 19, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 7 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Summarizes the MODERATE trial, where participants who ate boiled, baked or mashed potatoes in the previous 24 hours had 1.7% lower flow-mediated dilation, roughly the 1.2%-per-decade aging decline for 14 years, as a short-term effect. It is linked to cohort data (Nurses' Health Studies and Health Professionals) tying baked, boiled or mashed potatoes to hypertension.

Sweet potatoes are unrelated to white potatoes and not linked to weight gain or diabetes

Aug 27, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis shares Harvard Nutrition Source information that sweet potatoes are roots in the morning glory family, genetically unrelated to white potatoes, which are tubers. Unlike white potatoes, sweet potatoes are not associated with weight gain or diabetes in recent studies, and purple Japanese varieties may be especially healthy.

Plant-based diet quality and dementia: whole plant foods protect, refined ones raise risk

A new study shared via MedPage Today links whole grains, vegetables, vegetable oils, nuts and legumes to lower dementia risk and refined grains, juices, white potatoes and added sugars to higher risk. French fries, but not other potato dishes, linked to higher diabetes risk

Aug 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis reports that Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study analysis found french fries raised diabetes risk over 30 years, apparently due to trans fats, while boiled or baked potatoes did not versus the standard diet. Versus whole grains, all white potatoes were linked to higher risk.

Swapping 10% of carb calories for vegetable fats cut lethal prostate cancer 29%

Dec 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 2 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis quotes a UCSF-led study of nearly 4,600 men with non-metastatic prostate cancer. Substituting healthy vegetable fats (olive and canola oil, nuts, seeds, avocado) for carbohydrates or animal fats was associated with lower lethal prostate cancer and all-cause mortality. The study adjusted for age, treatment, BMI, smoking, exercise and other factors.
