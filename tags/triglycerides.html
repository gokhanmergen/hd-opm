Triglycerides still matter below 400 mg/dL but are already counted in ApoB

Louis explains a common confusion: triglycerides below 400 mg/dL are atherogenic when carried by VLDL, an ApoB-tagged particle, so measuring ApoB already accounts for them. Adding triglycerides separately on top of ApoB double counts risk.

VLDL cholesterol (triglycerides / 5) may be more atherogenic than LDL

May 02, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 11 ↩ 8 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that VLDL is an emerging atherosclerosis risk factor and may be more atherogenic than LDL, so people with low LDL but high VLDL may still be at risk. VLDL can be roughly estimated by dividing triglycerides by 5 on a standard lipid panel, which is one reason to watch triglycerides. A normal VLDL is below 30 mg/dL, corresponding to triglycerides below 150 mg/dL, though optimal is likely lower in high-risk people.

Why insulin resistance lowers HDL, and why improving insulin sensitivity beats chasing HDL

Mar 21, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 ↩ 10 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis lists six mechanisms by which insulin resistance lowers HDL: impaired HDL maturation (lower LCAT), reduced apoA-I production, faster HDL catabolism (hepatic lipase, CETP), altered HDL composition and function, higher triglycerides making small dense HDL, and inflammation and oxidative stress. He concludes the best way to raise HDL is improving insulin sensitivity (lose visceral fat, exercise, cut refined starch and sugar), since insulin resistance is causal in heart disease while large RCTs suggest HDL itself is probably not.
