Blood omega-6 biomarker cohorts link higher levels to lower heart disease and diabetes

Louis stresses that evidence for omega-6 protection does not rest on food questionnaires but on blood and tissue measurements tracked prospectively for years before disease. He cites a pooled analysis of 30 cohorts in which people with higher linoleic acid levels had less CVD. He argues that avoiding nuts and seeds to cut omega-6 is likely harmful, and that RCTs on this timescale are impractical.

Replacing saturated with polyunsaturated fat cut non-fatal heart attacks 25%

Dec 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 19 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis summarizes an Annals of Internal Medicine systematic review of 17 randomized trials with hard endpoints. Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowered non-fatal heart attacks by 25% in higher-risk people over 5 years, similar to a lower-dose statin; other outcomes trended favorably but were not fully significant when the replacement was the general diet.

Seed oil intake rose 5x since 1950 while US heart disease death rates fell over 4x

Nov 27, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 30 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis argues that since 1950 vegetable oil consumption has risen about 5x and butter and lard intake has fallen 2-3x, while heart disease death rates fell by over 4x and life expectancy rose from 60 to 78. He presents the raw unadjusted correlation as evidence against claims that seed oils drive heart disease. It is an ecological correlation, not a controlled test.

Finland's North Karelia Project: less saturated fat, CHD deaths down nearly 80%

Dec 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis shares a Tom Rifai summary of Finland's 1970s turnaround, led by the North Karelia Project, where the population cut saturated fat and shifted to rapeseed, sunflower and soybean-based margarines and oils. Serum cholesterol fell about 15-20% and CHD mortality fell nearly 80% in working-age men, although smoking and blood pressure improvements also contributed.

Nurses' Health Study: five lifestyle factors cut heart disease 82% and diabetes 90%

Jul 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 4 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

In the Harvard Nurses' Health Study, a combination of five lifestyle factors was associated with 82% lower heart disease and 90% lower diabetes risk over about 14 years, larger than most drugs. Only 3-4% of nurses achieved all five, and it is a cohort study that cannot establish causation.

Higher blood linoleic acid predicts lower CVD risk in 68,000-person pooled analysis

Jul 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 7 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis shares a Johns Hopkins piece citing the 2019 Marklund pooled analysis of over 68,000 participants in 30 cohorts across 13 countries. People with the highest blood linoleic acid had lower CVD risk, especially for CV mortality and stroke; controlled trials also show LA lowers LDL and may improve glucose metabolism. The association is observational, which the author notes.

Minnesota Coronary Experiment did not show that omega-6 fats cause heart disease

Jun 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 5 ↩ 2 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

The poster says the 1989 report of the Minnesota trial found no difference in cardiovascular events, cardiovascular deaths or total mortality between treatment and control, and that the 2016 BMJ re-analysis added no new data changing this. The BMJ paper's observational cholesterol-mortality analysis is criticized as unreliable because causes of death were unknown, and hundreds of observational studies show omega-6 inversely related to heart disease.

Layne Norton rebuts seed-oil claims with human evidence on inflammation, LDL and oxidation

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
