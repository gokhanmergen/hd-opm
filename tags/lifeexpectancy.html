Glucosamine linked to lower mortality and CVD events in three cohort studies

Jun 21, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 19 Supplements & vitamins

The poster reviews observational studies: a 2012 US cohort (n = 77,510) with 33% lower mortality, UK Biobank (nearly 500,000) with 15% lower cardiovascular events, and a 2020 US cohort (~16,700) with 65% lower CV mortality. The longer the lower the better: lifelong LDL lowering and an estimated 60% risk reduction

Nov 20, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 17 · 💬 21 ↩ 2 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Argues that the benefit of LDL lowering depends on both how much and how long LDL is reduced. The author, one of the group's admins, is betting on very low-dose statins to hold LDL at 70-100 mg/dL, and estimates about 60% lifetime risk reduction from a 70-point lowering sustained for 30 years. The post also cites Charles Grashow as a case of CAC regression.

US heart disease mortality fell 66% from 1970 to 2022; heart attack deaths fell 89%

Jul 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 22 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

Louis quotes an AHA journal analysis: age-adjusted heart disease mortality fell 66% (761 to 258 per 100,000) from 1970 to 2022, ischemic heart disease deaths fell 81% and MI deaths 89%, while other heart disease including heart failure rose 81%. Life expectancy rose from 70.5 to 77.9 years.

Hong Kong's 86-year life expectancy goes with a grain, vegetable and fruit heavy diet

Sep 03, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 15 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis notes Hong Kong life expectancy of 86 years versus 76 in the US and cites a 2011 government Total Diet Study of 5,008 adults aged 20-84. Daily intake by weight was led by cereal grains (491 g), then vegetables (177 g), fruit (147 g), meat and poultry (113 g) and fish (71 g). Dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts, sugars and added fats were low, and he uses it to counter carnivore claims. It is an ecological observation.

Eight lifestyle factors linked to 87% lower mortality in 276,132 veterans

Louis reshares a post summarizing a Harvard and VA study of 276,132 people tracking eight lifestyle factors. Each added factor reduced mortality about 19%, and having all eight versus none was linked to 87% lower mortality, with a linear dose-response.
