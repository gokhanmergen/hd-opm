High CAC score is not a death sentence: weight loss, LDL below 70, diet and exercise

May 28, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 31 · 💬 21 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis tells group members with high CAC that the risk is high but manageable: about 30 lb weight loss clears liver and pancreas fat and can reverse soft plaque, LDL below 70 mg/dL often halts or reverses plaque, a Mediterranean diet has the most evidence, and exercise lowers risk at the same CAC.

At the same CAC score, exercisers have much lower heart attack risk over 20-25 years

May 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 38 · 💬 24 Exercise & fitness

Louis states that among people with identical coronary calcium scores, the fitter and more active have substantially lower heart attack risk over the following 20-25 years. The post links a short sports-cardiology video and gives no specific study or numbers.

With the same CAC score, fitter people have much lower heart attack risk over 20-25 years

Aug 23, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 26 · 💬 14 ↩ 4 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Sharing a clip of sports cardiologist Andre La Gerche, the post says that for any degree of coronary calcification, higher fitness goes with lower heart attack risk. Two people with the same CAC score differ substantially in long-term risk depending on whether they exercise.

For the same CAC score, fitter people have much lower heart attack risk over 20-25 years

Oct 09, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 1 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis reposts a point from a sports cardiologist video: at the same coronary calcium score, people who exercise have a much lower heart attack risk over the next 20 to 25 years, and greater fitness means lower risk at any degree of calcification. He notes CAC may overestimate risk in endurance athletes.
