Big swings in Lp(a) usually reflect acute inflammation, not an intervention working

Jun 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Lp(a)

Louis explains that Lp(a) is largely genetic but behaves as an acute-phase reactant, so infection, surgery, extreme exercise, vaccine reactions or inflammatory flares can shift test results temporarily. Large changes are therefore usually due to inflammation, and blaming big jumps on statins can hide an underlying problem.

Autoimmune and inflammatory diseases linked to elevated Lp(a) in ~50,000 patients

Sep 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 2 Lp(a)

A JACC Advances study of nearly 50,000 patients found people with gout, ankylosing spondylitis and IBD are more likely to have elevated Lp(a), with rates approaching those in established heart disease. Louis notes this fits Lp(a) being highly responsive to acute-phase inflammation.
