Why a cross-sectional NHANES keto mortality study sits low on the evidence hierarchy

Jan 24, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 4 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis comments on a Scientific Reports NHANES study (43,776 adults, 2001-2018) suggesting a ketogenic diet may lower all-cause mortality without raising CVD mortality. He explains cross-sectional studies capture one snapshot and are only hypothesis-generating. Quality nutrition evidence comes from prospective cohorts and RCTs.

Higher Lp(a) is linearly associated with lower risk of fatty liver in NHANES data

Mar 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Louis shares a Scientific Reports cross-sectional analysis of 2,308 NHANES participants finding a significant linear negative association between Lp(a) and NAFLD, suggesting Lp(a) could serve as a biomarker of NAFLD risk. He notes that at this point only the inverse relationship is known, and the headline claim that high Lp(a) might protect against fatty liver is speculative.

Beef-industry-funded NHANES analysis claiming red meat lowers cancer risk is weak evidence

Aug 27, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 2 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis criticizes a National Cattlemen's Beef Association-funded NHANES III analysis that links 12-18 years of mortality to just two days of dietary recall. He says this is far weaker than prospective cohorts, which led the WHO to classify processed meat as a carcinogen.

Beef-industry NHANES red meat and cancer paper rests on just 2 days of diet data

Oct 06, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis criticizes a paper circulating on social media that claims red meat lowers cancer risk. It was funded by the National Cattlemen's Beef Association and is an analysis of public NHANES data that links 12-18 years of outcomes to just two days of reported diet, which he argues is unreliable compared with long-term prospective cohorts.

Two-day NHANES diet recall cannot support conclusions about long-term disease risk

Oct 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 How to read research

Louis criticizes the stream of papers that use NHANES dietary data based on just two days of recalled eating to draw conclusions about heart disease, cancer and death. Two days of diet cannot represent a lifetime of eating.
