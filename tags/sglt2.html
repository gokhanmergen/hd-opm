SGLT2 inhibitors may offer benefits beyond diabetes, argues Dr. Lawenda

Mar 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 15 Insulin resistance & diabetes

A physician explains why he takes an SGLT2 inhibitor off-label: the drugs cause daily urinary glucose loss and may reduce risks of heart disease, cancer and dementia and even extend lifespan. Most evidence comes from people with diabetes, and Louis frames the class as possibly slowing aging itself.

Insulin resistance may be an adaptive response to nutrient excess

Jul 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 2 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis discusses a Cell Metabolism paper proposing insulin resistance and T2D as allostatic responses to chronic nutrient excess. He notes glucose-only drugs (sulfonylureas, insulin) don't improve outcomes consistently while metformin, SGLT2i and GLP-1 agonists do.

5:2 fasting beat metformin and empagliflozin for weight and HbA1c in new type 2 diabetes

Jun 30, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 5 Fasting & meal timing

A Healthline report on a trial in recently diagnosed type 2 diabetes found 5:2 fasting with meal replacements produced more weight loss and larger HbA1c falls than metformin or empagliflozin over 16 weeks, plus improvements in waist, hips and blood pressure.

Karl Nadolsky shares a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials in Endocrine Practice. SGLT2 inhibitors modestly reduced fat mass while preserving fat-free mass, suggesting no muscle loss penalty.

GLP-1 agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors linked to lower dementia risk in type 2 diabetes

Apr 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 1 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)

JAMA Neurology report shared by Louis: in people with type 2 diabetes, both GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors were associated with decreased risk of Alzheimer disease and related dementias compared with other glucose-lowering drugs. Louis sees this as evidence for benefits beyond diabetes treatment.

Dapagliflozin linked to lower heart failure risk in carriers of cardiomyopathy variants

Jun 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Heart rhythm & heart failure

Louis shares a Nature Medicine study of over 12,000 adults with type 2 diabetes in which dapagliflozin was associated with significantly lower heart failure risk in those carrying inherited cardiomyopathy-related variants. Fox News describes an 8-fold reduction in heart failure hospitalization, and suggests genetic screening could eventually guide early treatment.

ARPA-H PROSPR program ($144M) funds first phase 3 aging trials of three drugs

May 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 Aging & longevity

Eric Verdin's shared post says ARPA-H awarded $144 million to seven teams to lay groundwork for human aging trials, including a phase 3 in healthy adults in their 60s testing rapamycin, an SGLT2 inhibitor and semaglutide. The key gap is a validated aging biomarker regulators accept. Louis adds that age is the biggest CVD risk factor.

Louis shares a summary of the 2026 ESC guideline on cardiovascular disease and CKD. Key messages include managing CVD and CKD together, testing both eGFR and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio, treating albuminuria as a cardiovascular vital sign, and staging CKD with both measures.

Empagliflozin reduced monocyte-platelet aggregates, hinting at vascular anti-inflammation

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Inflammation & CRP

A Circulation pilot study led by Vanderbilt found participants treated with the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin had reduced monocyte-platelet aggregates, clusters of immune cells and platelets linked to cardiovascular damage. It suggests diabetes drugs may also reduce vascular inflammation, though this is a small pilot.

Heart failure in 2026: SGLT2 inhibitors foundational, HFpEF now treatable, early GDMT

A Cardiophilia summary of the latest heart failure evidence update lists several shifts: SGLT2 inhibitors are foundational across the ejection fraction spectrum, HFpEF now has options (finerenone, GLP-1 and incretin therapies), guideline therapy starts in-hospital, decongestion becomes guided by urine sodium, and devices are integrated into care.

SGLT2 inhibitors consistently raise hemoglobin and hematocrit, a possible anemia benefit

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Insulin resistance & diabetes

A JAMA editorial presented at Kidney Week says SGLT2 inhibitors consistently raise hemoglobin and hematocrit in people with cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, with or without diabetes.
