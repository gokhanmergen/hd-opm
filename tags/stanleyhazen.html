Eggs and phosphatidylcholine did not raise TMAO in an RCT, unlike choline bitartrate

Jun 10, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 10 ↩ 5 Meat, eggs & dairy

A Cleveland Clinic randomized trial (not egg-industry funded, with TMAO discoverer Stanley Hazen) found choline bitartrate supplements raised TMAO and platelet reactivity, but four large eggs a day or phosphatidylcholine supplements did not.

Eggs did not raise TMAO in a Cleveland Clinic randomized trial, unlike choline bitartrate

Jul 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 13 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis rebuts claims that eggs raise AFib risk via TMAO by pointing to an earlier randomized trial from the same Cleveland Clinic group. In that trial, eating eggs or taking phosphatidylcholine did not raise TMAO, whereas choline bitartrate supplements did. The trial was not funded by the egg industry.

Whether TMAO directly causes heart disease is unresolved; gut bacteria and insulin matter

Jun 10, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 9 Gut microbiome

The poster shares a Kevin Klatt video concluding that the science on TMAO causality is complicated and uncertain. He summarizes that gut bacteria turn choline, carnitine and betaine into TMA, the liver converts TMA to TMAO in an insulin-dependent step, kidneys clear it, and fish raise it transiently despite lower heart risk.

Erythritol and xylitol heart risk is unclear; the Cleveland Clinic studies were limited

Oct 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 5 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis notes it is unclear whether sugar alcohols raise CVD risk. A shared post explains that the group linking higher blood erythritol and xylitol to more heart attacks and strokes also showed that 30 g doses made platelets more reactive, but both studies had serious problems, including that the association study did not measure intake.

Erythritol may damage the blood-brain barrier, adding to stroke concerns

Feb 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis flags a news report that the sweetener erythritol may damage the blood-brain barrier. He notes earlier observational work from Stanley Hazen's Cleveland Clinic group linked erythritol to higher stroke risk.

Erythritol raised platelet reactivity in healthy volunteers in a Cleveland Clinic study

Jul 21, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis shares a brief report from Cleveland Clinic authors showing that ingesting a typical amount of erythritol, but not glucose, enhanced platelet reactivity and thrombosis potential in healthy volunteers. The authors suggest that erythritol's status as a food additive may need re-evaluation.
