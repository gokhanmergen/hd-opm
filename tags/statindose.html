Most of a statin's LDL lowering comes from low doses; cut the dose rather than quit

Jan 24, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 27 · 💬 19 Statins

Citing a slide from Tom Dayspring, Louis shows that each statin dose doubling adds a diminishing LDL reduction. If side effects occur, lowering the dose keeps most of the benefit and is better than stopping. He recommends maximizing diet and lifestyle first, then using the lowest effective statin dose.

Red yeast rice vs statins: lovastatin removed, other monacolins remain, no outcome RCTs

Feb 11, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 15 Supplements & vitamins

Louis explains that the FDA forced US red yeast rice products to remove lovastatin (monacolin K), the first statin, approved in 1986 as Mevacor, yet they still lower cholesterol through related monacolins. Some people intolerant of statins tolerate it, but it is unregulated with variable potency and lacks large long-running RCTs proving fewer heart attacks and strokes, so it is a gamble for high-risk people.

Statin dose-response: most LDL lowering comes from the lowest dose (Dayspring slide)

Jun 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 16 Statins

Louis explains with atorvastatin figures that each dose doubling adds a progressively smaller extra LDL drop. He argues for lowering LDL as much as possible with lifestyle first, then using the lowest effective statin dose, with the LDL target depending on personal risk.

Statin dose-response: the lowest dose gives the biggest percentage LDL drop per mg

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 7 Statins

Louis shares a Tom Dayspring slide showing diminishing returns with statin dose: 10 mg atorvastatin lowers LDL about 37%, with 20 mg adding 6%, 40 mg adding 5% and 80 mg adding 3%. His advice is to lower LDL with lifestyle first, then use the lowest effective statin dose, taking a low dose being better than quitting.

Statins cause under 10% of reported muscle pain, analysis of 23 RCTs finds

Jun 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 2 Statins

Louis shares the Oxford/CTT Lancet analysis of 23 large randomized trials (155,000 people) showing muscle pain is common with or without a statin. Over 90% of muscle pain reported on statins was not caused by the drug, and true statin-related cases occurred mainly in the first year, more with high-intensity dosing.
