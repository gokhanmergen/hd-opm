100 mg vitamin B6 reduced depression symptoms in a new randomized trial; B12 failed

Aug 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis notes a new RCT reports that 100 mg vitamin B6 significantly reduced depression symptoms while B12 did not. The same authors found in 2022 that B6 reduces anxiety. He notes depression and anxiety are CVD risk factors and warns of nerve toxicity with prolonged high doses.

Daily peanut butter improved muscle power in older adults and memory in young adults

Jun 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 26 · 💬 3 ↩ 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis shares two randomized trials of peanut butter. In 120 older adults at fall risk, 43 g/day for six months improved sit-to-stand speed and muscle power without changing weight; a separate 6-month trial in young adults (ARISTOTLE) found better memory and stress response.

Magnesium glycinate and rapid depression recovery: a 2006 case series, not a trial

Sep 17, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Supplements & vitamins

Louis comments on a shared claim that magnesium glycinate (125-300 mg with each meal and at bedtime) produced rapid recovery from major depression. He stresses that the underlying 2006 paper was a case series with no control group, not a randomized trial, so it is hypothesis-generating only. Depression is a major risk factor for CVD, which is why the topic is relevant.

Magnesium 100 mg/day tied to 22% lower heart failure risk, but not CHD, in a meta-analysis

May 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary of a 2016 BMC Medicine meta-analysis of 40 studies covering over a million people: magnesium was not linked to total CVD or CHD, but each extra 100 mg/day was linked to lower heart failure, stroke, type 2 diabetes and mortality.

CoQ10 100-500 mg/day shows moderate benefit for depression in a meta-analysis of RCTs

May 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 2 Supplements & vitamins

William Wallace shares a meta-analysis of randomized trials finding CoQ10, mostly as add-on therapy, improved depressive symptoms with a moderate effect size. He notes depression is a CVD risk factor. Dose and form conclusions are limited.

Magnesium chloride 248 mg/day improved mild-to-moderate depression in a crossover RCT

Sep 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

A randomized crossover trial in 126 adults with mild-to-moderate depression gave 248 mg elemental magnesium daily as magnesium chloride for 6 weeks. PHQ-9 depression scores improved with a net difference of about 6 points versus the control period. Louis shared the paper with tags on magnesium, depression and anxiety.

Vitamin D lowered depression symptoms dose-dependently in meta-analysis of 31 RCTs

Apr 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Examine.com summary of a dose-response meta-analysis of 31 RCTs: vitamin D reduced depressive symptoms, with most benefit near 5,000 IU/day and benefit seen only in people already depressed and not vitamin D sufficient. Most trials were under six months. Off the cardiovascular focus.

Saffron may help SSRI-related sexual dysfunction

May 24, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 2 Hormones, women's & men's health

Dr. Brian Lawenda shares a Medscape report that saffron may ease sexual dysfunction from SSRIs, which affects 30-70% of users. Not heart-related.

CTT meta-analysis: of 66 label-listed statin side effects, 62 showed no causal link

Feb 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Statins

Shared summary of the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists meta-analysis in The Lancet using individual data from 23 double-blind RCTs. Most side effects on statin labels were not caused by the drug, while muscle symptoms, new-onset diabetes, mild liver enzyme rises and a few minor effects were confirmed.

Silexan lavender oil showed antidepressant effects in a 500-patient trial

Oct 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 · 💬 1 Sleep, stress & social health

A randomized trial of nearly 500 patients with mild-to-moderate major depression found oral lavender oil (Silexan) beat placebo at 8 weeks on five of nine MADRS symptoms. Researchers described an independent direct antidepressant effect, with a symptom profile similar to sertraline. Depression is a heart disease risk factor but the post is not about heart disease directly.
