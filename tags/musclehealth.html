Muscle aging involves falling chaperone-mediated autophagy; fasting and exercise raise it

Dec 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 7 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis shares two Nature Metabolism papers from Ana Maria Cuervo's group showing chaperone-mediated autophagy declines with age in muscle and that restoring it improved strength and regeneration in aging mice. He notes fasting and resistance training increase CMA, rapamycin activates it and excess high-quality protein inhibits it, and ties it to heart muscle.

Athletes in their 70s and 80s have leg muscle density similar to athletes in their 40s

May 16, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 5 Exercise & fitness

Louis shares University of Pittsburgh MRI findings in 40 high-level recreational athletes aged 40-81 training 4-5 times per week. Muscle density and strength held steady with consistent activity, suggesting much age-related decline is due to inactivity. Sedentary adults may lose up to 8% of muscle per decade after 40.

Review of 33 systematic studies finds little evidence omega-3 supplements build muscle

Mar 14, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares Stuart Phillips' summary of a 2025 Clinical Nutrition ESPEN review of 33 systematic studies, which found little evidence that omega-3 supplements improve lean mass, muscle strength or physical function in healthy adults or clinical groups. Some small benefits in older adults were suggested, but exercise and protein remain the best strategies.
