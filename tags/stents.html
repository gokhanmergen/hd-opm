SCOT-HEART: CT angiography cut 5-year coronary death or MI risk 41% in chest pain patients

Jan 03, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 18 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Summarises SCOT-HEART (4,146 patients with stable chest pain, standard care vs standard care plus coronary CTA). CTA guidance lowered coronary death or nonfatal MI to 2.3% from 3.9%, mainly by directing aspirin and statin use to patients with plaque. Total procedures were similar by year 5; results apply to chest-pain patients only.

Rapamycin did not slow atherosclerosis in marmosets, and why stents use it anyway

Apr 18, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis cites Adam Salmon's marmoset work showing rapamycin had no clear effect on aging cardiovascular function, with plaque developing at the same rate as controls. He considers it unwise to rely on rapamycin to prevent heart disease. He also explains rapamycin on stents works by blocking scar tissue (neointimal hyperplasia), not plaque growth.

Rapamycin on stents blocks scar-tissue restenosis, not plaque; it failed in marmosets

Dec 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Louis explains that rapamycin is used on drug-eluting stents because it inhibits vascular smooth muscle overproliferation (neointimal scar tissue), which is what usually causes restenosis, not because it prevents plaque. He distinguishes plaque from scar tissue and notes Adam Salmon's group found rapamycin did not inhibit plaque growth in marmosets.

IVUS-guided PCI did not beat angiography alone in two NEJM trials

Apr 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Two NEJM trials found IVUS-guided PCI gave no clinical benefit: IVUS-CHIP in complex high-risk PCI showed 13.9% vs 11.1% events (HR 1.25) and OPTIMAL in left main PCI 33.7% vs 30.9% (HR 1.11), despite better stent optimization.
