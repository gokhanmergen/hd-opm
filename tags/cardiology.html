Why a coronary calcium score can rise as plaque stabilizes on statins

Dec 03, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 28 · 💬 47 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Explains that the Agatston score is not true calcium volume: it multiplies area by a stepwise density factor (1-4), so slightly denser plaque can jump a category and raise the score. Statins turn soft lipid-rich plaque into stable calcified plaque, so a rising CAC on treatment can reflect stabilization rather than failure.

Lipoprotein(a) explained: 80-90% genetic, stable for life, a risk factor beyond LDL

Oct 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 8 Lp(a)

Explainer on Lp(a): an LDL-like particle with apo(a) attached, 80-90% genetically determined, fully expressed by 1-2 years of age, and elevated in about 1 in 5 US individuals (1.4 billion globally). Levels above 50 mg/dL or 125 nmol/L raise ASCVD risk, possibly via impaired fibrinolysis and cholesterol deposition, and may occur with normal lipid panels.

REACT: silent atherosclerosis found in 57% of 16,808 adults, 7-9% of 18-29-year-olds

Sep 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The NEJM REACT study used carotid and femoral ultrasound plus coronary CT angiography in 16,808 adults aged 18-70 without known disease. Plaque was present in 57% overall, appeared in young adults, and a zero calcium score often missed early non-calcified plaque. Ten-year risk scores detected very little silent disease, so keeping risk factors favorable throughout life is the priority.
