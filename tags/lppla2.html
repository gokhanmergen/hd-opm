hsCRP is not artery-specific: aging skin and visceral fat drive it in older people

Jun 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 14 ↩ 1 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares an explanation that hsCRP is made by the liver in response to IL-6 and flags inflammation anywhere in the body. The post says age-related skin barrier breakdown and visceral fat are major contributors to 'inflamm-aging' and raised hsCRP in older people.

Statins, especially with ezetimibe, lower Lp-PLA2, a plaque-specific inflammation marker

Jun 22, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 5 Inflammation & CRP

Louis explains that Lp-PLA2 reflects inflammation inside atherosclerotic plaque (unlike systemic hsCRP) and is recognised as a CVD risk factor. A quoted review reports pravastatin, atorvastatin, rosuvastatin and simvastatin significantly lower it, with ezetimibe added lowering it further.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO) from visceral fat immune cells stiffens arteries and may be causal

Sep 04, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares an 8-minute video explaining MPO, an enzyme secreted into the blood by immune cells in visceral fat. He says it attacks and stiffens arteries, can be lowered by losing visceral fat and reducing inflammation, and is now measurable with commercial blood tests.

hsCRP needs averaging; GlycA may be a better inflammation marker than Lp-PLA2 or MPO

Aug 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 4 Inflammation & CRP

Louis discusses inflammation markers: hsCRP is noisy and not artery-specific but predicts risk when averaged over time; Lp-PLA2 and MPO are more artery-specific but poor predictors; GlycA predicts well without needing averaging.
