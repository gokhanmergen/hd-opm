Centenarians' higher LDL reflects survivorship bias, not a benefit of high LDL

Oct 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 36 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that centenarians tend to have higher LDL simply because higher LDL is common in the general population; they reach 100 because of protective genes that let them survive despite high LDL. Low LDL is uncommon in Western countries, genetics is the main determinant of reaching 100, and low LDL may delay cardiovascular death but will not by itself get someone to 100.

Victorian-diet longevity claim is survivorship bias from excluding deaths under age 5

Dec 01, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 6 ↩ 1 How to read research

Louis uses a paper on mid-Victorian diets as an example of survivorship bias. The authors exclude deaths before age 5, but those who survive early childhood were genetically selected for health, so their long lives do not show that the diet made average adults live as long as people today. Including the weaker people would have produced a much lower average age of death. The linked paper itself says life expectancy at age 5 was as good or better than today.

A 92-year-old athlete's fitness mixes exercise benefit with genetic reverse causality

Dec 06, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 1 How to read research

Louis comments on a CNN story about a 92-year-old athlete, arguing her health is probably both caused by exercise and a reason she can exercise so hard. He notes many observational exercise studies are inflated by reverse causality and survivorship bias, and welcomes research separating the two.
