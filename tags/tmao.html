L-carnitine supplements increased carotid plaque stenosis in a 6-month RCT

Jul 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 47 Supplements & vitamins

Louis highlights a phase 2 randomized trial in which L-carnitine supplements increased carotid plaque stenosis within 6 months versus placebo in people with metabolic syndrome. TMAO production is the suspected mechanism, but he stresses that the progression concerns the supplement regardless of mechanism.

Berberine lowered plaque score 3.2% and TMAO in a small 21-patient trial

Jun 10, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 23 Gut microbiome

Louis highlights a small human trial in which berberine reduced atherosclerotic plaque score, with a proposed mechanism of lowering gut-derived TMAO by acting on TMAO-producing gut bacteria. The sample is small and the comparison group was not randomized to the same degree as a large trial, so this is hypothesis-generating.

Strategies for lowering TMAO: diet, supplements and repurposed drugs

Aug 29, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 22 · 💬 18 ↩ 1 Gut microbiome

A written review of how TMAO forms from choline and carnitine via gut bacteria and the liver, and how to lower it. It points to less red meat and egg yolk, a fiber-rich Mediterranean-style diet, polyphenols and allicin, and statins and metformin. Gokhan says TMAO is not his top concern but worth knowing; he mentions a small human berberine trial that lowered TMAO and plaque.

Rush study: more than 1 egg per week linked to 47% lower Alzheimer's dementia risk

Mar 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 13 ↩ 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis shares a summary of a 6.7-year Journal of Nutrition study of 1,024 older adults (about 81 years) finding that eating more than one egg per week was associated with 47% lower Alzheimer's dementia risk, with choline explaining 39% of the effect. He notes the egg choline form (phosphatidylcholine) does not raise TMAO. The study is observational with self-reported diet.

Eggs and phosphatidylcholine did not raise TMAO in an RCT, unlike choline bitartrate

Jun 10, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 10 ↩ 5 Meat, eggs & dairy

A Cleveland Clinic randomized trial (not egg-industry funded, with TMAO discoverer Stanley Hazen) found choline bitartrate supplements raised TMAO and platelet reactivity, but four large eggs a day or phosphatidylcholine supplements did not.

Eggs did not raise TMAO in a Cleveland Clinic randomized trial, unlike choline bitartrate

Jul 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 13 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis rebuts claims that eggs raise AFib risk via TMAO by pointing to an earlier randomized trial from the same Cleveland Clinic group. In that trial, eating eggs or taking phosphatidylcholine did not raise TMAO, whereas choline bitartrate supplements did. The trial was not funded by the egg industry.

Whether TMAO directly causes heart disease is unresolved; gut bacteria and insulin matter

Jun 10, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 9 Gut microbiome

The poster shares a Kevin Klatt video concluding that the science on TMAO causality is complicated and uncertain. He summarizes that gut bacteria turn choline, carnitine and betaine into TMA, the liver converts TMA to TMAO in an insulin-dependent step, kidneys clear it, and fish raise it transiently despite lower heart risk.

Raw garlic's allicin lowered TMAO and improved gut microbiome in a human trial and mice

Nov 17, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 7 Gut microbiome

Louis highlights an npj Biofilms and Microbiomes paper showing raw garlic juice and allicin reduce TMAO production via gut microbiota. In people with high TMAO production, a week of raw garlic juice reduced TMAO formation, increased microbial diversity and beneficial bacteria; in mice allicin ameliorated atherosclerosis. He notes cooking destroys allicin.

L-carnitine supplements worsened carotid plaque stenosis within 6 months in an RCT

Nov 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis highlights a phase 2 RCT in patients with metabolic syndrome where L-carnitine did not change total carotid plaque volume but increased plaque stenosis within 6 months versus placebo. The proposed mechanism is TMAO production, since carnitine is abundant in red meat, but Louis notes TMAO could be a marker for another causal factor. He calls it extremely concerning.

Cleveland Clinic links gut-bacteria byproduct TMAO to atrial fibrillation risk

Jul 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 · 💬 1 ↩ 2 Heart rhythm & heart failure

Cleveland Clinic researchers analyzed blood samples from over 5,000 people undergoing heart procedures and found TMAO, made by gut bacteria from nutrients in red meat and other animal products, was linked to higher AFib risk. Cell experiments suggested TMAO may alter the heart's electrical environment. Louis frames it as TMAO possibly raising AFib risk.
