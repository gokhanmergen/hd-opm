Normal liver enzymes can hide fatty liver; use FibroScan and look beyond FIB-4

Jun 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 11 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares a dietitian post and note that half of people with fatty liver have normal liver enzymes, so enzymes and enzyme-based scores like FIB-4 poorly predict liver fat. He recommends FibroScan, with fatty liver diagnosed above 5% liver fat and insulin resistance linked to levels above 1.85%.

Fatty liver and liver stiffness reversed in one year on a Mediterranean diet

Statin use linked to less liver cancer and decompensation in 16,500 liver disease patients

Mar 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 2 Statins

Louis shares an NEJM Journal Watch summary of a Boston study of 16,500 patients with chronic liver disease (mostly MASLD, viral and alcoholic hepatitis) at intermediate or high fibrosis risk. Statin users had lower 10-year incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma and less hepatic decompensation than nonusers. The data are observational.

Overfeeding saturated fat raised liver fat 55%, sugar 33%, unsaturated fat 15% in an RCT

Apr 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A randomized trial overfed three groups by 1000 calories for 3 weeks from butter/dairy, olive oil/nuts or added sugar. Liver fat rose 55%, 15% and 33% respectively, showing calories are not equal for the liver.

Gut-microbe D-lactate raises blood sugar and liver fat; an oral 'trap' helped obese mice

Jul 30, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Gut microbiome

Canadian researchers found D-lactate made by gut bacteria signals the liver to produce excess glucose and fat in obese mice and humans. An oral polymer trap binding it in the gut improved glucose control, insulin resistance and liver inflammation in mice without changing diet or weight.

Why low LDL-C correlates with higher mortality: causation versus correlation

Nov 17, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 1 ↩ 2 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Points to a video on how ignoring the difference between correlation and causation can mislead, and argues that the association of low LDL-C with higher mortality is a marker of illnesses such as cancer or liver disease rather than a cause. Also links a paper from the author's cardiologist on causal roles of LDL-C, HDL, triglycerides and inflammation.
