Niacin inhibited vascular calcification in mice via SIRT1/SIRT6 activation

Dec 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 21 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Louis flags a new mouse study (a rarity for the group) showing niacin reduced smooth-muscle osteogenic differentiation and CKD/vitamin D3-induced vascular calcification, by maintaining SIRT1 and SIRT6 and autophagy. He notes other B3 forms also raise NAD, which activates sirtuins; this is preclinical only.

Gly-Low glycation-lowering cocktail extended lifespan in mice

Nov 30, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 12 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

A Buck Institute study in Cell Reports tested a five-ingredient mix (nicotinamide, thiamine, pyridoxamine, alpha lipoic acid, piperine) that lowers glycation. In mice it extended lifespan, reduced appetite and body fat and improved insulin sensitivity.

Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.

Glycation and AGEs in cardiovascular aging: Kapahi on methylglyoxal and the GLYLO suppleme

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis says he underestimated glycation and AGEs in aging after Pankaj Kapahi's work at the Buck Institute. Kapahi's post explains methylglyoxal as a reactive glycolysis byproduct that drives AGEs and aging, and describes GLYLO, a combination of lipoic acid, nicotinamide, piperine, pyridoxine and thiamine that lowered methylglyoxal and AGEs and extended mouse lifespan even when started late. Louis says Kapahi plans a paper showing it reduces arterial stiffening. Evidence is preclinical and the product is commercial.

Blood NAD+ is a useless aging biomarker: stable across seven cohorts (Nature Metabolism)

May 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

Louis argues that Charles Brenner is vindicated by a Nature Metabolism study quantifying NAD+ in seven human cohorts by mass spectrometry. Whole-blood NAD+ stayed stable with age and lifestyle and changed only with nicotinamide riboside supplementation, so blood NAD+ tests do not reflect tissue levels.

Vitamin B3 (nicotinamide) shows early promise for protecting retinal cells in glaucoma

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary says replenishing NAD+ with vitamin B3 may help retinal ganglion cells keep their energy supply and function longer in glaucoma. A 2020 randomized clinical trial of 57 people with glaucoma is cited as human evidence. Evidence is early and not specific to heart health.

Whole-blood NAD+ does not change with age or lifestyle, so it is a poor aging marker

May 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis, echoing Charles Brenner, shares a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that human whole-blood NAD+ is stable across age and not altered by exercise or lifestyle interventions. Nicotinamide riboside raised NAD+, but that does not prove clinical or longevity benefit; tissue NAD matters.

Vitamin B3 forms and NAD: strong evidence in skin cancer, only preliminary in glioblastoma

Jun 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 ↩ 2 Cancer

Louis notes randomized trials show nicotinamide can reduce certain skin cancers, and early niacin trials are testing glioblastoma, with both likely acting through NAD. The shared analysis stresses the brain-cancer result comes from 15 patients with no control group, while the skin-cancer effect is real but modest.

Nicotinamide plus pyruvate improved visual-field function in glaucoma in a phase 2 trial

Oct 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

A randomized Columbia University trial tested high-dose nicotinamide (vitamin B3) with pyruvate in patients being treated for open-angle glaucoma. After about two months the supplement group had significantly more improving visual-field locations than placebo, and Louis notes the mechanism likely involves NAD.

Louis shares a Substack critique of the glycation-focused supplement GLYLO, which questions the mouse lifespan data, the role of reduced food intake and differences between the research and consumer formulas.
