U-shaped mortality curves for cholesterol, BMI, A1C and BP reflect reverse causality

Jun 12, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 24 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

A post explaining that U-shaped hazard curves in observational data appear for cholesterol, BMI, A1C and blood pressure alike, and are explained by reverse causality rather than benefit of high values. Illness lowers LDL, BMI, A1C or blood pressure, creating false appearances of protection.

U-shaped mortality curves for cholesterol, BMI, A1C and BP reflect reverse causality

May 11, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 12 How to read research

Louis explains why observational studies show higher mortality at both low and high LDL or total cholesterol: the same U-curves appear for BMI, A1C and blood pressure, and illnesses like cancer lower these values. RCTs and Mendelian randomization show lower LDL causes lower mortality, so high LDL does not protect.

Waist size beats BMI for obesity-related cancer risk in men, likely via visceral fat

Mar 26, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 11 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares a Swedish study showing that waist circumference was a stronger marker than BMI for obesity-related cancers in men but not women. He suggests the reason is that waist size in men mostly reflects visceral fat, whereas women store more waist fat subcutaneously, and visceral fat is the dangerous kind.

Nurses' Health Study: five lifestyle factors cut heart disease 82% and diabetes 90%

Jul 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 4 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

In the Harvard Nurses' Health Study, a combination of five lifestyle factors was associated with 82% lower heart disease and 90% lower diabetes risk over about 14 years, larger than most drugs. Only 3-4% of nurses achieved all five, and it is a cohort study that cannot establish causation.

Louis shares an AHA statement describing BMI as a screening tool for weight-related heart and stroke risk and recommending small steps like moving more and sitting less.

A normal BMI does not guarantee health: discordant cardiometabolic profiles

Feb 21, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 1 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares Luigi Fontana's summary of a Nature Medicine paper on discordant health profiles. Many people with normal BMI have poor lipids, blood pressure or glucose, especially with visceral fat, and metabolic markers such as insulin, glucose, cholesterol, hsCRP and blood pressure predict risk better than BMI, with differences by ethnicity.

People with higher BMI respond less to vitamin D supplements in a VITAL trial reanalysis

Sep 06, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Supplements & vitamins

Louis notes that overweight people may need more vitamin D. The shared summary describes a VITAL reanalysis in 16,515 participants showing a blunted rise in vitamin D biomarkers with higher BMI.

JAMA Network Open study using a new definition estimated 75.2% of US adults have obesity, including 38.5% with normal BMI, reclassifying an additional 34% of the study population.
