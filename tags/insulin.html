Weight gain and liver fat feed insulin resistance in a self-reinforcing cycle

Aug 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis describes a vicious cycle in which weight gain and liver fat raise insulin through insulin resistance, which in turn promotes more weight gain and liver fat, often ending in obesity, fatty liver, diabetes and heart disease. He says breaking it requires a diet that removes excess abdominal and liver fat.

100+ RCTs and 20 cohorts: omega-6 improves glucose control, 43% lower diabetes risk

Oct 21, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis summarizes evidence that omega-6 polyunsaturated fat lowers fasting glucose and A1C and improves insulin resistance and secretion across more than 100 randomized trials. A pooled analysis of about 40,000 people from 20 prospective cohorts with biomarker-measured omega-6 found the top fifth had 43% lower diabetes risk than the bottom fifth. He lists the supporting meta-analyses.

Daily peanuts for 16 weeks improved brain insulin responsiveness in older adults

Oct 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A Maastricht University trial in healthy adults aged 60 to 75 gave 60 g of unsalted skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks. Functional brain imaging showed enhanced insulin responsiveness in memory regions including the hippocampus and parahippocampal gyrus. The shared post says more research is needed.

High-carb diet raised insulin yet people ate 689 fewer calories a day in an NIH ward study

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 0 · 💬 3 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis shares a post arguing that normal insulin spikes from healthy carbohydrate do not drive hunger or overeating. In a controlled NIH study, 20 adults lived in a research facility on a high-carb low-fat diet (about 75% carbohydrate) and a ketogenic diet (about 10% carbohydrate) for two weeks each. Despite higher glucose and insulin, they ate an average of 689 fewer calories per day on the high-carb diet, contradicting the carbohydrate-insulin model's prediction, although the diets differed in other ways.
