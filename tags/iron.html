Should you take iron for low-normal ferritin? Probably not without symptoms or a cause

Mar 21, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 24 ↩ 2 Risk assessment & lab tests

Louis discusses low-normal ferritin with no anemia or symptoms: see a doctor to find sources of iron loss, treat true iron deficiency or anemia, but in otherwise healthy asymptomatic people he thinks extra iron is generally a bad idea. He notes iron deficiency can promote clotting and reduce oxygen delivery.

Blood donation halved cancer deaths in an RCT but did not lower heart attacks

Nov 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 13 Cancer

Louis reports a randomized trial where blood donation did not reduce heart attacks or strokes but halved cancer deaths after 6 years, possibly through lower iron.

Monkey study links iron buildup ('ferro-aging') to organ decline; vitamin C may protect

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 19 ↩ 2 Aging & longevity

A Cell Metabolism paper describes chronic age-related iron buildup in liver, heart, lung, brain and muscle driving ACSL4-mediated lipid peroxidation and senescence in a 40-month monkey study. Louis suggests keeping ferritin toward the low-normal end if no iron deficiency, with vitamin C possibly protective.

Iron deficiency without anemia is common and missed when labs use low ferritin cutoffs

Mar 31, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 8 Risk assessment & lab tests

Louis flags that both high and very low iron stores raise heart risk and shares a post explaining iron deficiency without anemia (IDWA). Hemoglobin stays normal until stores are badly depleted, and many labs set the ferritin lower limit below the clinically useful threshold.

Vitamin C reduced iron-driven 'ferro-aging' in monkeys by blocking ACSL4 lipid damage

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 11 Aging & longevity

Louis shares an article summarizing a monkey study in which vitamin C was the most effective of 100 ferroptosis-related molecules screened at reducing lipid damage from ACSL4. He notes the article's headline is overstated.

Adventist study: vegans 85+ had higher neurological death risk than non-vegetarians

Aug 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 8 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Among AHS-2 participants (about 88,000, over 12,000 deaths), vegans aged 85 and older had higher risk of death from stroke (17%), dementia (13%) and Parkinson's (37%) than healthy non-vegetarians, while pesco-vegetarians had the lowest risk. The comparison was against very healthy non-vegetarians, not the standard American diet.

Meta-analysis: vitamin C adds no clinically relevant change to iron levels with oral iron

Jun 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 5 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a meta-analysis of randomized trials finding that vitamin C taken with oral iron did not produce clinically relevant changes in hemoglobin or ferritin in iron-deficient patients. He notes excess iron may contribute to CVD and aging, and added an April 2026 note about a monkey study suggesting vitamin C protects against iron's pro-aging effects.

Hypothesis: disrupted iron handling after SARS-CoV-2 infection may trigger long COVID

Mar 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 5 Infections & vaccines

Shares a news summary proposing that infection makes the body sequester iron, which if prolonged could reduce iron available for red blood cells and contribute to persistent symptoms. Louis calls it one of the more interesting long-COVID hypotheses; it is not yet clinically proven.

Vitamin C does not meaningfully raise iron levels in iron deficiency, meta-analysis finds

Apr 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 5 Supplements & vitamins

A systematic review of 10 RCTs and 1 cohort (over 2,000 patients) found vitamin C given with oral iron had no clinically relevant effect on hemoglobin or ferritin. Louis notes excess iron may contribute to CVD and aging.

Iron deficiency can falsely raise HbA1c even before anemia develops

Nov 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis shares a physician's explanation that low iron can inflate HbA1c, possibly by slowing red blood cell turnover so older cells with more glycation remain in circulation. If A1c seems high on a healthy diet, check iron status.
