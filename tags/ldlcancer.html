Low LDL in cancer patients reflects reverse causality: tumours scavenge LDL

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 12 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that rapidly dividing tumour cells take up circulating LDL, so LDL falls in people with cancer and observational data show low LDL with higher cancer mortality. The shared 2026 Science Advances study found aggressive breast cancers with TP53 mutations depend on cholesterol, and blocking PI5P4K enzymes trapped cholesterol and disrupted mTOR signalling.

Worse CVD risk scores predict higher cancer risk, especially lung and colorectal cancers

Jul 24, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Cancer

A JACC CardioOncology study shared by Louis found worse cardiovascular health metrics predict greater cancer risk, with the strongest links for lung and colorectal cancer. This supports shared risk factors across CVD and cancer.

Low LDL's link to cancer is reverse causality: cancer lowers cholesterol, not vice versa

Dec 21, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains why observational studies linking low LDL to cancer are not causal. Citing a Circulation review on reverse causality, he notes cholesterol declines before cancer diagnosis (likely from cancer-related inflammation), statin RCTs show no increase in cancer, and Mendelian randomization with an average of 52 years of lifetime exposure shows no higher cancer rates in people with genetically lower cholesterol.

Mendelian randomization shows genetically lower LDL does not raise cancer risk

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 ↩ 1 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that old observational links between low LDL and cancer are largely reverse causality. MR studies find genetically lowered LDL does not increase overall cancer risk, and genetically higher LDL is linked to more breast and prostate cancer; statin proxies lower colorectal cancer risk.
