Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

In MESA, high Lp(a) mattered mainly when CAC was 100 or higher

Jun 01, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 15 ↩ 5 Lp(a)

Louis shares a new MESA analysis suggesting that elevated Lp(a) raises 10-year heart attack and stroke risk mainly in people with CAC of 100 or more. With CAC between 1 and 99, high versus low Lp(a) mattered less, and a zero CAC appeared to 'trump' high Lp(a) over a decade.

MESA: CAC under 100, hsCRP under 2 and low visceral fat may protect those with high Lp(a)

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 6 Lp(a)

Louis summarizes MESA findings that three markers seem to protect people with high Lp(a): CAC score below 100, hsCRP below 2 mg/L and low visceral fat. He warns MESA is an observational cohort that shows associations, not causality.

CARDIA: low-carb diets in young adults tied to more coronary calcium if animal-based

Dec 01, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 3 · 💬 7 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Posts the abstract of a CARDIA analysis of 2,226 participants (mean age 40.4) followed 8.3 years. Higher carbohydrate intake was inversely associated with CAC progression (HR 0.731); an animal-based low-carb score predicted higher progression (HR 1.456) but a plant-based score did not (HR 1.016). 26.5% had CAC progression.

MESA: high Lp(a) mostly matters when CAC is 100 or above

Jul 18, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

A repost of a MESA analysis suggesting that people with high Lp(a), CAC under 100 and hsCRP under 2 mg/L have 10-year risk not much above those with normal Lp(a). The highest risk is with elevated Lp(a) and CAC of 100 or more. Louis notes this is a cohort study, not an RCT.
