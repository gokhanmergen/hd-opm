Does dietary sugar cause diabetes? A low-fat, high-carb remission example

Dec 08, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 42 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Gokhan questions the belief that sugar and carbs cause diabetes, citing a person who resolved diabetes on a high-carb, very low-fat diet and Denise Minger's article on low-fat diets with diabetes remission. His argument rests on an anecdote and a blog, not controlled trial data.

A balanced look at the ketogenic diet: anti-inflammatory potential and caveats

The author, who uses keto for 3-12 months at a time, lists potential benefits of a ketogenic diet, such as anti-inflammatory effects of ketones (BHB) and possible anti-cancer effects, with a longer blog post. It acknowledges he cannot do keto all the time and alternates with Mediterranean eating.

Fasting is the most effective way to generate ketones, without eating a keto diet

Dec 14, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 15 ↩ 1 Fasting & meal timing

Louis argues fasting is the best way to produce ketones, since calorie-restricting fasting extends lifespan in nearly every animal species tested and helps control weight. If ketones protect against heart disease (unproven but plausible), fasting offers the benefit without force-feeding unhealthy food.

Stanford finds BHB-amino acids, a new keto metabolite family that suppresses appetite

Nov 25, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 15 · 💬 13 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Stanford researchers led by Jonathan Long discovered a third ketosis pathway in which enzymes attach beta-hydroxybutyrate to amino acids, forming BHB-amino acids that suppress appetite and promote weight loss. It may explain reduced hunger during fasting or keto diets; heart relevance is indirect.

Ketosis does not prove humans are carnivores; most carnivore dieters are not in ketosis

Feb 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 16 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues all primates can enter ketosis as a starvation defense, so it is not evidence of a carnivorous evolution. He claims high protein intake and insulin prevent ketosis on carnivore diets unless fasting, needing about 60-65% of calories from fat.

Paleolithic humans were omnivores who relied heavily on cooked starchy tubers

May 09, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 13 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis rebuts the idea that paleolithic humans ate keto or carnivore diets, pointing to evidence of cooked starch use and Richard Wrangham's 'Catching Fire' hypothesis that cooked tubers and roots, a reliable glucose source, helped drive brain growth. Starchy underground foods were available even when meat and fish were not.
