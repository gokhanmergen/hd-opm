Statin RCT meta-analyses show no increase or decrease in cancer risk

Dec 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 13 Statins

Louis compiles meta-analyses of randomized trials showing statins neither raise nor lower cancer incidence or mortality, plus cohort studies suggesting neutral or lower cancer risk, e.g. prostate cancer. He adds that RCTs and prospective cohorts sit at the top of the evidence hierarchy.

Reliable medical knowledge does not require RCTs: smoking and trans fats as examples

Jan 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 How to read research

Louis argues that long RCTs are often unethical or impractical for diseases that take decades to develop, and that no human RCT showed smoking causes lung cancer or trans fats cause heart disease. Instead, causality is established by combining high-quality prospective cohorts, short-term biomarker RCTs and animal mechanistic studies, applying Bradford-Hill criteria, as with the Framingham Heart Study. He says much nutrition knowledge was built this way, so dismissing it for lacking long RCTs is mistaken.

Dietary fat and mortality meta-analysis: polyunsaturated favorable, trans fat harmful

Dec 01, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Summarizes a meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (over 1 million participants) on dietary fat and mortality. Higher total, monounsaturated and especially polyunsaturated fat were associated with lower mortality, trans fat with higher mortality, and saturated fat showed a non-linear pattern with excess risk up to about 11% of energy.

Fat type beats amount: PUFA tied to lower mortality, trans and saturated fat to higher

Aug 24, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 10 · 💬 8 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A 2020 Clinical Nutrition meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (1,013,273 participants, 195,515 deaths) found polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fats associated with lower mortality, and saturated and trans fats with higher. Gokhan cautions that data guide rather than prescribe.

Protein and mortality: BMJ 2020 meta-analysis finds weak links, plant protein favorable

Jan 06, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 5 ↩ 1 Protein

Summarizes a BMJ 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of 32 prospective cohorts. Higher total protein was associated with slightly lower all-cause mortality, and plant protein with lower all-cause and CVD mortality, while animal protein had a minuscule effect up to about 20% of calories.

Why a cross-sectional NHANES keto mortality study sits low on the evidence hierarchy

Jan 24, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 4 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis comments on a Scientific Reports NHANES study (43,776 adults, 2001-2018) suggesting a ketogenic diet may lower all-cause mortality without raising CVD mortality. He explains cross-sectional studies capture one snapshot and are only hypothesis-generating. Quality nutrition evidence comes from prospective cohorts and RCTs.

Statins associated with lower advanced and fatal prostate cancer and longer survival

Aug 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 ↩ 1 Statins

Louis cites a 2022 review of the epidemiology of statins and prostate cancer. Registry and cohort studies find statin use linked to lower advanced and fatal prostate cancer risk and longer survival in men already diagnosed. These are observational associations.
