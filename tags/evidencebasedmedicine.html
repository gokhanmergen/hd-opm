Rosuvastatin lowers LDL slightly more than atorvastatin; LODESTAR outcomes were similar

Aug 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 10 Statins

Comparison of the two high-intensity statins: both lower LDL-C by 50% or more, rosuvastatin is more potent per milligram, but in the randomized LODESTAR trial 3-year clinical outcomes did not differ significantly. The goal is reaching the appropriate LDL target on maximally tolerated therapy.

Whey protein raised IGF-1 but not muscle or strength in 24-week RCT of older adults

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 17 Protein

A 24-week placebo-controlled trial raised protein to 1.5 g/kg/day with whey in healthy older adults. It did not improve muscle power, strength, performance or mass, though IGF-1 rose; potassium bicarbonate was also ineffective. Louis argues this means the harms of high IGF-1 without benefit.

Pooled blinded trials: statins cause only about 1 in 15 reported muscle aches

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 12 Statins

A pooled analysis of 19 blinded trials with 123,940 people found that over about four years, 27.1 of 100 on a statin reported muscle pain or weakness versus 26.6 of 100 on placebo. This implies that the vast majority of muscle complaints on statins are not caused by the drug, consistent with a nocebo effect.

Whole-blood NAD+ does not change with age or lifestyle, so it is a poor aging marker

May 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis, echoing Charles Brenner, shares a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that human whole-blood NAD+ is stable across age and not altered by exercise or lifestyle interventions. Nicotinamide riboside raised NAD+, but that does not prove clinical or longevity benefit; tissue NAD matters.

NEJM review: LDL targets should scale with absolute risk, not 'lower for everyone'

Feb 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis shares an NEJM review on the inherited basis of coronary artery disease. Takeaways: LDL and apoB are causal risk factors, genetic risk is modifiable, and treatment should be proportionate to absolute risk with lifestyle emphasis in lower-risk people.

Senolytic dasatinib plus quercetin caused brain demyelination in a 2026 PNAS study

Mar 21, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis shares Luigi Fontana's warning that senolytics are not a magic bullet. A 2026 PNAS study found dasatinib plus quercetin induced demyelination and oligodendrocyte dysfunction without killing cells; Louis adds that old senescent cells still serve functions such as skin barrier integrity.
