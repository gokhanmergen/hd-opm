Claim that blending fruit causes bigger glucose spikes is untrue, per Louis

Dec 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 24 · 💬 14 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis states that blending fruit does not cause a larger glucose spike than whole fruit and can lower or leave unchanged the glycemic index, criticizing Glucose Goddess and Gary Brecka's claims. The post links earlier group posts but cites no new study.

Blending whole fruit does not cause bigger blood-sugar spikes than eating it whole

May 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 13 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis rebuts the claim that blending fruit spikes blood sugar. Per the shared response, an RCT comparing whole apples and blackberries with a smoothie of the same fruits found a slightly smaller glucose rise with the smoothie, and blending mangoes also did not raise the spike. Fiber remains in the blend and still slows sugar absorption.

Blending fruit into smoothies does not spike blood sugar more than whole fruit

Aug 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 4 ↩ 4 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis argues that smoothies made from whole fruit do not spike blood sugar more than eating the fruit whole, and may lower the glycemic response. He cites several PubMed studies of whole vs blended fruit and offers a fiber-binding explanation.

Blending fruit into smoothies does not raise the blood sugar spike and may lower it

Oct 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Louis disputes the claim that blended fruit spikes blood sugar, saying randomized trials show blending binds soluble fiber to sugar and slows absorption, reducing the glucose spike. The quoted post says smoothies may be better for you than the whole fruit.
