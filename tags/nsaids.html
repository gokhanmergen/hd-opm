Chronic ibuprofen and NSAID use raises heart attack, stroke, kidney and BP risks

Jan 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 4 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

Louis agrees ibuprofen is hard on kidneys and adds that chronic NSAID use can worsen heart failure, raise blood pressure and bleeding risk, and raise heart attack and stroke risk (aspirin excluded). Occasional use is fine.

Methotrexate may lower heart disease risk in rheumatoid arthritis and be safer than NSAIDs

Oct 19, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Inflammation & CRP

Louis says methotrexate has an undeserved bad reputation and newer research suggests it may reduce cardiovascular risk in people with rheumatoid arthritis or other inflammatory diseases. He notes long-term NSAIDs, especially Cox-2 inhibitors, raise heart attack and stroke risk and are hard on the kidneys, and advises talking to a cardiologist. No specific study is cited.

Statins may lower hsCRP by stabilizing plaque; fixing upstream causes beats blocking IL-6

Aug 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a clip arguing statins lower inflammation because they stabilize unstable plaque, which generates the inflammation. The clip cites JUPITER, where rosuvastatin lowered hsCRP 37%. Interventions that address upstream causes, like statins or diet, work better than blocking IL-6 downstream.
