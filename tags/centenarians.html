Centenarians' higher LDL reflects survivorship bias, not a benefit of high LDL

Oct 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 36 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that centenarians tend to have higher LDL simply because higher LDL is common in the general population; they reach 100 because of protective genes that let them survive despite high LDL. Low LDL is uncommon in Western countries, genetics is the main determinant of reaching 100, and low LDL may delay cardiovascular death but will not by itself get someone to 100.

Ancel Keys' 1975 Mediterranean diet book: plants, olive oil, fish, little red meat

Jan 19, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 23 ↩ 5 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Follow-up to the longevity post, summarizing (via a ChatGPT summary by Pankaj Kapahi) the diet Ancel and Margaret Keys described in their 1975 book. It emphasizes vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, extra virgin olive oil as the main fat, fish, limited red meat and modest dairy, herbs instead of salt, fruit for dessert and wine in moderation. The Keys also stressed activity and shared meals. Louis also notes the 7 Countries scientists' longevity (3 of 18 reached 100).

Centenarians' longevity is tied to genes that lower insulin/IGF-1 signaling (Barzilai)

May 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis shares a Nir Barzilai clip arguing that centenarians owe their long lives largely to genetic variants lowering insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR signaling, strong enough to offset habits like smoking and obesity. Barzilai says women with the lowest IGF-1 in old age live twice as long as those with the highest. Louis adds that diet and lifestyle can help keep insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR lower.
