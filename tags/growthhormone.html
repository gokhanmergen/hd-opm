High insulin/IGF-1 tied to cancer, diabetes; Laron syndrome and low-IGF-1 mice live longer

May 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 23 · 💬 13 Aging & longevity

Louis praises a video on height, insulin/IGF-1 and longevity and lays out his argument: diet (refined starch/sugar and excess animal protein) is the main driver of IGF-1, and low IGF-1 is linked to lower cancer and diabetes risk. He cites Laron syndrome, with no documented cancer deaths, little diabetes and less heart disease and Alzheimer's, and a long-lived low-IGF-1 mouse. Claims come from the post and videos, not named papers.

Lowering IGF1 extends lifespan in mice, so be wary of 'anti-aging' growth hormone claims

Jun 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 2 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis argues that growth hormone or IGF1 should not be marketed as anti-aging, because lowering them consistently extends lifespan in rodents. The record mouse, a growth hormone receptor knockout, lived almost 5 years versus about 2.5 for a normal lab mouse.

Short post pointing to Loyal's IGF1-lowering dog drug and suggesting interventions that lower IGF1 might reduce heart disease, diabetes, cancer and Alzheimer's risk. Claims are speculative and based on the company's own statements.

Chronic growth hormone elevation is dangerous, as Bryan Johnson's CJC-1295 test showed

May 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Hormones, women's & men's health

Louis explains that the effects Bryan Johnson reported after the peptide CJC-1295 are textbook growth hormone responses: GH counter-regulates insulin, raising blood glucose and causing insulin resistance while shifting the body to fat burning.

Thirty years of trials: raising IGF-1 adds lean mass but not strength in older adults

Jul 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Hormones, women's & men's health

Louis compiles trials of interventions raising circulating IGF-1, such as MK-677, showing reliable gains in lean mass but almost no improvement in strength or function in older adults. He argues the extra lean mass is largely fluid and non-contractile tissue.

Growth-hormone peptides raise IGF-1, linked to cancer and mortality risk at high levels

May 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Hormones, women's & men's health

Joel Kahn's post, shared by Louis, warns that injecting growth-hormone-type peptides (ipamorelin, sermorelin, CJC-1295) can chronically elevate IGF-1. High IGF-1 is associated in observational data with breast and prostate cancer and with higher cancer, cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, and excess GH signaling in acromegaly causes cardiometabolic complications.
