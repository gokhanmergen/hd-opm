A low-dose statin could roughly halve lifetime heart attack risk if taken for decades

Feb 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 29 · 💬 47 Statins

Louis explains a graph combining RCT, cohort and Mendelian randomization data: an LDL reduction of 39 mg/dL (reachable with a very low dose statin) lowers heart attack risk more the longer it is sustained. Translated to absolute risk, a roughly 20% lifetime risk could fall to about 10%, and the rest requires diet, lifestyle and other therapies.

LDL down 39 mg/dL: 20%+ fewer heart attacks in RCTs, 30%+ in cohorts, 50%+ genetically

Louis presents a graph combining RCTs (5 years), prospective cohorts (12 years) and Mendelian randomization (52 years) to show that the benefit of lowering LDL grows with duration of exposure. A 39 mg/dL reduction, achievable with a very low-dose statin, is associated with larger risk reductions over longer periods. He translates the 50% figure to a lifetime absolute risk falling from 20% to 10%, and describes his own 10 mg atorvastatin. The absolute estimate is his extrapolation.

Statin absolute risk reduction is small over years but can exceed 10% over a lifetime

Nov 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 25 ↩ 2 Statins

Louis explains that short statin trials show only 1-2% absolute risk reduction because baseline risk over a few years is only 2-5%, the same as diet and exercise trials. Over a lifetime, with a 20% baseline risk of heart attack, cohort and Mendelian randomization data suggest cumulative reductions of 10% or more.

A 1.1-4.7% absolute risk reduction from statins is large against a 20% lifetime risk

Apr 28, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 20 ↩ 2 Statins

Louis explains relative versus absolute risk in statin trials. Statins cut absolute heart attack or stroke risk by 1.1% to 4.7% in roughly 5-year RCTs, which is large against a lifetime risk of about 20%, and sustained over decades could lower lifetime risk toward 10%. He shares a graph combining RCTs (over 20% risk reduction after 5 years), prospective cohorts (over 30% at 12 years) and Mendelian randomization (over 50% at 52 years) for a 39 mg/dL LDL reduction.

Mendelian randomization: lifelong lower LDL tracks linearly with lower heart disease risk

Oct 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 8 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

The post explains a chart from a European Atherosclerosis Society consensus statement showing that genetic variants affecting LDL through different pathways (LDLR, PCSK9, APOB, HMGCR, NPC1L1, SORT1, ABCG5/8) change coronary risk in proportion to the LDL change. This matches the linear relationship seen in statin trials. The poster also shows how to convert relative risk reductions into lifetime absolute risk.

Statin absolute risk reduction is cumulative: 1-2% in trials, far more over a lifetime

Louis counters the claim that statin trials show only 1-2% absolute risk reduction: trials last about 5 years when baseline risk is just 2-5%, whereas lifetime heart attack risk is about 20%. Cohort and Mendelian randomization data suggest long-term statin use can cut this to under 10%.

Starting LDL control earlier gives larger lifelong benefit by delaying the event threshold

The poster explains a chart from a JACC: Advances paper showing that earlier LDL lowering produces a bigger lifelong benefit than starting late. Lowering LDL does not cure disease but delays crossing the risk threshold for heart attack or stroke, buying time.

Lower lifetime LDL exposure means lower CVD risk across genetic variants and drugs

Jun 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Quoted post arguing LDL is causal: genetic variants, statins, PCSK9 inhibitors and ezetimibe all show a log-linear, consistent relation between lower LDL and lower cardiovascular risk regardless of how LDL is lowered. It rebuts claims that LDL does not matter.
