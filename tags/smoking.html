Reliable medical knowledge does not require RCTs: smoking and trans fats as examples

Jan 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 9 ↩ 1 How to read research

Louis argues that long RCTs are often unethical or impractical for diseases that take decades to develop, and that no human RCT showed smoking causes lung cancer or trans fats cause heart disease. Instead, causality is established by combining high-quality prospective cohorts, short-term biomarker RCTs and animal mechanistic studies, applying Bradford-Hill criteria, as with the Framingham Heart Study. He says much nutrition knowledge was built this way, so dismissing it for lacking long RCTs is mistaken.

High blood xylitol linked to 57% higher MACE risk in observational cohorts (ESC 2026)

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A study presented at ESC Congress 2026 analyzed 17,710 participants from CLSA and EPIC-Norfolk. Highest-quartile xylitol levels were associated with 57% higher MACE risk over six years in CLSA and 18% higher over 30 years in EPIC-Norfolk, with a dose-response pattern. It is observational and cannot show xylitol causes events.
