PREDIMED follow-up: mild-to-moderate wine linked to 50% lower CV risk, not proven causal

Jun 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 37

Louis describes a PREDIMED follow-up that tracked wine intake via a urinary tartaric acid biomarker. Mild-to-moderate wine consumption over a 9-year follow-up was associated with lower heart attack, stroke, heart failure and CV mortality, but he stresses causation is unproven and alcohol's role is actively debated.

Medscape: cannabis users had about 6x the heart attack risk in a retrospective study

Louis shares a Medscape item reporting that a large retrospective study found cannabis users had six times the risk of heart attack, while a meta-analysis linked cannabis to a 50% higher heart attack risk. Details of the studies are not given in the post.

Meta-analysis of 4.3 million: moderate wine not linked to higher overall cancer risk

Jan 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 17 ↩ 3

Louis shares Dr. Brian Lawenda's post on a Frontiers in Nutrition meta-analysis of 73 studies, covering over 4.3 million participants, that found moderate wine consumption (up to one drink/day for women, two for men) was not associated with increased overall cancer risk and might lower some cancer types. The post questions treating wine like beer and spirits.

Harvard: 100+ cohorts show 25-40% lower CVD risk with light-to-moderate alcohol

Dec 03, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 22 ↩ 7

Louis quotes Harvard's Nutrition Source: more than 100 prospective studies show light-to-moderate drinking is linked to a 25-40% lower risk of heart attack, ischemic stroke, peripheral vascular disease, sudden cardiac death and CVD death, and Harvard epidemiologists consider it almost certainly causal. Above 4 drinks a day, risks of hypertension, arrhythmia, stroke, heart attack and death rise. An NIH-funded alcohol trial was stopped. This view is contested and observational.

NASEM alcohol review: moderate drinking tied to lower all-cause and CVD mortality

Sep 30, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 14

The National Academies' evidence review on alcohol and health, published in JAMA on September 30, 2025, graded certainty using the USPSTF framework. It reached three moderate-certainty conclusions, including that moderate drinking is associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, while stressing biases and the known harms of heavy drinking.

AHA statement: low alcohol shows no risk to possible benefit, heavy drinking harms

Jun 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 9 ↩ 2

Louis quotes the 2025 AHA scientific statement on alcohol and cardiovascular disease. Low intake (up to 1 to 2 drinks per day) is associated with no risk to possible risk reduction for CAD, stroke, sudden death and possibly heart failure, whereas binge or 3+ drinks per day is consistently linked with worse outcomes across every CVD entity.

UNATI: 10,000-person randomized trial of moderate red wine vs reducing alcohol

Aug 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 3 ↩ 1

UNATI, led by Martinez-Gonzalez, will randomize about 10,000 drinkers (men 50-70, women 55-75, 3-40 drinks per week) to reduce alcohol or drink moderately (under 7/week women, under 14/week men, red wine with meals) for about 4 years. Outcomes include mortality, cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and dementia.

New 5-year RCT from the PREDIMED lead investigator will test red wine versus CVD

Dec 12, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 3 ↩ 7

Louis describes a funded randomized trial at the University of Navarra led by PREDIMED's principal investigator, which will randomize physicians aged 50-75 to moderate red wine or an alcohol-free alternative for five years, with cancer and all-cause mortality as secondary endpoints. He reviews PREDIMED's finding of 30% lower CVD with a Mediterranean diet including wine, and that the Mediterranean diet loses up to 23.5% of its protective effect without wine, per Martinez-Gonzalez.
