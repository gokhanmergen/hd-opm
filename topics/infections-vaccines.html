French cohort of 22.7 million: mRNA COVID shots cut COVID death 74%, all-cause death 25%

Dec 05, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 27 · 💬 47

Reports a French cohort of 22.7 million vaccinated and 5.9 million unvaccinated adults followed nearly four years: 74% lower COVID death risk and 25% lower risk of death overall, with no turbocancer signal. The admin adds that vaccination protects against heart disease deaths in high-risk groups and that he prefers flu vaccine to COVID mRNA.

Shingles vaccine linked to 28% fewer heart attack and 42.5% fewer stroke hospitalizations

Aug 29, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 28 · 💬 14 ↩ 1

A Kaiser Permanente Southern California cohort matched 102,766 Shingrix recipients to 411,064 unvaccinated patients. Vaccinated people had lower shingles complications and fewer hospitalizations for heart attack and stroke. Gokhan notes the effect sizes are striking and that flu vaccine has similar heart-protective effects.

Shingles vaccination tied to less dementia and fewer deaths in people with dementia

Dec 05, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 22

Short post sharing a Cell paper suggesting shingles vaccination may be a strong tool to prevent or slow dementia: associated with less mild cognitive impairment and dementia in healthy people and fewer deaths in those who already have dementia.

Shingles vaccine tied to 46% fewer major cardiac events in high-risk people

Mar 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 23 · 💬 13

Louis notes growing evidence that the shingles vaccine protects against dementia, and a new study suggests it also protects the heart. In high-risk people it was associated with 46% fewer major cardiac events and 66% fewer deaths within a year, but this is observational and confounding by healthier behavior is possible.

Non-traditional heart disease risk factors converge on inflammation and oxidative stress

An overview of residual-risk drivers beyond cholesterol, blood pressure, diabetes and smoking: gum disease, chronic immune activation, clonal hematopoiesis, gut microbiome metabolites and pollutants. The common thread is chronic inflammation and oxidative stress.

Honey plus coffee beat prednisolone for persistent post-infectious cough in a 97-adult RCT

Feb 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A double-blind randomized trial compared a honey-coffee mixture, prednisolone and guaifenesin for one week in adults with persistent post-infectious cough. The poster highlights that the homemade remedy worked better than the steroid. Not about heart disease directly.

Shingles vaccine tied to slower biological aging and less inflammation in 3,800 elders

Feb 28, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 22 · 💬 11

Reports a large national study of more than 3,800 Americans aged 70+, in which shingles-vaccinated people showed slower biological aging, lower chronic inflammation and slower aging-related changes in gene activity. This suggests the vaccine may calm 'inflammaging', the low-grade inflammation tied to heart disease, frailty and cognitive decline.

Treating gum disease slows carotid artery thickening in a clinical trial

Aug 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 8 ↩ 2 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Explains how periodontitis lets oral bacteria and inflammatory products reach the bloodstream and vessel wall, and cites a clinical trial in which intensive gum treatment reduced carotid artery thickening. Gokhan's advice is to brush and floss diligently.

Controlled infection study: mild SARS-CoV-2 impaired cognition for a year in 18 adults

Oct 04, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 16

Louis shares a post about a Lancet eClinicalMedicine study in which 36 healthy unvaccinated young adults were deliberately inoculated with SARS-CoV-2 and 18 became infected, almost all with mild symptoms. Their cognitive function was measurably impaired during acute illness and at least one year later, though they reported no symptoms. The post is about cognition, not the heart.

Baby aspirin during and after Covid may be worth discussing if you have heart disease

Jan 06, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 12

Louis suggests people with heart disease who get Covid ask their doctor about baby aspirin during infection and for a few weeks after. Infections temporarily raise heart attack and stroke risk through inflammation, and Covid raises clot risk more than other infections. A linked 2022 meta-analysis found aspirin use associated with lower Covid mortality without more bleeding.

Infectious hypothesis of heart disease: oral streptococci in about 42% of coronary plaques

A long essay argues that chronic hidden infections may help drive plaque growth and rupture. It highlights a 2025 JAHA study finding viridans-group oral streptococci in roughly 42% of coronary plaque samples, reinforcing the oral health and heart link.

Personalized mRNA vaccine succeeds in phase 3 melanoma trial after ~50% phase 2 benefit

A personalized mRNA vaccine plus Keytruda improved recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival in a phase 3 trial of more than 1,100 patients with melanoma. The earlier phase 2 trial showed roughly a 50% reduction in recurrence or death at 5 years. Approval might come as early as 2027, and trials are under way in other cancers.

Meta-analysis of 155 studies: flu, shingles and COVID raise heart attack and stroke risk

Oct 29, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 8

Louis shares a report on a meta-analysis of 155 studies finding that common viral infections raise heart attack and stroke risk, both soon after infection and long term. Flu carried up to six times higher heart attack risk the month after infection, and COVID-19 nearly doubled risk.

Flu vaccination linked to lower all-cause mortality, per admin's earlier evidence summary

Neutrophil vitamin C saturates near 200 mg twice daily; infection raises the need

May 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Supplements & vitamins

William Wallace's shared explanation: neutrophils use vitamin C as an internal antioxidant pool to survive the respiratory burst they use to kill microbes. In healthy people neutrophil levels saturate at around 200 mg twice per day, and the dose needed to saturate them rises during infections.

Dormant streptococcal biofilms in plaque can be woken by infection and rupture the plaque

Sep 13, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 4 ↩ 1

A JAHA study reports that oral-type bacteria can form protective biofilms inside atherosclerotic plaques, shielded from the immune system. A trigger such as a viral infection may awaken them, causing inflammation that ruptures the fibrous cap and causes a clot. This challenges the view that only oxidized LDL initiates coronary disease.

Infections temporarily raise heart attack and stroke risk; vaccines may do so far less
