The EWG 'Dirty Dozen' list ignores dose, and conventional produce is still good for you

May 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 24 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis argues the Dirty Dozen is built on flawed methodology (EWG weights all pesticides equally and ignores EPA safety thresholds). Microplastics in carotid plaque tied to 4.5x higher MI, stroke or death risk; early data

Louis shares a summary of microplastics research in blood and plaque. The 2024 NEJM study (Marfella) detected polyethylene in 58% of carotid plaque samples, and those patients had a much higher rate of heart attack, stroke or death. He stresses research is early stage.

Commercial blood microplastics tests mostly measure contamination, says Louis

Dec 28, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 20

Louis argues that the microplastics blood test sold by Bryan Johnson measures plastic contamination from blood draw, transport and processing. Single-digit particle counts in a 100 uL sample require a cleanroom and plastic-minimized lab, which no commercial lab offers.

Conventional produce beats no produce: natural plant carcinogens dwarf pesticide residues

May 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 10 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis argues that, while organic is fine if affordable, eating conventionally grown fruit and vegetables is much better than skipping them. He cites Ames's PNAS papers showing 99.99% by weight of dietary pesticides are plants' own natural defense chemicals, and that natural carcinogens in produce far exceed synthetic residues.

Microplastics raised plaque 63% in male mice, with no change in cholesterol or weight

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 12

Summarises a UC Riverside mouse study: atherosclerosis-prone mice given 10 mg/kg/day microplastics for nine weeks. Males had 63% more plaque in the aortic root and 624% more in the brachiocephalic artery; females had no significant increase. Weight and cholesterol were unchanged, suggesting direct endothelial damage and inflammation. Admin suggests high fiber and Finnish sauna as defenses (not from the study).

Foods high in oxalate may worsen gadolinium retention after MRI contrast

Nov 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 10

Louis shares University of New Mexico research that oxalic acid in foods like chocolate, spinach and vitamin C can form gadolinium nanoparticles in tissues, and suggests avoiding such foods before contrast MRI.

How to reduce microplastic exposure: less plastic, glass, natural fibers, HEPA filters

Aug 31, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 15

Practical tips from a Bloomberg article: use less single-use plastic, avoid microwaving in plastic, choose glass or stainless steel, wear natural-fiber clothing, use HEPA filters and remove shoes indoors. Complete avoidance is impossible, and links to heart disease are potential.

Small Swedish study: colesevelam lowered blood PFAS up to 38%

Nov 11, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 10 · 💬 12

Summarises a 10-person Swedish study of adults with high PFAS exposure: cholestyramine increased fecal PFAS excretion and a 12-week course of colesevelam reduced blood PFAS up to 38%. Authors say it was very small and observational, needs larger trials, and these drugs should not be used off-label.

Urinary metals linked to coronary calcium comparable to smoking and diabetes in MESA

Sep 18, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 8 · 💬 5

A JACC study in 6,418 MESA participants followed over 10 years found higher urinary levels of non-essential (cadmium, tungsten, uranium) and essential (cobalt, copper, zinc) metals corresponded to more coronary artery calcification, with associations similar in size to traditional risk factors like smoking and diabetes.

Oat beta-glucan may help clear PFAS and lowers LDL about 5-7% via bile acid binding

May 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 2 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

FoundMyFitness shares that beta-glucan from oats and mushrooms reduces bile acid reabsorption, which in animal studies increases fecal PFAS excretion and also underlies LDL lowering of about 5-7% at roughly 3 g/day of beta-glucan. PFAS clearance in humans is a proposed mechanism.

Micronanoplastics 50 times higher in carotid plaque than in plaque-free arteries

Apr 23, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 14 · 💬 3 ↩ 1

A small preliminary study presented at an AHA meeting found carotid plaque, especially from people with stroke, TIA or transient vision loss, held 50 times more micronanoplastics than plaque-free arteries. It cannot prove causation and no direct link to inflammation markers was found, though gene activity related to plaque stability differed.

Glyphosate: usage surge, conflicting safety assessments and residues in food
