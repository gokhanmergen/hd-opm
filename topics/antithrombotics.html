Heart attack clots form on ruptured unstable plaque, not in plaque-free arteries

Mar 17, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 29 · 💬 12 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Louis argues that although most heart attacks are caused by clots, clots form when unstable plaque ruptures, so people with near-zero plaque have near-zero risk of clot-caused heart attacks. Blood thinners like aspirin are proven to lower risk in people with considerable plaque but are unlikely to help, and may harm, those with little or no plaque.

Heart attacks peak in the early morning; bedtime dosing of BP drugs may help

Jun 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 14 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Louis explains that most heart attacks occur shortly after waking, when cortisol surges, vessels tighten and blood pressure spikes. He suggests taking blood pressure medication, aspirin and statins at bedtime, and corrects his post to say people are found dead in the 'bathroom' rather than 'toilet', since defecation syncope is much rarer than a standard heart attack.

Olive oil may reduce clotting by raising apoA-IV, which limits platelet aggregation

Jul 02, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 10 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A Life Extension article describes mechanistic studies in which apoA-IV, a protective HDL component, reduces platelet aggregation and thrombosis, and monounsaturated fats such as extra virgin olive oil raise apoA-IV, especially after meals. This is mechanistic and partly animal evidence.

Baby aspirin during and after Covid may be worth discussing if you have heart disease

Jan 06, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 12 Infections & vaccines

Louis suggests people with heart disease who get Covid ask their doctor about baby aspirin during infection and for a few weeks after. Infections temporarily raise heart attack and stroke risk through inflammation, and Covid raises clot risk more than other infections. A linked 2022 meta-analysis found aspirin use associated with lower Covid mortality without more bleeding.

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.

Aspirin acts as an immune modulator via platelets and T cells, an NEJM analysis suggests

Jan 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 27 · 💬 3

Louis shares commentary on a new NEJM analysis explaining how aspirin reduces cancer metastasis. The mechanism involves platelet-derived thromboxane A2 activating an immunosuppressive pathway in T cells, which aspirin interrupts, rather than simple pain or inflammation relief.

Aspirin blocks platelet TXA2 suppression of T cells and reduces metastasis in mice

Dec 07, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 4

Louis shares a 2025 Nature study showing that platelet-derived thromboxane A2 binds CD8+ T cells and, via ARHGEF1, weakens their anti-tumor activity. Aspirin or COX-1 inhibitors lifted this and reduced metastatic burden in mouse melanoma and lung models, only in animals with intact T-cell immunity, suggesting a mechanism for aspirin's apparent cancer protection.

ASPREE: aspirin tied to 70% lower dementia with high platelet-count genetic scores

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 4

Monash researchers analysed genetic data from 13,541 ASPREE participants and found that among those in the top 20% of a platelet-count polygenic score, 1% on 100 mg aspirin developed dementia versus 3.3% on placebo. Aspirin is not recommended for dementia prevention, and this is a genetic subgroup finding that needs confirmation, with bleeding risk to weigh.

2026 ACC statement: daily aspirin for primary prevention adds bleeding with little benefit

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 5

A quoted summary of the 2026 ACC antiplatelet statement says pooling 13 trials of 165,265 people, aspirin modestly reduced heart attacks and strokes but raised major bleeding and did not reduce all-cause death. Benefit faded in trials after 2000, likely because statins now supply the protection.

A Nordic randomized trial of more than 3,500 colon and rectal cancer patients found 160 mg of aspirin daily for three years roughly halved recurrence in those whose tumors had a PIK3 pathway mutation, present in about 40% of patients.

Infections temporarily raise heart attack and stroke risk; vaccines may do so far less

Oct 14, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 Infections & vaccines

Louis explains that infections such as flu raise short-term heart attack and stroke risk through inflammation and coagulation effects that destabilize plaque, and that the effect fades in a week or two. Vaccines can in theory do this to a much smaller degree. He suggests asking a doctor about a baby aspirin during and shortly after infection, and avoiding hs-CRP tests during that window.

Higher stored EPA linked to 36% lower bleeding stroke risk in a 21-year Danish study

Nov 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Louis shares a 21-year Danish study of 54,546 adults using fat-tissue samples of EPA and DHA. People with the highest EPA had a 36% lower risk of bleeding stroke, DHA was neutral, and ALA was linked to 32% higher risk (needs confirmation). Pooled RCTs found no extra brain bleeding, except small risk with high-dose prescription EPA. The study is observational.

Lp(a) makes clots form more easily and resist breakdown via OxPL and apo(a)

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Lp(a)

Louis shares a summary of an ATVB commentary by Rafael Zubiran explaining why high Lp(a) may be worse than LDL. Its oxidized phospholipids activate monocytes to make tissue factor, and apo(a) resembles plasminogen and competes with clot breakdown, producing denser, harder-to-dissolve clots. Louis notes statins, especially atorvastatin, may neutralize OxPL and aspirin lowers clotting risk.

CHIP not tied to heart attack or stroke in a 9,434-person aspirin trial, but to bleeding

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 · 💬 1

Louis shares a JAMA Cardiology secondary analysis of a primary-prevention aspirin trial (9,434 participants) on clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), a common aging-related blood cell mutation. CHIP was not associated with more cardiovascular events but was associated with more clinically significant bleeding, and aspirin's effects did not differ by CHIP status. He also explains what CHIP is.

A genetic analysis of 13,541 ASPREE participants found aspirin did not prevent dementia overall, but in the top 20% of a platelet-count genetic score dementia occurred in about 1% on aspirin versus 3.3% on placebo. Major bleeding was more than twice as common in this subgroup, so the post cautions against starting aspirin for dementia prevention.
