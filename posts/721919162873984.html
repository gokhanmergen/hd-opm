Check where a study sits on the evidence hierarchy before citing it

Louis Nervegna · Dec 10, 2023 · ♥ 27 reactions · 15 comments · original Facebook post
How to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an evidence-hierarchy graphic and warns that people often cite low-level studies (such as ecological studies) to refute higher-level ones. He advises asking first where on the hierarchy a posted study falls.

  • Ecological studies sit near the bottom of the hierarchy
  • Common error: low-tier studies cited against high-tier ones

Everyone reading medical studies should understand this hierarchy of evidence.

A common technique used by quacks and charlatans— or people who just don’t know what they are talking about— is to cite studies from the bottom of this hierarchy to try to refute studies at the top.

This technique is used all the time in health groups on Facebook. The first question you should ask when you see someone post a study is: Where on this hierarchy does the study fall?
