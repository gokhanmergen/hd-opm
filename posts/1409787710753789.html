Clonal hematopoiesis: mutant bone-marrow cells as a heart disease risk factor

Louis Nervegna · Oct 01, 2026 · ♥ 1 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRPPlaque, CAC & CT angiographyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

The poster explains that clonal hematopoiesis, in which mutated blood stem cell clones expand in bone marrow, is a heart disease risk factor that becomes likely with advancing age, appears earlier in some people through genetics or chance, and a minority avoid. A shared video describes how these cells can fuel atherosclerosis.

  • Clonal hematopoiesis is a risk factor for heart disease
  • Most people eventually acquire it with age; some earlier; a minority avoid it

What he is describing here is called clonal hematopoiesis. It is a risk factor for heart disease.

This post has a good explanation of it.

If you live long enough, odds are that you will eventually get it. Some people get it young, due to genetics or bad luck. Most people ultimately get it as they become old. A minority avoid it entirely.

Clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) mutations precede and raise atherosclerosis risk in PESA study

Sep 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 12 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

CNIC researchers report in Nature Medicine, using the PESA-CNIC-Santander cohort, that people with clonal hematopoiesis mutations at baseline were more likely to develop atherosclerosis later, supporting it as a cause rather than a consequence. A related European Heart Journal study is also noted.

High vitamin B12 in a multivitamin did not raise disease risk in a 14-year Harvard trial

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 9 Supplements & vitamins

The poster argues it is unlikely high B12 intake causes cancer or clonal disease. The Physicians' Health Study multivitamin trial, which contained 10 times the RDA of B12, found no increase in major disease over 14 years and an 8% lower overall cancer risk. Nurses' and Physicians' cohorts also found no B12-disease link, except combined B12 and B6 with hip fracture, which vanished for B12 alone.

EVITA trial: vitamin C lowered deaths in clonal blood conditions (exploratory result)

Sep 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 8 Supplements & vitamins

In the small phase 2 EVITA randomized trial of oral vitamin C in clonal cytopenia and lower-risk myeloid cancers, the vitamin C group had 65% lower risk of death (11 vs 24 deaths) and fewer serious adverse events. Survival was exploratory and the primary endpoint of clonal growth showed no difference. The poster speculates relevance to CHIP and cardiovascular disease but calls it a big unproven leap.

CHIP (clonal hematopoiesis): a little-known risk factor for heart disease and stroke

Apr 08, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Risk assessment & lab tests

Explains clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential: blood cells carrying acquired mutations that raise the risk of blood cancers, coronary disease and stroke. It becomes more common with age, can be detected with a next-generation sequencing panel, and has no specific treatment yet. The post notes testosterone therapy and anything strongly stimulating bone marrow may help CHIP expand.
