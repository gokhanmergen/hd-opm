LDL and atherosclerosis drive ischemic stroke risk; lifestyle supports vessel health

Louis Nervegna · Mar 10, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Heart attack, stroke & proceduresLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

A neurosurgery practice post explains strokes often arise from silent atherosclerosis, higher LDL is associated with ischemic stroke, and lipid control and lifestyle support vascular health. General information.

#strokeHD #brainHD

Shared from Jagannathan Neurosurgery · original post

Many strokes are the result of vascular changes that develop silently over years.

One of the main processes behind this is atherosclerosis — the buildup of cholesterol and inflammatory material inside artery walls. As plaques grow, they can narrow vessels or eventually block blood flow to the brain.

Studies show that higher LDL cholesterol is associated with increased risk of ischemic stroke, while healthier lipid levels are linked to better long-term vascular outcomes.

That’s why doctors evaluate cholesterol as part of overall vascular health, not just heart health.

Evidence-based ways to support healthy blood vessels include:

  • Regular physical activity
  • A diet rich in fiber and healthy fats
  • Controlling blood pressure and blood sugar
  • Avoiding smoking
  • Maintaining healthy cholesterol levels

Brain health depends on healthy circulation.
Understanding your numbers is one step toward protecting it. 🧠
