Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.

Muscle biological age is the top cell-type predictor of mortality in 60,542 people

Jun 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Aging & longevity

Louis shares a 2026 Nature Medicine study using plasma proteins from 60,542 people to estimate the biological age of over 40 cell types. Skeletal muscle aging best predicted all-cause mortality. He cautions about reverse causality but notes it offers a biological rationale for resistance training lowering mortality.

Whey protein raised IGF-1 but not muscle or strength in 24-week RCT of older adults

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 17 Protein

A 24-week placebo-controlled trial raised protein to 1.5 g/kg/day with whey in healthy older adults. It did not improve muscle power, strength, performance or mass, though IGF-1 rose; potassium bicarbonate was also ineffective. Louis argues this means the harms of high IGF-1 without benefit.

COSMOS multivitamin matches Centrum-type RDA doses except B12 at 10x the RDA

Nov 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 8 ↩ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shows the ingredient list of the multivitamin used in the COSMOS trial next to the one used in Physicians' Health Study II. Nearly all nutrients are near RDA levels, but B12 is about 10x the RDA, and he suspects that dose may have driven the cognitive benefit seen in COSMOS. COSMOS showed slower cognitive decline in older adults, and PHS II showed an 8% cancer reduction.

Daily multivitamin slowed cognitive aging by about two years in a COSMOS meta-analysis

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 10 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a summary of COSMOS cognition results: in the main analysis of 573 adults 60+, a daily multivitamin modestly improved overall cognition and episodic memory over two years. Pooling with two earlier COSMOS cognition studies (more than 5,000 older adults) gave clear benefits, estimated at about two years of slower cognitive aging. Effects were modest, it is unclear which nutrient matters, dementia prevention was not tested, and the manufacturer donated tablets. He adds that this was an RDA-level multivitamin (Centrum Silver).

Metformin associated with over 50% lower dementia risk in 20-year DPP/DPPOS follow-up

Aug 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 5 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis shares a New Scientist report on observational analysis of the Diabetes Prevention Program randomized trial and its follow-up, suggesting metformin may cut dementia risk by more than half. It aligns with monkey data on slower brain aging. The finding is not conclusive and the mechanism is unclear.
