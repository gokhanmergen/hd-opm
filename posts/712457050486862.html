Starting lipid lowering at 40-49 could cut 30-year ASCVD risk from 17% to 6-12%

Gokhan Can · Nov 20, 2023 · ♥ 5 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelStatins
AI summary & key facts

Summarizes a 2020 Circulation modeling study of early versus delayed primary prevention. Treating people with high cholesterol in their 40s lowered projected 30-year cardiovascular risk much more than waiting 10-20 years, especially with non-HDL-C of 160 mg/dL or higher.

  • Early treatment at 40-49: 30-year risk down from 17% to 6-12%
  • Delaying treatment 10-20 years left 13-15% remaining risk
  • Greatest benefit with non-HDL-C of 160 mg/dL or higher

The Expected 30-Year Benefits of Early Versus Delayed Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease by Lipid Lowering (2020)

  • Starting treatment at age 40-49 in people with high cholesterol could lower their 30-year risk of heart problems from 17% down to 6-12%, depending on the model used.
  • Waiting 10-20 years to start treatment instead left them with a higher remaining risk of 13-15%.
  • The benefits of early treatment were greater in those with higher cholesterol levels.

Conclusions:

  • Substantial ASCVD risk reduction over 30 years is achievable with early intensive lipid lowering in individuals in their 40s-50s with non-HDL-C ≥160 mg/dL.

Notes:

  • You can lower your LDL in many ways including diet and exercise.
  • This study hasn't quantified harms regarding elevated diabetes risk from statins.

Meta-analysis: lowering LDL with statins had no effect on cancer incidence or death

Summarizes an individual-participant meta-analysis from the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists showing that about five years of statin therapy did not change new cancer diagnoses or cancer deaths. This is reassuring evidence against fears that lowering LDL promotes cancer.

Statins work as well in women as men: 27-trial CTT meta-analysis of 174,000 participants

Dec 19, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 11 Statins

Louis rebuts the claim that statins are less effective in women using the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' meta-analysis of 27 randomized trials. Per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction, proportional reductions in major vascular events and all-cause mortality were similar in women and men, with no adverse effect on cancer or non-CV mortality. Trials averaged 4.9 years, so longer-term benefit is likely larger.
