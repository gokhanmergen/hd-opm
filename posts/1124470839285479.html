Butter raises LDL and offers no CVD protection; look at the totality of evidence

Responding to claims that butter is a superfood, the shared post says the butter literature shows neutral to slightly higher all-cause mortality and no CVD protection, and that butter's saturated fat reliably raises LDL-C in controlled feeding trials. Somehow in 2025 we’re still out here calling butter a “superfood.” 🤦‍♂️

The butter literature shows neutral to slightly higher all-cause mortality and no protective effect for CVD. (PMID: 27355649)

Butter is concentrated saturated fat, which reliably raises LDL-C in controlled feeding trials. (PMID: 12716665)

And LDL-C isn’t just a marker — it’s a causal driver of atherosclerotic CVD. (PMID: 28444290)

Not only is LDL causative, you can LITERALLY draw a straight line through lifetime exposure to LDL and the probability of a cardiovascular event! The more LDL you are exposed to, the greater the risk of a CVD event and CVD death(PMID: 23083789)

When you replace SFA with PUFA, CHD events drop in RCTs (~19% in meta-analysis). (PMID: 20351774)

A Cochrane review of long-term trials found fewer combined CVD events when people cut saturated fat (often replacing it with PUFA or carbs). (PMID: 32827219)

RCTs also show butter raises LDL more than cheese (food-matrix effect). (PMIDs: 28251937, 22030228)

Butter can fit in a balanced diet — but calling it a health food is a stretch. If your goal is heart health: lean toward unsaturated fats, fiber, plants, and adequate protein. 🫀

Summary of Examine's article on saturated fat. A 2016 WHO meta-analysis of 84 RCTs (2,353 healthy adults) examined replacing 1% of calories from carbohydrates or unsaturated fat with saturated fat. Saturated fat raised LDL-C and apoB (suggesting higher risk), and HDL rose only about one-tenth as much as LDL-C, raising the LDL/HDL ratio.

Replacing saturated with polyunsaturated fat cut non-fatal heart attacks 25%

Dec 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 19 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis summarizes an Annals of Internal Medicine systematic review of 17 randomized trials with hard endpoints. Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowered non-fatal heart attacks by 25% in higher-risk people over 5 years, similar to a lower-dose statin; other outcomes trended favorably but were not fully significant when the replacement was the general diet.

Finland's North Karelia Project: less saturated fat, CHD deaths down nearly 80%

Dec 03, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis shares a Tom Rifai summary of Finland's 1970s turnaround, led by the North Karelia Project, where the population cut saturated fat and shifted to rapeseed, sunflower and soybean-based margarines and oils. Serum cholesterol fell about 15-20% and CHD mortality fell nearly 80% in working-age men, although smoking and blood pressure improvements also contributed.

Harvard: unsaturated fats protect, trans fats harm; PREDIMED backs olive oil and nuts

Louis quotes Harvard's Nutrition Source page correcting myths about dietary fat. It says polyunsaturated fats from vegetable oils, nuts and seeds help ward off type 2 diabetes, trans fats do the opposite, and the PREDIMED Mediterranean diet with olive oil or nuts reduced cardiovascular events.
