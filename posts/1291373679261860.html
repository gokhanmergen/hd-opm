Scoping review of about 500 studies finds seed oil concerns lack scientific foundation

Louis Nervegna · May 14, 2026 · ♥ 12 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Myths, misinformation & controversiesInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a 2026 review in Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition covering RCTs, cohorts, biomarker and Mendelian randomization data on seed oils and linoleic acid. It found no inflammatory effect of linoleic acid and supports replacing saturated with polyunsaturated fat for CVD.

  • 2012 review of 15 RCTs: no increase in inflammatory markers
  • Meta-analysis of 30 RCTs: no change in 11 inflammatory markers including CRP
  • AHA analysis: swapping saturated for polyunsaturated fat gave 29% lower coronary heart disease risk
  • Higher linoleic acid intake linked to lower CVD and all-cause death

“The team of researchers reviewed studies on seed oils and linoleic acid across multiple health outcomes: cardiovascular disease, inflammation, LDL (or “bad”) cholesterol oxidation, cancer, liver fat, body weight, type 2 diabetes and oil processing. The researchers examined randomized controlled trials, prospective cohort studies, biomarker studies and Mendelian randomization analyses.”

Link to the full journal publication:

Paper · Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition · 2026Concerns about the health effects of industrially produced seed oils are without scientific foundation: a scoping narrative review of the clinical and observational evidencetandfonline.com

“A 2012 systematic review of 15 randomized controlled trials found virtually no evidence that adding linoleic acid [main fat in seed oils] to the diet increases inflammatory markers in healthy adults. A follow-up meta-analysis of 30 randomized trials found no significant changes in 11 different inflammatory markers, including C-reactive protein, when linoleic acid intake increased.”

One American Heart Association analysis of trials that swapped saturated fat for polyunsaturated fat found a 29% lower risk of coronary heart disease.”

“The review also tackled concerns about heating oils and oil processing. Repeatedly overheating any oil at very high temperatures can degrade it, but this isn’t something most home cooks need to worry about.”

“Many people also flag safety concerns with hexane, which is a solvent sometimes used during oil extraction. The review notes how residual hexane levels in commercially available oils are well below conservative regulatory limits. In fact, one cited calculation estimated you would need to consume about 4,900 grams of seed oil daily (about 22 cups) to exceed a conservative daily exposure limit.”

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
