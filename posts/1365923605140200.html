Persistently low lymphocyte count is linked to higher mortality; check ALC on your CBC

Louis Nervegna · Aug 10, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Risk assessment & lab testsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes persistent lymphopenia is associated with higher mortality, possibly via thymus involution, and that absolute lymphocyte count is on a standard CBC. The quoted explanation links low ALC with worse outcomes in critically ill patients including those with heart failure.

  • ALC = WBC x lymphocyte percentage
  • Low ALC associated with mortality in sepsis, pneumonia, heart failure and some cancers

Persistently low lymphocyte count (lymphopenia) is associated with significantly increased mortality.

The mechanism may be thymus involution— the age-related shrinkage of the thymus gland. More and more research is linking thymus involution with risk for age-related diseases.

You can find the absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) in the CBC section of your regular bloodwork.

Quoted:

Absolute Lymphocyte Count (ALC) is the actual number of lymphocytes—a type of white blood cell involved in fighting infections, regulating the immune response, and identifying abnormal cells—in a given volume of blood, usually reported as cells/µL or ×10⁹/L.

ALC is calculated from the white blood cell (WBC) count and the percentage of lymphocytes: ALC = WBC × lymphocyte percentage.

A low ALC, called lymphopenia, can be a marker of immune dysfunction or severe physiological stress and has been associated with increased mortality risk in critically ill and hospitalized patients, including those with sepsis, pneumonia, heart failure, and some cancers.
