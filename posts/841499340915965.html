A normal coronary CTA can still miss early wall plaque that has not narrowed the lumen

Louis Nervegna · Jul 25, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

The poster explains that contrast CCTA mainly shows plaque that protrudes into the lumen, so early-stage plaque growing within the vessel wall may not be seen unless it narrows the lumen. A normal CCTA therefore does not rule out early plaque. Based on a cardiologist's video.

  • CCTA mainly shows plaque from inside the lumen
  • Early wall plaque may be missed if lumen is not narrowed

A coronary CT angiogram scan with contrast (CCTA) mainly shows plaque from inside the lumen.

The lumen is the hollow space inside the artery that allows blood to flow.

A CCTA does not show all of the earlier stage plaque that is growing inside the arterial walls, unless it is extensive enough to narrow the lumen.

So you can have a perfect CCTA, but still have early stage plaque in the artery walls that hasn’t yet had an effect on the lumen.

Same CT plaque, lower MI risk in women than men in a 10,003-person Danish cohort

Aug 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 3 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

A Danish population study of 10,003 asymptomatic adults over 40 found subclinical coronary plaque less frequent in women at every age, for example 6% vs 20% at age 40-44. After 6 years, obstructive disease carried 4.1-fold higher MI risk in women versus 12-fold in men, though the sex interaction was not statistically significant.

A CT angiogram shows the lumen, so it can miss early plaque inside the artery wall

Apr 07, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 25 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains that coronary CT angiography is 'lumenography': contrast dye fills only the lumen, so early plaque growing inside the artery wall is mostly invisible until it narrows the lumen. A perfect CCTA therefore does not rule out early-stage plaque. He links a Dr Alo video on this blind spot.

DISCO-CT: DASH diet plus statins reversed ~40 mm3 of soft plaque in one year

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains the DISCO-CT randomized trial, where a DASH diet plus statins produced the largest soft (non-calcified) plaque regression of any dietary trial on CT angiography. Adjusted to one year it was -39.5 mm3, versus an average at-risk person growing about 5 mm3 per year, so roughly 8 years of progression reversed. He notes the DASH diet was about 30% fat, mostly unsaturated, and adds caveats about interpretation.

Six statin trials show 5 to 17% total plaque reversal, with the best results at lowest LDL

Oct 31, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 ↩ 1 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis shares a chart of randomized trials showing plaque reversal with statins, ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors, with total atheroma volume change plotted against LDL-C. Total plaque reversal ranged from about 5% to 17% in six older statin trials, measured by CT angiography or IVUS, and came from soft rather than calcified plaque. The chart suggests total plaque reversal begins once LDL falls below roughly 90 mg/dL.
