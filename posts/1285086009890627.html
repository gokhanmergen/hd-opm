Shared from Layne Norton · original post

You’ve been lied to about seed oils…

They aren’t inflammatory 🤷🏻‍♂️

Yep, I said it. On their own, seed oils are NOT inflammatory. If you eat a ton of them and gain body fat, inflammation can go up, but that’s because excess body fat is pro-inflammatory, not because seed oils are uniquely harmful.

When you actually compare them calorie-for-calorie with saturated fat, seed oils are neutral or slightly beneficial for inflammation. They also reduce liver fat compared to saturated fat (PMID: 28752873, 22492369).

“But what about oxidized LDL?!” This is one of the biggest anti-seed oil talking points… and the data doesn’t support it. A recent human trial (PMID: 41695083) gave people ~30g/day of soybean oil and measured inflammatory markers and oxLDL. Result?

  • No increase in inflammatory markers (IL-6 actually trended DOWN)
  • No increase in oxLDL
  • Decrease in arachidonic acid (AA), a precursor to inflammatory compounds

That last point is especially ironic since critics claim seed oils increase inflammation via AA. In humans, we see the opposite. Here’s what they miss about oxLDL:

LDL doesn’t oxidize much in the bloodstream—it’s cleared quickly and protected by antioxidants. Oxidation happens mainly AFTER LDL enters the artery wall, where it can stick around for weeks. So what determines oxLDL?

LDL levels in the blood, which means…

More saturated fat → higher LDL → more LDL enters artery wall → more oxLDL
More PUFA (seed oils) → lower LDL → less entry → less oxLDL

This is basic lipidology.
