Higher omega-6 in subcutaneous fat linked to lower death rates in older men

Louis shares a Harvard Health item on a JAMA Cardiology study finding that high omega-6 levels in subcutaneous fat, mostly reflecting dietary linoleic acid from plant oils, correlated with lower death rates in older men.

  • Omega-6 in subcutaneous adipose tissue correlated with lower mortality in older men

Higher levels of omega 6 in subcutaneous fat tissue are associated with lower risk of death in older men

Quoted:

“Omega-3 fatty acids are touted for their potential health benefits, and rightfully so, but a new study suggests men may want to get enough omega-6 fatty acids, too. Research published online Aug. 17, 2016, by JAMA Cardiology found that high levels of omega-6s in the subcutaneous adipose tissue—the layer of fat just beneath the skin—correlated with lower death rates among older men. Omega-6s in the diet mostly come in the form of linoleic acid, found in plant oils (such as corn, soybean, and sunflower oils) as well as nuts and seeds.

In the study, adipose tissue biopsy specimens and blood samples were taken from 853 men at an average age of 71. The men also filled out a seven-day food diary. After taking into account known risk factors for cardiovascular disease, the researchers found that a higher proportion of linoleic acid in the adipose tissue was associated with a lower risk of death during the 15-year follow-up period.”

Replacing 10 g/day of butter with plant oils linked to 17% lower mortality

Louis shares Harvard research on 221,054 people from the Nurses' Health Studies I and II and the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study, published in JAMA Internal Medicine. Higher plant-oil intake (especially soybean, canola, olive) was linked to lower total, cancer and cardiovascular mortality, while butter was linked to higher total and cancer mortality; a modeled swap of 10 g butter for plant oils lowered mortality by 17%.
