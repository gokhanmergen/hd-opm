Higher extra virgin olive oil share was linked to lower Lp(a) in PREDIMED-Plus

Louis Nervegna · Mar 14, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Lp(a)Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)
AI summary & key facts

Louis reports a PREDIMED-Plus analysis of 348 participants: those with the highest EVOO proportion of olive oil had about 22% lower Lp(a) after adjustment, significant only in the non-energy-restricted Mediterranean diet group.

  • 348 participants of one PREDIMED-Plus node
  • Adjusted coefficient -21.62 (95% CI -41.17 to -2.07), p trend 0.030
  • Significant only in the control group

Can extra virgin olive oil lower Lp(a) ?

Maybe.

In the PREDIMED randomized trial, participants who consumed the most extra virgin olive oil (as a proportion of total olive oil consumption) had roughly 22% lower Lp(a) than participants who consumed the least, even after correcting for confounders.

Quoted:

“A total of 348 participants of one node of the PREDIMED-Plus study were included. We calculated the proportion of EVOO in relation to total olive oil consumption.”

“After adjusting for potential confounders, a higher proportion of EVOO intake (proportion = 1) was associated with lower LpA levels (adjusted beta-regression coefficient: -21.62 [95% CI: -41.17; -2.07]; p for trend = 0.030), in comparison to participants with a lower proportion of EVOO intake (proportion = 0-0.49). In stratified analysis, results remained statistically significant only in the control group (non-energy-restricted Mediterranean diet).”

“In a high cardiovascular risk Spanish population, higher intake of EVOO in relation to total olive oil consumption was associated with reduced LpA levels.”
