Esselstyn's olive oil claim rests on Vogel 2000, which used refined, not extra virgin, oil

Louis Nervegna · May 11, 2026 · ♥ 19 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis points out that the 2000 Vogel study Caldwell Esselstyn cites for olive oil harming the endothelium tested refined olive oil, not EVOO. He says all randomized trials directly testing EVOO show improved or neutral endothelial function and FMD, both acutely and long term.

  • Vogel 2000 (J Am Coll Cardiol) used refined olive oil
  • Randomized EVOO trials show improved or neutral FMD, acutely and long term

This is the study that Caldwell Esselstyn cites to claim that extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) causes heart disease, by damaging the endothelium (Vogel, 2000):

Paper · J Am Coll Cardiol · 2000The postprandial effect of components of the Mediterranean diet on endothelial function.Conclusion: In terms of their postprandial effect on endothelial function, the beneficial components of the Mediterranean and Lyon Diet Heart Study diets appear to be antioxidant-rich foods, including vegetables, fruits, and their derivatives such as vinegar, and omega-3-rich fish and canola oils.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Vogel’s study used refined olive oil.
Not EVOO.

ALL the randomized trials that have looked directly at EVOO show either an improvement in endothelial function and FMD, or a neutral effect. This is true whether you measure the effect immediately (postprandial) or longer term.

↪ RCT: one dose of high-polyphenol EVOO raised FMD 4.8% in adults at risk for diabetes

↪ Meta-analysis of 30 trials: olive oil lowers CRP and IL-6 and improves FMD

It is intellectually dishonest to cite Vogel’s study to justify the claim that EVOO causes heart disease.

In addition to only testing refined olive oil, Vogel’s study only looked at the immediate postprandial effect on FMD, within 3 hours of consuming the refined olive oil. It did not look at the longer term effect on FMD.

Many things that are good for the endothelium actually temporarily impair it in the short term. This is called hormesis. The temporary stress on the endothelium turns on defensive pathways that protect it in the longer term. Intense exercise works this way. It temporarily impairs endothelial function and FMD, but improves it in the longer term.

↪ Olive oil's brief FMD dip resembles exercise's transient, hormetic endothelial effect

Randomized trials that have looked at the impact of refined olive oil on endothelial function and FMD in the longer term have consistently shown either a benefit or a neutral effect.

↪ Meta-analysis of 30 trials: olive oil lowers CRP and IL-6 and improves FMD

↪ Meta-analysis of 16 RCTs: olive oil Mediterranean diet improves endothelial markers

The Vogel study is over 25 years old now. It never tested EVOO. It only looked at the immediate postprandial effect of refined olive oil, and never looked longer term. There are many newer studies which directly test EVOO, and do look longer term. Esselstyn completely ignores ALL of these newer studies. And the Vogel study was NOT a randomized trial.

Research cited

  • Vogel et al., postprandial effect of Mediterranean diet components on endothelial function · RCT · J Am Coll Cardiol · 2000
