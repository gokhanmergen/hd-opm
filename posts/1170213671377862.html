Arginine reduced amyloid and inflammation in Alzheimer's mice and flies

Louis comments on a mouse and fruit-fly study where arginine reduced amyloid buildup and improved behavior. He cautions it may not translate to humans and that people who recently had a heart attack should not take arginine.

  • Animal and lab studies only
  • Less amyloid, fewer plaques, lower inflammation in models
  • Warning: avoid arginine after a heart attack

This is an interesting study in mice and flies. It may have no value at all in humans— but given the lack of effective treatments for Alzheimer’s, it might be worth a try.

Research cited

  • Arginine in Alzheimer's mouse and fly models · animal
Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

Researchers gave arginine to mice and fruit flies engineered to develop Alzheimer’s-like pathology.
The result?

Far less amyloid buildup, fewer brain plaques, lower inflammation, and better behavior, even though the disease genes were still there.

Before you jump: this is NOT a human treatment.
These are animal and lab studies only.

But here’s why people are talking about it:
Alzheimer’s starts decades before symptoms, with proteins beginning to misfold and clump. Arginine seems to act like a “chemical chaperone,” keeping those proteins from sticking together in the first place.

If we can understand how nutrients or molecules do this in animals, we can design better human therapies that target the same early mechanism.

That’s the real story:
Not “arginine cures Alzheimer’s”…

but a safe, inexpensive molecule showing a repeatable mechanism that scientists can now chase in humans.

Doi: 10.1016/j.neuint.2025.106082

Johns Hopkins arginine RCT stopped early: 6 deaths vs 0 in heart-attack survivors

Nov 29, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 25 ↩ 3 Supplements & vitamins

Louis warns that L-arginine supplements, taken to raise nitric oxide, can be dangerous after a heart attack. A Johns Hopkins randomized trial of 153 heart attack survivors given 3 g arginine three times daily was stopped after six months when six deaths occurred in the arginine group and none on placebo. Citrulline, which the body converts to arginine, might carry a similar concern, which is his speculation.

Caffeinated coffee linked to 37% lower Parkinson's and 34% lower Alzheimer's risk

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A shared summary says that in one large study unsweetened caffeinated coffee was linked to a 37% lower risk of Parkinson's disease and a 34% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias. Caffeine blocks adenosine A2A receptors that regulate dopamine signaling in movement circuits. The evidence described is observational, with some animal mechanistic work.

Higher midlife vitamin D linked to lower brain tau 16 years later in Framingham

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Brain health & dementia

A Framingham Heart Study analysis followed 793 adults for 16 years, starting at average age 39. Vitamin D was measured in 2002-2005 and brains were scanned in 2016-2019, and higher midlife vitamin D was linked to lower tau tangles but not to amyloid. The post notes vitamin D acts like a steroid hormone with receptors in memory regions.
