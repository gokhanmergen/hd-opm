Aging causes VO2max to decline, not the reverse

Louis Nervegna · Nov 01, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that aging drives VO2max decline regardless of activity habits, and that Mendelian randomization suggests the association comes mainly from reverse causality. He cites a longitudinal Circulation study showing the decline accelerates in each successive decade.

  • Rate of VO2 decline accelerates with each age decade
  • Occurs regardless of physical activity habits

Aging is massively causal of declining VO2max.

In other words, as you get older, the aging process itself causes your VO2max to rapidly decline. This decline is an inevitable consequence of aging, and happens regardless of physical activity habits.

I’ve yet to see any credible evidence whatsoever of any significant causality in the other direction. In other words, there is no evidence that shows declining VO2max (per se) causes aging.

There are studies that show lack of exercise may accelerate aging, but no evidence that VO2max per se is responsible.

Mendelian randomization studies have demonstrated that the association between declining VO2max and aging is explained primarily by reverse causality: ↪ Mendelian randomization finds no causal effect of VO2-max on longevity or type 2 diabetes

Quoted:

“Conclusions: The longitudinal rate of decline in peak VO2 in healthy adults is not constant across the age span in healthy persons, as assumed by cross-sectional studies, but accelerates markedly with each successive age decade, especially in men, regardless of physical activity habits. The accelerated rate of decline of peak aerobic capacity has substantial implications with regard to functional independence and quality of life, not only in healthy older persons, but particularly when disease-related deficits are superimposed.”
