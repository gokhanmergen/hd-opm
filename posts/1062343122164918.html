Mediterranean diet wins on weight but misses on memory in diverse aging population

In a 14-month study of older, primarily African American adults with obesity, the Building Research in Diet and Cognition (BRIDGE) trial found that an eight-month Mediterranean diet intervention led to sustained physical health improvements. Over 14 months, a group that combined the diet with calorie restriction lost an average of 3.8 kg, and both Mediterranean diet groups significantly improved their diet adherence scores (by +3.2 and +3.4 points, respectively) compared to a control group (+0.2 points). Despite these successful changes in diet and weight, which also included reduced visceral fat, the study found no significant improvements in cognitive functions like memory, attention, or executive function compared to the control group. The authors conclude that while the Mediterranean diet is effective for improving cardiometabolic health, a longer or more intensive intervention might be necessary to produce measurable cognitive benefits in this population.
