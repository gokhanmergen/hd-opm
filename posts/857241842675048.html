Metformin cut SARS-CoV-2 viral load 3.6-fold in the COVID-OUT randomized trial

Louis Nervegna · Aug 23, 2024 · ♥ 14 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Quoting a Clinical Infectious Diseases analysis of the COVID-OUT trial, the post says metformin suppresses viral protein translation via host mTOR and reduced viral load versus placebo. Metformin also lowered hospitalization/death and long Covid odds.

  • Hospitalization/death through 28 days: -58%; long Covid through 10 months: -42%
  • Viral load reduced 3.6-fold (-0.56 log10 copies/mL; p = 0.027)
  • Detectable virus at day 5 or 10: OR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55-0.94)
  • Viral rebound 3.28% vs 5.95% (OR 0.68, CI 0.36-1.29)

Metformin has antiviral activity against RNA viruses, including Covid (SARS-CoV-2).

The mechanism seems to be suppression of viral protein translation via targeting the host mTOR pathway.

Quoted:

“In the COVID-OUT randomized trial for outpatient coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), metformin reduced the odds of hospitalizations/death through 28 days by 58%, of emergency department visits/hospitalizations/death through 14 days by 42%, and of long COVID through 10 months by 42%.”

“The mean SARS-CoV-2 viral load was reduced 3.6-fold with metformin relative to placebo (−0.56 log10 copies/mL; 95% confidence interval [CI], −1.05 to −.06; P = .027). Those who received metformin were less likely to have a detectable viral load than placebo at day 5 or day 10 (odds ratio [OR], 0.72; 95% CI, .55 to .94). Viral rebound, defined as a higher viral load at day 10 than day 5, was less frequent with metformin (3.28%) than placebo (5.95%; OR, 0.68; 95% CI, .36 to 1.29). The metformin effect was consistent across subgroups and increased over time.”
