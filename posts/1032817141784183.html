Graph of how foods raise or lower LDL relative to a Western diet

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a graph of foods' LDL effects serving-for-serving against a typical Western diet baseline, e.g. almonds lower LDL and sugar raises it. Excess calories raise LDL regardless.

  • Almonds lower LDL vs Western baseline; sugar raises it
  • Excess calories raise LDL regardless of food

This graph has a nice summary of how various foods affect LDL cholesterol.

The horizontal line in the middle represents neutral. Foods above the line raise LDL. Foods below the line lower LDL.

Keep in mind that these changes on the graph are relative to the baseline diet in the studies— a typical western diet.

Also keep in mind that this is a serving-for-serving comparison. (Eating too many calories will raise LDL, regardless of what you eat.)

For example, a serving of almonds lowers LDL relative to a baseline western diet, whereas a serving of sugar raises it.

Overfeeding saturated fat raised liver fat 55%, sugar 33%, unsaturated fat 15% in an RCT

A randomized trial overfed three groups by 1000 calories for 3 weeks from butter/dairy, olive oil/nuts or added sugar. Liver fat rose 55%, 15% and 33% respectively, showing calories are not equal for the liver.
