Alzheimer's-related cognitive changes observed as early as age 24

Shared from Dale Bredesen, MD · original post

Contrary to public perception, Alzheimer’s is not solely an old-timer’s disease. Cognitive changes associated with Alzheimer’s have now been observed as early as age 24, emphasizing that, regardless of your age, a proactive preventive plan is essential. Please, for anyone who is 35 or over, get evaluated and begin active prevention—let’s all make Alzheimer’s a rare disease. Caffeinated coffee linked to 37% lower Parkinson's and 34% lower Alzheimer's risk

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A shared summary says that in one large study unsweetened caffeinated coffee was linked to a 37% lower risk of Parkinson's disease and a 34% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias. Caffeine blocks adenosine A2A receptors that regulate dopamine signaling in movement circuits. The evidence described is observational, with some animal mechanistic work.

Higher midlife vitamin D linked to lower brain tau 16 years later in Framingham

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Brain health & dementia

A Framingham Heart Study analysis followed 793 adults for 16 years, starting at average age 39. Vitamin D was measured in 2002-2005 and brains were scanned in 2016-2019, and higher midlife vitamin D was linked to lower tau tangles but not to amyloid. The post notes vitamin D acts like a steroid hormone with receptors in memory regions.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
