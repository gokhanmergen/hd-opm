PREDIMED: eating nuts more than 3 times a week linked to 39% lower all-cause mortality

AI summary & key facts

Louis asks whether nuts are a longevity drug, using a post-hoc PREDIMED analysis (BMC Med 2013). Participants who ate nuts more than three times a week had lower all-cause, cardiovascular and cancer mortality, but this is an observational analysis within an RCT and cannot prove causality.

  • PREDIMED: about 7,500 participants, 4.8 years, primary CVD prevention
  • More than 3 nut servings/week: 39% lower all-cause mortality
  • Upper nut category: 55% lower CV mortality and 40% lower cancer mortality
  • Post-hoc analysis; cannot prove causality

Should we be thinking of nuts as a life extension drug?
Nut consumption (>3 times per week) was associated with a 39% reduction of all-cause mortality in the PREDIMED randomized controlled trial:

Paper · BMC Med · 2013Frequency of nut consumption and mortality risk in the PREDIMED nutrition intervention trial.Conclusion: Increased frequency of nut consumption was associated with a significantly reduced risk of mortality in a Mediterranean population at high cardiovascular risk.Please see related commentary: http://www.biomedcentral.com/1741-7015/11/165.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

↪ PREDIMED nut eaters had 63% lower total mortality and 55% lower CV mortality

This was in comparison to people in the trial who did not consume nuts.

The 7500 person trial ran for 4.8 years, and was designed as a primary prevention trial for CVD. The trial compared a control diet (similar to American Heart Association recommendations at the time), a Mediterranean diet + additional EVOO, and a Mediterranean diet + nuts.

Participants who also consumed a traditional Mediterranean diet, along with nuts >3x per week, saw a whopping 63% reduction in total mortality, compared to people in the trial who did not consume nuts.

Not only was there a protective effect against dying of CVD from nut consumption, but there was a similar protective effect against dying of cancer.

Participants in the upper category of nut consumption had a 55% reduction in cardiovascular mortality and a 40% reduction in cancer mortality, compared to participants who did not consume nuts.

The y-axis of the graph in this post is the hazard ratio. It gives the risk of dying of any cause for people in the trial, relative to a baseline of 1. (That baseline is people who did not consume nuts.)

All types of nuts were included in this analysis (including nut-like legumes such as peanuts). But most of the nut consumption came from walnuts, hazelnuts, and almonds, since these were the types provided free of charge to people in the Mediterranean diet + nuts intervention group (in raw form). The recommended dose in the intevention group was 30g per day (a small handful), consisting of 15g walnuts, 7.5g hazelnuts, 7.5g almonds.

Note that these mortality results are from a prospective observational analysis of the trial cohort. As with all observational analyses, they do not prove causality.

Nut consumption reversed arterial thickening in the carotid artery in this same trial:

Paper · Arterioscler Thromb Vasc Biol · 2014Changes in ultrasound-assessed carotid intima-media thickness and plaque with a Mediterranean diet: a substudy of the PREDIMED trial.Conclusion: Compared with a control diet, consumption of a MedDiet supplemented with nuts is associated with delayed progression of ICA-IMT and plaque. The results contribute mechanistic evidence for the reduction of cardiovascular events observed in the PREDIMED trial.ahajournals.org

↪ PREDIMED: nuts regressed carotid plaque and a nut-rich Mediterranean diet cut stroke 50%.

People in the nut group saw a large 46% reduction in risk of stroke, consistent with the reversal of thickening in the carotid:

American College of CardiologyPrimary Prevention of CVD With a Mediterranean Diet - American College of Cardiologyacc.org

This reduction in carotid thickening and strokes was seen in the randomized trial itself, and therefore demonstrated a strong causal link. It was not observational.

Quoted:

“During a median follow-up of 4.8 years, 323 total deaths, 81 cardiovascular deaths and 130 cancer deaths occurred. Nut consumption was associated with a significantly reduced risk of all-cause mortality (P for trend <0.05, all). Compared to non-consumers, subjects consuming nuts >3 servings/week (32% of the cohort) had a 39% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio (HR) 0.61; 95% CI 0.45 to 0.83). A similar protective effect against cardiovascular and cancer mortality was observed. Participants allocated to the Mediterranean diet with nuts group who consumed nuts >3 servings/week at baseline had the lowest total mortality risk (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.22 to 0.66).”

“Compared to non-consumers, subjects who consumed >3 servings of nuts per week at baseline had a significant 39% lower risk of all-cause mortality. Relative risk reductions were similar for the upper baseline category of non-walnut nuts (34%) or walnut consumption (45%), and when we evaluated the repeated measurements of total nut consumption over time (32%).

Moreover, those in the upper category of baseline nut consumption had a 55% lower risk of cardiovascular mortality and 40% lower risk of cancer mortality compared to those who never consumed nuts. The study subjects participated in the PREDIMED study, a long-term, randomized nutrition intervention trial [14], and those consuming more nuts at baseline and allocated to intervention with a MedDiet supplemented with nuts showed a significantly reduced total mortality risk of 63%.”

“Conclusion- Increased frequency of nut consumption was associated with a significantly reduced risk of mortality in a Mediterranean population at high cardiovascular risk.”

Quoted:

“We evaluated 164 subjects with complete data. In a multivariate model, mean ICA-IMT progressed in the control diet group (mean [95% confidence interval], 0.052 mm [−0.014 to 0.118 mm]), whereas it regressed in the MedDiet+nuts group (−0.084 mm [−0.158 to −0.010 mm]; P=0.024 versus control).”
