Stem-cell heart patch (BioVAT) raised ejection fraction at 3 months in phase 1-2 trial

Louis shares a NEJM interim report of a phase 1-2 trial in which stem-cell-derived engineered tissue was placed on the surface of failing human hearts. Mutated blood stem cells (CHIP) may be a major cause of heart disease, doubling MI risk

Apr 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 13 Inflammation & CRP

Louis explains clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP): age-related mutations in bone-marrow stem cells yield white blood cells that drive inflammation and plaque growth, and the same mutations link to leukemia. A quoted report says CHIP carriers had a 54% higher chance of dying within a decade, from heart attacks and strokes, and mouse transplants of CHIP-mutated cells produced fast-growing, inflamed plaques. Louis credits Gokhan Can for pointing him to this.

TARGET-D: tailored vitamin D dosing missed its main endpoint but cut heart attack 52%

Summarises TARGET-D (630 adults with acute coronary syndrome, ~4.2 years): doses adjusted to reach 40-80 ng/mL did not significantly reduce the composite of death, heart failure hospitalization or stroke, though heart attack risk was 52% lower. Authors call for validation to rule out data mining.

The four stages of coronary artery disease, from soft plaque to ischemia

Jul 27, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 17 · 💬 2 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Explainer on how coronary artery disease develops over decades: soft plaque forms from childhood or early adulthood, plaques harden and calcify, arteries narrow causing ischemia with angina or silent ischemia, and later events. Many people stay asymptomatic through the early stages.

Personalized vitamin D3 dosing halved repeat heart attacks in an Intermountain trial

Nov 11, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary describes an Intermountain Health trial of 630 recent heart attack survivors in which vitamin D3 was dosed individually to keep blood levels above 40 ng/mL. Repeat heart attacks were lower, though total major cardiovascular events were not, and larger trials are needed.
