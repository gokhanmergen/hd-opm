RCT: one dose of high-polyphenol EVOO raised FMD 4.8% in adults at risk for diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Mar 14, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 22 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis rebuts Caldwell Esselstyn's claim that EVOO impairs endothelial function, citing a randomized crossover trial in adults at risk for type 2 diabetes. A single 50 mL dose of high-polyphenol EVOO improved flow-mediated dilation compared with refined olive oil, with no acute blood pressure effect, and the authors note that reports should specify the oil's characteristics.

  • EVOO change in endothelial function 1.2 +/- 6.5% vs -3.6 +/- 3.8% for refined olive oil (p = 0.0086)
  • Absolute FMD improvement of 4.8% with 50 mL high-polyphenol EVOO vs refined oil
  • 1.5% absolute FMD change is considered clinically significant
  • No acute blood pressure effects (NCT04025281)

Caldwell Esselstyn repeatedly claims that extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) impairs flow mediated dilation (FMD) and endothelial function in arteries after consumption.

This is FALSE.

When you run a proper randomized controlled trial and actually test it in humans, EVOO improves both FMD and endothelial function immediately after ingestion, compared to baseline.

Quoted:

“EVOO acutely improved EF [endothelial function] as compared to refined olive oil (1.2 ± 6.5% versus −3.6 ± 3.8%; p = 0.0086).”

[Note that the numbers above in parentheses, 1.2 and -3.6, show the change in endothelial function relative to baseline after consuming EVOO and regular olive oil respectively.]

Quoted:

“Our data suggest that ingestion of a single dose of 50 mL of high-polyphenolic EVOO, compared with 50 mL of refined olive oil without polyphenols, improved FMD, a marker of cardiovascular risk, by an absolute improvement of 4.8% in adults at risk for T2DM. Based on EF [Endothelial Function] studies guidelines, an absolute improvement of as little as 1.5% in FMD is considered clinically significant [16]. Therefore, the FMD improvement observed was clinically meaningful and statistically significant.”

“Our data suggest that ingestion of high-polyphenolic EVOO, relative to refined olive oil without polyphenols, improved EF [Endothelial Function] among individuals at risk for T2DM. No acute effects on blood pressure were observed with either olive oil. Therefore, the inclusion of high-polyphenolic EVOO, rather than refined olive oil without polyphenols, in the diets of those at risk for T2DM may reduce their cardiovascular risk.”

Louis argues that over 30 human RCTs show olive oil improves flow-mediated dilation and endothelial function, quoting a systematic review of 30 studies and 3,106 participants. Olive oil lowered CRP by 0.64 mg/L and IL-6 by 0.29 and raised FMD by 0.76 percentage points versus controls. Refined olive oil may briefly impair FMD after a meal, while EVOO improves it acutely and over the long term.
