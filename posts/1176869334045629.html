Polyphenol-rich diets in 3,100 TwinsUK adults linked to better BP, lipids and CVD risk

Louis Nervegna · Dec 08, 2025 · ♥ 30 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsBlood pressure, salt & potassium
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a BMC Medicine study of more than 3,100 TwinsUK adults followed over a decade. Diets rich in tea, coffee, berries, nuts and whole grains were associated with healthier blood pressure and cholesterol and lower CVD risk scores, and urinary polyphenol metabolites showed the same pattern.

  • More than 3,100 adults, over 10 years (TwinsUK)
  • Foods: tea, coffee, berries, nuts, whole grains
  • Higher polyphenol metabolites linked to lower CVD risk scores and higher HDL
  • Observational study

Regular consumption of polyphenol-rich foods like tea, coffee, berries, nuts, and whole grains may significantly support long-term heart health. A decade-long study of more than 3,100 adults found that those who consistently ate polyphenol-packed diets had healthier blood pressure and cholesterol levels, as well as lower predicted cardiovascular risk.

Quoted:

“The study, published recently in BMC Medicine, tracked more than 3,100 adults from the TwinsUK cohort for more than ten years. It found that diets rich in certain groups of polyphenols were linked to healthier blood pressure and cholesterol levels, which contributed to lower CVD risk scores.

For the first time, the researchers also assessed a large set of urine metabolites that appear when the body processes polyphenols.

These biomarkers showed that people with higher levels of polyphenol metabolites (especially those linked to flavonoids and phenolic acids) had lower cardiovascular risk scores. They also tended to have higher HDL cholesterol, also known as 'good' cholesterol.”

Quoted:

Professor Ana Rodriguez-Mateos, senior author and Professor of Human Nutrition at King's College London, said: "Our findings show that long-term adherence to polyphenol-rich diets can substantially slow the rise in cardiovascular risk as people age. Even small, sustained shifts towards foods like berries, tea, coffee, nuts, and whole grains may help protect the heart over time."

Dr. Yong Li, first author of the study, added: "This research provides strong evidence that regularly including polyphenol-rich foods in your diet is a simple and effective way to support heart health. These plant compounds are widely available in everyday foods, making this a practical strategy for most people."

CORDIOPREV: Mediterranean diet reversed carotid plaque and cut recurrent events ~25%

Apr 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis summarizes the 2021 CORDIOPREV RCT in patients with prior heart attack or stroke on standard medical care: a Mediterranean diet rich in olive oil, nuts and fish reduced carotid thickness at 5 and 7 years, unlike a low-fat diet, and reduced maximum plaque height. Recurrent major cardiovascular events were roughly 25% lower, even though participants were already on statins and other drugs.

CORDIOPREV: Mediterranean diet shrank carotid plaque and cut recurrent events about 25%

Mar 28, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 13 ↩ 27 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis summarizes CORDIOPREV, an RCT of 1002 coronary patients comparing a Mediterranean diet rich in extra virgin olive oil with a low-fat diet. The Mediterranean diet reduced carotid intima-media thickness at 5 and 7 years while the low-fat diet showed no change, and also lowered carotid plaque height. He also cites roughly 25% fewer recurrent major cardiovascular events over 7 years, with benefits seen despite statin treatment and mean LDL-C below 90.
