Selenium plus CoQ10 cut cardiovascular death by over half; benefit persisted 10-12 years

Louis Nervegna · Sep 07, 2025 · ♥ 25 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a trial in older adults where selenium plus CoQ10 for four years lowered cardiovascular mortality, with persistent benefit in follow-up. He suggests adding about 200 mcg selenium (two Brazil nuts) to CoQ10 for people already taking it, while acknowledging the study's limitations.

  • CV mortality reduced by more than half after 4 years of selenium plus CoQ10
  • 49% lower CV mortality 10 years after the trial ended
  • 41% lower risk 12 years after treatment ended
  • Benefits held in subgroups with heart disease, diabetes and hypertension (per quoted article)

This is an example of an intervention that might actually be working by slowing down the aging process of the cardiovascular system.

Many people in the group take CoQ10 anyway (particularly if using a statin). It’s just a matter of adding about 200ug of selenium to get the possible benefits of this intervention. (That’s the amount of selenium found naturally in 2 brazil nuts.)

You can certainly poke holes in the quality of this study. I won’t argue with that. Skeptcism is warranted. But the risk seems to be very low.

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

“A clinical trial found that older people who took a combination of selenium and coenzyme Q10 for a period of four years, experienced a lower rate of death due to cardiovascular causes (including heart attack, stroke, and heart failure), which was reduced by more than half. [12]

Researchers continued to collect data from participants, and results of two subsequent sub-studies have been consistent in regard to cardiovascular mortality.

Ten years after the end of this trial, the selenium-CoQ10 group was still 49% less likely to die from cardiovascular causes. [11] Even 12 years after the end of the treatment period, the benefit persisted. A 41% lower risk in the active treatment group, as compared to placebo, was found in the second follow-up analysis. [13] These benefits remained when looking at subgroups who had preexisting conditions, including heart disease, diabetes, and high blood pressure.”
