About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Louis Nervegna · Oct 03, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyHeart attack, stroke & proceduresStatins
AI summary & key facts

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.

  • About 64% of thrombotic heart attacks: rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque
  • About 25%: erosion of soft plaque
  • About 5%: calcified nodules; the post totals soft plaque at about 95%
  • Statins reported to reverse soft plaque in acute coronary syndrome patients

About 64% of heart attacks caused by acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque:

This is the same kind of plaque that statins and other lipid-lowering drugs have been shown to stabilize and reverse: facebook.com/share/v/1EhCbEQzek.

Another 25% of heart attacks caused by acute thrombosis come from gradual erosion of soft plaque:

When the blood touches exposed tissue in an eroded soft plaque, it can trigger a clotting response. (This doesn't happen with calcified plaque.)

Only about 5% of heart attacks caused by acute thrombosis involve calcified plaque:

Calcified nodules can cause heart attacks when dense pieces of calcium break through the inner wall of a heart artery, tear the protective covering, and trigger an acute thrombosis.

All together:

These acute thromboses drive what are called Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS), and represent the classic presentation of an acute heart attack. 95% are caused by soft plaque.

Statins have been shown to be very effective at reversing soft plaque in patients with acute coronary syndromes: ↪ Japan-ACS: pitavastatin and atorvastatin both regressed coronary plaque by about 17-18%.

Reference:
“Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Coronary Syndromes: A Review” (JAMA, Feb 2022)
