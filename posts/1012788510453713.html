Young-adult colon cancer 'doubling' means 0.0032% to 0.0064% a year, not soaring cancer

Louis Nervegna · Apr 23, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 17 comments · original Facebook post
CancerMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis puts the 'colon cancer doubled in young people' claim in context: in 2024, 6.4 per 100,000 people aged 20-39 were diagnosed, versus about half that before. He argues this tiny change barely affects total cancer rates and that overall cancer death rates have fallen sharply over 40 years. Off the cardiovascular focus.

  • 6.4 per 100,000 aged 20-39 diagnosed with colon cancer in 2024 (0.0064%)
  • Rate previously about 0.0032%
  • Total US cancer death rates have fallen over 40 years

6.4 out of 100,000 people ages 20-39 were diagnosed with colon cancer in 2024.

That is 0.0064% of all people 20-39.

So when people say that colon cancer rates have “doubled among young people”, they are talking about:

  • increases from 0.0032% to 0.0064% per year, in 20-39yo’s

These are tiny numbers.

Influencers are using this to claim that "cancer rates are skyrocketing" — which is a lie.

This tiny increase has virtually no effect at all on total cancer rates in the USA.
