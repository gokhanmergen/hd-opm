At high risk of a heart attack? Find a good cardiologist and add, don't replace, treatment

Louis Nervegna · Feb 26, 2024 · ♥ 46 reactions · 15 comments · original Facebook post
Myths, misinformation & controversiesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis advises high-risk people to follow a good cardiologist rather than social media claims, which often cherry-pick one study out of many. He argues the window to experiment has passed once risk is high, and that natural or alternative approaches are safest as additions, with treatment reduced later with the cardiologist if risk markedly improves. It is opinion, not research.

  • Do natural approaches in addition to, not instead of, cardiologist treatment
  • Medications can be reduced with a cardiologist if condition markedly improves

If you’re at high risk of a heart attack, then Rule #1 is to find a good cardiologist and listen to them.

Don’t gamble with your life based on nonsense that you hear on social media. Most of what you hear on social media is just people’s guesses and opinions, backed up by very little (if any) hard science. This includes the many YouTube videos where the person in the video goes to great length to cherry pick 1 study out of 100 that supports their particular conspiracy theory, while ignoring the other 99 that show exactly the opposite. This is not how science is really done. Science considers the totality of all the evidence.

The sad reality is that people at high risk have mostly lost the window of opportunity to experiment. The chance to experiment was 20 years ago before their heart disease became so severe. Once they become high risk, these experiments could end up costing them their lives.

You hear occasional stories about very high risk people who experimented and “cured” their own heart disease. But what you don’t hear are the many more stories of high risk people who experimented and died. Dead people don’t tell their stories.

If you are at high risk and want to use alternative or natural approaches (including various diets), it is safest to do them in addition to what your cardiologist suggests, not in place of them. If the natural or alternative approaches really do work for you, then your risk will drop, and you can work with your cardiologist to reduce the recommended amount of treatment as appropriate. There is no hard rule that says that you can not stop or reduce the amount of a particular medication if your condition markedly improves.

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. SCOT-HEART: CT angiography cut 5-year coronary death or MI risk 41% in chest pain patients

Jan 03, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 18 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Summarises SCOT-HEART (4,146 patients with stable chest pain, standard care vs standard care plus coronary CTA). CTA guidance lowered coronary death or nonfatal MI to 2.3% from 3.9%, mainly by directing aspirin and statin use to patients with plaque. Total procedures were similar by year 5; results apply to chest-pain patients only.
