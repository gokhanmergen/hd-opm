Insulin resistance starts with fat accumulating in muscle, before liver fat

Louis Nervegna · Nov 10, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights Yale research (Kitt Petersen) showing insulin resistance begins with fat buildup inside muscle, even in lean young people predisposed to diabetes, and that liver fat accumulates later. A linked Yale article reports small amounts of liver fat are linked to insulin resistance and cardiometabolic risk factors.

  • Fat accumulation in muscle is the earliest step
  • Can occur in lean, healthy young people with a diabetes predisposition
  • Liver fat comes later in the process

Insulin resistance begins with accumulation of fat inside muscle. This is the very beginning of the process.

It can happen even in healthy young people who are lean, if they have a predisposition towards diabetes. This has been rigorously studied.

It’s only later on in the process that fat begins to accumulate in the liver.

Quoted:

Kitt Petersen, MD, professor of medicine (endocrinology) in the Department of Internal Medicine at Yale School of Medicine, has been studying young, lean, healthy people with insulin resistance for more than 20 years.

“The main determinant for insulin resistance is really when we start to build up fat in the organs where it doesn’t belong such as liver and skeletal muscle,” said Petersen, co-director of the Translational Research Core at the Yale Diabetes Research Center and first author on the study.

“In healthy young people, we find that the first place ectopic fat builds up is mainly in the muscles and is associated with muscle insulin resistance.”

The second occurrence in some people is that fat also begins to build up in their liver. “This increase in liver fat content can then result in hepatic insulin resistance which in turn can lead to type 2 diabetes, NASH and cirhhosis,” Petersen said.
