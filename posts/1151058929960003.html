Harvard cohorts: protein type matters more than amount, and processed meat is worst

Louis Nervegna · Nov 02, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares Walter Willett's summary of nearly 300,000 people followed for 30+ years. Plant protein is protective, fish and poultry slightly protective, dairy and eggs neutral, unprocessed red meat modestly harmful, and processed meat strongly increases mortality.

  • ~300,000 people followed 30+ years (Nurses' Health Studies I & II, Health Professionals)
  • Processed meat has the worst hazard ratios apart from trans fats
  • Plant protein from legumes, nuts, seeds and whole grains is the most protective
  • Results may not apply to protein powders or bars

Actual evidence-based information on protein from a Harvard professor who has published over 2000 peer-reviewed journal papers on nutrition— and was, for a long time, the second most academically-cited author on all of pubmed.

Protein intake and mortality risk

Summary from tracking nearly 300,000 people prospectively for over 30 years in the Harvard Nurses Study I & II and the Harvard Physicians Follow-up Study.

These people were tracked all the way from midlife to old age and death.

The main discovery is that the TYPE of protein matters far more than the AMOUNT.

Plant protein is protective. Poultry and fish are slightly protective. Dairy and eggs are neutral. Fermented dairy (cheese and yogurt) does better than unfermented (milk). Red meat (unprocessed) modestly increases mortality.

Processed meat very strongly increases mortality, much worse than any other protein source by far. And much worse than unprocessed red meat.

Summary:

  1. Processed meat is an unmitigated disaster. The mortality risk is horrible.

Except for trans fats, no food category has worse hazard ratios in high-quality prospective cohort studies than processed meat.

  1. Unprocessed red meat increases mortality risk, but only by a little.

Eating it once a week has little effect on risk.

  1. Fish and poultry confer a small benefit.
  1. The benefit from plant protein is the largest.

The plant protein in these studies came primarily from foods like legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains, etc… NOT from supplements. We don’t know if these same results would apply to plant protein powders or processed foods that include them, like protein shakes, bars, etc.

Notice that the benefit from plant protein is no where near enough to cancel out the huge harm from processed meat.

(Unfortunately, you can’t cancel out bacon by eating beans.)

  1. Dairy is neutral.

He doesn’t mention this in the video, but when you break out dairy as fermented vs non-fermented in the HNS 1&2 and HPFS, the fermented forms like yogurt and cheese tend to confer a small benefit whereas non-fermented forms tend to show the opposite. When combining both, they tend to cancel out for an overall neutral effect.

  1. Eggs are not mentioned in the video, but these also come out roughly neutral with respect to mortality, in the HNS 1&2 and HPFS.
