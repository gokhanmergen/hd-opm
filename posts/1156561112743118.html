AHA: coffee drinkers with treated AFib were 39% less likely to have a recurrence

The American Heart Association summarizes the DECAF trial: adults treated for AFib who drank at least one cup of coffee daily for 6 months had 39% fewer recurrences than those who abstained. The 200-person trial was published in JAMA, though some people still find caffeine triggers AFib symptoms.

Shared from American Heart Association · original post

Coffee may not raise the risk of atrial fibrillation and may even reduce it, new research suggests.

Adults treated for AFib who drank at least one cup of coffee a day for 6 months were 39% less likely to have a recurrent episode of irregular heart rhythm, compared to those who did not have any coffee.

The results of the 6-month, 200-person trial were presented this week at Scientific Sessions, the premier global meeting for cardiovascular science and medicine. This trial is simultaneously published today as a full manuscript in the peer-reviewed scientific journal JAMA.

Some people may still find that caffeine, including coffee, triggers or worsens their AFib symptoms. If you’ve recently been treated for AFib, ask your doctor if continuing to drink coffee is safe for you.

Louis warns that the atrial fibrillation risk from high-dose omega-3 is real. A 2021 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (81,210 people) found higher AF risk, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs (114,592 people) confirmed it at higher doses in people at high CVD risk, but not in those without high risk, and not at low doses in either group.

Cleveland Clinic researchers analyzed blood samples from over 5,000 people undergoing heart procedures and found TMAO, made by gut bacteria from nutrients in red meat and other animal products, was linked to higher AFib risk.
