Atherosclerosis drivers such as high apoB and blood pressure also raise dementia risk

BRAINS AND BLOOD VESSELS
Over the past few years, the link between atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) has become more and more clear.

Studies have shown that atherosclerosis, endothelial dysfunction, high BP, high LDL/apoB, and other ASCVD drivers are consistently associated with higher risk of dementia, including Alzheimer’s. Poor blood vessel health can impair cerebral blood flow, disrupt the blood–brain barrier, and promote chronic inflammation and oxidative stress, all of which are things that likely accelerate Alzheimer-type pathology in the brain. ￼

Various types of papers all show that coronary artery disease, hypertension, and even just high LDL levels all track with greater dementia risk over time.

Now genetic work is adding another layer, as analyses also show an overlap between Alzheimer’s disease risk, genetic tendencies towards higher blood lipids, and coronary artery disease, suggesting at least some shared biology. ￼

Taken together, the message is pretty clear: ASCVD prevention is also helping prevent brain degeneration. Healthy arteries lead to a healthier brain. So keeping ApoB and blood pressure optimal, improving endothelial function, enhancing insulin sensitivity, and reducing chronic inflammation are not just going to help reduce chances of heart attacks and strokes. They are crucial parts of a comprehensive strategy for protecting brain health and cognition as we age. ￼
