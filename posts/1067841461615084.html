Rhonda Patrick interview: daily multivitamin may reverse cognitive aging ~3 years (COSMOS)

Louis Nervegna · Jul 14, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a podcast summary claiming COSMOS trial data show older adults taking a daily multivitamin improved memory and cognition, equivalent to about 3 years of cognitive aging. This is a secondary-source claim from an interview.

  • Claim: multivitamin improved memory, learning and executive function in older adults
  • Claim: effect equal to up to 3 years of cognitive aging

Quoted:

Daily multivitamin supplementation might actually reverse brain aging by multiple years

Robust research, including randomized controlled trials summarized in the comprehensive COSMOS study, found that older adults consistently taking a standard daily multivitamin saw measurable improvements in memory, learning, executive function, and overall cognitive health

Remarkably, regular multivitamin use effectively reversed cognitive aging by up to 3 years, making brains functionally younger

COSMOS: daily multivitamin slowed biological aging by about 4 months over 2 years

Sep 04, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 3 Supplements & vitamins

Louis quotes Dr JoAnn Manson on a COSMOS substudy of about 950 older adults using epigenetic clocks. All five clocks showed slower aging with a multivitamin, and two second-generation clocks showed significant slowing.

COSMOS: RDA-level multivitamin slowed epigenetic aging by about 4 months

Mar 28, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 9 Supplements & vitamins

Louis calls the COSMOS epigenetic-aging analysis arguably the highest quality RCT on multivitamins ever done. All five clocks showed a signal for slower aging, with significant effects on PCGrimAge and PCPhenoAge amounting to roughly 4 months of aging averted over 2 years, and larger effects in those with accelerated aging.

Louis argues clinicians should not give public health advice on multivitamins

Louis argues that epidemiology is a specialized field and that clinicians dismissing multivitamins ignored high-quality cohort data and COSMOS RCTs. The shared FoundMyFitness text claims multivitamins improved processing speed, cognition and episodic memory in older adults.
