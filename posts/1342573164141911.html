Analysis of 6 randomized controlled trials shows Atorvastatin improves kidney function.

Quoted:

“Conclusion - In patients at risk of or with cardiovascular disease, atorvastatin improved kidney function over time in a dose‐dependent manner. In the 3 treatment groups, kidney function improvement was strongly associated with lower cardiovascular risk.”

“In this post hoc analysis of 6 double‐blind randomized controlled cardiovascular outcome trials comprising 30,621 patients at risk of or having cardiovascular disease, we demonstrate that atorvastatin treatment improves kidney function over time in a dose‐dependent fashion.

Kidney function improvement, regardless of treatment arm, was associated with lower cardiovascular risk.”

“Our data suggest that both the cardio‐ and vasculoprotective efficacy of a pharmacological agent, such as atorvastatin, is reflected by the course of kidney function over time, indicating that this kidney‐related parameter might represent a surrogate end point for long‐term outcomes in cardiovascular risk patients.”

Louis cites a JAHA post hoc analysis of six double-blind outcome trials in patients at risk of or with cardiovascular disease. The authors suggest kidney function could serve as a surrogate end point.

Statins do not slow coronary calcium; severe-calcification odds hit 4.48 after 10+ years

Evidence review concluding statins reduce events and soft plaque but do not slow Agatston CAC progression, and that long-term cohorts show calcification accelerating without plateau, interpreted as plaque repair into stable scar. Trials: St. Francis (CAC unchanged, events down 33%), SALTIRE (progression 26% vs 18%) and BELLES (similar progression, ~11-14%).

Meta-analysis: lipid-lowering therapy regresses coronary plaque (Li 2021, SATURN)

Summarizes Li et al. 2021, a meta-analysis of 31 IVUS studies (4,997 patients) showing lipid-lowering therapy lowers atheroma volume, with maximal regression at LDL below 80 and HDL above 45 mg/dL. It also covers the SATURN analysis, where rosuvastatin 40 mg regressed plaque more than atorvastatin 80 mg.
