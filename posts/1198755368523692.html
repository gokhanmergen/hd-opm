'Uncured' and 'nitrate-free' processed meats still get nitrates from celery and sea salt

Louis Nervegna · Jan 10, 2026 · ♥ 13 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis warns that natural-labeled bacon, sausage, ham, jerky and deli meats use celery-derived nitrates that chemically cure the meat just like sodium nitrite. He says the result is nitrosamines, which the WHO links to colon cancer, and that they are also atherogenic and diabetagenic.

  • Celery powder and sea salt supply nitrates that cure the meat
  • Chemistry is the same as with sodium nitrite
  • Nitrosamines are linked to colon cancer by the WHO (per post)

Beware of “natural” processed meat products deceptively labeled as “uncured” or “nitrate-free”. These include bacon, sausage, ham, jerky, hot dogs, deli meat, etc.

These products use nitrates naturally found in celery powder or sea salt to chemically transform the meat, in the same way that sodium nitrite is used to cure meat. The chemistry is exactly the same in both cases. As a consequence, carcinogenic nitrosamines are produced, which the WHO warns directly cause colon cancer. These nitrosamines are also atherogenic and diabetagenic.

Please note that nitrates are not bad for you. In fact, they help to lower blood pressure and protect the endothelial lining of arteries. They are naturally found in fruits and vegetables.

The problem occurs when nitrates/nitrites react with animal flesh during the chemical transformation process that cures meat, and the exposure to high heat or acidic environments afterward. This is when toxic nitrosamines are formed. It is the nitrosamines that are bad for you, not the nitrates.
