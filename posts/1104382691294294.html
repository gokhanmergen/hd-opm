Evidence mostly supports Ramadan fasting as safe, improving metabolic and CVD risk markers

Louis Nervegna · Sep 03, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fasting & meal timingInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that systematic reviews and meta-analyses show Ramadan fasting is generally safe and healthy, improving insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome markers and other CVD risk factors. He adds that this is unsurprising for calorie restriction in an overweight population.

  • Improvements in weight, body composition, lipid profiles, glucose homeostasis, inflammatory and oxidative markers (per editorial)
  • Most cited references are systematic reviews and meta-analyses

There is a large body of medical literature on health benefits of fasting during Ramadan. The preponderance of evidence supports it as both safe and healthy. Many studies have shown that fasting during Ramadan improves markers of insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, as well as other risk factors for CVD.

Most of the references cited below are systematic reviews and meta-analyses of the many studies on this topic of Ramadan fasting.

This is not surprising. Any intervention that restricts calories and reduces visceral fat in a predominantly overweight population is likely to have health benefits.

Quoted:

“The many health benefits of Ramadan diurnal intermittent fasting (RDIF) include improvements in body weight and body composition (4–6), reducing complications of the metabolic syndrome (4), improving lipid profiles and other cardiometabolic risk factors (7–10). Further, in healthy people, RDIF helps improve glucose homeostasis (11), ameliorate inflammatory and oxidative stress markers (10), improve liver function tests (3), and modulate gene expression of various components of the anti-inflammatory and antioxidant defense and circadian rhythm systems (12, 13). In subjects with metabolic syndrome, RDIF was shown to improve several components of metabolic syndrome, upregulate tumor-suppressor proteins and downregulate tumor-promoter proteins (14).”
