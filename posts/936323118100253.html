Loyal's dog longevity drug targets insulin/IGF-1; claims lack publications so far

Louis Nervegna · Dec 26, 2024 · ♥ 1 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares news that Loyal raised $125M to launch LOY-002 in 2025, a daily pill intended to extend dog lifespan by at least a year by lowering insulin/IGF-1. He notes he has not seen publications supporting the claim and suggests diet can also lower insulin and IGF-1.

  • $125M raised; launch planned early 2025
  • Claim: at least 1 extra year of dog life
  • Louis has not seen publications backing the claim

Biotech startup Loyal has raised $125M to launch their longevity drug for dogs in 2025.

This drug works by lowering insulin/IGF1.

They claim that it will extend dog lifespan by at least a year.

(I have yet to see any publications from them so far to back that claim up, but it’s a big deal if true.)

Reference on 2025 launch:

Quoted:

“U.S. biotech startup Loyal plans to launch a lifespan-extending drug for dogs in early 2025, potentially offering insights into human longevity. The San Francisco-based company has secured $125 million in funding for LOY-002, a beef-flavored daily pill designed to extend canine lifespans by at least one year. The drug works by targeting age-related metabolic changes and insulin regulation [IGF1], according to Loyal CEO Celine Halioua.”

Note that there are many ways to lower insulin and IGF1 naturally by diet and lifestyle. Two of the most effective are not eating refined starch/sugar, and avoiding excessive amounts of insulinogenic animal proteins.

High insulin tells the liver to make cholesterol, tying insulin resistance to high LDL

Louis explains that insulin signals the liver to produce cholesterol, one reason insulin-resistant people tend to have high LDL. Losing visceral and especially liver fat to correct insulin resistance should therefore bring LDL down naturally. Supported by a Mass General Brigham explainer.

Insulin stimulates liver cholesterol production, linking insulin resistance to high LDL

Jun 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 22 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

The post explains that insulin signals the liver to make more cholesterol by keeping HMG-CoA reductase active, one reason insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome go with high cholesterol. It argues protein, especially animal protein, also raises insulin, partly offset by glucagon, and that swapping animal for plant protein lowers cholesterol beyond saturated fat and fiber effects. Much of the protein argument is the poster's inference.
