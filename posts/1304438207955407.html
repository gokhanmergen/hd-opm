Ez-PAVE: LDL target below 55 mg/dL cut major events 33% versus below 70 mg/dL

Shared summary of the Ez-PAVE NEJM trial randomizing 3,048 patients with ASCVD to LDL target below 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) or below 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) for 3 years. The intensive target had fewer events with equivalent safety, supporting combination lipid-lowering earlier.

  • Non-fatal MI cut by more than half; revascularisation down 37%
  • Fewer than 25% of eligible patients reach LDL below 55 mg/dL; under 10% get ezetimibe

Shared from Dr Paddy Barrett · original post

Guidelines have told us to target LDL below 1.4 mmol/L in patients with established heart disease. The randomised trial evidence to support that specific number has been missing. Until now.
The Ez-PAVE trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, randomised 3,048 patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease to one of two LDL targets — below 1.4 mmol/l (55 mg/dL) or below 1.8 mmol/l (70 mg/dL) and followed them for 3 years.
The result was clear. The intensive target group had a 6.6% event rate versus 9.7% in the conventional group. That is a 33% lower relative risk of the primary composite endpoint — cardiovascular death, heart attack, stroke, revascularisation, or hospitalisation for unstable angina. Hazard ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.52–0.86), p=0.002.
Non-fatal MI was cut by more than half. Revascularisation dropped by 37%. Safety was equivalent across all prespecified endpoints — and kidney function was actually better in the intensive arm.
The treatment gap in practice remains stark. Fewer than 25% of eligible patients reach LDL below 1.4 mmol/l (55 mg/dL). Fewer than 10% receive ezetimibe. Around 1% receive a PCSK9 inhibitor. This trial provides the evidence base to change that — and a clear argument for earlier, more aggressive combination lipid-lowering therapy.
Lower is better. The data now say so directly.
