Mayo Clinic study: skin generates senescent cells that may age the heart and other organs

Louis Nervegna · Oct 23, 2024 · ♥ 18 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Mayo Clinic study reported by Neuroscience News suggesting skin makes senescent cells that travel to other tissues, including the cardiovascular system, and accelerate aging. He adds that most circulating CRP originates in skin and advises caring for your skin to lower inflammation. The linked article is framed around brain aging, and the CRP claim is Louis's own.

  • Senescent skin cells may accelerate aging in distant tissues
  • Louis: most blood CRP originates in skin

This is a fascinating study from the Mayo Clinic.

It’s been known for a while that skin is a major source of inflammation in the human body. In fact, most CRP measured in blood circulation actually originates in the skin. This makes sense because skin is by far your largest organ.

Now this new study suggests that skin doesn’t just generate inflammatory molecules that travel elsewhere in circulation— but it also generates senescent cells that travel elsewhere and cause other tissues to age, including the heart and cardiovascular system.

So take good care of your skin. It will lower your CRP and other inflammatory markers, and maybe even slow aging itself by reducing the number of senescent skin cells that travel elsewhere and cause damage.
