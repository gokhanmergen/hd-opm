Olezarsen cut triglycerides 64% but did not change plaque in ESSENCE-TIMI 73b imaging

Louis Nervegna · Mar 31, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs
AI summary & key facts

The ESSENCE-TIMI 73b imaging study found olezarsen lowered triglycerides about 64% and remnant cholesterol about 72%, with ApoB down only about 16%, yet 12-month CCTA showed no difference in plaque. Louis reads it as showing TG lowering alone, without treating insulin resistance, does not slow plaque.

  • TG -64%, remnant cholesterol -72%, ApoB -16%, LDL-C unchanged
  • 12-month CCTA: no difference in non-calcified, low-attenuation or total plaque
  • Primary endpoint p=0.36; 97% already on therapy

This trial shows very clearly that lowering high triglycerides— without treating the underlying insulin resistance that causes it— does not slow plaque growth.

🧬🫀 Massive triglyceride reduction… zero plaque impact?

Very interesting study but IMHO with methodological flaws affecting the results.

The Essence–TIMI 73b imaging study tested a bold hypothesis:
👉 If you dramatically reduce triglyceride-rich lipoproteins,
👉 you should slow atherosclerosis.

Enter olezarsen (APOC3 inhibition).

🔬 What did they achieve?

Impressive biology:
🔻 Triglycerides ↓ ~64%
🔻 Remnant cholesterol ↓ ~72%
🔻 ApoB ↓ ~16%
➖ LDL-C unchanged

And yet…

📊 What happened to coronary plaque?

Measured by CCTA over 12 months:
👉 No difference vs placebo in:
✔️ Non-calcified plaque volume
✔️ Low-attenuation plaque
✔️ Total plaque burden

📉 Primary endpoint: neutral (p=0.36)

⚠️ So what’s going on?

This is the key message:
👉 Not all lipid lowering is equal.

Despite massive triglyceride reduction:
✔️ ApoB reduction was modest (~16%)
✔️ Patients were already aggressively treated (97% on therapy)
✔️ Follow-up may be too short for structural change

💡 The uncomfortable implication

We’ve been told:
“Triglyceride-rich lipoproteins are highly atherogenic.”

But this study challenges the clinical translation of that concept.

👉 Lowering TRLs ≠ necessarily shrinking plaque (at least short-term)

🎯 Clinical perspective
This is not a negative study.

It’s a clarifying one:
✔ Biology ≠ imaging effect
✔ Surrogate ≠ disease modification
✔ Mechanism matters

🔮 Bottom line

If you want to move plaque, you still need to move:
👉 atherogenic particle burden (apoB)

Everything else may be… secondary.

And maybe it’s time to stop asking:
“Did we lower triglycerides?”
…and start asking:
👉 “Did we actually change the disease?”
