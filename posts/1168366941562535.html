Low-carb diets lose their diabetes advantage by 12 months, trials and meta-analyses show

Gokhan Can · Nov 25, 2025 · ♥ 5 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Argues low-carb gives better remission and HbA1c at 6 months, but the edge fades. A 2021 BMJ meta-analysis (23 RCTs) found benefit at 6 months diminishing by 12; Silverii 2020 (37 RCTs, 3,301 patients) found HbA1c differences vanishing and reversing by 24 months; a 2-year calorie-matched RCT showed identical weight loss. Concludes the benefit comes from adherence, not metabolic magic.

  • BMJ 2021 meta-analysis (23 RCTs): improvements at 6 months diminished at 12; 12-month remission sparse or trivial
  • Silverii 2020 (37 RCTs, 3,301 pts): HbA1c vs control -0.17% at 3 months, -0.27% at 6 months (very low carb only), no difference at 12 months, +0.23% worse at 24 months
  • Tay 2-year RCT: low-carb ~14% vs high-carb ~53% energy; weight loss ~6.7 kg both; HbA1c -0.6% vs -0.9%

Low-carb does put more people with type-2 diabetes into remission at 6 months.

That part is real. But what happens after 6 months is the part nobody shares.

Let's look at the data.

BMJ 2021 meta-analysis (23 RCTs, Goldenberg et al.):

“Large clinically important improvements... at six months, which diminished at 12 months.”

Remission at 12 months was "sparse" or showed "trivial increased risk" depending on the definition used.

The data shows a clear U-turn. Early dominance, rapid regression.

PaperThe BMJ articlebmj.com

Silverii 2020 (3,301 patients, 37 RCTs) paints an even starker picture of the timeline.

HbA1c Advantage (Low Carb vs. Control):

  • 3 months: –0.17% (Statistically significant)
  • 6 months: –0.27% (Only in the Very Low Carb subgroup)
  • 12 months: No difference
  • 24 months: LC actually +0.23% behind

The trend line is brutal. Early advantage → Neutral → Long-term reversal.

Paper · Acta Diabetol · 2020Low-carbohydrate diets and type 2 diabetes treatment: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.Conclusion: LC diets may produce small short-term improvements in HbA1c and weight, which are not maintained in the long term. Data on their renal safety are insufficient.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Tay et al., 2-year RCT (Diabetes Obes Metab 2018).

  • Low-carb: ~14% energy
  • High-carb: ~53% energy
  • Calories & Exercise Matched

The result?

  • Weight loss: Identical (~6.7 kg both groups)
  • HbA1c reduction: Statistically similar (–0.6% vs –0.9%)

When you control for calories, the "metabolic magic" vanishes.

Paper · Diabetes Obes Metab · 2018Effects of an energy-restricted low-carbohydrate, high unsaturated fat/low saturated fat diet versus a high-carbohydrate, low-fat diet in type 2 diabetes: A 2-year randomized clinical trial.Conclusion: Both diets achieved comparable weight loss and HbA1c reductions. The LC sustained greater reductions in diabetes medication requirements, and in improvements in diurnal blood glucose stability and blood lipid profile, with no adverse renal effects, suggesting greater optimization of T2D management.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Takeaway: The magic of LC, regarding weight loss & diabetes, is in adherence. Early adapters feel more satisfied on LC foods, and get great weight-loss and blood sugar control results, while cutting calories. Long term, adherence challenges come back, sustainability drops. Very few people end up maintaining LC long-term, whereas you'll find millions of people who tried it and found it useful for short periods.
