Vegetable nitrates tied to lower dementia risk; processed-meat nitrates to higher

Louis Nervegna · Jun 28, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaMeat, eggs & dairyCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis adds that 'natural' or 'uncured' processed meats also form N-nitrosamines. The shared Dale Bredesen post describes a recent study in which the source of nitrates mattered more than the amount: vegetable nitrates were tied to lower dementia risk, while processed meat or drinking-water nitrates were tied to higher risk.

  • Nitrate-rich vegetables associated with lower dementia risk
  • Nitrates from processed meat or drinking water associated with higher dementia risk
  • Vegetable vitamins and antioxidants may steer nitrate toward nitric oxide rather than N-nitrosamines

This includes so-called “natural” or “uncured” or “nitrate-free” processed meats that use celery powder or sea salt in place of sodium nitrate. They also result in formation of toxic N-nitrosamines:

Shared from Dale Bredesen, MD · original post

It may sometimes feel like “everything causes dementia,” so a recent study showing that where dietary nitrates came from was more important than the amount of nitrates consumed caught my attention. Those consuming nitrate-rich vegetables saw their dementia risk decrease, while those consuming nitrates from processed meat or even drinking water saw their risk rise. The vitamins and antioxidants in vegetables may help nitrate turn into nitric oxide instead of N-nitrosamines, which are carcinogenic and potentially damaging to the brain.
