The appendix is a reservoir that repopulates gut bacteria

Louis Nervegna · Jan 24, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Gut microbiome
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains the appendix is not vestigial: it is part of the lymphatic system and shelters beneficial bacteria to repopulate the colon after illness or diarrhea.

  • Appendix is immune tissue like tonsils and spleen
  • Acts as a gut bacteria reservoir

We now know that the appendix is not a vestigial organ.

It serves an important purpose.

It is part of the lymphatic system, like the tonsils and spleen. And it acts as a reservoir for gut bacteria. During periods where the population of gut bacteria is wiped-out for whatever reason, the appendix is responsible for re-populating it.

Quoted:

The appendix is a small pouch located at the start of the large intestine. It contains a lining rich in immune tissue and a stable supply of beneficial bacteria. The structure of the appendix creates a protected environment where these microbes can live undisturbed. The narrow opening limits the flow of intestinal contents, allowing the bacterial colonies inside to remain sheltered.

During illness or diarrhea, large numbers of gut bacteria may be flushed out of the digestive tract. The appendix serves as a reservoir. When conditions improve, the helpful bacteria from the appendix move back into the colon and repopulate it. This helps restore normal digestion and supports a balanced microbiome.

The immune cells in the appendix monitor the bacterial community and help maintain its stability. This combination of storage and immune support makes the appendix an important backup system for gut health.
