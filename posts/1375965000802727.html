Dietary choline may protect mitochondria and slow brain-cell aging, a dementia mechanism

Louis Nervegna · Aug 22, 2026 · ♥ 31 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaMeat, eggs & dairyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis revisits a study offering a more plausible mechanism for why prospective studies link choline, especially from eggs, with lower dementia risk. Controlled feeding shows dietary choline barely raises brain acetylcholine, so the new proposal is that choline protects mitochondria from premature aging via phosphatidylcholine.

  • Prospective studies link dietary choline (eggs) to lower dementia risk
  • Acetylcholine mechanism is weak
  • New mechanism: phosphatidylcholine protects mitochondria

I posted about this remarkable study previously.

See this older post:
↪ Falling phosphatidylcholine may fragment mitochondria with age; choline may restore them

It might solve a big mystery in brain aging.

Prospective studies have repeatedly linked dietary choline (particularly from eggs) with decreased risk of dementia.

A proposed mechanism was that dietary choline raises acetylcholine levels in the brain. But this mechanism never made much sense: Controlled feeding studies have shown over and over again that dietary choline has only a very small effect on brain levels of acetylcholine.

Now this new study has elucidated a more plausible mechanism: Dietary choline might prevent premature aging of brain cells, by protecting the delicate membrane that encases the contents of the mitochondria inside these cells.

In a nutshell, dietary choline might slow aging of mitochondria in the brain (and elsewhere).
