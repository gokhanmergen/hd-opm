Each daily yogurt serving linked to 14% lower CVD mortality in a 17-cohort meta-analysis

AI summary & key facts

A systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of 17 cohort studies (896,871 participants) found higher yogurt intake was associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, with the largest benefit around 0.5 servings per day and no clear further gain above that. No significant association was seen with cancer mortality. As an observational analysis, it shows association, not causation.

Yogurt consumption and risk of mortality from all causes, CVD and cancer

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 cohort studies, encompassing 896,871 participants, investigated the relationship between yogurt consumption and mortality. The study found that higher yogurt intake was significantly associated with a reduced risk of death from all causes (pooled relative risk of 0.93) and cardiovascular disease (CVD) (pooled relative risk of 0.89). Specifically, each additional serving of yogurt per day was linked to a 7% lower risk of all-cause mortality (pooled relative risk of 0.93) and a 14% lower risk of CVD mortality (pooled relative risk of 0.86), with the most significant benefit observed at around 0.5 servings/day, and no further reduction in risk was observed above that amount. However, the analysis revealed non significant association between yogurt consumption and cancer mortality. The findings suggest that incorporating yogurt into the diet may contribute to improved longevity, particularly by reducing the risk of CVD-related deaths.
