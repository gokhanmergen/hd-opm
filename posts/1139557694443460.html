Cold pack on the neck raised heart rate variability in a small RCT; viral post overstates

Louis Nervegna · Oct 18, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Sleep, stress & social health
AI summary & key facts

Louis links the actual study behind a viral post claiming an ice pack on the back of the neck calms anxiety in seconds via the vagus nerve. The randomized trial in healthy participants found cold stimulation at the lateral neck raised heart rate variability and lowered heart rate versus control, but Louis says the post exaggerates and it still seems a safe thing to try.

  • Randomized trial in healthy participants of cold stimulation of the neck
  • Higher heart rate variability and lower heart rate than control (cardiac-vagal activation)
  • Viral post claims relief in 30-90 seconds, which the study did not show (per Louis)

The link to the actual study is below:

Paper · JMIR Form Res · 2018Effects of Cold Stimulation on Cardiac-Vagal Activation in Healthy Participants: Randomized Controlled Trial.Conclusion: The results confirmed our main hypothesis that cold stimulation at the lateral neck region would result in higher heart rate variability and lower heart rate than in the control condition. This sets the stage for further investigations of stress reduction potential in the neck region by developing a wearable prototype that can be used for cold application. Future studies should include a stress condition,pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

The post seems to be hyperbolizing the results, compared to what the actual study showed. But this does seem like a safe thing to try. It might work.

Shared from Techmed Times · original post

Feeling anxious? You might not need a pill just an ice pack. New findings show that placing a cold compress or ice pack on the back of your neck can calm anxiety in seconds by stimulating the vagus nerve, which controls your body’s relaxation response.

The vagus nerve is a major part of the parasympathetic nervous system, responsible for slowing heart rate, calming the mind, and restoring balance after stress. When activated by cold exposure, especially around the neck and upper spine, it sends a signal to the brain to downshift from “fight or flight” into “rest and digest.”

This technique has been used in therapy rooms, military training, and even emergency settings to rapidly reduce panic symptoms, lower cortisol levels, and restore a sense of control. Many people report that within 30 to 60 seconds, their heart rate slows, their breathing deepens, and their thoughts become noticeably clearer.

The best part? It’s safe, drug-free, and incredibly simple. Just wrap a cold pack (or even a bag of frozen veggies) in a cloth and apply it gently to the back of your neck for 30–90 seconds.

This quick vagus nerve hack isn’t just a trend, it’s neuroscience in action. Whether you’re facing social anxiety, test stress, or just an overwhelming moment, this small trick can offer fast relief.

Sometimes, regulating your nervous system doesn’t require willpower just a little cold.
