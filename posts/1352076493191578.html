Summary of Dudley Lamming's isoleucine restriction research in rodents

Louis Nervegna · Jul 25, 2026 · ♥ 0 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinAging & longevityInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a simplified summary of Dudley Lamming's rodent work on restricting the amino acid isoleucine, linking earlier posts. Human translation is unclear, though some short-term trials of protein restriction show metabolic benefits that might be due to lower isoleucine intake.

  • Research is in rodent models at University of Wisconsin
  • Some human trials show short-term metabolic benefits of protein restriction
  • Unclear if effects translate to humans

A very simplified summary of Dudley Lamming’s research on isoleucine restriction in rodent models at the University of Wisconsin.

I’ve done several previous posts on this research, which can be found here:

  1. ↪ Cutting dietary isoleucine by two-thirds extended male mouse lifespan 33%
  1. ↪ Isoleucine restriction extended male HET3 mouse lifespan 33% despite higher calorie intake
  1. ↪ Valter Longo says higher whole-food plant protein likely protects against heart disease

It’s still unclear to what degree, if any, this will translate to humans.

But some recent clinical trials have shown short term metabolic benefits to protein restriction in humans, and it is conceivable that this is due primarily to the lower isoleucine intake:

  1. facebook.com/share/v/1DGMpPZjmA
  1. ↪ Cutting protein to 10% of calories matched 25% calorie restriction in a 27-day RCT
  1. ↪ Keto influencer with LDL near 500 mg/dL discovers isoleucine restriction (Lamming work)
  1. ↪ Isoleucine restriction extends mouse lifespan 33%, and may explain the old rice diet

The chief source of isoleucine in the human diet is high-quality animal protein. By contrast, plant sources of protein tend to be a lot lower in isoleucine.

This research does suggest a plausible biological mechanism by which excessive high-quality animal protein can be harmful.

Six lines of evidence favor whole-food plant protein over red and processed meat

Six converging evidence lines favor plant protein over red and processed meat

Louis shares an Ageproof article by group member Mert Erogul MD arguing that genetics, IGF-1 biomarkers, rodent experiments, amino-acid decomposition, short-term human trials and epidemiology converge: methionine, isoleucine and valine, not leucine or total protein, are implicated. Favoring whole-food plant protein over red and processed meat likely extends lifespan.

Cutting dietary isoleucine by two-thirds extended male mouse lifespan 33%

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 11 ↩ 4 Protein

Louis shares a Cell Metabolism mouse study in which restricting a single essential amino acid, isoleucine, lengthened life (33% in males, 7% in females), improved leanness, blood sugar, strength and frailty, and reduced age-related cancer. He notes the male effect matches major calorie restriction in mice, and suggests getting protein mainly from whole plant foods, which are naturally lower in isoleucine than animal protein. Mouse data, not proven in humans.

Isoleucine restriction extended male HET3 mouse lifespan 33% despite higher calorie intake

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 · 💬 2 ↩ 4 Protein

Louis reposts a summary of the HET3 (Interventions Testing Program) mouse study from Dudley Lamming's group. Mice fed a diet with isoleucine cut by two-thirds from 6 months of age lived 33% longer in males and 7% in females with better health scores, leaner bodies and less cancer, despite higher calorie intake. Louis says this beats rapamycin in males and recommends whole plant protein as a way to lower isoleucine intake.

Protein restriction and aging: animal benefits, no proven human lifespan gain

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Protein

Louis shares a summary of a review (Knopf and Lamming) of protein and amino-acid restriction. Restricting methionine or the branched-chain amino acids isoleucine and valine drives many animal metabolic benefits, FGF21 is involved, but lifespan extension has not been shown in humans. The field is shifting from 'less protein' to which amino acids matter, and older or active adults may need more protein. Louis says protein is complicated and influencers oversimplify it.

Review of 350+ studies links protein restriction to better metabolism and healthier aging

Aug 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 ↩ 1 Protein

Dudley Lamming and colleague reviewed over 350 studies and report that lower protein intake is associated with improved metabolism and healthier aging via mechanisms like a liver hormone. Lamming says benefits of protein are clear for active people but sedentary people may overeat it.
