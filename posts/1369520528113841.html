High protein intake not linked to kidney function decline in a 10-year measured-GFR cohort

Louis Nervegna · Aug 15, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinKidney health
AI summary & key facts

A decade-long population cohort using precise iohexol-measured GFR found higher self-reported protein intake was not associated with GFR decline in adults largely free of chronic kidney disease. The authors conclude protein restriction may not be a useful strategy to prevent CKD.

Shared from Medscape · original post

A decade-long study using precise kidney function measurements challenges concerns about dietary protein harming kidney health in adults largely free of chronic kidney disease.

"Our findings from a population-based cohort with repeated iohexol-based GFR measurements over 10 years suggest that higher self-reported protein intake is not associated with GFR decline. These findings indicate that protein restriction may not be a lifestyle strategy for preventing CKD," wrote the authors of the study. https://mdsc.pe/4yW3Mzj (t.co)

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.
