Louis highlights a Cell Metabolism randomized trial of 42 people with obesity, prediabetes and fatty liver assigned to ketogenic, Mediterranean or low-fat plant-forward diets with weight loss matched. Keto cut liver fat 67% versus 45% for the other diets. He notes it is small and wonders about a Mediterranean keto diet.

  • 42 people with obesity, prediabetes and fatty liver
  • All groups lost about 10% of body weight
  • Liver fat down 67% (keto) vs 45% (others)
  • Prediabetes resolved in about half of keto group vs 29% Mediterranean and 7% plant-forward

Given that liver fat is a principle driver of insulin resistance, this study has certainly caught my attention.

Research cited

  • Ketogenic vs Mediterranean vs low-fat plant-forward diets for liver fat · RCT · Cell Metabolism · 2026
Shared from Amazing Science Facts · original post

Researchers randomly assigned 42 people with obesity, prediabetes and fatty liver disease to ketogenic, Mediterranean or low-fat plant-forward diets while controlling weight loss across the groups. All groups lost about 10% of body weight, but liver fat fell 67% with keto versus 45% with the other two diets. About half of the ketogenic group also no longer met the criteria for prediabetes after four to five months, compared with 29% on Mediterranean and 7% on the plant-forward diet.

Original source: Cell Press / Washington University School of Medicine / Cell Metabolism
