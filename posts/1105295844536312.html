Flavanol-rich cocoa improved 6-minute walk distance by 42.6 m in peripheral artery disease

AI summary & key facts

A six-month double-blind pilot RCT in 44 older adults with PAD tested a daily flavanol-rich cocoa drink against placebo. The cocoa group walked farther 2.5 hours after consumption while placebo declined, with better calf muscle mitochondrial activity, capillary density and perfusion.

  • n = 44 older adults with PAD, 6 months, double-blind
  • 6-minute walk distance +42.6 m vs placebo at 2.5 h after consumption
  • Improved mitochondrial activity, capillary density and calf perfusion

Cocoa for Better Circulation

In a six-month, double-blind, pilot randomized clinical trial involving 44 older adults with peripheral artery disease (PAD), researchers investigated whether a daily flavanol-rich cocoa beverage could improve walking performance compared to a placebo. The primary outcome was the change in the distance participants could walk in six minutes. The study found that participants consuming the cocoa beverage significantly improved their 6-minute walk distance by 42.6 meters when measured 2.5 hours after consumption, relative to the placebo group, whose performance declined. The cocoa group also showed beneficial physiological changes, including improved mitochondrial activity, increased capillary density, and better perfusion in their calf muscles. Although these preliminary results suggest a therapeutic effect of cocoa on walking ability for individuals with PAD, the authors conclude that larger studies are needed to definitively confirm these findings.

Paper · Circulation Research · 2020Cocoa to Improve Walking Performance in Older People With Peripheral Artery DiseaseRationale: Cocoa and its major flavanol component, epicatechin, have therapeutic properties that may improve limb perfusion and increase calf muscle mitochondrial activity in people with lower extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD). Objective: In a phase II randomized clinical trial, to assess whether 6 months of cocoa improved walking performance in people with PAD, compared with placebo. Methods and Results:…ahajournals.org
