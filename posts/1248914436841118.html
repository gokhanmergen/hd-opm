Nearly 70 trials show protein alone does not increase strength; training does the work

Louis Nervegna · Mar 19, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

Louis states that randomized trials in aggregate show protein alone does not raise muscle strength unless combined with resistance exercise, and that Americans already eat more than enough protein to build muscle. He shares FoundMyFitness saying training is the main driver of adaptation.

  • Nearly 70 RCTs in aggregate
  • USA has the highest total and animal protein consumption of any major region

Nearly 70 randomized trials have demonstrated in aggregate that protein consumption alone does NOT increase muscle strength (regardless of the amount), unless it is combined with resistance exercise. Even when combined, the vast majority of the benefit comes from the resistance training:

↪ Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting.

If you live in the USA, you likely get more than enough protein to build muscle on a resistance training program. People in the USA have the largest total protein consumption of any major region of the world, as well as the largest consumption of high-quality animal protein:

↪ The US leads the world in protein intake, about 1.5 g/kg per day

As Rhonda explains in the video, the major problem in the USA is a deficiency of resistance training, not a deficiency of protein.

Most people don’t need more protein to build muscle.

They need to train more.

Protein isn’t the main driver of adaptation, training is. Muscle growth, strength, and metabolic health are primarily stimulated by mechanical tension and progressive overload, not just a higher protein intake.

Protein’s role is supportive. It helps repair and build after you’ve given your body a reason to adapt. But without a meaningful training stimulus, more protein doesn’t translate into better outcomes (e.g., more strength, greater lean mass).

Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 6 ↩ 11 Protein

A dose-response meta-analysis of 69 randomized trials found protein alone does not increase muscle strength without resistance training. With training, strength rose 0.72% per extra 0.1 g/kg/day up to 1.5 g/kg/day, with no further gain; going from the RDA to 1.5 g/kg adds only about 5% more strength.
