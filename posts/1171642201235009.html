A traditional Nordic diet may add years to your life.

A major study involving over 76,000 adults in Sweden has found that people who closely follow the updated Nordic dietary guidelines have significantly lower mortality rates – including from cancer and cardiovascular disease.

The guidelines, updated in 2023, recommend eating more whole grains, legumes, low-fat dairy, fish, and vegetables while cutting back on red meat, processed foods, and added sugars. Importantly, they were designed not just for human health, but also to reduce climate impact.

Researchers found that people who stuck most closely to the guidelines had a 23% lower risk of premature death – even when controlling for income, education, and physical activity. Those individuals also saw reduced risk of death specifically from cancer and heart disease.

This research offers a powerful insight: dietary choices can support both individual health and global sustainability. Food production contributes to roughly 30% of global greenhouse gas emissions, and the Nordic model could serve as a blueprint for other sustainable regional diets.

Read the study:
“Development of the Nordic Nutrition Recommendations 2023 Food-Based Diet Score and Its Association with All-Cause Mortality in Two Swedish Cohorts.” The Journal of Nutrition, 2025.
