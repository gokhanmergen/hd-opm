Hormone therapy started after 65 linked to higher cancer and stroke risk in Israeli cohort

Louis Nervegna · Mar 18, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post

Medscape reports an Israeli study of over 80,000 women (2000-2022) where hormone therapy was linked to several cancers, with some cardiovascular benefit when started at 50-65 but higher stroke and cancer risk, and significantly increased cancer and cerebrovascular risk when started at 65 or older. It is observational.

Shared from Medscape · original post

Initiation of menopausal hormone therapy (HT) after age 65 is linked to significantly increased risks for cancer and vascular events, new data suggested.

A study of more than 80,000 women aged 50 years or older in an Israeli health system during 2000-2022 showed that HT use overall was associated with several types of hormone-sensitive and nonhormone-sensitive cancers. While HT offered some cardiovascular benefit when started at age 50-65, it also raised stroke and cancer risks. And HT initiated in those aged 65 years or older significantly raised the risk for both cancer and cerebrovascular events. Hormone Therapy in Older Women May Raise Cancer, Heart Risk (Medscape)

Statins work as well in women as men: 27-trial CTT meta-analysis of 174,000 participants

Dec 19, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 11 Statins

Louis rebuts the claim that statins are less effective in women using the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' meta-analysis of 27 randomized trials. Per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction, proportional reductions in major vascular events and all-cause mortality were similar in women and men, with no adverse effect on cancer or non-CV mortality. Trials averaged 4.9 years, so longer-term benefit is likely larger.
