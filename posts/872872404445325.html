Metal Exposure: A Hidden Threat to Heart Health?

A new study published in JACC reveals that exposure to environmental metals is associated with increased calcium buildup in coronary arteries, comparable to traditional risk factors like smoking and diabetes. Researchers analyzed data from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA), examining both non-essential (cadmium, tungsten, uranium) and essential (cobalt, copper, zinc) metals in 6,418 participants over a 10-year period. The study found that higher levels of these metals in urine corresponded to significantly higher levels of coronary artery calcification (CAC), a predictor of future cardiac events.

The findings highlight the importance of considering metal exposure as a significant risk factor for atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease. The associations between metals and CAC progression were comparable in magnitude to those of traditional risk factors. This research underscores the need for increased awareness and regulatory measures to limit exposure to these metals, which are widespread due to industrial and agricultural activities. The study's lead author, Katlyn E. McGraw, emphasized that pollution is the greatest environmental risk to cardiovascular health, calling for heightened awareness and protective measures.
