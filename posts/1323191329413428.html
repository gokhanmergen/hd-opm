VITACOG: B vitamins cut brain atrophy 30% in mild cognitive impairment

Louis Nervegna · Jun 20, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis reshares his summary of the Oxford VITACOG randomized trial of folate, B6 and B12 in elderly people with MCI. B vitamins slowed brain shrinkage, most in those with high baseline homocysteine, and later analyses suggested benefit depended on high omega-3 status.

  • B vitamins reduced brain shrinkage by an average of 30% over two years
  • Up to 53% reduction in those with high baseline homocysteine
  • Later analyses: benefit only in participants with high EPA and DHA blood levels
  • Improved executive function, global cognition and memory in high-homocysteine participants

The VITACOG study (2004–2009) was a landmark randomized, placebo-controlled trial conducted by the University of Oxford. It investigated whether high-dose B vitamin supplementation (folate, B6, B12) could slow the rate of brain atrophy and cognitive decline in elderly individuals with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI).

Key Findings

Brain Atrophy Reduction: The study found that B vitamin treatment (folic acid, B6, and B12) reduced the rate of brain shrinkage by an average of 30% over two years.

Targeted Protection: In participants with high baseline homocysteine levels, the reduction in atrophy was even more significant, reaching up to 53%.

Synergy with Omega-3: Later analyses revealed that B vitamins were only effective in slowing brain atrophy and cognitive decline in participants who also had high blood levels of Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA):

Paper · Am J Clin Nutr · 2015Brain atrophy in cognitively impaired elderly: the importance of long-chain ω-3 fatty acids and B vitamin status in a randomized controlled trial.Conclusion: The beneficial effect of B vitamin treatment on brain atrophy was observed only in subjects with high plasma ω-3 fatty acids. It is also suggested that the beneficial effect of ω-3 fatty acids on brain atrophy may be confined to subjects with good B vitamin status. The results highlight the importance of identifying subgroups likely to benefit in clinical trials. Cognitive Performance: Treatment stabilized executive function and improved global cognition and memory in those with elevated homocysteine.

Mechanisms & Recent Insights

Homocysteine Lowering: B vitamins act as cofactors to convert homocysteine—a neurotoxin linked to Alzheimer's—into safer amino acids like methionine.

Metabolic Reprogramming: Recent 2025 metabolomics data from the original VITACOG samples shows that B vitamins also influence the TCA cycle and glutamine-glutamate cycling, which are critical for brain energy and neurotransmission. These findings reveal that B vitamins influence metabolic pathways in the brain that go well-beyond homocysteine reduction:

Treatment Protocol

The daily active treatment used in the study (TrioBe Plus) consisted of:

0.8 mg Folic Acid
0.5 mg Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin)
20 mg Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)
