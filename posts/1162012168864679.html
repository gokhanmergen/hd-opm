Should you take Vitamin K2 for Calcified Arteries & Heart Disease?

In this video, Dr. Gil Carvalho addresses the popular claim that Vitamin K2 can remove calcium from arteries and reduce heart disease risk. He explains that while the theory is based on K2-dependent proteins that inhibit calcification, the evidence for supplementation is weak. Dr. Carvalho emphasizes that arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, and that focusing on lowering a calcium score can be misleading—much like how statins can increase calcium while simultaneously stabilizing plaque and lowering heart attack risk. He concludes that because there are no large-scale, properly powered clinical trials showing that K2 supplements actually reduce hard events like heart attacks or strokes, he cannot recommend them for heart health. He also warns that the biggest danger is "false reassurance," where individuals might take K2 while foregoing proven treatments.
