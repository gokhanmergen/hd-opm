Chronic venous insufficiency explained: faulty leg vein valves, DVT risk and ulcers

Triggered by a public figure's diagnosis, Louis explains that in CVI the valves in leg veins fail and blood does not return properly. The condition is common in older adults and usually benign but raises DVT risk and can cause hard-to-heal ulcers when advanced.

  • Valves in lower-leg veins stop working properly
  • Common in older people in Western countries
  • Raises risk of deep vein thrombosis
  • Can cause non-healing leg ulcers

This diagnosis will likely raise awareness for chronic venous insufficiency (CVI).

It is a very common condition in older people living in western countries.

In a nutshell, the valves in veins in the lower legs stop working properly, preventing the veins from properly returning blood to where it came from.

The condition is troublesome, but relatively benign, unless very advanced.

It significantly raises the risk for deep vein thrombosis (DVT), which can be deadly.

It can also result in painful skin ulcers on the lower legs that refuse to heal, due to inadequatete blood flow to the skin. These can be just as dangerous as diabetic ulcers, or worse. They can easily become infected and lead to sepsis, especially in older people, which can also be deadly.

Standard treatment is compression stockings, walking, leg elevation, and weight loss. There are also surgical procedures that help. Baby aspirin and certain supplements might help. Anticoagulants are used when the risk of DVT is very high.
