Adventist Health Study-2: pesco-vegetarians had lowest mortality; vegans only 3% lower

Louis Nervegna · Sep 03, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 17 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes the Adventist Health Study-2 cohort, where all participants eat a relatively healthy diet, allowing head-to-head comparison of healthy patterns. Pesco-vegetarians had 18% lower all-cause mortality than non-vegetarians, beating lacto-ovo vegetarians and vegans.

  • Pesco-vegetarians: 18% lower all-cause mortality vs non-vegetarians
  • Lacto-ovo vegetarians: 15% lower; vegans: 3% lower
  • More than 88,000 subjects and about 12,500 deaths analyzed; cohort of nearly 96,000, followed through 2015
  • Adventists live about 7 years longer than average Americans (per post)

People who eat a pescatarian style plant-based diet have the lowest risk of death in the Adventists Health Study.

This pesco-vegetarian dietary pattern beat non-vegetarian, semi-vegetarian, lacto-ovo-vegetarian, and vegan dietary patterns in the study.

Adventists who were pesco-vegetarian had 18% lower all-cause mortality than Adventists who were non-vegetarian. Lacto-ovo-vegetarians had 15% lower all-cause mortality. Pure vegans had only 3% lower all-cause mortality than non-vegetarians.

The Adventists Health Study is a long-running prospective cohort study tracking the diets of Seventh Day Adventists living primarily in the Loma Linda area of California. This is recognized as a “blue zone” where people live a long time.

The Adventists live about 7 years longer than the average American. The ones who practice the diet/lifestyle recommendations of their religion the strictest live about 10-12 years longer.

What’s interesting about the Adventists Health Study is that all the participants consume some sort of healthy diet, as encouraged by their religion. Because of this, it is possible to compare various styles of healthy eating head-to-head. This is in contrast to most prospective cohort studies where people eat the standard western diet.

Quoted:

“The study used data from the Adventist Health Study-2, a massive cohort of nearly 96,000 people who identify as Seventh-day Adventist and lived in the United States and Canada during the study’s baseline recruitment between 2002 and 2007, with follow-up through 2015. Data from that group has been used for numerous studies on health, disease, and mortality over the years. This study analyzed data from more than 88,000 subjects and approximately 12,500 deaths in the study cohort. Dietary data were collected using a questionnaire and then categorized into five patterns: non-vegetarian, semi-vegetarian, pesco-vegetarian, lacto-ovo-vegetarian, and vegan.”

“Fraser said his team found that Adventist vegetarians overall had about a 12% less risk of death compared to Adventist non-vegetarians. Study participants with a pesco-vegetarian diet had an 18% less risk of death. Those with a lacto-ovo-vegetarian diet (including dairy and eggs) had a 15% less risk of death. Vegans overall had a less than 3% decreased risk of death, but male vegans fared much better than non-vegetarians, in contrast to females.”

“Gary Fraser, MBCHB, PhD, distinguished professor at Loma Linda University School of Public Health and principal investigator of the study, said a vegetarian diet appears to offer protection from risk of death through middle-aged years, but once it helps people get into their 80s that overall advantage seems to disappear for those adhering to a strict vegetarian diet.

“These increased risks of neurological conditions among vegetarians in their 80s weren’t huge, but something is going on there that we shouldn’t ignore if we wish the vegetarian advantage to continue for all vegetarians in their later years,” Fraser said.”
