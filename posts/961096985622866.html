Gokhan summarizes a Peter Attia podcast episode with lipidologist Tom Dayspring on why cardiovascular disease is the leading killer. It covers cholesterol imbalance, inflammation and metabolic factors, plus early detection, diagnostic tools and targeted therapies.

  • Episode topics: atherosclerosis development, biomarkers, apoB
  • Stresses early detection and emerging diagnostics

The video, titled "334 - Cardiovascular Disease, the Number One Killer," features renowned clinical lipidology expert Tom Dayspring as he delves into why cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of death. In the episode, Dayspring explains how factors like cholesterol imbalance, inflammation, and other metabolic irregularities contribute to the development of heart disease. He highlights the importance of early detection and discusses emerging diagnostic tools and targeted therapies that can help manage and potentially mitigate these risks. Overall, the episode offers a detailed yet accessible exploration of cardiovascular health, providing both medical professionals and the general public with valuable insights into prevention and treatment strategies.

Over a third of 'low-risk' adults had coronary plaque; low LDL and ApoB were protective

Aug 18, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 5 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

A JACC Advances study of 1,033 asymptomatic adults aged 40-65 classified as low risk found 35.9% had coronary plaque and 25.3% early calcification, increasing with higher LDL, non-HDL cholesterol and apoB, even below guideline levels. Gokhan reads it as showing that keeping LDL under 70 mg/dL greatly reduces risk.

Berberine 500 mg twice daily lowered ApoB in two trials; CVD prevention unproven

Oct 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary says two randomized trials of 500 mg berberine twice daily reduced ApoB, with a reduction nearly three times greater in women, though across separate trials. A newer study of 84 men identified a 'Berberine Signature' of 92 proteins linked to about 13% lower odds of ischemic heart disease in UK Biobank, but those participants were not necessarily taking berberine, so it does not prove prevention.

Lowering triglycerides alone does not shrink plaque: olezarsen cut TG 64%, plaque flat

May 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 12 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that unless triglycerides exceed about 400-500 mg/dL, drug-lowering them makes little difference to plaque or hard outcomes. In BRIDGE-TIMI 73a olezarsen cut triglycerides 64% with no plaque volume change; VLDL particles are atherogenic but are already counted by apoB.

WOSCOPS 20-year follow-up: 5 years of pravastatin gave lasting mortality and event benefit

Apr 07, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Statins

Describes the 20-year follow-up of the West of Scotland Coronary Prevention Study: 5 years of pravastatin in middle-aged men with high cholesterol and no prior CHD produced a lasting legacy effect, with reduced all-cause, CV and coronary mortality and fewer heart failure and MI hospitalizations. No excess cancer or non-CV mortality was seen.

The case for LDL lowering: statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors and diet all reduce events

Sep 07, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 15 · 💬 13 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Long article arguing skepticism about statins is no longer warranted. About 16 FDA-approved drugs lower LDL, nearly every method of lowering LDL reduces cardiovascular events, and high-dose statins combined with other agents can slow or possibly halt plaque progression. Diet changes such as reducing saturated fat also lower LDL.

Imidazole propionate, a gut-bacterial metabolite, drives atherosclerosis in mice

Jul 24, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 14 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Gut microbiome

A Nature paper reports that imidazole propionate (ImP) is independently associated with cardiovascular disease and causes atherosclerosis in mice through inflammation via the I1 imidazoline receptor. Blocking that receptor could become a cholesterol-independent therapy.
