Pooled blinded trials: statins cause only about 1 in 15 reported muscle aches

Louis Nervegna · Oct 23, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post

This implies that the vast majority of muscle complaints on statins are not caused by the drug, consistent with a nocebo effect.

IF 15 PEOPLE ON A STATIN COMPLAIN OF ACHING MUSCLES, HOW MANY ACHES DID THE STATIN CAUSE?

Most people guess most of them. The largest analysis ever done on this question says about one.

Researchers pooled 19 blinded trials with 123,940 people. None of them knew whether they were taking a statin or a dummy pill. Over about four years, 27.1 in 100 people on a statin reported muscle pain or weakness. On the dummy pill, 26.6 in 100 did.

In the first year, the researchers worked out that only about 1 in every 15 muscle complaints from people on a statin was actually caused by the statin. The rest would have happened anyway.

A London trial called SAMSON went further. People who had already quit statins because of side effects took a statin for some months, a dummy pill for others, and nothing for the rest. Their symptoms were almost as bad on the dummy pill as on the statin. Six months later, half of them were back on statins.

The aches are real. Very often the cause is expectation, not the tablet.

WHAT A STATIN ACTUALLY DOES

Your liver makes cholesterol through a long chain of steps. One enzyme sets the pace, called HMG CoA reductase. A statin slips into the pocket of that enzyme and blocks the natural molecule from getting in.

When the liver makes less cholesterol, it grabs more LDL, the cholesterol that clogs arteries, out of your blood. LDL falls. In the big trials it fell by between a quarter and a half.

The common statins are atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and pravastatin. They all hit the same enzyme, but they leave the body by different routes.

Atorvastatin and simvastatin are cleared by a liver enzyme that many other medicines and grapefruit juice can block.

Rosuvastatin and pravastatin depend on it much less.

That is the reason the list of dangerous combinations differs from statin to statin.

WHAT IT REALLY BUYS YOU, IN REAL NUMBERS

The answer depends almost entirely on how likely you were to have a heart attack or stroke in the first place.

People who already have heart disease. In the Scandinavian 4S trial of 4444 people with angina or a past heart attack, about 12 in 100 died within 6 years on a dummy pill, against about 9 in 100 on simvastatin. About 28 people treated for 6 years for one life saved. Heart attacks and other major coronary events fell from 28 in 100 to 19 in 100, one prevented for every 12 people treated.

People at middle risk with no heart disease. In the HOPE-3 trial of 12,705 people in 21 countries, heart attack, stroke or heart death fell from 4.8 in 100 to 3.7 in 100 over 5.6 years. About 91 people treated for one to be spared.

Healthy people with a raised inflammation marker in the blood. In the JUPITER trial, events fell from 1.36 to 0.77 in every 100 people each year. About 170 people treated for a year for one to benefit.

Same tablet. Very different value. The higher your starting risk, the more a statin can do for you.

THE 10,000 PEOPLE PICTURE

Oxford researchers who lead the pooled statin trial analyses drew up this picture. Imagine 10,000 people lowering their LDL cholesterol by 2 mmol per litre with a statin for 5 years.

If they already have artery disease, about 1000 avoid a heart attack, stroke or artery operation.

If they are at raised risk but have no disease yet, about 500 avoid one.

  • On the other side, about 5 develop real muscle damage.
  • Between 50 and 100 develop diabetes.
  • Between 5 and 10 have a bleed in the brain.
  • Up to 50 to 100 have symptoms truly caused by the drug.

These are estimates built from trial data, not results from one trial. Keep this picture somewhere you can find it before your next appointment, and share it with someone who has been told they need a statin. It shows both sides of the scale in the same units.

THE SIDE EFFECTS, STRAIGHT

  • Real muscle damage is rare, about 1 in 2000 people in everyday practice, and severe muscle breakdown fewer than 1 in 10,000. The risk rises with higher doses, age over 65, kidney problems, an underactive thyroid and certain other medicines. Simvastatin 80 mg carries the highest muscle risk and is now limited to people already used to it.
  • Statins nudge blood sugar up. Across 13 trials, treating 255 people for 4 years caused one extra case of diabetes, more often in older people.
  • Liver injury is rare. In atorvastatin trials, liver tests stayed above three times normal in about 7 in 1000 people.
  • Statins may harm an unborn baby, and the prescriber reviews treatment when a pregnancy is recognised. Breastfeeding is not recommended while taking one.
  • Across all the pooled trials, statins did not increase cancer.

THE MEDICINES, HERBS AND FOODS THAT CLASH

The dangerous combinations push statin levels up and raise the risk of muscle damage.

  • Clarithromycin and some antifungals with atorvastatin or simvastatin.
  • Cyclosporine and gemfibrozil with most statins.
  • Some heart medicines, including verapamil, diltiazem, amiodarone, amlodipine and ranolazine, with simvastatin above set doses.
  • Several HIV and hepatitis C medicines.
  • Grapefruit juice with simvastatin, and large amounts with atorvastatin.
  • Red yeast rice can contain the same chemical as the statin lovastatin, in amounts no one can predict. Taking it with a statin is like taking two statins.
  • If you take a statin with warfarin, your INR needs checking when the statin starts or changes.

Whenever a new medicine is prescribed, ask your pharmacist to check it against your statin.

COENZYME Q10, THE MIXED EVIDENCE

Two large reviews of the trials disagree. One found less muscle pain with coenzyme Q10, the other found no real effect. The evidence is mixed, so raise it with your prescriber rather than relying on it.

STOPPING AND STAYING ON

Statins do not cause a rebound when stopped. The risk is losing the protection. In France, 75 year olds who stopped their statin had a 33 percent higher chance of hospital admission for a heart or stroke event than those who kept going.

That study could not prove the stopping caused it, but it fits the trial evidence that the benefit builds the longer a statin is taken.

If muscles ache, talk to your prescriber. A different statin, a lower dose, a thyroid test or a check of your other medicines may solve it. That decision belongs to your prescriber.

DOES IT CURE ANYTHING?

No. A statin lowers your risk while you take it. It does not clear artery disease.

For people who have had a heart attack or stroke, a strong statin is first line treatment in the major guidelines. Lifestyle helps, but it does not replace it.

For everyone, food matters. In the Spanish PREDIMED trial of 7447 older adults at high risk, a Mediterranean diet with extra olive oil or nuts lowered heart attack, stroke or heart death from 4.4 in 100 to 3.8 or 3.4 in 100 over almost 5 years. Movement and a healthy weight help too, especially if your blood sugar is creeping up.

If you also take blood pressure or diabetes medicines and your diet or activity changes, tell your prescriber, because those doses may need review as your numbers improve.

GET HELP NOW IF

  • You have chest pain or pressure, especially with breathlessness or sweating.
  • You have sudden weakness or numbness on one side, a drooping face or trouble speaking.
  • Your face, lips or throat swell, or you struggle to breathe.

Call your clinician the same day for unexplained muscle pain or weakness, especially with fever, yellow skin or eyes, a possible pregnancy, or a new antibiotic, antifungal, heart rhythm medicine or antiviral.

Book a review within the week for thigh or shoulder weakness that continues after a statin has been stopped.
