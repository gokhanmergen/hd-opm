Tom Seest's CAC drop from 66 to 42 over three years, and the debate about reversal

AI summary & key facts

Summarizes a video on whether coronary calcium can be reversed. Tom's score fell from 66 to 42 (36%) between 2018 and 2021 amid many simultaneous changes (keto diet, fasting, supplements, less exercise, weight gain), and he admits he does not know which mattered.

  • CAC 66 (2018), 58 (second scan 2018), 42 (2021), a 36% decrease
  • Many cardiologists believe progression can be slowed but not reversed to zero; scores over 400 mean substantially higher risk
  • Confounders included moving, more sleep, keto diet, fasting and supplements

Tom's Story

The video discusses whether it is possible to reverse coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores. The presenter, Tom Seest, reviews his personal experience with improving his CAC score over a 3-year period. He outlines lifestyle and dietary changes he made during that time which may have contributed to his improved scores. However, he honestly admits he does not know for certain what specifically helped reverse his CAC score.

The Presenter's Personal CAC Score History

  • In 2017, Tom was diagnosed with atrial fibrillation and heart failure.
  • He decided to get a CAC scan in 2018 which showed a score of 66 initially. A second scan the same year was 58.
  • In 2021, after another case of atrial fibrillation, Tom's CAC score was 42.
  • This means his score decreased by 36% over 3 years, which he considers reversing his CAC score.

Debate Over Reversing CAC Scores

  • Many cardiologists think it is not possible to reverse CAC scores to zero. At best, progression can be slowed.
  • Once scores reach very high levels (e.g. over 400), heart disease risk increases substantially.
  • Some cardiologists believe CAC scores can be lowered through lifestyle changes.
  • But there is debate and uncertainty around whether scores can be reversed or just slowed.

Lifestyle Changes Between 2018-2021

While Tom does not know for certain what impacted his CAC scores, he outlines the following lifestyle and dietary changes made during the 3 years between his tests:

  1. Moved from Illinois to Tennessee for less pollution and a less stressful lifestyle
  2. Slept more hours per night instead of overworking
  3. Tried to improve relationships with spouse and children
  4. Followed a ketogenic, high-fat, high-protein diet since 2014
  5. Did intermittent and extended fasting, mostly between 18-120 hours duration
  6. Took many supplements like Vitamin D, Vitamin K2, Coenzyme Q10, glutathione, etc.
  7. Received thoughts, prayers, and emotional support during health struggles
  8. Unintentionally gained 80 pounds during his heart issues
  9. Reduced exercise significantly compared to prior very high levels
  10. Drank 4-5 zero-calorie Monster energy drinks daily
  11. Ate significant bacon, eggs, pork, steak, cheese etc.
  12. Worked with a supportive cardiologist over the years

While these changes may have contributed to Tom's CAC score improvement, he candidly admits multiple times that he does not know for certain what had an effect or not. He presents some scientific rationale when available but ultimately concedes the complexity of the human body means causation remains elusive.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the presenter shared his personal experience of lowering his CAC heart scan scores by 36% over 3 years but freely acknowledges he does not know what lifestyle factors were responsible. He reviewed a number of dietary, supplements, activity levels and other changes he made during that time which might have had an influence. However, despite being the subject of his own "lab experiment", he concedes that determining causation is difficult and would require further research. He presents an honest perspective that while scores can potentially be lowered to some degree, the body remains highly complex and more studies are needed on reversible risk factors. Nonetheless, his experience and some early research offer hope for improving long-term heart health.

New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Aug 31, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 38 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.

Vitamin C has not reduced heart attacks or strokes and plaque data are mixed

Apr 12, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Admin summarises that vitamin C alone has been tested widely without reducing hard outcomes (MACE), and plaque-reversal data are mixed: supportive in animals, better/worse/no effect in human trials. He does not advise it as a heart-disease cure, though physiological doses appear safe; he takes 250 mg/day to avoid deficiency. Links an (largely AI-generated, author-vetted) summary document.

Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.
