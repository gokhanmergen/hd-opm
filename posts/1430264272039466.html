Shared from Dr. Brian Hoeflinger, MD · original post

As a retired brain surgeon, I want to share one of the most striking studies I know on fitness, and be honest about what it does and doesn’t show.

Cleveland Clinic researchers followed 122,007 adults for a median of about 8 years after a treadmill stress test. People who were slightly below average in fitness had about a 41 percent higher risk of death than people slightly above average. In the same study, smokers had about the same added risk compared with nonsmokers.

That is a big finding. But here is what it does not prove.

This was an observational study. It shows an association, not cause and effect. Nobody was assigned to get fit. Healthier people are also simply more able to exercise, and the researchers said themselves they can’t tell how much of the difference comes from fitness and how much comes from people already being healthier. The study also looked at people sent for a stress test, not the general public.

So I can’t tell you that getting fitter will add a set number of years to your life. What I can tell you is that fitness tracks with survival as closely as almost anything else we measure. That’s enough for me to take seriously and act on.
