💊 Which statin should you choose?

All statins block HMG-CoA reductase → the liver makes less cholesterol → more LDL receptors → more LDL cleared. LDL ↓↓↓ · HDL ↑ · TG ↓ — and they lower mortality in CAD.

The choice comes down to the patient 👇

🫀 ATORVASTATIN — after MI / ACS
Moderate or high intensity, easy to titrate. Minimal renal excretion, so it's usable in CKD.

💧 ROSUVASTATIN — the most potent
Also a post-MI choice. Few drug interactions, long half-life. Hydrophilic, so it's often better tolerated after muscle symptoms (not guaranteed) — start low, at 5 mg.

💊 PRAVASTATIN — many medications
Very few drug interactions and often tolerated after muscle symptoms. Lower potency: no high-intensity dose.

🎗️ PITAVASTATIN — HIV
In the REPRIEVE trial it cut major cardiovascular events by about 35% in adults with HIV. Always check the antiretroviral regimen.

⚠️ SIMVASTATIN — interactions
Older, with outcome data, but many interactions: amiodarone and some calcium-channel blockers need dose limits.

🧠 Exam point: muscle pain on a statin? Think myopathy — the risk rises with fibrates or niacin. Also watch for ↑ LFTs.

Two RCTs of statins in Alzheimer's disease were neutral: no benefit, no harm

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as treatment for existing Alzheimer's disease, one with simvastatin and one with atorvastatin. Neither slowed progression, and neither worsened it. This concerns treating established AD, not preventing it.

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as a treatment for established Alzheimer's: simvastatin in 406 people with normal lipids over 18 months, and atorvastatin 80 mg (LEADe) in people already on donepezil over 72 weeks. Both were neutral on cognition and safe.
