ACE inhibitors can raise potassium; risk rises with potassium-rich foods or other drugs

A pharmacy-exam review explains that ACE inhibitors can cause hyperkalemia, and the risk increases when combined with potassium-rich foods or other potassium-raising drugs. Relevant to people on blood pressure medication who change diet or supplements.

  • ACE inhibitors can cause high blood potassium (hyperkalemia)
  • Risk is higher with potassium-rich foods or other potassium-increasing drugs

ACE inhibitors can raise potassium levels, leading to hyperkalemia (high blood potassium).

This risk is heightened when combined with potassium-rich foods or other potassium-increasing drugs. In this review, we highlight the essential points you should know about this interaction.

ACE inhibitors can cause angioedema, even years after starting

Sep 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

A health-safety post warns that people taking ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, ramipril, benazepril) can develop angioedema, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, even years later and often without itching. Risk is higher over 65, in smokers, people of African descent and those on immunosuppressants. Real swelling or breathing trouble needs emergency care.
