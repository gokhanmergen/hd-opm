Defending a lean, nut-and-olive-oil vegetarian diet against 'junk food' labels

Louis Nervegna · Jul 25, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetesWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues a diet heavy in nuts, avocado, unsaturated oils, some cheese and seafood, and low in refined starch, sugar and saturated fat is healthy. He says unsaturated fat improves insulin sensitivity and lowers LDL, and that his example's good lipids reflect leanness, whereas a person avoiding all fat but staying overweight needs to lose visceral fat first.

  • Claim: over 100 randomized trials in aggregate show unsaturated fat, particularly polyunsaturated, improves insulin sensitivity
  • Excess visceral fat must be lost before lipid numbers improve (Louis's view)

In a crazy alternative universe somewhere on the internet, this person is apparently a “junk food vegetarian” because they eat a lot of nuts and avocados and foods cooked in unsaturated vegetable oil, and even— heaven forbid— some cheese and seafood.

Guess what this person is NOT eating: Lots of white potatoes, refined starch, sugar, and saturated fat.

This person has excellent lipid numbers because he is lean, and all of the unsaturated fat that he is eating improves insulin sensitivity and lowers LDL. Over 100 randomized trials in humans have shown in aggregate that unsaturated fat, particularly polyunsaturated fat, improves insulin sensitivity: ↪ 100+ trials: polyunsaturated fat improves glucose and insulin markers, saturated doesn't. That’s why his TG and HDL look so good, while his wife is struggling.

He stays lean on this “junk food vegetarian” diet, which is clearly working for him.

Meanwhile, his wife who avoids every drop of fat is chronically overweight. No magic diet is going to improve her numbers until she first loses the excess visceral fat.

Swapping 10% of carb calories for vegetable fats cut lethal prostate cancer 29%

Louis quotes a UCSF-led study of nearly 4,600 men with non-metastatic prostate cancer. Substituting healthy vegetable fats (olive and canola oil, nuts, seeds, avocado) for carbohydrates or animal fats was associated with lower lethal prostate cancer and all-cause mortality. The study adjusted for age, treatment, BMI, smoking, exercise and other factors.

Louis summarizes a meta-analysis of randomized feeding trials showing polyunsaturated fat, mostly omega-6 from vegetable oils, lowers fasting glucose and A1C and improves insulin resistance and insulin secretion.
