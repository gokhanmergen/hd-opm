Caution on the high-protein craze: Harvard cohorts show no mortality gain from protein

Louis Nervegna · Aug 03, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis warns that benefits of high protein are exaggerated and long-term risks possible, citing a Cell Press review and ~300,000 people tracked for 30+ years.

  • ~300,000 people, 30+ years

I’ve brought this topic up multiple times before in the group.

There are lots of reasons to be cautious about this high protein craze that we are going through right now.

The possible benefits are repeatedly over-exaggerated by influencers, and there may be long-term risks to overdoing it.

Link to journal article:

PaperCell press bluecell.com

We now have good long-term data on protein intake and mortality in humans, which can be found in this post: ↪ Protein type matters more than amount: processed meat worst, plant protein best. That post summarizes data from tracking nearly 300,000 people prospectively for over 30 years in the Harvard Nurses Study I & Il and the Harvard Physicians Follow-up Study. These people were tracked all the way from midlife to old age and death.

Protein type matters more than restriction: whole-food plant protein beats processed meat

Nov 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 38 Protein

Louis argues against the 'protein craze': there is no good evidence protein restriction slows human aging, but long-term data show whole-food plant protein beats animal protein, especially processed and red meat. Among animal sources, seafood, poultry and fermented dairy have the lowest mortality and processed meat is worst. Plant protein's lower isoleucine, valine and methionine may explain part of it.

Review of 350+ studies links protein restriction to better metabolism and healthier aging

Dudley Lamming and colleague reviewed over 350 studies and report that lower protein intake is associated with improved metabolism and healthier aging via mechanisms like a liver hormone. Lamming says benefits of protein are clear for active people but sedentary people may overeat it.

Protein restriction and aging: animal benefits, no proven human lifespan gain

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Protein

Louis shares a summary of a review (Knopf and Lamming) of protein and amino-acid restriction. Restricting methionine or the branched-chain amino acids isoleucine and valine drives many animal metabolic benefits, FGF21 is involved, but lifespan extension has not been shown in humans. The field is shifting from 'less protein' to which amino acids matter, and older or active adults may need more protein. Louis says protein is complicated and influencers oversimplify it.
