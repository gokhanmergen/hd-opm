Fermented foods do not appear to raise stomach cancer risk, but salt-preserved foods may

Louis Nervegna · Oct 24, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
CancerNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods
AI summary & key facts

Louis says the totality of evidence suggests plant- and dairy-based fermented foods do not raise stomach cancer risk. Salt preservation does seem to be associated with higher risk, likely from the high sodium content rather than fermentation, and many fermented foods are also salty, which confounds studies.

  • Salt preservation associated with higher stomach cancer risk
  • Sodium is the likely culprit, not fermentation

Does eating fermented foods raise risk for stomach cancer?

The totality of evidence suggests no, at least for plant and dairy-based fermented foods.

However, preserving foods with salt (“salt preservation”) does seem to be associated with increased stomach cancer risk. The issue appears to be the high sodium content, not the fermentation. The problem is that many fermented foods are also high in salt, which introduces a confounding factor.
