Lp(a) and triglycerides are ApoB particles, so accepting them but not LDL is odd

Louis Nervegna · Jul 21, 2024 · ♥ 17 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelLp(a)Myths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

The poster points out that keto and carnivore influencers who deny LDL/ApoB causality but accept Lp(a) and triglycerides as causal are contradicting themselves: Lp(a) and VLDL (measured as triglycerides) are ApoB-tagged particles just like LDL. Most triglyceride is carried by VLDL, which differs little from LDL.

  • Lp(a) and VLDL both carry ApoB
  • Triglycerides largely reflect VLDL
  • All ApoB particles add to total ApoB

It’s so weird to me that these keto and carnivore influencers on social media claim that LDL and ApoB are not causal in heart disease in their videos… BUT they also claim that Lp(a) and Triglycerides are?? These are both part of ApoB!

Lp(a) and Triglycerides are ApoB tagged, and they add to your ApoB just like LDL does. Most triglyceride is carried by VLDL. Triglycerides are just measuring your VLDL. There is very little difference between LDL and VLDL.

Why would you accept that ApoB particles like Lp(a) and VLDL (triglycerides) cause heart disease, but deny that other ApoB particles like LDL do not?

This makes no sense to me.

Do these people even realize that Lp(a) and Triglycerides (VLDL) are part of ApoB?
