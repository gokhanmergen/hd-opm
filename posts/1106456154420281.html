Shared from Science Sphere · original post

Research suggests that body weight influences how effectively the body responds to vitamin D supplementation.

A reanalysis of data from the VITAL trial, indicates that individuals with higher body mass index (BMI) show a blunted response to vitamin D, potentially explaining why supplementation benefits are more pronounced in people with lower BMI.

The VITAL trial included over 25,000 U.S. adults and originally investigated whether vitamin D and omega-3 supplements could reduce the risk of cancer, heart disease, and stroke.

While the overall trial showed limited benefit of vitamin D supplementation for these outcomes, further analysis of 16,515 participants with baseline blood samples revealed that vitamin D metabolism differs by body weight. People with BMI under 25 experienced stronger increases in vitamin D biomarkers, whereas those with elevated BMI had significantly smaller responses despite supplementation.

Vitamin D plays a key role in calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, and muscle strength, and has been linked to cancer, diabetes, and autoimmune disease outcomes.

The study highlights the importance of personalized dosing strategies and suggests that adjusting vitamin D supplementation according to BMI may help achieve more consistent health benefits across populations.

RESEARCH PAPER 📄

Deirdre K. Tobias et al, “Association of Body Weight With Response to Vitamin D Supplementation and Metabolism.” JAMA Network Open (2023)
