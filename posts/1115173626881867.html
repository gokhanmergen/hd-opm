Clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) increasingly linked to cardiovascular disease and cancer

Louis Nervegna · Sep 18, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A JACC CardioOncology review examines clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential, an age-related mutation in blood stem cell lines, as a shared risk factor for both cardiovascular disease and cancer. Louis notes that evidence keeps accumulating.

  • CHIP is a common aging-related phenomenon
  • Shared risk factor for CVD and solid-organ cancers

More and more evidence keeps pointing to clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) as a risk factor for CVD and other age-related diseases like cancer.

Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential, or CHIP, is a common aging-related phenomenon in which hematopoietic stem cells (produced by the bone marrow) or other early blood cell progenitor cells contribute to the formation of a genetically distinct mutated subpopulation of blood cells. This mutated subpopulation of blood cells is pathological and contributes to the formation of disease.

If we live long enough, all of us will ultimately develop CHIP. It appears to be an inevitable consequence of the aging process itself, except in rare cases. This is one of the mechanisms by which aging itself causes diseases like CVD and cancer.

It can also be caused earlier in life by exposure to certain mutagenic agents, such as cytotoxic chemotherapy drugs for cancer treatment.

Atherosclerotic plaques contain large clonal, mutated cell populations, like tumors

Jun 18, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 14 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

A JCI Insight study used deep whole-exome sequencing of carotid plaques and non-atherosclerotic arteries and found somatic mutations in plaques but not in healthy arterial tissue. This suggests plaques grow by clonal expansion, paralleling clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP), which is causally linked to cardiovascular disease.

High vitamin B12 in a multivitamin did not raise disease risk in a 14-year Harvard trial

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 9 Supplements & vitamins

The poster argues it is unlikely high B12 intake causes cancer or clonal disease. The Physicians' Health Study multivitamin trial, which contained 10 times the RDA of B12, found no increase in major disease over 14 years and an 8% lower overall cancer risk. Nurses' and Physicians' cohorts also found no B12-disease link, except combined B12 and B6 with hip fracture, which vanished for B12 alone.
