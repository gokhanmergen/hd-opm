Higher Lp(a) is linearly associated with lower risk of fatty liver in NHANES data

Louis Nervegna · Mar 16, 2025 · ♥ 16 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Lp(a)Weight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Scientific Reports cross-sectional analysis of 2,308 NHANES participants finding a significant linear negative association between Lp(a) and NAFLD, suggesting Lp(a) could serve as a biomarker of NAFLD risk. He notes that at this point only the inverse relationship is known, and the headline claim that high Lp(a) might protect against fatty liver is speculative.

  • Cross-sectional analysis, 2,308 NHANES participants
  • Significant linear negative association between Lp(a) and NAFLD risk

High Lp(a) might actually protect against fatty liver.

This is a very interesting new paper that was just published.

At this point, all we know is that there is a strong inverse relationship between Lp(a) and risk of developing NAFLD.

Quoted:

“There is a significant linear negative association between Lp(a) levels and the risk of NAFLD, suggesting that Lp(a) may serve as a potential biomarker for assessing NAFLD risk.”
