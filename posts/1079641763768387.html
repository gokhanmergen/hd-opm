An oral glucose tolerance test is the definitive check that diabetes is truly resolved

Louis Nervegna · Jul 31, 2025 · ♥ 21 reactions · 38 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues a lower A1C on low-carb or keto does not prove diabetes is cured, because liver and pancreas fat may persist. Only passing an OGTT is definitive, and he advises scepticism about unverified cure claims.

  • OGTT is the definitive test for diabetes status
  • Lower A1C alone does not mean cured

The definitive test for diabetes is an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT).

If someone has truly cured their diabetes, then they will pass an OGTT.

No other test is definitive.

There are lots of people who lower their A1C a great deal with low carb or keto diets. But they still have significant fat in their liver and pancreas— and their diabetes is certainly not cured. They will fail an OGTT miserably.

Don’t believe anyone on social media who claims to have cured their diabetes, unless they show PROOF from an OGTT.

Most will fail this test. They are very far from cured.

They are just managing the symptoms of the diabetes (like high A1C) by not eating any carbohydrate. Managing symptoms is very different from a cure.

(I don’t want to minimize the importance of managing symptoms in diabetes. This is obviously important. But it is inaccurate to claim that just managing symptoms is a true reversal of the disease.)

You can potentially cure diabetes with low carb or keto diets, but you must lose enough weight to completely clear the liver and pancreas of fat. If the ectopic fat in the liver and pancreas is kept very low in the long term, then the pancreas can regenerate and normal beta function can be restored. Then passing an OGTT actually becomes possible. This has been proven in a large randomized controlled trial:

↪ Roy Taylor shows the pancreas can regenerate in type 2 diabetes, by diet alone

Regeneration of the pancreas (as proven on imaging) and restoral of normal beta cell function is a cure for type 2 diabetes, not just a reversal or a remission. It would be inaccurate to not use the word “cure” in a case where the pancreas is literally regrown on imaging and restored to normal function, and where all clinicals signs of diabetes have disappeared.

There is only one randomized controlled trial in the history of medicine that has ever accomplished this. It used extreme calorie restriction to rapidly clear the liver and pancreas of fat, and then a traditional Mediterranean diet with normal calories for long term maintenance:

↪ Roy Taylor shows the pancreas can regenerate in type 2 diabetes, by diet alone

The liver and pancreas were completely cleared of ectopic fat with this approach. After 2 years of keeping the ectopic fat in the liver and pancreas very low, the tissue in the pancreas literally regrew back to (near) normal again. This is proven on imaging. All clinical signs of the diabetes disappeared. I absolutely stand by the use of the word “cure” in this case.
