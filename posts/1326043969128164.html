ARBs, alone or with a calcium channel blocker, are best-tolerated BP drugs in 700+ trials

Louis Nervegna · Jun 23, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassium
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes the JAMA network meta-analysis showing ARBs have fewer treatment discontinuations from side effects than even placebo. ARB plus CCB was the best-tolerated regimen, ARBs appeared in four of the top five, and CCB monotherapy was worse, suggesting ARB-based regimens may improve long-term adherence.

  • Over 700 short-term clinical trials analysed
  • ARB plus CCB was the best-tolerated regimen overall
  • ARBs featured in four of the top five regimens
  • CCB monotherapy associated with more discontinuations due to side effects

A massive new network meta-analysis published in JAMA demonstrates that ARBs (alone or combined with calcium channel blockers) are the best-tolerated hypertension medications, with fewer treatment discontinuations due to adverse events than even a placebo.

Links to full study:

Paper · JAMAJAMA articlejamanetwork.com Paper · JAMAJAMA articlejamanetwork.com

The landmark study, which analyzed over 700 short-term clinical trials, highlighted specific tolerability data:

  • Top Monotherapy: ARBs as a single drug class were significantly better tolerated than placebos, boasting remarkably low dropout rates.
  • Best Combination: The combination of an ARB plus a Calcium Channel Blocker (CCB) was identified as the overall best-tolerated treatment regimen.
  • Four of the Top Five: In the study's rankings, ARBs were featured in four out of the five highest-tolerated treatment strategies.
  • CCB Monotherapy: In contrast, calcium channel blockers (CCBs) alone were associated with a higher likelihood of side effects that led to patients stopping treatment.

The clinical takeaway is that prescribing ARB-based regimens can drastically improve long-term adherence, making them a top-tier choice when initiating or adjusting high blood pressure treatments.
