Green Mediterranean Diet Slows Brain Aging

Neuroscience NewsGreen Mediterranean Diet Slows Brain Aging - Neuroscience NewsA large-scale dietary trial has shown that a green-Mediterranean diet can slow brain aging by altering key blood proteins linked to neurodegeneration.neurosciencenews.com

If you haven't heard of the "Green Mediterranean Diet", don't feel bad: it was only described by researchers in 2020.

But now a large-scale dietary trial suggests that a calorie-controlled green-Mediterranean diet, combined with access to gyms, nutritional guidance and coaching support, can significantly shape the "brain age gap". The "brain age gap" is the difference between a person's chronological age and the brain age suggested by their MRI.

These findings highlight the potential of diet-based strategies to modulate biological pathways tied to Alzheimer’s and age-related cognitive decline.

In an unusual trial design, this study randomized 300 adults in a single worksite in Israel in 2017. All participants had either excess abdominal fat (waist circumference >35 inches in women or 40 inches in men), or high triglycerides (>150mg/dL) or low HDL (≤ 40 in men; ≤ 50 in women).

Using MRI scans brain-age prediction models, researchers estimated each participant’s brain age before and after the dietary intervention. Participants were randomized to one of three diet plans: 1) a control group given health promoting diet guidelines; 2) an intervention group assigned to a standard low-carb Mediterranean (MED) diet, rich in vegetables and with protein from poultry fish and 28 g of walnuts per day. (about 14 walnut halves); or 3) an intervention group assigned the Green-Mediterranean, lower in processed and red meat than the MED diet and richer in plants and polyphenols, with 3–4 cups of green tea and 500 ml of a Mankai-based (cultivated duckweed) green shake at dinner.

Both MED diets were equally calorie-restricted, with 1500–1800 kcal/day for males and 1200–1400 kcal/day for females. All participants were provided with free gym membership. Additionally, they attended periodic 90-min nutritional and physical activity sessions at their workplace, facilitated by a multidisciplinary team of physicians, clinical dietitians, and fitness instructors.

Previous studies, published in The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, revealed that both the traditional Mediterranean diet and the green-Mediterranean diet slowed age-related brain atrophy by approximately 50% within 18 months. In addition, improved glycemic control – particularly reductions in HbA1c – also contributed to this protective effect.

The present study found that participants whose brain age was older than their chronological age exhibited distinct shifts in their blood protein profiles over the 18 months of the intervention. The green-MED diet significantly reduced, or slowed the increase of, two proteins associated with accelerated brain aging: Galectin-9 and Decorin. The anti-inflammatory activity of these components may explain the potential neuroprotective effect observed in the study.

Dr. Anat Yaskolka-Meir, postdoctoral research fellow at Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health and the co-first author of the paper, explains that “This approach gives us a dynamic window into brain health..and powerful new insights into how interventions, such as diet, may help preserve cognitive function as we age.”
