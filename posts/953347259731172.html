NEJM 2020 dairy review: a mixed bag, best for fermented forms, weak for fractures

Louis Nervegna · Jan 23, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 21 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights the widely cited 2020 NEJM review on dairy and disease risk. It concludes that high dairy intake is not supported for preventing fractures, is not clearly linked to weight, diabetes or CVD, and may raise prostate and endometrial cancer risk while lowering colorectal cancer risk. Effects depend on the comparison food: dairy fares better than processed red meat or sugary drinks but worse than nuts.

  • Evidence does not support high dairy for fracture reduction
  • Total dairy not clearly related to weight control, diabetes or CVD risk
  • Likely higher risk of prostate cancer, possibly endometrial; lower colorectal cancer risk
  • Fermented dairy (yogurt, maybe cheese) shows benefits

This is the most widely cited review article (NEJM 2020) in medical publications on the topic of dairy and disease risk.

The conclusion is that dairy is a mixed bag. There are benefits, particularly for fermented forms like yogurt and maybe cheese, but also risks.

scribd.comLink to scribd.comscribd.com

Quoted from the Conclusion:

“For adults, the overall evidence does not support high dairy consumption for reduction of fractures, which has been a primary justification for current U.S. recommendations. Moreover, total dairy consumption has not been clearly related to weight control or to risks of diabetes and cardiovascular disease. High consumption of dairy foods is likely to increase the risks of prostate cancer and possibly endometrial cancer but reduce the risk of colorectal cancer. It is important to note that the reported health effects of dairy foods depend strongly on the specific foods or beverages to which they are compared; for many outcomes, dairy foods compare favorably with processed red meat or sugar-sweetened beverages but less favorably with plant-protein sources such as nuts.”
