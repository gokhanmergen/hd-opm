Are oxidized fish oil capsules really causing you harm?
Likely not.

This is a video clip of Bill Harris speaking on the subject. He is a well-regarded fatty acid scientist, who has spent most of his career studying essential fatty acids. He has authored several of the most important (and highest-impact) journal papers in the field.

He’s the expert that I pay most attention to on the subject of omega 3 and omega 6 fats.

There are trials in humans that have directly tested this hypothesis. None show evidence of harm.

The trial that he talks about in this clip is the most egregious.

The researchers intentionally super oxidized fish oil. They then fed the super-oxidized fish oil to participants for 7 weeks. There was no detectable difference in measures of oxidation, peroxidation, or inflammation in any of the participants, compared to controls taking non-oxidized fish oil. Nor was there any detectable difference in any other health parameter that they attempted to measure. This included BMI, serum level of glucose, lipids (total cholesterol, LDL-cholesterol, HDL-cholesterol and triglycerides), and serum markers of liver enzyme activity (aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, γ-glutamyl transferase and alkaline phosphatase).

The levels of oxidation in the super-oxidized fish oil product used in this trial were much higher than typical fish oil products on the market.

One of the proposed mechanisms for these results is that the human digestive tract and liver are remarkably efficient at neutralizing or filtering out small amounts of oxidized lipids before they enter systemic circulation.

The bottom line is that this is likely not an issue in actual humans, as he explains. The trials back that up.

If this is something that you insist on worrying about, then you can always pay more for a product that has a 5-star rating from IFOS. This is the international fish oil standards certification program: IFOS (Certifications by Nutrasource). They rigorously test all products which they certify. To achieve a 5-star rating, oxidation levels must be very low.

Incidentally, similar trials have been run with omega 6 fats.

The claim that omega 6 fats cause oxidation and lipid peroxidation in the human body is also false, as Harris has noted many times. This claim has been tested over and over again in human randomized trials, and it has been repeatedly shown to be wrong. See this post: ↪ Claim that omega-6 fats cause oxidation and lipid peroxidation is contradicted by RCTs.
