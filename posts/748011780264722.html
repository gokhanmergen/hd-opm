Harvard: 1-2 weekly servings of fatty fish linked to 36% lower heart-disease death

Louis Nervegna · Jan 29, 2024 · ♥ 9 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Quoting Harvard's Nutrition Source, the post says an analysis of 20 studies with hundreds of thousands of participants found that one to two 3-ounce servings of fatty fish a week (salmon, herring, mackerel, anchovies, sardines) reduces the risk of dying from heart disease by 36 percent. Fish and fish oil are described as good for the heart and blood vessels.

  • Analysis of 20 studies, hundreds of thousands of participants
  • 1-2 servings (3 oz) of fatty fish per week
  • 36% lower risk of dying from heart disease

From the Harvard School of Public Health:

“There is strong evidence that eating fish or taking fish oil is good for the heart and blood vessels. An analysis of 20 studies involving hundreds of thousands of participants indicates that eating approximately one to two 3-ounce servings of fatty fish a week—salmon, herring, mackerel, anchovies, or sardines—reduces the risk of dying from heart disease by 36 percent. (1)”
