Glucosamine linked to lower mortality and CVD events in three cohort studies

Louis Nervegna · Jun 21, 2024 · ♥ 20 reactions · 19 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCardiovascular disease: the big pictureAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

The poster reviews observational studies: a 2012 US cohort (n = 77,510) with 33% lower mortality, UK Biobank (nearly 500,000) with 15% lower cardiovascular events, and a 2020 US cohort (~16,700) with 65% lower CV mortality. He proposes anti-inflammatory and anti-aging mechanisms, noting RCT proof is lacking.

  • 2012 US cohort, n = 77,510: 33% lower all-cause mortality
  • UK Biobank, 466,039 participants: 15% lower CVD risk
  • 2020 US cohort, ~16,700: 65% lower CV mortality, 39% lower overall mortality
  • Mechanisms are hypothetical; not proven in RCTs

Positive human studies on glucosamine, with respect to CVD and overall mortality

A prospective cohort study published in 2012 involving 77,510 people in the United States, aged 50-76, concluded that glucosamine with or without chondroitin was associated with reduced mortality from each cause. This study showed that the use of glucosamine supplements at baseline was associated with a 33% reduced risk of mortality, compared with those who did not use it.

A large prospective cohort study in the United Kingdom published in 2019 showed that glucosamine is associated with an increase in life expectancy. In this study of nearly 500,000 people, glucosamine use was associated with a 15% lower risk of cardiovascular events.

A US cohort study published in 2020 involving nearly 16,700 people over the age of 40 found that taking glucosamine / chondroitin supplements daily was associated with a 65% reduction in cardiovascular mortality and a 39% reduction in overall mortality.

Proposed mechanisms for why glucosamine might be protective against CVD include lowering arterial inflammation and improving the structural integrity of arteries. Emphasis on “might”, because this is not proven in RCT’s.

Hong Kong's 86-year life expectancy goes with a grain, vegetable and fruit heavy diet

Sep 03, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 15 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis notes Hong Kong life expectancy of 86 years versus 76 in the US and cites a 2011 government Total Diet Study of 5,008 adults aged 20-84. Daily intake by weight was led by cereal grains (491 g), then vegetables (177 g), fruit (147 g), meat and poultry (113 g) and fish (71 g). Dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts, sugars and added fats were low, and he uses it to counter carnivore claims. It is an ecological observation.
