Taller people tend to live shorter lives, likely via growth hormone, IGF-1 and mTOR

Louis Nervegna · Aug 23, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityCancerMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

The post notes that taller people have shorter lifespans and higher cancer risk, as with larger versus smaller dogs, probably because of growth hormone, IGF-1 and mTOR signaling. It uses this to criticize a carnivore influencer video about nutrition and height; the claims are not backed by cited studies.

  • Taller people live shorter lives and have higher cancer risk (stated, no source)
  • Smaller dogs live longer than larger dogs
  • Lower GH/IGF-1/mTOR signaling extends lifespan in many animals but reduces size

It’s pretty well known that taller people live shorter lives, unfortunately. They are also at higher risk for cancer.

It’s true in other mammals as well. Dogs are a classic example: Smaller dogs live considerably longer than larger dogs.

This likely has a lot to do with growth hormone, IGF1, and mTOR signaling during development. Interventions that reduce this signaling reliably extend lifespan in many different animals, including mammals. But it also makes the animal smaller.

Hong Kong's 86-year life expectancy goes with a grain, vegetable and fruit heavy diet

Sep 03, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 15 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis notes Hong Kong life expectancy of 86 years versus 76 in the US and cites a 2011 government Total Diet Study of 5,008 adults aged 20-84. Daily intake by weight was led by cereal grains (491 g), then vegetables (177 g), fruit (147 g), meat and poultry (113 g) and fish (71 g). Dairy, eggs, legumes, nuts, sugars and added fats were low, and he uses it to counter carnivore claims. It is an ecological observation.
