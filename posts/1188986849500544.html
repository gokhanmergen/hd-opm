One avocado a week linked to 16% lower CVD and 21% lower CHD over 30 years

Louis Nervegna · Dec 26, 2025 · ♥ 37 reactions · 41 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Harvard cohort data from about 110,000 people found weekly avocado eaters had lower cardiovascular and coronary heart disease risk than rare or non-eaters.

  • 16% lower CVD risk
  • 21% lower CHD risk
  • About 110,000 people from Nurses' Health Study and HPFS, ~30 years

Eating at least one avocado per week is associated with a 16% reduction in risk of developing cardiovascular disease (CVD) and a 21% reduction in risk of developing coronary heart disease (CHD), over a 30 year period of time.

These results are in comparison to people not eating avocados, or only eating them rarely.

Harvard GazetteAn avocado a week may lower heart disease riskA Harvard study finds that people who eat two or more servings of avocado each week may lower their risk of cardiovascular disease compared to people who rarely eat avocado.news.harvard.edu

This data comes from tracking 110,000 people in both the Harvard Nurses Study and the Harvard Physicians Followup Study.

The researchers tracked most of these people from middle age to near the end of their lives— 30 years on average— which is an extremely long duration for a prospective study of this type.

Major strengths of this study are its large size and very long duration.

The results were published in 2022 in the Journal of the American Heart Association.

Substitutiting half of a serving of avocado (1/4 of an avocado) in place of half of a daily serving of margarine, butter, egg, yogurt, cheese or processed meats, was also associated with a 16-22% reduction in risk of cardiovascular events like heart attacks and strokes, over this same 30 year time period.

Substituting half a serving of avocado for the equivalent daily amount of olive oil, nuts or other plant oils showed no additional benefit.

Quoted:

The researchers looked at 30 years of data from more than 110,000 female and male participants in the Nurses’ Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-Up Study. After taking into account a wide range of cardiovascular risk factors and overall diet, they found that people who ate at least two weekly servings of avocado — with a serving defined as half an avocado — had a 16 percent lower risk of cardiovascular disease and a 21 percent lower risk of coronary heart disease during the study period. Those who swapped half a daily serving of animal products [specifically: butter, egg, yogurt, cheese, or processed meats] for avocado had a 16–22 percent lower risk of cardiovascular disease events.

Quoted:

Researchers believe this is the first, large, prospective study to support the positive association between higher avocado consumption and lower cardiovascular events, such as coronary heart disease and stroke.

“Our study provides further evidence that the intake of plant-sourced unsaturated fats can improve diet quality and is an important component in cardiovascular disease prevention,” said Lorena S. Pacheco, Ph.D., M.P.H., R.D.N., lead author of the study and a postdoctoral research fellow in the nutrition department at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health in Boston. “These are particularly notable findings since the consumption of avocados has risen steeply in the U.S. in the last 20 years, according to data from the U.S. Department of Agriculture.”

Based on statistical modeling, replacing half a serving daily of margarine, butter, egg, yogurt, cheese or processed meats such as bacon with the same amount of avocado was associated with a 16% to 22% lower risk of cardiovascular disease events.

Substituting half a serving a day of avocado for the equivalent amount of olive oil, nuts and other plant oils showed no additional benefit.
