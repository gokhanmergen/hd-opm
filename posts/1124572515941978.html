Frequent sauna use linked to 50% lower CVD mortality, but evidence is mostly observational

Louis Nervegna · Sep 30, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A shared summary reports that sauna use 4 to 7 times weekly is associated with about half the cardiovascular mortality and 40% lower all-cause mortality, possibly because heat mimics moderate aerobic exercise. Louis cautions that the evidence is mostly observational, though the potential benefit could be large if causal.

  • 4-7 sauna sessions per week associated with 50% lower CV mortality
  • 40% lower all-cause mortality
  • Evidence is mostly observational (Louis's caveat)

The evidence for this is mostly observational, but the possible benefit might be very high if it actually turns out to be causal.

Shared from FoundMyFitness · original post

Regular sauna use (4–7x weekly) is associated with a 50% lower cardiovascular-related mortality and a 40% lower all-cause mortality

These benefits occur because heat exposure from sauna use closely mimics the physiological effects of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise

Similar to a workout, sauna sessions raise your heart rate, boost core body temperature, increase blood flow, and induce sweating

When combined with regular moderate exercise, sauna bathing provides additive benefits by further enhancing cardiorespiratory fitness, cholesterol levels, and blood pressure beyond exercise alone
