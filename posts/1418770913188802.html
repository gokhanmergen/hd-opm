Daily peanuts for 16 weeks improved brain insulin responsiveness in older adults

Shared from Science & Nature · original post

Recent research published in the journal Food & Function highlights a straightforward dietary habit that offers significant protection for the aging brain. Scientists have discovered that consuming a daily portion of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts can directly enhance neurological health in older adults.

In a comprehensive 16-week clinical trial, healthy individuals between the ages of 60 and 75 were given 60 grams of peanuts each day. Advanced imaging techniques revealed that this consistent intake led to a remarkable improvement in how specific memory-related regions of the brain respond to insulin. This regional insulin responsiveness is a critical factor in ensuring our brain cells efficiently utilize energy to maintain essential cognitive functions over time.

The physiological benefits of this dietary addition are uniquely targeted to the central nervous system, as researchers observed no broader systemic changes in overall body metabolism or liver fat levels.

These new findings provide a vital biological mechanism that explains earlier observations by the same scientific team, which linked regular peanut consumption to better memory retention and increased cerebral blood flow. Incorporating a modest handful of unsalted peanuts into a daily routine presents a natural and highly accessible strategy to actively support long-term cognitive resilience as we age.

Citation: Lucia Kerkhof et al, Daily consumption of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts beneficially affects regional brain insulin responsiveness in older adults: Results of a longer-term randomized controlled trial, Food & Function (2026). DOI: 10.1039/d6fo03208g
