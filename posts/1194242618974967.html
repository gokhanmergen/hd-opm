Sweet potatoes are not actually potatoes

Reference:

The Nutrition Source - Harvard Chan SchoolSweet Potatoes • The Nutrition SourceSweet potatoes are typically recognized by their copper-colored skin and vibrant orange flesh, though the hundreds of varieties grown worldwide display colorsnutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu

Sweet potatoes are a root (like carrots and beets) which come from a family of plants that are genetically unrelated to white potatoes. White potatoes are tubers, not roots. (Tubers are underground stems.)

Unlike white potatoes, sweet potatoes are not associated with increased risk for weight gain and diabetes in recent studies.

The purple ones that were traditionally eaten in Japan may be especially healthy:

facebook.com/share/v/1BsCBwrXZ3

Quoted:

“Unlike a potato (edible tubers of the nightshade family), the sweet “potato” is a large edible root within the morning glory family. They’re also different from yams, which are edible tubers within the lily family and native to Africa and Asia. Chances are the “yams” found in your local supermarket are actually a variety of sweet potato. True yams are distinguishable by their blackish/brown, bark-like skin and white or purple-toned flesh.”

“Most people don’t eat sweet potatoes in the same over-sized quantities as they do white potatoes, which is perhaps why research studies haven’t found sweet potatoes to be a major culprit for weight gain and diabetes.”
