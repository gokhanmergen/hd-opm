Prunes for Bone Health

Shirin Hooshmand, Ph.D., RD, a professor of nutrition at San Diego State University, gave a presentation during UAMS' National Nutrition Month seminar series, advocating for the consumption of prunes to support bone health, especially in postmenopausal women. Her research highlights that prunes, rich in essential nutrients, have been shown to help maintain bone density and prevent bone loss in both animal models and human studies. In a study involving postmenopausal women with osteopenia, participants who ate prunes alongside calcium and vitamin D supplements showed better maintenance of bone density compared to those who only took supplements. The protective effects of prunes were observed across a range of ages, and similar findings were confirmed by a larger Penn State study.

Hooshmand's research suggests that prunes may also help men between 50 to 79 years old maintain their bone density. Additionally, while prunes contain natural sugars like sorbitol, which contribute to their sweetness, they are also high in fiber, which may help mitigate blood glucose spikes. Hooshmand noted that in her studies, prune consumption did not significantly affect fasting glucose levels, although A1C levels were not measured. She emphasized the importance of maintaining a balanced diet, including fruits and vegetables, to improve overall health, as many Americans fall short of daily fruit intake recommendations.
