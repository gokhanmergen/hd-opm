High protein intake linked to higher cardiac risk in adults over 55

Louis Nervegna · Nov 25, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a 2026 cohort study finding that high protein intake may raise CVD risk in older adults and notes it matches 30-year Harvard cohort data on animal protein. He recommends mostly plant protein with some seafood and yogurt.

  • Observational cohort, adults over 55
  • Harvard Nurses' data show more high-quality animal protein linked to higher CVD risk
  • High animal protein diets never tested as CVD treatment

Links to article and study:

EatingWellScientists Just Discovered a Surprising Risk of a High-Protein DietA new study finds high-protein diets may increase heart risk in adults over 55. Here's what to know.eatingwell.com Paper · The Journal of nutrition, health and aging · 2026Associations between High Protein Intake and Cardiovascular Diseases by Age Groups: A Cohort Studysciencedirect.com

If it were just this one observational study, I wouldn’t be paying much attention. But you see the same thing in the 30 year data from the Harvard Nurse’s Study. The larger the amount of high-quality animal protein consumed from middle age into old age, the higher the risk for CVD and other major diseases:

↪ Midlife plant protein raised odds of healthy aging 22-58% per 3% of calories (NHS)

There are real concerns here for people with CVD, or those of us trying hard to prevent it. This protein craze may be causing long term harm.

You are better off getting the majority of your protein from whole plant foods, with smaller amounts from seafood and yogurt. And maybe some cheese, poultry, eggs in moderation. We know from large randomized trials on the Mediterranean diet that this is safe for the heart:

↪ Lyon Diet Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut cardiac events 73% and total deaths 70%

↪ Meta-analysis of 4 Mediterranean diet RCTs: MACE down 48%, CVD death down 46%

High animal protein diets have never been properly tested as a treatment for CVD. We have no idea what effect they actual have, especially in the long term. The data from long term prospective cohort studies on high animal protein diets is not good with respect to CVD, especially diets high in red and processed meat.

Full-fat yogurt lowered ApoB while low-fat yogurt plus butter raised it in a crossover RCT

Jun 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 15 ↩ 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis summarizes a randomized crossover trial matching saturated fat between full-fat yogurt and low-fat yogurt with added butter. Despite equal saturated fat, the butter arm raised ApoB while full-fat yogurt lowered it, which the study attributes to the milk fat globule membrane (MFGM) that is intact in yogurt but lost in butter. He suggests this may partly explain why yogurt looks better than other dairy in epidemiology.
