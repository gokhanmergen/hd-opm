A vitamin D-based drug may have found a way to weaken pancreatic cancer’s hidden shield.

For years, one reason pancreatic cancer has been so hard to treat is that tumors build a dense, fibrotic barrier around themselves packed with activated fibroblasts that can block drugs and suppress immune attack.

But a new Nature Cancer study led by Dana-Farber and built on Salk Institute research suggests that paricalcitol, a prescription vitamin D analog, may help remodel that protective tumor environment.

In a small clinical trial of 36 patients with untreated metastatic pancreatic cancer, paricalcitol was safely combined with standard chemotherapy. Tumor biopsies showed reduced fibroblast activation and increased T-cell infiltration signs that the tumor’s defenses may have become more vulnerable.

The trial was not designed to prove survival benefit, but early signals were intriguing: patients receiving paricalcitol had higher response rates, and those with high vitamin D receptor expression appeared to do best.

This is not “take vitamin D for cancer.” It is a targeted drug strategy and it may be pointing toward a new way to make one of medicine’s deadliest cancers more treatable.

Would you want your tumor tested for vitamin D receptor levels if it could help guide treatment?

Source: Perez KJ, Dias Costa A, Jordan A, et al. Gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel with or without the VDR agonist paricalcitol for metastatic pancreatic cancer: a randomized, multiarm, run-in phase trial. Nature Cancer. 2026; published May 25.
