Muscle biological age is the top cell-type predictor of mortality in 60,542 people

Louis Nervegna · Jun 24, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a 2026 Nature Medicine study using plasma proteins from 60,542 people to estimate the biological age of over 40 cell types. Skeletal muscle aging best predicted all-cause mortality. He cautions about reverse causality but notes it offers a biological rationale for resistance training lowering mortality.

  • 60,542 participants, over 7,000 plasma proteins, 40+ cell types
  • Skeletal muscle was the strongest predictor of all-cause mortality
  • Most accelerated muscle agers died at over 4x the rate of those with youthful muscle
  • Linked 147,374-adult analysis: resistance training tied to 13% lower all-cause and 27% lower neurological mortality

Biological age of muscle is the strongest predictor of mortality of any tissue type measured.

While there is likely a lot of reverse causality involved here (the aging process causes deterioration of muscle), this result does provide some biological explanation why exercise— and resistance training in particular— lowers mortality.

Quoted:

One cell type predicts mortality better than any other

This month, Nature Medicine published the largest cellular aging study to date: plasma proteins from 60,542 people used to estimate the biological age of 40+ cell types independently, from one blood draw.

Tissues age at different rates, each shedding a distinct protein signature, so blood can reveal how old each cell type is behaving.

Of every cell type, skeletal muscle was the strongest predictor of all-cause mortality. The most accelerated muscle agers died at over 4x the rate of those whose muscle stayed youthful, ahead of brain, cardiac, and immune cells.

Muscle stands out as the most modifiable tissue on that map. A separate 2026 analysis (147,374 adults, up to 30 years) linked resistance training to 13 percent lower all-cause and 27 percent lower neurological mortality, leveling off at two hours a week.

The cell type that most strongly forecasts mortality is also the one with the most robust, lowest-cost intervention behind it.
