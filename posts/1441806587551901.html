A 50:50 salt/potassium blend cuts sodium and adds potassium; avoid with kidney disease

Dec 04, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 17 ↩ 1 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

Louis recommends Morton Lite Salt, a 50:50 mix of regular salt and potassium chloride that is also iodized (60 mcg iodine per 1/4 teaspoon, 40% of the RDA). He warns people with kidney problems should not use potassium salts because excess potassium can cause fatal cardiac arrest.

Kimchi meta-analysis: lower triglycerides, glucose and blood pressure across nine studies

Sep 21, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 8 · 💬 5 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

A University of Connecticut meta-analysis of nine studies linked regular kimchi intake to better cardiometabolic markers. The blood pressure benefit is attributed to potassium and fermentation bacteria despite kimchi's high sodium, though most studies were done in Korea.

Potassium-enriched salt cut recurrent stroke 14% and stroke deaths 21% in stroke survivors

Feb 11, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 17 · 💬 10 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

A cluster randomized trial in rural northern China (a subgroup analysis of a larger trial) gave stroke survivors a salt substitute containing 25% potassium chloride. It reduced recurrent stroke, hemorrhagic stroke and stroke-related death compared with regular salt, suggesting a cheap, widely usable strategy where sodium intake is high.

Dr Stanfield: systolic below 120 cut deaths 25-27% in SPRINT and a Chinese follow-up

Sep 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 10 · 💬 14 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

Summary of a video saying SPRINT and a Chinese follow-up show targeting systolic pressure below 120 mmHg rather than 140 reduces heart attacks, strokes and death, with 25-27% lower mortality. Advice includes home monitoring, high potassium and low sodium, exercise, sleep and medication when needed.

Americans average 2,500 mg potassium daily; more lowers blood pressure in hypertension

May 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

A shared post notes average potassium intake (about 2,500 mg) falls short of the Adequate Intake (3,400 mg men, 2,600 mg women). A 2013 BMJ review of 22 randomized trials found about 3.5 mmHg lower systolic BP with more potassium, roughly 7 mmHg at 90-120 mmol/day.
