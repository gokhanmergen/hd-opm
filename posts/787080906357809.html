PCSK9 inhibitors like Repatha have real side effects and a shorter safety record

Louis Nervegna · Apr 15, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugsStatins
AI summary & key facts

Louis pushes back on the claim that PCSK9 inhibitors lack significant side effects. The manufacturer lists flu-like symptoms, back pain and high blood sugar as the most common, and long-term effects are not yet known, which is why guidance reserves them for high-risk patients or those who cannot tolerate statins.

  • Most common Repatha side effects: flu-like symptoms, back pain, high blood sugar
  • Guidance: reserve PCSK9 inhibitors for high-risk patients or statin-intolerant people

It is false that PCSK9 inhibitors like repatha do not have significant side effects.

The most common side effects of repatha are flu-like symptoms, back pain, and high blood sugar. And there are multiple other less common side effects.

The manufacturer of the drug (Amgen) officially lists these as the most common side effects in their documentation on the drug (see link below).

.

repatha.comRepatha side effectsrepatha.com

The jury is still out on whether or not they are safer than statins. People making this claim are not doing so based on the preponderance of evidence that we have so far. Statins have been studied for decades and the side effects are well known. PCSK9 inhibitors are still relatively new, and we do not yet know the long term side effects.

Because of the unknowns, the evidence-based guidance is that PCSK inhibitors should be reserved for high risk patients or people who can not tolerate statins.

PCSK9 inhibitor plus statin nearly halted coronary calcium progression versus statin alone

Limiting diabetes risk on LDL-lowering drugs: statin risk is modest, alternatives exist

Review explaining that statins modestly increase new-onset diabetes in a dose-dependent way, mostly in people with existing metabolic risk factors, and that cardiovascular benefit clearly outweighs this. It suggests statins mainly bring forward diagnosis in predisposed people, outlines mechanisms (beta-cell dysfunction, insulin resistance) and proposes ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors and pitavastatin as options that limit glycemic harm.

Review: aggressive LDL lowering outweighs theoretical risks, yet few reach <55 mg/dL

Oct 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Summarises a July 2025 review: guidelines now target LDL-C <55 mg/dL (<40 after a recurrent event), PCSK9 inhibitors add 59-62% LDL reduction, and evidence (FOURIER, CTT meta-analysis, PCSK9 genetics) supports benefit to very low levels. It says safety concerns such as hemorrhagic stroke, cognitive decline (EBBINGHAUS) and hormone disruption are unsupported, while fewer than one in four high-risk patients reach target.

Lipoprotein(a) explained: 80-90% genetic, stable for life, a risk factor beyond LDL

Oct 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 8 Lp(a)

Explainer on Lp(a): an LDL-like particle with apo(a) attached, 80-90% genetically determined, fully expressed by 1-2 years of age, and elevated in about 1 in 5 US individuals (1.4 billion globally). Levels above 50 mg/dL or 125 nmol/L raise ASCVD risk, possibly via impaired fibrinolysis and cholesterol deposition, and may occur with normal lipid panels.
