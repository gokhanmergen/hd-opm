NASEM alcohol review: moderate drinking tied to lower all-cause and CVD mortality

Louis Nervegna · Sep 30, 2025 · ♥ 7 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

The National Academies' evidence review on alcohol and health, published in JAMA on September 30, 2025, graded certainty using the USPSTF framework. It reached three moderate-certainty conclusions, including that moderate drinking is associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, while stressing biases and the known harms of heavy drinking.

  • No high-certainty conclusions for low-dose alcohol and specific outcomes
  • Three moderate-certainty conclusions
  • Moderate drinking associated with lower all-cause mortality
  • Moderate drinking associated with lower CVD mortality

Quoted:

“Report Results -

The committee used the evidence certainty assessment framework from the US Preventive Services Task Force to grade conclusions. According to this framework, the certainty of evidence is assessed as high, moderate, or low. Although no high-certainty conclusions could be made regarding associations between [low dose] alcohol and specific outcomes, the committee made 3 conclusions with moderate certainty. Moderate certainty implies that “as more information becomes available, the magnitude or direction of the observed effect could change, and this change may be large enough to alter the conclusion.”

The committee’s 3 moderate-certainty conclusions were:

  • Moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality.
  • Moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular disease mortality.
  • Moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a higher risk of (female) breast cancer.”

A 2012 meta-analysis of 134,537 participants in 22 RCTs found identical cancer incidence (1.4% per year) and mortality (0.5% per year) in statin users and non-users, and a 2023 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found the same.
