Berberine modestly lowers LDL, triglycerides and ApoB: meta-analysis of 18 RCTs

Louis Nervegna · Sep 03, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post

A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized placebo-controlled trials in adults with dyslipidemia found berberine produced small reductions in LDL cholesterol, triglycerides and apoB, with possible sex-specific effects on HDL. The conclusion calls for larger trials assessing clinical outcomes.

  • 18 randomized placebo-controlled trials, 1,788 adults with dyslipidemia
  • Doses roughly 900-1,500 mg/day
  • Small reductions in LDL, triglycerides, total cholesterol, ApoB
  • No outcome data; larger trials needed

Shared from Genuinely Healthy · original post

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism analyzed 18 randomized, placebo-controlled trials involving 1,788 adults with dyslipidemia. Across the included trials, berberine supplementation was associated with significant improvements in several blood-lipid markers, including reductions in LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, total cholesterol, and ApoB, along with changes in HDL cholesterol.

The clinical trials generally used berberine doses in the range of approximately 900–1,500 mg per day, although doses and treatment durations varied between studies. The meta-analysis provides evidence that berberine may improve lipid profiles, but it does not establish that 900–1,500 mg is an appropriate dose for everyone. Berberine can cause gastrointestinal side effects and interact with medications, so supplementation should be discussed with a healthcare professional, particularly for people taking prescription medicines.
