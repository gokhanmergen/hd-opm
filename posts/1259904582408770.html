Flu vaccine halves the heart attack and stroke spike after influenza, even if infected

Louis Nervegna · Apr 02, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

A study reports influenza raises stroke risk about threefold and heart attack about fivefold, but vaccination cut that increase roughly in half even in people who still got infected.

  • Stroke ~3x and heart attack ~5x higher after influenza
  • Vaccination halved that increase

A new study shows that the influenza vaccine might also protect against serious heart problems. Getting the flu significantly increases the risk of heart attack and stroke, especially during the first week after infection.

Interestingly, the vaccine appears to reduce that risk even if a person still gets infected. In the study, the risk of stroke increased about threefold and the risk of heart attack fivefold after influenza—but this increase was cut in half among those who had been vaccinated.

This means the vaccine may provide additional protection against dangerous heart-related complications of influenza infection.

I’ll add my usual caveat here:

Please remember that this is an observational study. It only demonstrates an association between flu vaccination and lower heart attack and stroke risk. It does not prove causality. For example, it’s possible that people who get the flu vaccine take better care of their health in general, compared to people who do not (confounding). That could also explain why their risk is lower, as opposed to a direct causal effect from the vaccine itself.
