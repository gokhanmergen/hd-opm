Ezetimibe use linked to 86% lower Alzheimer's and dementia risk in database analysis

Louis Nervegna · Oct 19, 2026 · ♥ 38 reactions · 27 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

A 2024 Aging Biology paper analysed clinical databases and found that people taking the LDL-lowering drug ezetimibe had a much lower risk of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias. Brain tissue from patients also showed more of two proteins, HK1 and 14-3-3 gamma. This is an observational association, so it does not prove the drug prevents dementia.

  • Ezetimibe use associated with 86% lower risk of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
  • More than 88% lower risk among high-risk people with heart disease
  • Postmortem brains of Alzheimer's plus heart disease patients showed more HK1 and 14-3-3 gamma than healthy controls

Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Alzheimer’s disease is characterized by the progressive loss of memory and cognitive function, accompanied by the accumulation of abnormal proteins in the brain that contribute to neuronal damage and chronic inflammation. Researchers are increasingly investigating whether existing medications could influence these processes and potentially reduce the risk of neurodegenerative diseases.

A 2024 study published in Aging Biology examined ezetimibe, an FDA-approved cholesterol-lowering medication commonly prescribed to reduce LDL cholesterol.

By analyzing clinical databases, researchers found that ezetimibe use was associated with an 86% lower risk of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, with an even greater reduction of more than 88% observed among high-risk individuals with heart disease.

The researchers also examined postmortem brain tissue and identified increased accumulation of two proteins, hexokinase-1 (HK1) and 14-3-3 gamma (14-3-3γ), in patients with Alzheimer's disease and heart disease compared with healthy controls.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).
