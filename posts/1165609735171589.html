Metformin eased knee osteoarthritis pain more than placebo in a 6-month Australian RCT

Apr 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 ↩ 2 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Luigi Fontana's summary of a JAMA RCT in people with knee osteoarthritis and overweight/obesity: metformin reduced pain more than placebo. He frames it as aging biology (AMPK, mTOR, inflammation) as a treatment target, and notes lifestyle approaches like caloric restriction act on the same pathways. Little direct heart relevance.

MSM 6 g/day reduced knee osteoarthritis pain over 12 weeks in a small 2006 pilot RCT

Jun 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a 2006 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial in 50 people aged 40-76 with knee osteoarthritis. MSM 3 g twice daily for 12 weeks reduced pain and physical-function impairment on WOMAC versus placebo, though the authors say long-term benefit and safety cannot be confirmed.

Largest collagen review (113 trials): helps skin and osteoarthritis, little for muscle

Jun 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 3 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares an umbrella review of 16 systematic reviews covering 113 trials. Collagen improved skin elasticity and hydration and reduced osteoarthritis pain and stiffness with high-certainty evidence, plus a small muscle benefit in older adults. The shared post notes collagen is a poor muscle protein compared with whey.

How semaglutide appears to repair cartilage in mice and humans, independent of weight loss

Mar 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 2 ↩ 1 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)

Louis shares a ScienceAlert article on the semaglutide osteoarthritis study. Semaglutide reprogrammed the metabolism of cartilage-forming cells, reduced pain and cartilage degeneration, and in mice caused fewer bone spurs; the authors describe a weight-loss-independent repair mechanism.

Semaglutide raised knee cartilage thickness 17% in 6 months in a small pilot

Mar 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 19 ↩ 2 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)

Louis highlights a Cell Metabolism study in mice and people with obesity and osteoarthritis. After six months on low-dose semaglutide plus hyaluronic acid, MRI showed a 17% increase in cartilage thickness versus under 1% in the comparison group, with better knee function; Science News notes bigger trials are needed.
