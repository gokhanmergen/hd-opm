Whole wheat over white bread: fiber, spermidine and satiety arguments

Gokhan Can · May 27, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Lists reasons to prefer whole wheat bread over white: more spermidine, more fiber that can help lower LDL, and a denser texture that is more filling. The claims are presented without linked studies, apart from a mention that spermidine extended lifespan in some mouse studies.

  • Whole wheat has more spermidine than white bread; spermidine supplementation extended lifespan in some mouse studies
  • Higher fiber content can lower LDL cholesterol and supports gut health
  • Denser, higher-fiber bread is more filling and less likely to be overeaten

Why Choose Whole Wheat Over White Bread?

  1. Spermidine: Whole wheat bread offers a significant advantage in spermidine content compared to white bread. This naturally occurring polyamine has been linked to various health benefits including increased longevity, reduced arterial aging, improved memory. Supplementing Spermidine to mice increased lifespan in some studies.
  2. Fiber: The higher fiber content of wheat bread promotes a healthier gut and cardiovascular system. Fiber aids detoxification by supporting regular bowel movements and can actively lower LDL cholesterol levels, reducing the risk of heart disease.
  3. Less appetizing and less conducive to overeating: Due to its denser texture and higher fiber content, wheat bread is more filling and less likely to be overeaten than its refined counterpart. This can be an advantage for those aiming to manage weight or simply make healthier food choices.

References:

  • Eisenberg, T., et al. (2009). "Induction of autophagy by spermidine promotes longevity." Nature Cell Biology, 11(11), 1305-1314. This study illustrates how spermidine can trigger autophagy, a process important for cell renewal and longevity.
  • Madeo, F., et al. (2018). "Spermidine in health and disease." Science, 359(6374). This comprehensive review discusses spermidine’s role in promoting longevity across various species, providing a detailed look at its mechanisms and potential.
  • LaRocca, T.J., et al. (2013). "The autophagy enhancer spermidine reverses arterial aging." Mechanisms of Ageing and Development, 134(7-8), 314-320. This study highlights spermidine’s benefits in reversing age-related arterial deterioration and improving cardiovascular health.
  • Sigrist, S.J., et al. (2014). "Spermidine-triggered autophagy ameliorates memory during aging." Autophagy, 10(1), 178-179. This research indicates spermidine's potential to mitigate memory loss associated with aging through autophagy.
