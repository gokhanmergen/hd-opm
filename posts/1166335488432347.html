Why is too much insulin bad?

Insulin is a growth promoter that directly drives aging and disease.

For example, excess insulin directly promotes cancer growth.

Excess insulin is involved in the pathogenesis of heart disease.

It also drives pathological growth pathways like IGF1 and mTOR that directly accelerate aging.

Interventions that lower insulin/IGF1 consistently extend lifespan in animals, including mammals like rodents. The biotech company Loyal seems to have proven that it works for dogs, as well. And the genetic disease Laron Syndrome proves that it works for humans, at least in terms of preventing cancer, diabetes, and Alzheimer’s.

This is all independent of glucose.

You can read more about these topics by searching the group for the tags:
#igf1HD
#laronsyndromeHD
#mtorHD.

The best way to keep insulin low is to strictly avoid eating refined starch/sugar, and to avoid consuming excess amounts of high-quality animal protein.

The only macronutrient that does not significantly raise insulin is FAT.

This is one reason why including healthy fats in your diet like olive oil, nuts, seeds, etc. can be beneficial— especially if you substitute them in place of unhealthy insulinogenic foods like refined starch/sugar and processed meat.

Fasting also keeps insulin very low, by definition. If you don’t eat anything, you will automatically have low insulin.

Restricting calories lowers insulin. When you eat less, you need less total insulin to process your food.
