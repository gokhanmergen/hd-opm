Red meat sugar Neu5Gc linked to 63% higher type 2 diabetes risk in 89,953 French adults

Louis Nervegna · Oct 27, 2026 · ♥ 12 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Preliminary results presented at EASD 2026 followed 89,953 NutriNet-Santé participants for about 7 years. Those eating the most Neu5Gc, a sugar found in red meat that humans do not make, had a 63% higher risk of type 2 diabetes, holding after adjusting for exercise, diet quality and smoking. Louis suggests this may partly explain why cohorts like the Nurses' Health Study link red meat to diabetes; the study is observational and not peer reviewed.

  • 89,953 adults, about 7 years of follow-up
  • 63% higher T2D risk in highest vs lowest Neu5Gc intake
  • Observational, preliminary, not yet peer reviewed
  • Neu5Gc is found mostly in beef and lamb

Neu5Gc is a possible mechanism that might partially explain why prospective cohort studies (like the Harvard Nurses Study) link high red meat consumption with increased risk for diabetes.

Summary of the study from Gemeni AI:

A specific sugar molecule found in red meat called Neu5Gc is linked to a 63% higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

The preliminary findings, presented at the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) Annual Meeting in Milan, analyzed data from 89,953 French adults enrolled in the long-term NutriNet-Santé study managed by the Université Sorbonne Paris Nord.

Key Findings & Study Details

  • The Culprit: N-glycolylneuraminic acid (Neu5Gc) is a non-human sugar molecule naturally abundant in red meat like beef and lamb. Humans do not naturally synthesize it.
  • The Biological Mechanism: When humans consume red meat, Neu5Gc is absorbed into body tissues. The immune system recognizes it as foreign, triggering low-grade chronic inflammation, which researchers believe damages insulin sensitivity over time.
  • The Risk Breakdown: The study tracked cancer- and diabetes-free participants over a median of 7.2 years. Those in the highest fifth of meat-derived Neu5Gc intake faced a 63% increased relative risk of developing type 2 diabetes compared to the lowest fifth.
  • Dairy Disconnect: Interestingly, while Neu5Gc is also found in smaller amounts in dairy products, the study identified no significant association between dairy-derived Neu5Gc and type 2 diabetes risk.

Critical Limitations to Consider

As highlighted by coverage on ScienceDaily and Medical News Today, the data remains preliminary. Because this is an observational cohort, it establishes a correlation rather than direct causation. Crucially, when researchers adjusted the model to account for overall total red meat consumption, the specific statistical link to Neu5Gc weakened, meaning it is still unclear if Neu5Gc acts independently or alongside other compounds in red meat.

Yes, this is real.

Neu5Gc is a sugar found in red meat, mostly beef and lamb. Most mammals make it in their own bodies. Humans lost the gene to make it a very long time ago.

When people eat it, the body takes it into its own tissues. The immune system sees it as foreign and makes antibodies against it. Scientists think this may cause long-term inflammation.

Researchers in France followed 89,953 adults from the NutriNet-Santé study for about 7 years. People who ate the most Neu5Gc from meat had a 63% higher risk of type 2 diabetes than people who ate the least. This held up even after the researchers took exercise, diet quality, and smoking into account. In the top group, men ate about 97 grams of red meat a day and women about 73 grams. The results were shared at the EASD diabetes meeting in Milan, Italy, which ran Sept. 28 to Oct. 2, 2026.

To be fair: This was an observational study. It shows a link, not proof that Neu5Gc causes diabetes. The results are early and have not been peer reviewed yet. When the researchers also accounted for how much red meat people ate in total, the link was no longer statistically significant. A 63% higher risk does not mean 63% of heavy meat eaters will get diabetes. Neu5Gc from dairy showed no link at all.

Swapping beef and lamb for beans, lentils, or poultry on some days is one simple way people try to eat less of it.
