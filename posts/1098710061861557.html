Good cardiovascular health in young adulthood lowers later risk of CVD and kidney disease

Louis Nervegna · Aug 26, 2025 · ♥ 3 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Kidney healthCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Louis stresses that kidney and cardiovascular health are tightly linked: kidney disease raises CVD risk and protecting against atherosclerosis lowers the risk of kidney disease. The shared American Journal of Kidney Diseases study (Lee et al) found young adults with better cardiovascular health had lower later risk of both conditions, and improving or maintaining it helped.

  • Better cardiovascular health in young adulthood linked to lower later CVD and kidney disease risk
  • Improving cardiovascular health over time reduced risk; maintaining good health from the start was even better

The health of the kidneys and the cardiovascular system are intimately connected.

Kidney disease is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease.

But the reverse is true as well— protecting against atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease lowers the risk of ultimately developing kidney disease.

Shared from American Journal of Kidney Diseases · original post

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) and kidney disease are closely connected, influencing each other from an early age. Lee et al explored whether achieving and maintaining good cardiovascular health (CVH) in young adulthood was associated with the prevention of future CVD and kidney disease. Their findings showed that young adults with better CVH had a lower risk of developing these conditions later in life. Improving CVH over time led to reduced risk, and maintaining good CVH from the start was even more beneficial. These results highlight the importance of achieving and sustaining good CVH early in life to help prevent CVD and kidney disease over time.
