Adding CAC to the PREVENT risk calculator only modestly improves accuracy

Louis Nervegna · Aug 26, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that adding CAC score to the AHA PREVENT-ASCVD equations improves prediction only modestly in US adults 45-79, suggesting standard risk factors already capture much of what CAC reflects. He notes that a very high CAC should still be treated as high risk.

  • Applies to people with typical CAC for their age
  • CAC adds little beyond PREVENT-ASCVD for the average person
  • Very high CAC should be treated as high risk regardless of calculator

Adding CAC score to the standard AHA risk calculator (the PREVENT equations) only modestly improves accuracy.

↪ AHA PREVENT calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equations for ages 30-79

For the average person, the existing AHA calculator without CAC is almost as good at predicting risk of major cardiac events like heart attacks and strokes, as when CAC score is also included. The CAC score adds very little.

This hints that CAC score is not a major independent risk factor from the standard risk factors already included in the AHA calculator. It hints that the standard risk factors already do a pretty good job of implicitly accounting for a person’s CAC score without having to explicitly enter it as an additional parameter.

Note that all of this analysis applies to the average person (with a CAC score in the ballpark of what is typical for their age). Everybody is different. Once you know that you have a very high CAC score, you have to assume that you are at high risk, regardless of what the AHA calculator tells you.

Link to journal paper:

Paper · JAMAJAMA articlejamanetwork.com

Quoted:

“Among US adults aged 45 to 79 years, addition of CAC score to the PREVENT-ASCVD equations across all risk groups overall only modestly improved and, in some groups, did not change model discrimination and reclassification. Reclassification results support selective use of CAC in those with borderline to intermediate risk estimated by the PREVENT-ASCVD equations.”

AHA PREVENT calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equations for ages 30-79

Jan 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 21 ↩ 12 Risk assessment & lab tests

The AHA updated its cardiovascular risk calculator, PREVENT, for adults aged 30-79 without known CVD. It uses newer, larger and more diverse data than the 2013 Pooled Cohort Equations and includes kidney and metabolic disease, giving 10- and 30-year risk of both ASCVD and heart failure. Louis warns it will underestimate risk in people with prior heart attack/stroke, significant atherosclerosis or a high calcium score.
