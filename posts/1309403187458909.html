If you are interested in the topics of mTOR and IGF1 and how they relate to longevity, it is worthwhile to learn something about Laron Syndrome. This rare human disease is caused by a genetic mutation that prevents the liver from making IGF1. As a result, people with Laron Syndrome naturally have low IGF1 and mTOR for their entire lives.

Cancer and diabetes are almost unheard of in people with Laron Syndrome, and they have a large reduction in risk for Alzheimer’s.

In particular, they have a low risk for developing CVD:

Paper · Nature · 2024Could a rare mutation that causes dwarfism also slow ageing?nature.com

Even though they live a relatively normal lifespan, there has never been a documented case of a person with Laron Syndrome dying of cancer (the one case mentioned in this article has been disputed). Their low IGF1 and mTOR appears to make them almost completely immune to terminal cancer.
