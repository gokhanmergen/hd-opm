New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Gokhan Can · Aug 31, 2026 · ♥ 23 reactions · 38 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.

  • K2 slowed rate of arterial calcification
  • Total plaque buildup was not reduced; plaque became less calcified
  • No clinical evidence K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes

In this recent video, Dr. Gil Carvalho analyzes a new randomized trial challenging the popular belief that Vitamin K2 supplements improve heart health by keeping calcium out of arteries. While the study found that K2 slowed the rate of arterial calcification, it did not reduce the total amount of plaque buildup; instead, the plaque merely became less calcified. Dr. Carvalho explains that arterial calcium actually acts as a stabilizing "scar" that prevents plaque from rupturing, meaning that slowing calcification without reducing the underlying plaque itself could potentially be harmful. Because there is currently no clinical evidence that Vitamin K2 reduces actual cardiovascular events like heart attacks or strokes, he advises against taking it for heart disease prevention and recommends relying on proven medical therapies, diet, and exercise instead.

Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.

A shared post on a new 2-year randomized trial notes that MK-7 reduced progression of coronary calcification versus placebo. It cautions that calcified plaque is generally more stable, soft plaque was not measured, and the trial was not designed to show fewer cardiovascular events.
