Someone asked me an interesting question:

Why does triglycerides below 400 mg/dL not matter for CVD risk, if ApoB and Lp(a) are already accounted for?

————-

(Apparently, they heard this in a recent Peter Attia video.)

This is technically true. Studies have shown this. But stating it this way is very misleading.

The answer is the following:

Triglycerides absolutely do still matter for CVD risk below 400 mg/dL.

However, triglycerides are already counted in ApoB.

Most triglyceride is carried by VLDL which is an ApoB tagged particle.

The triglycerides are atherogenic and do matter when carried by VLDL. But you are already accounting for high triglycerides when you measure ApoB.

So it's not that triglycerides don't matter below 400mg/dL— but rather that they are already accounted for in ApoB.

You are double counting if you also measure triglycerides separately and then try to factor that into the risk calculation in addition to ApoB. You need to use one or the other to estimate risk— not both at the same time.
