Sumo wrestlers stay metabolically healthy because heavy exercise shifts fat under the skin

Louis Nervegna · Mar 09, 2025 · ♥ 21 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatExercise & fitnessInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains why sumo wrestlers, despite huge calorie and refined-carbohydrate intake, store little fat in the liver and pancreas and little visceral fat. He attributes this to up to 7 hours of daily exercise, which releases adiponectin and steers fat storage to subcutaneous tissue, a reason exercise protects against metabolic syndrome and diabetes.

  • Sumo diets reported up to 10,000 calories a day
  • Exercise up to 7 hours per day
  • Adiponectin drives fat storage away from internal organs toward the skin
  • Obesity-related disease typically appears after retirement from training

Sumo wrestlers eat diets very high in total calories and very high in refined carbohydrate (in the form of white rice) to fatten themselves up.

They are morbidly obese, but they typically do not suffer from diseases related to obesity (until after they retire and stop training).

We now know why:

This has been studied.

It turns out that they store very little fat in their liver and pancreas— the root cause of insulin resistance and beta cell disfunction. In fact, they don’t have much visceral fat, in general.

↪ Roy Taylor trials: 15 kg weight loss cleared liver and pancreas fat, reversing diabetes

Instead, they store most of their fat subcutaneously underneath the skin.

Why?

Because they exercise up to 7 hours per day.

All this exercise releases the hormone adiponectin, which drives fat storage away from the internal organs and towards the skin.

This is a major reason why exercise protects against metabolic syndrome and diabetes— even in people who overeat.
