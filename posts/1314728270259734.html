JAMA Cardiology trial: vitamin K2 (MK-7) for 2 years slowed coronary calcium progression

Gokhan Can · Jun 10, 2026 · ♥ 37 reactions · 35 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Admin says there is now solid evidence K2 slows coronary artery calcification, especially in people with existing calcification. In the new JAMA Cardiology RCT, MK-7 for two years reduced CAC progression versus placebo in symptomatic CAD, mainly by limiting new calcification in noncalcified plaques; effect on events is still unknown, and K2 is not recommended with warfarin.

  • 2-year MK-7 supplementation vs placebo reduced CAC progression
  • No effect on stenosis severity or total plaque volume
  • No relevant adverse effects
  • Effect on cardiovascular events unknown
  • Avoid K2 on warfarin without checking with a doctor

There's now solid evidence that Vitamin K2 slows down coronary artery calcification in various patient groups, most notably in those with existing calcification.

We still don't know how/if this affects cardiovascular events. (Supplementing with K2 is not recommended if you're on Warfarin -- always check with your Dr).

  • MK-7 coronary artery calcification trial · RCT · JAMA Cardiology · 2026
Shared from JAMA Cardiology · original post

In a randomized clinical trial, supplementation with #Menaquinone7 (MK-7) for 2 years produced a statistically significant reduction in the progression of #CoronaryArteryCalcification (CAC) compared to placebo in patients with symptomatic coronary artery disease.

The difference was most evident in the development of new calcifications within noncalcified plaques, although MK-7 had no impact on stenosis severity or total plaque volume. The trial found no relevant adverse effects, but the clinical significance for cardiovascular events remains to be determined.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Aug 31, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 38 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.

Vitamin C has not reduced heart attacks or strokes and plaque data are mixed

Apr 12, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Admin summarises that vitamin C alone has been tested widely without reducing hard outcomes (MACE), and plaque-reversal data are mixed: supportive in animals, better/worse/no effect in human trials. He does not advise it as a heart-disease cure, though physiological doses appear safe; he takes 250 mg/day to avoid deficiency. Links an (largely AI-generated, author-vetted) summary document.

Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.
