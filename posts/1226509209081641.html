Low-carb diets are no better for fat loss than other diets when calories are matched

Louis Nervegna · Feb 16, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that controlled metabolic ward studies by Kevin Hall found equal fat loss on low-carb and normal-carb diets with equal calories; low-carb works mainly by controlling appetite. James Krieger recounts changing his view.

  • Metabolic ward studies by Kevin Hall (PMID 27385608, 28193517)
  • Equal calories and protein: same fat loss
  • Satiety may explain real-world success

There’s no question that low carb diets work for weight loss. Many studies have proven that.

But here’s the truth:

When comparing low carb diets to normal carb diets where calories are kept strictly the same in both groups, they don’t work any better. Multiple studies have shown that, particularly the carefully controlled metabolic ward studies done by Kevin Hall.

It’s the calories that ultimately matter.

Some people find that eating low carb keeps them satiated for longer. It curbs their appetite. This makes the diet easier to stick with and controls the calories. This is probably the main reason that they work.

The most important thing is to find a diet that works for you, whether that is low carb or not.

Low Carb Diets are NOT Superior for Fat Loss

In this clip, @james.krieger talks about how he once believed low-carb diets had a true metabolic advantage for fat loss. He even published a meta-analysis in the early 2000s suggesting they did (PMID: 16469983).

But then metabolic ward studies, especially the tightly controlled work by Kevin Hall, showed something different (PMID: 27385608 & 28193517).

When calories and protein are equated under controlled conditions:

➡️ Fat loss is the same
➡️ Carbs and fats are largely interchangeable energy substrates
➡️ No inherent metabolic “edge” to low carb

The earlier advantage likely came from adherence differences. If one group sticks to the diet better, but self-reports equal calories, it can look like a metabolic effect when it’s really compliance.

This is why metabolic ward studies matter. When food intake is tightly controlled, the “magic” disappears.

Low carb can work.
High carb can work.
Keto can work.

But fat loss comes down to energy balance and protein sufficiency, not carb elimination. While low carb diets may produce better adherence short term, the research shows in the long term adherence isn’t different (PMID: 25514303). Therefore, pick the diet that is easiest for YOU to adhere to

Huge respect to James for being open about evolving his position with better data. That’s how science should work. That’s why James is the editor of REPS, my research review because I know he is only slave to the data, not ideals
