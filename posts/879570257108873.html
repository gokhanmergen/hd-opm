Higher LDL linked to undereye bags and full cheeks in faster facial aging

Louis Nervegna · Sep 28, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityLDL, ApoB & lipid panelInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a National Geographic piece on 3D facial imaging used to estimate biological age. Higher LDL and total cholesterol tracked with undereye bags and full cheeks, while inflammation and infection tracked with a narrowing forehead and sagging skin. Smoking, snoring and high cholesterol marked fast-agers, whereas yogurt, regular mealtimes and denser bones marked slow-agers.

  • High LDL/total cholesterol: full cheeks and undereye pouches
  • Inflammation: narrowing forehead and sagging skin
  • Fast-agers: smoking, snoring, high cholesterol; slow-agers: yogurt, regular mealtimes, higher bone density

People with higher LDL seem to experience faster aging of the face— which may be a marker for faster aging overall.

Higher LDL seems to be particularly associated with undereye bags and swelling in the cheeks.

Higher inflammation levels (another risk factor for CVD) are also associated with faster facial aging, and seem to manifest in a narrowing of the forehead as the skin in that area sags.

(See comments for a link that bypasses the paywall.)

Quoted:

“A picture is worth a thousand words. But when that picture is a high-precision 3D image of the face, it might be worth a thousand blood tests as well. That’s because those full cheeks and under-eye bags are not just unsightly harbingers of age, but also a reflection of our health.”

“To explore the links between appearance, age, and the underlying factors that influence health—Han collected blood samples and surveyed the habits of the research subjects. For example, smoking, snoring, and high levels of total cholesterol in the blood were often characteristics of fast-agers whereas yogurt consumption, regular mealtimes, and greater bone mineral density were a common trait of slow-agers.”

“For all clocks, “accelerated aging is highly associated with infections and inflammation,” which manifests in the face as a narrowing of the forehead as the skin sags, says Han. On the other hand, high LDL and total cholesterol levels in the blood translates to full cheeks and undereye pouches.”
