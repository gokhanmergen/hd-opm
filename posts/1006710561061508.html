Budoff recommends statins, aspirin and BP control for keto dieters with CT-detected plaque

Louis Nervegna · Apr 14, 2025 · ♥ 14 reactions · 15 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivorePlaque, CAC & CT angiographyStatins
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites a video in which Keto-CTA principal investigator Matthew Budoff recommends statins, low-dose aspirin and tight blood pressure control for patients on keto diets with significant plaque on CT angiography, and CT scans for his ketogenic patients. He says the statin is to treat plaque, since statins slow plaque progression even at low LDL.

  • Recommends statin, low-dose aspirin and tight BP control for keto dieters with significant plaque
  • Recommends CT angiogram for all his keto-diet patients
  • Statin aimed at treating plaque rather than keto-induced LDL

In light of the recently published LMHR study, Mathew Budoff (the principle investigator on the study) recommends that all patients on ketogenic diets showing significant plaque on CT-angiogram should be on statins, low dose aspirin, and tight blood pressure control.

He recommends CT-angiogram scans for all his patients on the ketogenic diet to properly ascertain risk.

Quoted:

“And I do recommend a statin for those patients. Not to treat the keto induced LDL, but to treat the underlying plaque that they have. Because we know that we can slow down plaque progression— regardless of the cause— with a statin. Statins work even in low LDL’s to lower plaque burden. We’ve studied that extensively.”

Repatha alone reversed soft plaque in 37 of 47 patients over 18 months at LDL near 30

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 1 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Louis explains a small uncontrolled study of the PCSK9 inhibitor evolocumab with CT angiography. Average LDL was about 30 mg/dL, 37 of 47 patients had some non-calcified plaque regression, and many had large reversals worth years of typical growth (about 5 mm3 per year). He cautions it was small with no randomization or control group, and that PCSK9 monotherapy is not yet proven to reduce heart attacks.
