Dr Brewer's Story

Dr. Ford Brewer describes his personal experience with discovering plaque buildup in his arteries, despite living what he believed was a healthy lifestyle. He walks through the science behind how plaque forms and leads to heart attacks. Ultimately, he made some medication and lifestyle changes that helped reverse his arterial plaque over the course of about 2 years.

Key points:

  • Plaque forms when LDL cholesterol gets stuck between the intima and media layers of artery walls, which can happen when arteries are inflamed. Inflammation causes the intima layer to lose its ability to allow LDL to pass through into tissues.
  • As plaque builds up over time, it can rupture, releasing liquid plaque contents into the bloodstream. This hot liquid plaque causes clotting when it touches blood, leading to heart attacks in over 90% of cases.
  • Despite living healthy with good diet and exercise habits, Dr. Brewer discovered at age 57 that he had the artery age of a 73 year old due to substantial plaque buildup seen on a CIMT heart scan.
  • Contributing factors were high blood pressure, insulin resistance/prediabetes, and genetics (9p21 and 4q25 genes). He likely had a genetic predisposition for heart disease and diabetes.
  • Over 2 years after discovering his plaque, Dr. Brewer dropped his arterial age by over 20 years through medication and lifestyle changes:Started taking crestor (rosuvastatin), a statin drug, at 5mg dose

Added omega-3 fatty acids from salmon
Added daily niacin supplement
Switched blood pressure med from ARB to ACE inhibitor ramipril
Lost 10 lbs through dietary changes focusing on reducing carbs/insulin resistance

  • He emphasizes that lifestyle should be the priority in prevention, but medication can also help in cases like his where there were unknown risk factors like insulin resistance contributing to plaque formation.

The second transcript summarizes a follow-up video in which Dr. Brewer reflects on the surprise of discovering significant arterial plaque at only age 57. He emphasizes how assessing and addressing inflammation and insulin resistance are key in prevention.

Drugs, supplements and other interventions that Dr Brewer used to reverse his artery disease

The key interventions Dr. Brewer used were:

  • Crestor (rosuvastatin) - a statin drug, taken at 5mg dose
  • Omega-3 fatty acids - from eating salmon
  • Niacin - vitamin B3 supplement
  • Ramipril - an ACE inhibitor blood pressure medication
  • Dietary changes - reduced carbs to address insulin resistance
  • Weight loss - lost 10 lbs

He also emphasizes the importance of assessing and addressing inflammation and insulin resistance, including through lab testing that many doctors do not order. Controlling glucose levels through diet and exercise is key.

New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Aug 31, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 38 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.
