There is an extensive body of sports science literature that says 1g/lb (2.2g/kg) of protein per day is a waste— even for bodybuilders— and certainly for normal people.

There is a consensus in academic sports science that muscle protein synthesis maxes out at 1.6g/kg in natural bodybuilders (not taking anabolic drugs).

To gain the muscle-building benefit of extra protein intake above the RDA, you must combine it with resistance training. Dozens of randomized trials have shown that protein consumption by itself does not add strength or muscle unless combined with resistance training. Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 6 ↩ 11 Protein

A dose-response meta-analysis of 69 randomized trials found protein alone does not increase muscle strength without resistance training. With training, strength rose 0.72% per extra 0.1 g/kg/day up to 1.5 g/kg/day, with no further gain; going from the RDA to 1.5 g/kg adds only about 5% more strength.
