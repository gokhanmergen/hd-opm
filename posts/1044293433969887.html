Meta-analysis: vitamin C adds no clinically relevant change to iron levels with oral iron

Louis Nervegna · Jun 10, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a meta-analysis of randomized trials finding that vitamin C taken with oral iron did not produce clinically relevant changes in hemoglobin or ferritin in iron-deficient patients. He notes excess iron may contribute to CVD and aging, and added an April 2026 note about a monkey study suggesting vitamin C protects against iron's pro-aging effects.

  • No clinically relevant change in hemoglobin or ferritin with vitamin C plus oral iron
  • Excess iron is linked to disease and aging (Louis)
  • April 2026 edit: monkey study suggests vitamin C protects against iron's pro-aging effects

This is a very interesting meta-analysis.

Excess iron is unhealthy, and may contribute to the development of several diseases, including CVD— as well as to the aging process itself.

It is commonly believed that vitamin C increases iron levels, when consumed along with iron-containing foods or supplements. This meta-analysis of randomized trials suggests that vitamin C has no clinically relevant effect on iron levels.

Edit (April 2026) - A newly published study suggests vitamin C actually protects against the pro-aging effects of iron in monkeys: ↪ Monkey study links iron buildup ('ferro-aging') to organ decline; vitamin C may protect.

Among AHS-2 participants (about 88,000, over 12,000 deaths), vegans aged 85 and older had higher risk of death from stroke (17%), dementia (13%) and Parkinson's (37%) than healthy non-vegetarians, while pesco-vegetarians had the lowest risk.
