Meta-analysis of 30 trials: olive oil lowers CRP and IL-6 and improves FMD

Louis Nervegna · Mar 14, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that over 30 human RCTs show olive oil improves flow-mediated dilation and endothelial function, quoting a systematic review of 30 studies and 3,106 participants. Olive oil lowered CRP by 0.64 mg/L and IL-6 by 0.29 and raised FMD by 0.76 percentage points versus controls. Refined olive oil may briefly impair FMD after a meal, while EVOO improves it acutely and over the long term.

  • 30 studies, 3,106 participants
  • CRP: mean difference -0.64 mg/L (95% CI -0.96 to -0.31; 15 trials)
  • IL-6: mean difference -0.29 (95% CI -0.7 to -0.02; 7 trials)
  • FMD: +0.76% absolute (95% CI 0.27 to 1.24; 8 trials)

Multiple randomized controlled trials in humans have shown that olive oil improves flow mediated dilation (FMD) and endothelial function (EF).

There are over 30 randomized controlled trials in humans which have demonstrated this in aggregate.

Some studies do show a very short term degradation in FMD immediately after consuming refined olive oil (similar to what happens after exercise), but FMD and endothelial function improve in the longer term (just like with exercise):

Quoted:

“The aim of the present systematic review was to synthesize data from randomized controlled trials investigating the effects of olive oil on markers of inflammation or endothelial function.”

“Thirty studies enrolling 3106 participants fulfilled the selection criteria.”

“Olive oil interventions (with daily consumption ranging approximately between 1 mg and 50 mg) resulted in a significantly more pronounced decrease in C-reactive protein (mean difference: −0.64 mg/L, (95% confidence interval (CI) −0.96 to −0.31), p < 0.0001, n = 15 trials) and interleukin-6 (mean difference: −0.29 (95% CI −0.7 to −0.02), p < 0.04, n = 7 trials) as compared to controls, respectively.”

“Values of flow-mediated dilatation (given as absolute percentage) were significantly more increased in individuals subjected to olive oil interventions (mean difference: 0.76% (95% CI 0.27 to 1.24), p < 0.002, n = 8 trials).”

“These results provide evidence that olive oil might exert beneficial effects on endothelial function as well as markers of inflammation and endothelial function, thus representing a key ingredient contributing to the cardiovascular-protective effects of a Mediterranean diet.”
