Reducing B-cell insulin signaling extended mouse lifespan about 36%

Louis Nervegna · Feb 08, 2026 · ♥ 10 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis cites a Science Immunology paper (Khan et al. 2026) showing that with aging B cells become insulin-responsive and drive chronic T cell overstimulation. He argues avoiding insulin resistance and high insulin is key to lowering risk for age-related diseases like CVD. Mouse data.

  • Reducing insulin signaling in B cells raised median lifespan about 36% in mice
  • Maximum lifespan exceeded 3 years in mice
  • Frailty, fibrosis and metabolic dysfunction reduced

Yet another study shows that the pathological growth-promoting effects of insulin directly drives aging and age-related diseases.

Avoiding insulin resistance (and the high levels of insulin that it causes) is one of the most important things you can do to lower the risk for age-related diseases like CVD.

This new Science Immunology paper shows something even more important:

👉 insulin is a major driver of immune aging.

PaperScience articlescience.org

What the study shows

Khan et al. (2026) demonstrate that with aging, B cells become insulin-responsive, “age-like” immune cells.

Through insulin receptor signaling (InsR → AKT/mTOR), B cells:
acquire a pro-inflammatory, age-associated phenotype:

  • increase MHCII expression
  • chronically overstimulate CD4 T cells

The result:

  • loss of naïve CD4 T cells
  • expansion of exhausted / PD-1⁺ T cells
  • reduced TCR diversity
  • tissue inflammation and fibrosis.

👉 The immune system itself ages, increasing vulnerability to infections and chronic disease.

The striking result

When insulin signaling in B cells is reduced — without removing B cells:

  • median lifespan increases by ~36%
  • maximum lifespan exceeds 3 years in mice
  • frailty, fibrosis, and metabolic dysfunction are markedly reduced.

The mechanism
Insulin turns B cells into metabolic sensors that translate chronic hyperinsulinemia into:
➡️ chronic antigen presentation
➡️ CD4 T-cell immunosenescence
➡️ systemic aging

The broader message
Visceral obesity → inflammation → insulin resistance → compensatory hyperinsulinemia

And insulin becomes a primary driver of aging, including immune aging.

The intervention is upstream

Lower insulin:
➡️ moderate caloric restriction with high fiber whole plant based diet
➡️ daily physical activity (resistance and endurance)
➡️ reduction of visceral fat
