TARGET-D: tailored vitamin D dosing missed its main endpoint but cut heart attack 52%

Gokhan Can · Nov 10, 2025 · ♥ 25 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Summarises TARGET-D (630 adults with acute coronary syndrome, ~4.2 years): doses adjusted to reach 40-80 ng/mL did not significantly reduce the composite of death, heart failure hospitalization or stroke, though heart attack risk was 52% lower. Authors call for validation to rule out data mining.

  • 630 adults, mean age 63, 78% male, ~4.2 years
  • 85%+ had baseline vitamin D <40 ng/mL; target 40-80 ng/mL
  • ~52% of treated needed >5,000 IU/day
  • Primary composite not significantly reduced; heart attack 52% lower
  • Needs validation, risk of data mining

The TARGET-D randomized clinical trial followed 630 adults (average age 63, 78% male) with acute coronary syndrome for an average of 4.2 years. Over 85% of participants began the study with vitamin D levels below 40 ng/mL. Participants were randomized into either a standard care group or a treatment group that received personalized vitamin D doses, which were adjusted every three months to achieve a target blood level of 40-80 ng/mL; notably, nearly 52% of this group required over 5,000 IU daily to reach this target. While this tailored approach did not significantly reduce the primary composite outcome of death, heart failure hospitalization, or stroke (MACE), it was associated with a 52% lower risk of heart attack. These preliminary findings require validation in a follow up cohort to eliminate the risk of data mining.

Selenium plus CoQ10 trial: over 50% lower CV death, benefit persisted 10-12 years

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 12 Supplements & vitamins

Louis reshares a post quoting Life Extension on a trial where older people took selenium plus coenzyme Q10 for four years and had more than half the cardiovascular mortality. Follow-up showed 49% lower CV death at 10 years and 41% at 12 years, including in subgroups with heart disease, diabetes and hypertension. He suggests adding about 200 ug selenium to CoQ10. The source is a supplement-company magazine and the trial is not named.
