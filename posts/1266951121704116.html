A quoted anecdotal post about a single patient and Bredesen's protocol with no controlled evidence and little relation to heart health.

Quoted from the original post by Dale Bredesen:

Anniversary of Hope, Anniversary of Progress

Fourteen years ago today, a woman named Judy came to see me at the Buck Institute for Research on Aging. She had flown out from the East Coast because she had been diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease, told that nothing could be done, and was planning to commit suicide. Her close friend Anita was aware of our lab’s research, and asked if I’d meet with Judy. I told her that we had discovered how to make mice with Mouzheimer’s better, but I had not seen a human patient in 20 years, so I was hesitant, but I agreed to talk with her about the proposed clinical trial we had just had turned down. Judy and I talked for nearly three hours about our research on the nature of Alzheimer’s and our finding that it is a network insufficiency and thus best treated with a comprehensive, precision medicine approach that identifies and addresses all of the contributors (which is what became ReCODE)—something that was completely foreign to the field at the time.

I thought I would never hear from Judy again, but three months later she called to say that her memory was better than it had been in 20 years and she was able to carry out her job duties once again. She then became a yoga instructor and brain health coach, then walked the 2700 miles across the US (at 81!!) to raise awareness that cognitive decline can indeed be reversed, founded the Alzheimer’s Survivors Foundation (their motto, coined by Lucy, one of their members: “We exist.”), and now, still sharp after 14 years, she is planning a major walk in England for this summer.

As Patient Zero, Judy’s diligence, commitment, and perseverance have offered hope to the millions in need. In a world that is plagued by wars on numerous fronts, the one against Alzheimer’s is getting closer and closer to victory.
