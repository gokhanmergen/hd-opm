Constipation linked to higher risk of major adverse cardiac events in a Monash-led study

Louis Nervegna · Aug 22, 2024 · ♥ 9 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post
Gut microbiomeCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

A news item on an international Monash University study connecting constipation with higher risk of MACE (heart attack, stroke, heart failure). Constipation affects about 14% of people, so the authors argue gut health deserves attention in cardiovascular prevention.

  • Constipation affects an estimated 14% of the global population
  • Outcome: MACE including heart attacks, strokes and heart failure

Add constipation to the long list of possible risk factors for CVD.

Quoted:

“With constipation affecting an estimated 14% of the global population, particularly older adults and women, these findings suggest that a significant portion of the population may be at an increased risk of cardiovascular disease due to their bowel health," she said.”

“This research challenges the traditional understanding of cardiovascular risk factors and underscores the importance of considering gut health in the prevention and management of heart disease.

As the global burden of cardiovascular disease continues to rise, these findings could pave the way for new strategies in personalized medicine, helping to identify individuals at higher risk and potentially reducing the incidence of life-threatening cardiac events.”
