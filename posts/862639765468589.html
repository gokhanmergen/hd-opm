UChicago fatty liver advice: 3 cups coffee, 4 tablespoons olive oil, Mediterranean diet

Louis Nervegna · Sep 01, 2024 · ♥ 7 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares UChicago Medicine dietitian advice for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: three cups of coffee a day, four tablespoons of olive oil a day and a Mediterranean diet. He reminds the group that liver fat is the main cause of insulin resistance for most people. It is clinic guidance, not a trial.

  • 3 cups coffee and 4 tablespoons olive oil daily recommended for NAFLD
  • Mediterranean pattern: plants, whole grains, nuts, fish, moderate dairy and poultry, little red meat and sweets

What foods help reverse fat in the liver?

(As discussed many times in the group, fat accumulation in the liver is the main cause of insulin resistance in most people.)

Quoted:

“They might surprise you. We recommend patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease drink three cups of coffee per day, eat four tablespoons of olive oil a day and follow a Mediterranean diet, which emphasizes eating primarily plant-based foods and healthy fats.

People usually can’t believe we’re asking them to add more food like coffee and olive oil to their diet, rather than restrict items or cut things out.”

“The Mediterranean diet is a well-known diet that gained popularity in the 1990s, known more as an eating pattern rather than a structured diet. It emphasizes eating fruits, veggies, whole grains, beans, nuts, legumes, olive oil, and flavorful herbs and spices; fish and seafood at least a couple of times a week; and poultry, eggs, cheese and yogurt in moderation, while saving sweets and red meat for special occasions.”

Swapping ghee for rapeseed (canola) oil cut liver fat and insulin resistance in an RCT

Sep 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 21 ↩ 1 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis highlights a 12-week randomized trial in 110 people with NAFLD who replaced ghee with the same amount of rapeseed (canola) oil. Liver steatosis, liver enzymes, lipids, glucose and insulin resistance improved versus controls, countering the idea that canola oil causes metabolic disease.
