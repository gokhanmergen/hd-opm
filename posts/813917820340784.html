Statin tendon problems are possible but uncommon; consider other causes and simvastatin

Louis Nervegna · Jun 03, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Statins
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares cardiologist Paul Thompson's answer on whether statins cause tendon rupture: it is theoretically possible but quite uncommon, and high cholesterol itself can cause tendinopathy (consider familial hypercholesterolemia in young athletes with Achilles tendinitis). Louis adds that if someone suspects a statin-related tendon issue, switching to simvastatin might be worth discussing since some evidence suggests it may protect tendons.

  • Statins reduce some MMP activity, which tendon repair requires, so rupture is theoretically possible
  • Hypercholesterolemia alone can cause tendinopathy; consider familial hypercholesterolemia in young athletes with Achilles tendinitis
  • Some evidence suggests simvastatin may protect tendons

Can statins cause tendon problems?

Here is an answer from Paul Thompson MD, who is emeritus chief of cardiology at Hartford Hospital, and a world recognized expert in cardiology care for athletes (sports cardiology).

As Thomson says in the article, tendon problems from statins are possible though quite uncommon.

If you do believe that statins might have caused you a tendon issue— before giving up— you might want to talk to your doctor about possibly switching to simvastatin. There is some evidence that simvastatin, in particular, might actually protect tendons. In the comments, I put a link to a review article that summarizes the evidence for a possible protective effect from simvastatin.

Quoted:

“Tendon rupture with statins is theoretically possible because matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) is required for tendon repair and statins reduce some MMP’s activity.

But before blaming the statin make sure to consider other possibilities. Hypercholesterolemia alone can cause tendinopathy. In fact, I always tell our sports folks that if they see Achilles tendinitis in a young athlete to consider familial hypercholesterolemia.”
