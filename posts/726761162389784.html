Statins cut deaths 31% in people 80+, showing absolute benefit depends on baseline risk

Apr 02, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 5 Statins

Quotes Dr Carvalho on a new analysis in people aged 80 or older: statin users had a 31% lower relative risk of dying and, as stated, a 17% lower absolute risk. The key principle is that absolute benefit depends on control-group risk, so it looks small in young people over the short term but grows with time, age or illness.

Even a cure for heart disease would show only 2-5% absolute risk reduction short-term

Nov 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 7 Statins

Louis explains that over a few years the absolute risk of a heart attack is only about 2-5%, so no intervention, even a complete cure, can cut absolute risk by more than that in a short trial. A 1-2% absolute reduction from statins or from diet and lifestyle trials is therefore a meaningful result.

Number needed to treat explained: short prevention trials understate long-term benefit

Oct 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 8 How to read research

Louis explains NNT (1 divided by absolute risk reduction) and why it looks poor in short prevention trials, where heart attack risk is only 2-3% over a few years. He argues that diet, exercise and statins taken for decades could cut lifetime risk from about 20% to 10%, an NNT of about 10, and that NNTs from short trials shouldn't mislead.

Lowering LDL about 80 mg/dL for 52 years could cut lifetime heart attack risk 20% to 4%

Sep 27, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 9 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Mendelian randomization studies suggest a lifelong LDL reduction of about 80 mg/dL lowers heart attack risk by about 80% in relative terms. Assuming a 20% lifetime absolute risk, as for the typical American, that drops to about 4%.

Statin absolute risk reductions of 1-5% over 5 years are large against lifetime risk

Jun 24, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 5 Statins

Reposting an explainer on relative versus absolute risk: statin trials show absolute risk reductions of 1.1% to 4.7% over about 5 years, which the poster argues is substantial against a roughly 20% lifetime heart attack risk. Over decades he suggests lifetime risk could fall toward 10%, an extrapolation.

Statins for all could halve heart attacks yet add only 10-20 days to life over 5 years

May 14, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Statins

Louis works through why universal statin use could cut lifetime heart attack and stroke risk from about 20% to 10% while extending average lifespan by only days over 5 years: only about 10% of heart attacks and strokes are fatal, and only 1 in 5 US deaths are from them, so the reduction in overall deaths is about 50 times smaller. His arithmetic suggests a 0.022% to 0.094% reduction in overall deaths over 5 years. Note that this is a population-averaged back-of-envelope calculation.
