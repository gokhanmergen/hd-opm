Nuts reduce plaque in the carotid artery.

This result is from a large randomized controlled trial (PREDIMED) run for multiple years.

This study compared a control diet (similar to AHA recommendations at the time), a Mediterranean diet + additional EVOO, and a Mediterranean diet + nuts.

The recommended dose of nuts in the intevention group was 30g per day (a small handful), consisting of 15g walnuts, 7.5g hazelnuts, 7.5g almonds.

Only the group with nuts saw regression of plaque in the carotid.

The reversal occurred after 2.4 years of daily nut consumption.

“We evaluated 164 subjects with complete data. In a multivariate model, mean ICA-IMT progressed in the control diet group (mean [95% confidence interval], 0.052 mm [−0.014 to 0.118 mm]), whereas it regressed in the MedDiet+nuts group (−0.084 mm [−0.158 to −0.010 mm]; P=0.024 versus control).”

This study demonstrated that the Mediterranean diet including nuts reduced MACE events by 30% compared with the control diet, which was the primary endpoint.

In particular, the group consuming nuts had a 50% reduced risk of having a stroke than the control group. This was consistent with the reversal of plaque seen in the carotid artery.

MACE (Major Adverse Cardiac Events) is a composite endpoint representing serious heart/vessel problems, typically including cardiovascular death, heart attack (MI), stroke, and unplanned revascularization (PCI/CABG) or heart failure hospitalization.

Because the results were so striking in such a short time, the PREDIMED trial was stopped early. The ethics committee felt that the control group should also be placed on the Mediterranean diet.
