Valter Longo says higher whole-food plant protein likely protects against heart disease

Louis Nervegna · May 18, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes that Longo, in a podcast, accepts the epidemiology showing whole-food plant protein protects the heart. The rationale is getting plant protein from legumes, nuts, seeds and whole grains without the excess of isoleucine, methionine and BCAAs found mainly in animal protein, which shorten lifespan in animal models.

  • Plant protein from legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains
  • Lower anabolic amino acids (isoleucine, methionine, BCAAs) than animal protein

Valter Longo acknowledges that higher intake of plant protein (from whole food) is likely protective against heart disease.

There’s been some very well-done epidemiology in the last 5-10 years demonstrating a protective effect of plant protein from whole food. This is the first time that I’ve heard Valter Longo accept it as true.

His rationale: You get the beneficial components of protein-rich plant foods (legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains), without the excess of anabolic amino acids like isoleucine, methionine, BCAA’s, etc. which shorten lifespan in animal models, and which are found primarily in higher-quality animal protein.

I think this is a very important admission.

Valter has been the chief proponent of protein restriction diets in humans.

He seems to be softening his position on this, and accepting some of the more recent evidence that higher intake of plant protein from whole food is protective in humans.

The study on the ratio of plant to animal protein that is referred to in the video was described in this post: ↪ Higher plant-to-animal protein ratio lowered CVD risk over 30 years in 203,000 people.
