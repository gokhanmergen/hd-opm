Greek yogurt matched whey protein for adolescent athletes' performance across a season

Louis Nervegna · Apr 13, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

A shared summary of a trial comparing isonitrogenous Greek yogurt and whey in adolescent athletes found no meaningful differences in performance, body composition, bone or inflammatory markers. Louis frames it as whole food equaling a supplement.

  • Two 175 g Greek yogurt servings daily, 17 g protein each
  • No differences in performance, body composition, bone, inflammation

Yogurt improves sports performance just as well as processed whey protein powder.

I like it... #greekYogurt and #whey, yes - But what I like even more is another whole food vs. supplement comparison that shows both have virtually identical benefits for #athletes

Study compared adolescent athletes who consumed isonitrogenous amounts of Greek yogurt or whey protein throughout an entire competitive season and found no meaningful differences in performance, body composition, bone, or inflammatory markers between groups.

🥛 Greek yogurt arm
Two daily servings of 175 g flavored Greek yogurt (Oikos), each providing 17 g casein-rich dairy protein, ~130 kcal, 0% fat, ~225 mg calcium, live cultures, and a full fermented dairy matrix.

🥤 Whey protein arm
Two daily servings of whey protein isolate powder (Iso Advanced) in water, each providing 17 g rapidly digested whey protein, ~70 kcal, 0% fat, ~76 mg calcium, and no yogurt cultures or fermented food matrix.

✅ Overall performance
No clear between-group differences in sport performance outcomes; both groups changed similarly over the season.

🦴 Bone outcomes
No statistically significant differences between Greek yogurt and whey groups in bone mineral content or density changes over the season.

🩸 Inflammation / immune markers
Group × time interactions for inflammatory or immune markers were not significant; patterns over time were broadly similar between groups.

💪 Body composition
Changes in fat-free mass and fat mass did not differ significantly between Greek yogurt and whey groups.

🥛 Calcium-related effects
Despite higher calcium from Greek yogurt, this did not translate into significantly different bone or body composition outcomes versus whey within the study period.

Ok, 🤷🏽 you COULD argue that's disappointing because the Greek Yogurt could not "monetize" on its theoretical superiority,

...but it is actually pretty encouraging for "team real food": in these adolescent athletes, a cheap, fermented dairy snack with more calcium and a richer bioactive profile performed on par with a premium whey isolate on bone and inflammation markers over a whole season, with only small, mostly sex-specific wobbles in osteokines and cytokines that are hard to translate into clear real-world advantages for either side.
