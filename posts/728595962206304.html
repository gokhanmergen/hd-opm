Intensive statin reversed atherosclerosis on IVUS in a 2004 Cleveland Clinic-led trial

Louis Nervegna · Dec 23, 2023 · ♥ 20 reactions · 40 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyStatinsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Cleveland Clinic video to counter the claim that statins do not reverse plaque. The 2004 trial used intravascular ultrasound (IVUS), which he calls the gold standard for plaque imaging, and showed regression with high-dose statin driving LDL-C down to about 60 mg/dL. He stresses LDL must be very low to achieve regression.

  • Trial led by Cleveland Clinic, 2004
  • IVUS considered more accurate than CT angiography for plaque
  • High-dose statin lowered LDL-C to ~60 mg/dL
  • Regression needed very low LDL

The claim that statins do not reverse plaque is a lie. The reversal of plaque by statins has been rigorously demonstrated at the Cleveland Clinic with invasive camera imaging directly into the arteries to film the lesions and document the regression.

This type of invasive camera imaging of plaque lesions (IVUS) is more accurate than even CT-angiogram scans. IVUS is considered the gold standard for imaging plaque (see comments for explanation).

The trial referred to in this video used high dose statins to lower LDL-C down to 60mg/dL (see comments for the full published paper). To get this kind of regression with statins, the LDL-C must be driven very low like this.
