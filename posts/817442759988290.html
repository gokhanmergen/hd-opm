Berberine lowered plaque score 3.2% and TMAO in a small 21-patient trial

Louis Nervegna · Jun 10, 2024 · ♥ 18 reactions · 23 comments · original Facebook post
Gut microbiomePlaque, CAC & CT angiographySupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a small human trial in which berberine reduced atherosclerotic plaque score, with a proposed mechanism of lowering gut-derived TMAO by acting on TMAO-producing gut bacteria. The sample is small and the comparison group was not randomized to the same degree as a large trial, so this is hypothesis-generating.

  • 21 patients on berberine 0.5 g twice daily for 4 months: plaque score fell 3.2%
  • Comparison group (n=12) on rosuvastatin plus antiplatelets: plaque score rose 1.9%
  • TMA and TMAO fell 38% and 29% in faeces, and 37% and 35% in plasma

Berberine decreases atherosclerotic plaque in a small human trial.

The mechanism appears to be reduction of TMAO. Berberine kills the bacteria in the gut involved in the production of TMAO.

Quoted:

“Moreover, 21 patients with atherosclerosis exhibited the average decrease of plaque score by 3.2% after oral BBR (0.5 g, bid) for 4 months (*P < 0.05, n = 21); whereas the plaque score in patients treated with rosuvastatin plus aspirin, or clopidogrel sulfate or ticagrelor (4 months, n = 12) increased by 1.9%. TMA and TMAO in patients decreased by 38 and 29% in faeces (*P < 0.05; *P < 0.05), and 37 and 35% in plasma (P < 0.001; *P < 0.05), after 4 months on BBR. BBR might treat atherosclerotic plaque at least partially through decreasing TMAO in a mode of action similar to that of vitamins.”

Comparison of the two high-intensity statins: both lower LDL-C by 50% or more, rosuvastatin is more potent per milligram, but in the randomized LODESTAR trial 3-year clinical outcomes did not differ significantly. The goal is reaching the appropriate LDL target on maximally tolerated therapy.

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.

SATURN: high-dose statins reversed plaque 1-2%; each 1% PAV cut means 20% fewer events

Aug 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 20 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis highlights the SATURN trial, where high-dose statins reversed plaque by about 1-2% PAV over two years, and each 1% PAV reversal is associated with about 20% fewer MACE. A shared meta-analysis of 31 studies and 4,997 patients (Li 2021) found lipid-lowering lowered TAV and PAV on IVUS.
