Microplastics Cause Atherosclerosis in Male Mice

A study led by the University of California, Riverside, indicates that exposure to microplastics can actively accelerate atherosclerosis, specifically in males. In experiments involving mice genetically prone to heart disease, subjects were administered 10 milligrams of microplastics per kilogram of body weight daily for nine weeks. While female mice showed no significant increase in plaque formation, male mice experienced a 63% increase in plaque buildup in the aortic root and a 624% increase in the brachiocephalic artery. This damage occurred despite no changes in body weight or cholesterol levels, suggesting that microplastics directly disrupt endothelial cells and trigger inflammation rather than acting through traditional risk factors like obesity.

PS: Two of the best defenses we have today against microplastics are (1) eating a high-fiber diet, and (2) using a Finnish sauna regularly, if you have access and it’s safe for you to do so.
