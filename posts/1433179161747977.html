🩺 HOW TO INTERPRET LIVER FUNCTION TEST (LFT)

📚 An Easy Stepwise Clinical Guide

💡 Liver Function Tests (LFTs) help assess hepatocellular injury, cholestasis, bilirubin metabolism, and liver synthetic function.

👉 EASY MNEMONIC: “A B C D E”

🔴 A — ALT/AST → Hepatocellular injury
🟠 B — Bilirubin → Bilirubin metabolism
🟢 C — Cholestatic enzymes (ALP/GGT)
🔵 D — Decreased synthesis (Albumin/PT/INR)
🟣 E — Etiology (identify the cause)

1️⃣ LOOK AT ALT & AST — HEPATOCELLULAR INJURY

🔹 ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)

  • More liver-specific.
  • Elevated in hepatocellular injury.

🔹 AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)

  • Also found in muscle, heart, and RBCs.
  • Less liver-specific.

🔍 Interpretation:

🟢 ALT > AST
→ Acute viral hepatitis
→ NAFLD/MASLD
→ Drug-induced liver injury

🟠 AST/ALT ratio > 2
→ Suggests alcohol-associated liver disease, especially with supportive clinical findings.

🔴 AST or ALT > 1,000 U/L
Think of:

  • Acute viral hepatitis
  • Ischaemic hepatitis (shock liver)
  • Acetaminophen (paracetamol) toxicity

2️⃣ LOOK AT BILIRUBIN 🟠

Total bilirubin:
Normal approximately 0.3–1.2 mg/dL (laboratory-dependent).

🔸 Direct (conjugated) hyperbilirubinaemia

  • Hepatitis
  • Cholestasis
  • Biliary obstruction

🔸 Indirect (unconjugated) hyperbilirubinaemia

  • Haemolysis
  • Gilbert syndrome
  • Ineffective erythropoiesis

3️⃣ LOOK AT ALP & GGT — CHOLESTASIS

🔹 ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) rises in:

  • Biliary obstruction
  • Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)
  • Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)
  • Bone disease

🔹 GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase) helps identify a hepatobiliary source of raised ALP.

🟢 Raised ALP + Raised GGT
→ Suggests hepatobiliary disease.

🟠 Raised ALP + Normal GGT
→ Consider bone disease rather than a hepatic source.

4️⃣ CHECK LIVER SYNTHETIC FUNCTION 🔵

🔹 Albumin — low in:

  • Chronic liver disease
  • Malnutrition
  • Nephrotic syndrome
  • Protein-losing enteropathy

🔹 PT/INR

  • A key marker of hepatic synthetic function.
  • Prolonged PT/INR may indicate significant liver dysfunction.
  • Also prolonged by vitamin K deficiency or warfarin.

⚠️ Albumin and PT/INR are not liver-specific; interpret them in clinical context.

5️⃣ IDENTIFY THE PATTERN 📊

🟢 Hepatocellular pattern
↑ ALT/AST disproportionately more than ALP.

Common causes:

  • Viral hepatitis
  • MASLD/MASH
  • Autoimmune hepatitis
  • Drug-induced liver injury

🟠 Cholestatic pattern
↑ ALP disproportionately more than ALT/AST.

Common causes:

  • Gallstones or biliary obstruction
  • PBC
  • PSC

🔵 Mixed pattern
Both aminotransferases and ALP are elevated.

Possible causes:

  • Drug-induced liver injury
  • Sepsis-associated liver injury
  • Some forms of viral hepatitis

📌 Clinical pearl: The R ratio can help classify hepatocellular, cholestatic, and mixed patterns.

6️⃣ THINK OF THE CAUSE 🔍

Ask about:

  • 🍺 Alcohol use
  • 🦠 Viral hepatitis risk
  • ⚖️ Obesity, diabetes, and metabolic dysfunction
  • 💊 Medications, supplements, and herbal drugs
  • 🪨 Gallstones or biliary symptoms
  • 🛡️ Autoimmune disease
  • 🩸 Haemolysis, if unconjugated bilirubin is raised

7️⃣ ORDER FURTHER TESTS 🧪

Choose investigations according to the clinical pattern:

  • Viral hepatitis serology
  • Autoimmune markers: ANA, ASMA, AMA
  • Iron studies (haemochromatosis)
  • Ceruloplasmin (especially in younger patients when Wilson disease is suspected)
  • α1-antitrypsin level in selected patients

🖥️ Imaging

  • Ultrasound hepatobiliary system — usually first-line imaging.
  • MRCP or CT — when biliary obstruction or other structural pathology is suspected.

🚨 RED FLAGS: Jaundice with fever and right upper-quadrant pain, confusion, bleeding, or prolonged INR with acute liver injury requires urgent assessment.
