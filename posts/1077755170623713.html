HDL is a poor intervention target: every large HDL-raising trial has failed

Louis Nervegna · Jul 28, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues HDL is basically only a crude marker of insulin resistance and notes every large randomized trial of HDL-raising has failed to prevent heart attacks and strokes. A shared post adds that Mendelian randomization found no benefit of genetically high HDL.

  • No HDL-raising RCT has reduced heart attacks or strokes
  • OGTT with insulin is more accurate for insulin resistance than HDL

HDL is basically useless as an intervention target in CVD, except as a crude marker of insulin resistance. And there are far more accurate ways to measure insulin resistance than using HDL, e.g. an oral glucose tolerance test which also measures insulin.

Every single large randomized trial testing HDL-raising as an intervention has failed to prevent heart attacks and strokes. Literally every single one. It has a perfect track record of failure in cardiology.

It is good to have higher HDL for the same reason that it is good to have better insulin sensitivity. That’s about the only evidence-based thing you can say about HDL as an intervention target in cardiology.

The focus should be on improving insulin sensitivity, not on HDL.

(If you focus on inproving insulin sensitivity, the HDL will likely go up naturally as a side effect.)
