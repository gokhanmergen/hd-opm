Kimchi meta-analysis: lower triglycerides, glucose and blood pressure across nine studies

Gokhan Can · Sep 21, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A University of Connecticut meta-analysis of nine studies linked regular kimchi intake to better cardiometabolic markers. The blood pressure benefit is attributed to potassium and fermentation bacteria despite kimchi's high sodium, though most studies were done in Korea.

  • Fasting glucose 1.93 mg/dL lower
  • Triglycerides 28.88 mg/dL lower
  • Systolic BP 3.48 mmHg and diastolic BP 2.68 mmHg lower
  • Based on 9 studies, mostly in Korean populations

Kimchi

A recent meta-analysis by University of Connecticut researchers found that regular consumption of kimchi, a traditional Korean fermented dish, is linked to significant cardiometabolic benefits. The review of nine studies revealed that kimchi intake was associated with 1.93 mg/dL lower fasting glucose, 28.88 mg/dL lower triglyceride levels, and meaningful reductions in blood pressure, with systolic pressure dropping by 3.48 mmHg and diastolic by 2.68 mmHg. Despite its high sodium content, kimchi's beneficial effects on blood pressure are attributed to its high potassium levels and the lactic acid bacteria produced during fermentation. Broader research also supports kimchi as a "gut superfood" that can improve digestion and has been linked to a lower prevalence of obesity and abdominal fat. Researchers noted, however, that most studies have been conducted on Korean populations and called for further research in more diverse groups to generalize the findings.

A cluster randomized trial in rural northern China (a subgroup analysis of a larger trial) gave stroke survivors a salt substitute containing 25% potassium chloride.
