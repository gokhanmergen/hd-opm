Olive oil raised GLP-1 and lowered post-meal glucose versus butter in type 1 diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Aug 25, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)Insulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Olive oil, like all fats, raises GLP-1, and the quoted crossover data suggest it does so more than butter, helping people feel full sooner. After a high-glycemic meal in people with type 1 diabetes, glucose exposure was lower with EVOO than with butter or a low-fat meal, plausibly via slower gastric emptying and glucose-lipid competition.

  • Post-meal GLP-1 iAUC: EVOO 261 vs butter 189 pmol/L x 180 min (p = 0.009)
  • Glucose iAUC (360 min): EVOO 690 vs butter 1320 vs low-fat 1007 mmol/L (p = 0.041)
  • Study population: type 1 diabetes, high-glycemic index meal

All fats increase GLP-1, but olive oil is especially potent at doing it.

Adding it to food makes you feel full sooner.

Cooking with olive oil also has a blood sugar lowering effect, when compared to the same meals cooked with no fat or with butter.

Quoted:

“EVOO significantly increased postprandial GLP-1 iAUC (261 ± 311) compared to Butter (189 ± 349) (pmol/Lx180 min, p = 0.009).”

“Blood glucose iAUC (mmol/lx360 min) was lower after the EVOO (690 ± 431) than after the Butter (1320 ± 600) and Low Fat meals (1007 ± 990) (M ± SD, p = 0.041 by repeated measures ANOVA).”

“Conclusion - Changes in gastric emptying and GLP-1 secretion and reduced glucose absorption through glucose-lipid competition may contribute to lower glycemia after a high-glycemic index meal with EVOO in T1D patients.”
