Influencers on social media are fond of cherry picking single studies (usually opinion pieces or highly biased review articles which they call studies) to support their particular conspiracy theory.

I asked Claude AI to summarize the totality of the evidence on whether or not Paleolithic humans were “carnivores”.

This is a good way to do a basic scientific fact check on the ridiculous claims that these influencers often make.

——

Based on the available scientific evidence, Paleolithic humans were not strictly carnivores, but rather omnivores who consumed a diverse diet that included both plant and animal foods. Here's a summary of what the evidence suggests:

Archeological evidence:

Fossilized human feces (coprolites) from Paleolithic sites have shown the presence of plant remains, such as seeds, nuts, and grains, indicating the consumption of plant-based foods.
Grinding stones and other tools used for processing plant materials have been found at Paleolithic sites.

Dental and skeletal evidence:

The teeth and jaws of Paleolithic humans show adaptations for an omnivorous diet, with features suited for both tearing meat and grinding plant materials.
Skeletal remains do not exhibit the characteristics typically associated with obligate carnivores, such as specialized carnassial teeth or a highly acidic stomach for digesting large amounts of meat.

Stable isotope analysis:

Studies of stable isotopes in Paleolithic human remains have shown ratios consistent with a mixed diet of plant and animal sources, rather than a purely carnivorous diet.

Hunter-gatherer studies:

Ethnographic studies of modern hunter-gatherer societies, which are often used as models for Paleolithic lifestyles, reveal diets that include a combination of hunted game, fish, gathered plant foods, nuts, seeds, and in some cases, wild grains.

While the ratio of plant to animal foods likely varied based on geographical location, climate, and available resources, the consensus among anthropologists and paleodieteticians is that Paleolithic humans were omnivores, relying on a diverse range of plant and animal sources for their nutrition.
It's important to note that the term "Paleolithic diet" in the modern context is often a broad interpretation and may not accurately reflect the true dietary patterns of our prehistoric ancestors, which were likely more varied and adaptable to local environments.
