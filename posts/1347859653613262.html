AHA: up to 400 mg caffeine/day is generally safe, but energy drinks may raise BP

AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes the AHA statement that up to 400 mg caffeine daily (about five 8-oz coffees) is generally safe for most adults and linked to lower risk of heart failure, heart disease and stroke in some, while higher amounts as in energy shots may raise blood pressure or arrhythmia risk.

  • 400 mg caffeine/day about five 8-oz cups
  • Higher caffeine amounts in energy drinks may increase blood pressure and arrhythmia risk
  • More research is needed

The American Heart Association has issued an official position statement on coffee and heart health.

Quoted:

The latest research indicates that consuming up to 400 mg of caffeine/day (equivalent to up to five 8-ounce cups of caffeinated coffee per day) is generally safe for most adults and appears to be linked to a lower risk of several cardiovascular conditions, including heart failure, heart disease and stroke in some individuals.

However, higher amounts of caffeine, such as levels found in energy drinks including energy shots, may increase the risk of high blood pressure and/or irregular heart rhythm or arrhythmia.

More research is needed to better understand caffeine’s impact on the body, how different sources of caffeine impact heart health and how its impact may vary among different people, according to the scientific statement.
