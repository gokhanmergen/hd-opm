Fat type beats amount: PUFA tied to lower mortality, trans and saturated fat to higher

Gokhan Can · Aug 24, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Cardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

A 2020 Clinical Nutrition meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (1,013,273 participants, 195,515 deaths) found polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fats associated with lower mortality, and saturated and trans fats with higher. Gokhan cautions that data guide rather than prescribe.

  • PUFA +5% energy: 7% lower all-cause, 5% lower CVD, 4% lower cancer mortality
  • Trans fat +1% energy: 6% higher all-cause and CVD mortality
  • Saturated fat +5% energy: 3% higher CVD mortality, 4% higher cancer mortality
  • Saturated fat risk rose up to about 11% of energy, then plateaued

Association between dietary fat intake and mortality from all-causes, cardiovascular disease, and cancer: A systematic review and metaanalysis of prospective cohort studies

This 2020 meta-analysis in Clinical Nutrition concludes that the type of dietary fat is more important than the total amount when it comes to mortality risk. Diets high in polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fats are linked to a lower risk of death, while diets high in saturated and trans fats are linked to a higher risk.

The study systematically reviewed and analyzed data from 19 prospective cohort studies, which included a total of 1,013,273 participants and 195,515 deaths.

Key Findings on Fat Types and Mortality

Fats Associated with Lower Mortality Risk 📉

  • Polyunsaturated Fat: Showed the strongest protective effect. A 5% increase in energy from polyunsaturated fat was associated with a:
  • 7% lower risk of all-cause mortality.
  • 5% lower risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality.
  • 4% lower risk of cancer mortality.
  • Monounsaturated Fat: A 5% increase in energy from this fat was linked to a 2% lower risk of all-cause mortality.
  • Total Fat: A 5% increase in energy from total fat was associated with a 1% lower risk of all-cause mortality.

Fats Associated with Higher Mortality Risk 📈

  • Trans Fat: Showed a significant increase in risk. Just a 1% increase in energy from trans fat was associated with a:
  • 6% higher risk of all-cause mortality.
  • 6% higher risk of CVD mortality.
  • Saturated Fat: A 5% increase in energy from saturated fat was linked to a:
  • 4% higher risk of cancer mortality.
  • 3% higher risk of CVD mortality.
  • The study found a non-linear relationship for all-cause and CVD mortality, where the risk increased significantly up to an intake of 11% of daily energy and then plateaued.

Conclusion

The authors state that their findings support current dietary guidelines that recommend reducing saturated and trans fat intake while emphasizing the consumption of monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats. The composition of dietary fat appears to be a more critical determinant of health outcomes than the total quantity of fat consumed.

My thoughts: Your personal situation might necessitate a particular diet, and one size doesn't fit all. Data can provide guidance but shouldn't be taken as a prescription, imo.

Meta-analysis of 102 feeding trials: polyunsaturated fat gives the best glycemic benefit

Nov 18, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 7 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Summarises a systematic review of 102 randomized feeding trials (4,220 participants) on 5% energy swaps. Replacing carbs with MUFA lowered HbA1c 0.09% and HOMA-IR 2.4%; with PUFA lowered HbA1c 0.11% and fasting insulin 1.6 pmol/L. PUFA alone improved insulin secretion capacity; saturated fat swaps did not change fasting glucose. Authors favor replacing refined carbs and saturated fat with unsaturated fats.

Examine: saturated fat raises LDL-C and apoB in WHO meta-analysis of 84 RCTs

Summary of Examine's article on saturated fat. A 2016 WHO meta-analysis of 84 RCTs (2,353 healthy adults) examined replacing 1% of calories from carbohydrates or unsaturated fat with saturated fat. Saturated fat raised LDL-C and apoB (suggesting higher risk), and HDL rose only about one-tenth as much as LDL-C, raising the LDL/HDL ratio.

100+ feeding trials: polyunsaturated fat improves glucose control, saturated fat does not
