Higher omega-6 (linoleic acid) biomarker levels linked to 43% lower type 2 diabetes risk

Louis Nervegna · Jan 20, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

A pooled analysis of 39,740 adults from 20 prospective cohorts used blood and tissue biomarkers rather than food questionnaires. Higher linoleic acid levels were associated with lower incident type 2 diabetes, and the authors conclude linoleic acid may have long-term preventive benefits and arachidonic acid is not harmful.

  • 39,740 adults, 20 prospective cohort studies
  • Top vs bottom quintile of linoleic acid: 43% lower relative risk of type 2 diabetes
  • Based on measured blood/tissue levels, not food questionnaires

Omega 6 polyunsaturated fats and DIABETES

A pooled analysis of nearly 40,000 people from 20 prospective cohort studies showed that higher blood and tissue levels of omega 6 are associated with LOWER rates of diabetes.

Note that these studies were NOT based on food questionnaires. They were based on direct measurements of omega 6 levels in blood and tissue. And they tracked the omega 6 levels in the blood and tissue periodically for years before people developed diabetes (hence the use of the term prospective in describing the studies).

People with blood levels of omega 6 in the top 20% of the range had a 43% lower risk of developing diabetes than people in the bottom 20%.

Quoted:

“The magnitude of the association between linoleic acid biomarkers and type 2 diabetes was substantial, with high linoleic acid levels associated with a 43% lower relative risk of type 2 diabetes across quintiles [top 20% verses bottom 20%] in the categorical analysis.”
