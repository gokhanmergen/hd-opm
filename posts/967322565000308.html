Harvard Nurses' Health Study: protein source matters for healthy aging over 30 years

Louis Nervegna · Feb 14, 2025 · ♥ 2 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a video on a prospective analysis of protein intake and aging, calling it the first rigorous long-term human data on protein in middle age and later health. It followed about 50,000 women for 30 years; Louis stresses prospective cohorts are the best feasible design for this question.

  • About 50,000 women, 30 years of follow-up
  • Prospective cohort from the Nurses' Health Study

I posted on this study before.

↪ Midlife plant protein raised odds of healthy aging 22-58% per 3% of calories (NHS)

It is the first human study to ever rigorously examine the question of how protein consumption in middle age and onward affects health 30 years later in old age.

It is the first human study to give us true long term data on how protein consumption really affects aging in humans in the long term.

It is what is called a prospective cohort study— specifically the Harvard Nurse’s Study— which is the highest quality study design short of a randomized controlled trial. Unfortunately, it is impossible to run a 30 year long RCT, so this is the only study design that can possibly examine this question in the long term in a rigorous way.

Midlife plant protein raised odds of healthy aging 22-58% per 3% of calories (NHS)

Aug 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 15 ↩ 7 Protein

Louis summarizes a Nurses' Health Study analysis of midlife protein and later-life health. Total and animal protein were unfavorably associated with chronic disease, while plant and dairy protein were favorable. Replacing 3% of energy with plant protein (from fats, carbs, animal or dairy protein) raised odds of healthy aging by 22-58%, and the protein came mainly from food rather than supplements.

US dietary guidelines panel pushes plant protein, citing Nurses' Health Study papers

Nov 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 2 Protein

Louis notes the US dietary guidelines committee is emphasizing plant protein for 2025-2030, partly because of Harvard Nurses' Health Study papers. Those reported that higher midlife protein lowered later-life disease risk only when it came mainly from plants. He advises getting as much protein as possible from legumes, nuts, seeds and whole grains while leaving room for animal foods.

Higher plant-to-animal protein ratio linked to lower heart disease risk over 30 years

Jul 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 7 Protein

Louis summarizes Harvard Nurses' and Physicians' follow-up data on over 200,000 people tracked for 30 years. The best results were in those with the highest plant-to-animal protein ratio and the most total plant protein, mainly from replacing red and processed meat.

Whole-food plant protein beats animal protein for long-term outcomes in Harvard cohorts

Mar 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 ↩ 2 Protein

Louis summarizes 30-year follow-up in the Harvard Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study: whole-food plant protein is associated with better outcomes, especially for heart disease. The benefit depends on the animal protein type: plant protein is only slightly better than fish, poultry and fermented dairy but clearly better than red and processed meat.

Nurses' Health Study: plant protein, not animal protein, linked to lower frailty risk

Nov 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 19 Protein

Louis cites a 2022 Nurses' Health Study analysis in which higher plant protein intake was associated with lower frailty risk, while animal and dairy protein were not. He adds that protein alone does not build muscle without resistance exercise.

Nurses' Health Study: plant but not animal protein linked to lower frailty in older women

Aug 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 10 ↩ 3 Protein

Louis cites a 2022 Nurses' Health Study analysis in older women: higher plant protein intake was associated with lower frailty risk, but animal and dairy protein were not. Substituting 5% of energy from plant protein for animal, dairy or non-dairy animal protein was linked to 38%, 32% and 42% lower frailty risk respectively.
