Quoted:

The hormone insulin plays an important role in the brain, where it supports memory and other cognitive processes. As people age, the brain may become less responsive to insulin, which has been linked to age-related cognitive decline," says Dr. Peter Joris, the study's senior author and an associate professor in the Department of Nutrition and Movement Sciences, NUTRIM, Maastricht University Medical Center.

"Our study found that daily peanut consumption beneficially affected insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions in older adults. These findings suggest that enhanced regional brain insulin responsiveness may contribute to the cognitive benefits of unsalted, skin-roasted peanut intake."

The NUTRIM study involved 31 healthy older adults who ranged in age from 60 to 75 and consumed 60 grams (2.1 ounces; approximately two servings) of peanuts daily for 16 weeks. It was a randomized, controlled crossover trial—a strong clinical research design.

In 2025, Joris found that the 16-week peanut intervention increased global cerebral blood flow (CBF) by 3.6% and verbal memory by 5.8%. CBF is an important physiological marker of brain vascular function and refers to the amount of blood that flows through the brain, delivering oxygen and nutrients that are essential for maintaining brain health. These findings were published in Clinical Nutrition.

The NUTRIM study combined a well-controlled, longer-term dietary intervention with advanced brain imaging techniques, specifically arterial spin labeling magnetic resonance imaging (ASL-MRI), to investigate the effects of daily peanut consumption on brain health.

Brain insulin responsiveness was assessed from changes in regional CBF after intranasal insulin administration. Intrahepatic lipid (IHL) content was quantified using abdominal MRI. Cognitive performance was evaluated using the Cambridge Neuropsychological Test Automated Battery (CANTAB).

“Multiple nutrients present in peanuts, including unsaturated fats, dietary fiber, vitamins and minerals and bioactive phytochemicals, could potentially contribute to the observed brain benefits," says Joris.

"Peanuts are also a good source of plant-based protein and are especially rich in L-arginine. L-arginine is a key precursor of nitric oxide (NO), which has been reported to facilitate learning and memory performance."
