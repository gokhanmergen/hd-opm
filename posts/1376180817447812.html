Caffeic acid in coffee activates the NR4A1 receptor and may explain health benefits

Louis Nervegna · Aug 23, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a study suggesting caffeic acid, not just chlorogenic acid, may drive coffee's health benefits by activating the stress-protective receptor NR4A1 (Nur77). Caffeic acid is unrelated to caffeine and also found in turmeric, thyme, sage and olive oil.

  • Caffeic acid activates NR4A1/Nur77
  • Chemically unrelated to caffeine
  • Also in turmeric, thyme, sage, olive oil

Chlorogenic acid seems to get a lot of the credit when it comes to the health benefits of coffee, but a new study suggests that caffeic acid might be playing a big role too.

(Despite its name, caffeic acid is chemically unrelated to caffeine. It is found in most plants, but is particularly high in coffee.)

#coffeeHD #caffeicacidHD

Research cited

  • Caffeic acid and NR4A1 activation by coffee compounds · in-vitro
Shared from Dale Bredesen, MD · original post

Caffeinated coffee has repeatedly been shown to provide benefits for health and aging - though the exact amount to consume and the mechanisms it uses have been incompletely explained. A new study solves some of the mystery by showing compounds in coffee, particularly caffeic acid, activate a receptor that helps protect cells from stress, inflammation, and damage (NR4A1, also called Nur77). Some other great sources of caffeic acid include turmeric, thyme, sage, and olive oil. Coffee may help the body fight stress and aging through a hidden cellular switch (ScienceDaily)

Caffeinated coffee linked to 18% lower dementia risk over 43 years in two US cohorts

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Brain health & dementia

Shared summary of a JAMA cohort of 131,821 US nurses and health professionals followed up to 43 years. The highest caffeinated coffee group had 18% lower dementia risk after adjustment, decaf showed no link, and the lowest risk was at about 2-3 cups of coffee or 1-2 cups of tea. Observational, with large confounding (heavy drinkers smoked more).
