Taurine deficiency may drive aging; Columbia study spans worms, mice, monkeys and humans

Louis Nervegna · Mar 01, 2024 · ♥ 23 reactions · 28 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevitySupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a 2023 Columbia University study on taurine and aging in worms, mice, monkeys and humans, noting taurine also benefits the cardiovascular system. He stresses that age is the biggest causal risk factor for heart disease and taurine might be an anti-aging intervention, with emphasis on 'might', and that it seems reasonably safe.

  • Columbia researchers identified taurine deficiency as a key driver of aging in animals
  • Taurine is made by the body and found in food

This study at Columbia University on the amino acid taurine and its effects on the aging process in worms, mice, monkeys, and humans, attracted a large amount of attention this past summer (2023).

Yet many people here are probably not aware of it.

Given that taurine also has beneficial effects on the heart and cardiovascular system, this study is very relevant to the group.

Age is by far the biggest causal risk factor for heart disease. Interventions that target the aging process of the cardiovascular system itself are the future of cardiology. The research is still in its infancy, but taurine might be one of those interventions. Emphasis on might. Most importantly, it appears to be reasonably safe. (It is naturally produced by the human body and found in food.)

Mouse taurine doses of 500-1000 mg/kg scale to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg human

Oct 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 7 Aging & longevity

Louis explains that the 2023 Columbia taurine study used 500-1000 mg/kg in mice, and that allometric scaling for faster mouse metabolism converts this to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg person. He suggests that is the range to test in human trials, since mouse mg/kg doses cannot be applied directly to humans.

Nature study challenges the idea that taurine declines with age

Jun 07, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 8 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

Louis summarizes a Nature paper from Rafael de Cabo's NIA group finding that taurine levels do not decline with age in humans, monkeys or mice, contradicting a high-profile Columbia study. He notes the new data are mostly longitudinal, whereas the Columbia data were largely cross-sectional, and that unchanged levels do not necessarily mean supplementation has no value.
