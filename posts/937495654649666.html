Commercial blood microplastics tests mostly measure contamination, says Louis

Louis Nervegna · Dec 28, 2024 · ♥ 17 reactions · 20 comments · original Facebook post
Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)Risk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that the microplastics blood test sold by Bryan Johnson measures plastic contamination from blood draw, transport and processing. Single-digit particle counts in a 100 uL sample require a cleanroom and plastic-minimized lab, which no commercial lab offers.

  • Test claims single-digit particle counts per 100 uL
  • A plastic- and phthalate-minimised lab/cleanroom is required (2024 J Hazard Mater blueprint)

The microplastics blood test that Bryan Johnson is selling is a scam.

Commercial labs have exposure to plastic.

The process of transporting, storing, and testing blood involves exposure to plastic.

The air itself contains a significant number of plastic particles. Commercial building materials are full of plastic.

All this test measures is the tiny amount of plastic contamination that occurs in your blood while it is being drawn, and afterward when it is transported, stored, and processed in a way that almost certainly involves exposure to plastic.

You need a full cleanroom setup to prevent contamination with plastics at the concentrations they are claiming to measure.

The test is claiming to report single digit particle counts in a 100uL blood sample. Even the plastic particles in the air from common building materials are going to totally contaminate that.

The blood draw itself would have to be done in a cleanroom to prevent contamination at single digit particle concentrations.

There’s a reason that NO commercial lab offers this test. It’s damn near impossible to do, without a full cleanroom setup.
