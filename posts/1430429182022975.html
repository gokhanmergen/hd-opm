Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Louis Nervegna · Oct 24, 2026 · ♥ 15 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevitySupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.

  • Whole-blood NAD+ stable with age across seven human cohorts
  • NR and NMN raise circulating NAD+, but lack evidence of slowing aging
  • No commercial test for tissue NAD

Contrary to what you may have heard, blood levels of NAD do not decline with age.

Your blood level of NAD mainly reflects your recent intake of vitamin B3, and very little else.

It is a useless biomarker. The companies selling this blood test are scamming you.

(Tissue and organ levels of NAD are a different story. This is where there might actually be a benefit. However, there is currently no commercial test that can measure NAD levels in your tissues or organs.)

Another reality check for the commercial longevity industry.

A new study in Nature Metabolism finds that whole-blood NAD⁺ levels remain remarkably stable with age across seven independent human cohorts.

NAD⁺ precursors such as NR and NMN can increase circulating NAD⁺ levels. But despite years of enthusiasm and aggressive marketing, there is still no convincing clinical evidence that these supplements slow human ageing, extend lifespan, or broadly improve healthspan.

Changing a biomarker is not the same as improving health.

Promoting expensive supplements as anti-ageing interventions before demonstrating meaningful clinical benefits is marketing ahead of science.

We need rigorous human trials, not more hype.
