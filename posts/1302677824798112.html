Ice cream's apparent diabetes benefit is probably not real; yogurt is better

Louis Nervegna · May 27, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an Atlantic article on odd observational links between ice cream and lower diabetes risk. He cautions that the comparisons are against the standard American diet, that interviewed scientists mostly attribute the association to reverse causality, and that fruit, nuts and dark chocolate are better desserts.

  • Comparison group is a poor standard American diet
  • Most scientists suspect reverse causality

Ice cream might not be as bad as originally thought, but yogurt is better

As you read this article, keep 3 things in mind:

  1. Comparisons of ice cream (in the studies mentioned in the article) are to the standard American diet as a whole, which is a very bad diet. Ice cream does not look very good when you compare it to healthier desserts like fruit, nuts, dark chocolate.
  1. Every single scientist interviewed for the article is not inclined to believe that any association between ice cream and lower risk of diabetes is real. Most of them believe it is due largely to reverse causality. Some of the association might be a real protective effect, but the majority is likely reverse causality.
  1. The totality of evidence for health benefits of yogurt (including protection against diabetes) is much stronger than for ice cream. Several of the scientists state this in the article.

Louis highlights the widely cited 2020 NEJM review on dairy and disease risk. It concludes that high dairy intake is not supported for preventing fractures, is not clearly linked to weight, diabetes or CVD, and may raise prostate and endometrial cancer risk while lowering colorectal cancer risk.
