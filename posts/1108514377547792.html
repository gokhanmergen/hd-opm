Loss of the Y chromosome in blood cells may drive heart failure in men

Shared from Wikinzie · original post

A recent study suggests that the loss of the Y chromosome in men, a phenomenon known as Y chromosome loss or YCL, may contribute significantly to heart disease, a leading cause of death among men. The Y chromosome, which determines male sex, gradually diminishes in some cells as men age, particularly in blood cells. This loss is linked to a condition called clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), where blood cells accumulate genetic mutations.

Researchers found that YCL impairs the function of immune cells, leading to chronic inflammation and tissue damage in the heart. This process accelerates the development of cardiovascular diseases, such as heart failure and atherosclerosis, by promoting fibrosis (scarring) in heart tissue. The study, conducted on mice and human data, showed that men with YCL had a higher risk of fatal heart conditions due to disrupted immune responses and increased oxidative stress.

The findings highlight a potential genetic factor in why men are more prone to heart disease than women. While YCL is not the sole cause, it could serve as a biomarker for heart disease risk. Future research may explore therapies targeting inflammation or immune function to mitigate these effects, offering new avenues for preventing heart disease in men.
