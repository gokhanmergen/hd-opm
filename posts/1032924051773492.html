VITAL: vitamin D3 2,000 IU slowed telomere shortening ~140 base pairs over 4 years

Jun 29, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 8 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a summary of a VITAL ancillary study in 1,031 older adults in which daily vitamin D3 2,000 IU preserved leukocyte telomere length versus placebo, roughly 3.5-4.5 years of reduced biological aging. Omega-3 (1 g/day) showed no effect on telomere length. Telomere length is a surrogate marker.

Vitamin D slowed telomere shortening by about 140 base pairs in VITAL

Jul 21, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 4 Supplements & vitamins

A shared analysis of the VITAL Telomere study found 2,000 IU/day vitamin D3 reduced leukocyte telomere shortening over four years (about 140 base pairs, roughly 2%), not '3.5 to 4.7 years younger'. Louis notes effects this subtle may not appear within trial durations and that vitamin D is no panacea.

Selenium plus CoQ10 cut cardiovascular death by over half; benefit persisted 10-12 years

Louis highlights a trial in older adults where selenium plus CoQ10 for four years lowered cardiovascular mortality, with persistent benefit in follow-up. He suggests adding about 200 mcg selenium (two Brazil nuts) to CoQ10 for people already taking it, while acknowledging the study's limitations.

Selenium plus CoQ10 trial: over 50% lower CV death, benefit persisted 10-12 years

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 12 Supplements & vitamins

Louis reshares a post quoting Life Extension on a trial where older people took selenium plus coenzyme Q10 for four years and had more than half the cardiovascular mortality. Follow-up showed 49% lower CV death at 10 years and 41% at 12 years, including in subgroups with heart disease, diabetes and hypertension. He suggests adding about 200 ug selenium to CoQ10. The source is a supplement-company magazine and the trial is not named.

Vitamin D 2000 IU cut autoimmune disease risk 40% in the VITAL trial, MS a key target

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis resurfaces a three-year-old post: the strongest evidence for higher-dose vitamin D is in autoimmune disease, especially MS, and he notes a new JAMA publication on vitamin D treating MS. The VITAL trial of about 25,000 people at Harvard found that 2000 IU vitamin D daily for over 2 years cut autoimmune disease risk by about 40%. CNN coverage of the same trial reports about 30% lower risk with vitamin D and fish oil supplements.
