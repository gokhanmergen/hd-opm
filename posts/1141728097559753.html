100+ RCTs and 20 cohorts: omega-6 improves glucose control, 43% lower diabetes risk

Louis Nervegna · Oct 21, 2025 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes evidence that omega-6 polyunsaturated fat lowers fasting glucose and A1C and improves insulin resistance and secretion across more than 100 randomized trials. A pooled analysis of about 40,000 people from 20 prospective cohorts with biomarker-measured omega-6 found the top fifth had 43% lower diabetes risk than the bottom fifth. He lists the supporting meta-analyses.

  • 100+ RCTs: PUFA improves fasting glucose, A1C, HOMA-IR and insulin secretion
  • Pooled analysis of 39,740 adults, 20 prospective cohorts, biomarker-based
  • Top vs bottom 20% omega-6 level: 43% lower risk of type 2 diabetes
  • Also cited: Cochrane review on omega-6 and CVD, a randomized trial on saturated vs polyunsaturated fat overfeeding and fatty liver

Over 100 randomized controlled trials have shown in aggregate that omega 6 polyunsaturated fat lowers fasting glucose and A1C, and improves both insulin resistance (Homa-IR) and insulin secretion capacity.

↪ 100+ trials: polyunsaturated fat improves glucose and insulin markers, saturated doesn't

A pooled analysis of nearly 40,000 people from 20 prospective cohort studies showed that higher blood and tissue levels of omega 6 are associated with LOWER rates of diabetes.

These studies were NOT based on food questionnaires. They were based on direct measurements of omega 6 levels in blood and tissue. And they tracked the omega 6 levels in the blood and tissue periodically for years before people developed diabetes (hence the use of the term prospective in describing the studies).

People with blood levels of omega 6 in the top 20% of the range had a 43% lower risk of developing diabetes than people in the bottom 20%.

↪ Higher omega-6 (linoleic acid) biomarker levels linked to 43% lower type 2 diabetes risk

Layne Norton rebuts seed-oil claims with human evidence on inflammation, LDL and oxidation

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
