Caffeine activates AMPK in yeast cells, which may explain coffee's longevity links

Gokhan Can · Jun 26, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A Queen Mary University of London study in Microbial Cell found that caffeine acts via AMPK, the cellular fuel gauge, influencing growth, DNA repair and stress response. It was done in fission yeast, so human relevance is unproven.

  • Caffeine's effect on TOR is indirect, working through AMPK

A new study from Queen Mary University of London reveals that caffeine, beyond its typical stimulant effects, may play a role in slowing cellular aging. Published in the journal Microbial Cell, the research, conducted on fission yeast (an organism with cellular similarities to humans), found that caffeine activates AMPK, a crucial cellular "fuel gauge" that helps cells manage stress and energy deficits. This activation of AMPK influences cell growth, DNA repair, and stress response—all factors linked to aging and disease. This discovery builds on previous findings that caffeine impacts TOR, a growth regulator, but clarifies that caffeine's effect is indirect, working through AMPK. These findings could help explain the observed health benefits and longevity links associated with coffee consumption and open doors for future research.
