Creatine and cancer metastasis: preclinical signal, but human evidence is reassuring

Louis Nervegna · Sep 18, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCancer
AI summary & key facts

A summary of the claim that creatine promotes cancer spread. Animal and cell models showed more metastasis and worse survival, but human data are much less alarming: higher dietary creatine was linked to lower cancer risk, and a trial giving creatine to cancer patients found no survival difference. The poster says people with cancer should discuss supplementation with their oncology team.

  • Preclinical: creatine increased metastatic behavior and worsened survival in animals
  • Metastatic cancers showed greater activity of a creatine-producing enzyme
  • Human: higher dietary creatine associated with lower cancer risk (limited study)
  • RCT in malignant cancer patients found no meaningful survival difference, but was not designed for that

#creatineHD #cancerHD

Shared from Physionic · original post

There’s a claim circulating that creatine may promote cancer metastasis—and unlike many claims about creatine, this one actually has some published research behind it.

In preclinical experiments, creatine increased metastatic behavior in cancer models, and animals exposed to creatine had worse cancer survival. Researchers also found that metastatic cancers had greater activity of an enzyme involved in producing creatine, with similar patterns appearing in human cancer samples.

That sounds concerning—but there’s a huge gap between those findings and saying creatine supplementation causes or accelerates cancer in humans.

Human evidence is much less alarming. Greater dietary creatine intake has actually been associated with lower cancer risk, although that study has important limitations. More importantly, a randomized controlled trial directly gave creatine to people who already had malignant cancer and found no meaningful difference in survival compared with placebo. That trial wasn’t designed specifically to test cancer survival, though, so it cannot completely rule out harm.

How to Use This: Current evidence does not show that creatine supplementation causes cancer in people without cancer. If you already have cancer, the evidence is much less certain, and avoiding supplementation out of caution is reasonable to discuss with your oncology team.
