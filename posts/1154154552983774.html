LDL particle size adds nothing once LDL-P or ApoB is known

AI summary & key facts

Louis notes that most cardiologists no longer order NMR particle-size tests because size does not predict risk once the total number of LDL particles is known. The NMR report itself says particle size is not associated with CVD risk after correcting for LDL-P, and ApoB is an equivalent count of particles.

  • Small LDL-P and LDL size are not associated with CVD risk after adjusting for LDL-P
  • ApoB is an equivalent way to count LDL particles

The vast majority of cardiologists do not order NMR cholesterol tests for particle size anymore, because it has been shown to be useless for predicting risk of heart attacks, strokes, and CVD mortality, once you already know LDL-P or ApoB:

↪ LDL particle size does not predict CVD risk once particle number (ApoB) is counted

↪ LDL particle size probably matters little once confounding and particle number are counted

↪ Eur Heart J 2025: apoB particle count, not size or type, drives coronary risk

It literally says right on the NMR test report itself that particle size is useless for predicting risk, once the total number of particles is already known (as measured by LDL-P or ApoB).
