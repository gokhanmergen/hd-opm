Meta-analyses: swapping sugary drinks for low-calorie sweetened ones does not harm weight

Louis Nervegna · Aug 25, 2025 · ♥ 0 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsWeight, visceral & liver fatMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

A quoted rebuttal to Peter Attia's claim that diet soda swaps would not aid weight loss cites a systematic review of 17 RCTs where replacing sugar-sweetened beverages with low/no-calorie sweetened beverages reduced weight, BMI, body fat and liver fat. A cohort meta-analysis found no cardiometabolic harm from these drinks used as a substitution.

  • Meta-analysis of 17 RCTs: low/no-calorie sweetened beverages replacing sugary drinks lowered weight, BMI, body fat percentage and liver fat
  • Replacing sugary drinks with water was not statistically significant, but few studies exist
  • Cohort meta-analysis found no cardiometabolic harm when used as a substitute for sugary drinks

Quoted:

@peterattiamd claims that replacing 3 cokes, (either 408 or 681 calories depending on cans or bottles) with diet cokes won’t move the needle on weight loss, but replacing them with Topo Chico (sparkling water) could.

For starters, his reasoning for why replacing coke with diet coke wouldn’t lead to weight loss doesn’t make sense on the surface. If the sweetness of the Diet Coke leads one to consume more, wouldn’t that also apply to sugary coke? It’s just as sweet, so presumably would lead to consuming more calories on top of the substantial amount of calories it already contains, no?

Nonetheless, he appears to be wrong about the mechanism anyway. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 RCTs found that replacing sugar-sweetened beverages with low/no-calorie sweetened beverages found that the diet options could reduce weight, BMI, body fat percentage, and liver fat !Surprisingly, replacing sugary beverages with water did not yield statistically significant results, but the caveat is that there were very few studies looking at that swap.

To further rebut Dr. Attia’s claim replacing water with low/no-calorie sweetened beverages actually led to weight loss! If he’s right that the sweetness somehow drives people to consume more calories, we should see the opposite.

Now, I’m not suggesting that people who regularly consume water switch to diet sodas. Based on long-term prospective cohort studies, we don’t see a meaningful difference in weight or cardiometabolic outcomes between water and diet sodas, but we do see a 12% lower risk of obesity, as well as lower risks of CHD, CVD mortality, and all-cause mortality when one replaces sugary drinks with low/no-calorie options after adjusting for baseline weight and cardiometabolic health.
