Niacin for CVD prevention: no reason to use it given proven options, says the poster

Louis Nervegna · Sep 19, 2026 · ♥ 21 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugsLp(a)Statins
AI summary & key facts

The poster gives his opinion that niacin is only better than nothing and no longer worth taking for cardiovascular prevention given proven alternatives. He would never combine it with high-dose statins, citing two large trials with no added benefit and more side effects, and no longer sees a role in high Lp(a) after the HORIZON trial failure.

  • Two large trials of niacin plus high-dose statin showed no added benefit and more side effects
  • Pooled, a small increase in deaths vs high-dose statin alone
  • No longer sees a use case in high Lp(a) after the HORIZON trial failure
  • Only remaining use: people who refuse all pharmaceuticals

Some people have asked for my opinion on niacin as a preventative/treatment for CVD.

Here it is:

If my choice were between doing nothing at all verses taking niacin, then I would take niacin.

But given how many proven options there now are, I no longer see any reason why I would take niacin to prevent CVD. The evidence is just not strong enough to justify taking it in place of proven treatments.

And I definitely would never combine niacin with high dose statins, because two large well-done trials showed that it added no benefit, but did increase side effects. When both trials were combined in meta-analysis, there was a small increase in deaths in the group taking niacin along with high dose statins, compared to high dose statins alone.

I used to think that there might still be a use case for niacin in people with high Lp(a). But given the recent failure of the HORIZON trial, I no longer think this.

The only use case I can still see for niacin in CVD is in people who refuse to take any pharmaceuticals at all. In that situation, I think it is better than doing nothing. That’s my opinion.

Pelacarsen (Lp(a)HORIZON) and ziltivekimab (ZEUS) cut their targets but not heart events

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Shared summary of two NEJM trials. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) sharply but event rates were 12.3% vs 12.1% on placebo, and ziltivekimab cut CRP and IL-6 but events were 17.6% vs 17.7%. The post notes prior biomarker failures (torcetrapib, dalcetrapib), and that both trials enrolled people with existing disease and so do not prove Lp(a) or inflammation are not causal.

Lp(a) makes clots form more easily and resist breakdown via OxPL and apo(a)

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Lp(a)

Louis shares a summary of an ATVB commentary by Rafael Zubiran explaining why high Lp(a) may be worse than LDL. Its oxidized phospholipids activate monocytes to make tissue factor, and apo(a) resembles plasminogen and competes with clot breakdown, producing denser, harder-to-dissolve clots. Louis notes statins, especially atorvastatin, may neutralize OxPL and aspirin lowers clotting risk.

Atorvastatin may 'disarm' Lp(a) by blocking oxidation, a theory for HORIZON's failure

Nov 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 12 ↩ 1 Lp(a)

Louis proposes that Lp(a)HORIZON failed because aggressive statin treatment (78% on high-intensity statins) may have already neutralized oxidized phospholipids carried by Lp(a). A shared abstract (Sherratt et al., Eur Heart J) found atorvastatin's active metabolite scavenges free radicals and inhibits oxidation of Lp(a) and small dense LDL better than other statins. This is a lab-based hypothesis.

Tsimikas: Lp(a) risk can be managed with LDL under 65 and tight risk-factor control

Louis quotes Sam Tsimikas, who calls Lp(a)HORIZON a success because it suggests Lp(a) risk can be reduced by keeping LDL-C at or below 65, blood pressure at 120 or lower, controlling other risk factors and taking proven medicines. In his Lp(a) clinic about 75% take a statin, 50% ezetimibe, 50% a PCSK9 inhibitor, plus aspirin.

Cleveland Clinic: pelacarsen did not cut CV events in Lp(a)HORIZON (12.3% vs 12.1%)

Nov 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Louis shares the Cleveland Clinic press release on Lp(a)HORIZON, a 797-site randomized trial in 8,322 patients with prior CVD and Lp(a) of at least 70 mg/dL. The primary endpoint occurred in 12.3% on pelacarsen versus 12.1% on placebo after a median 57 months. Senior author Steven Nissen said a single neutral trial does not prove lack of benefit from lowering Lp(a).
