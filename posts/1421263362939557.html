Daily blueberries altered 600+ blood-cell genes; metabolites tied to artery dilation

Louis Nervegna · Oct 14, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsBlood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

A FoundMyFitness podcast summary says daily blueberry intake altered the expression of over 600 genes in blood cells and produced anthocyanin-derived metabolites, some associated with better artery dilation. The point is that blueberries do more than act as antioxidants.

  • Gene expression of more than 600 genes in blood cells changed with daily blueberries
  • 63 circulating anthocyanin-derived metabolites identified; 14 associated with improved artery dilation
  • Podcast frames the dose as about 1 cup of blueberries per day

Daily blueberry intake changed the expression of more than 600 genes in blood cells

Researchers identified 63 circulating metabolites derived from anthocyanins (polyphenols abundant in blueberries), with 14 associated with improved artery dilation

The gene-expression changes involved pathways related to immune signaling and cell behavior

Together, these findings suggest blueberries do more than provide antioxidants, helping shape the signals that guide how cells function

About one cup of blueberries a day: best human evidence is for vascular function

Oct 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A podcast excerpt says one cup (150-180 g) of fresh or frozen blueberries daily matches the dose in most positive human studies. The clearest benefit is better endothelial function, with possible memory benefits in at-risk older adults and glucose benefits when metabolism is strained. Randomized trials have not shown they prevent dementia, diabetes, heart attacks or strokes.

Blending bananas with berries does not cancel the berries' benefits

Jul 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 6 Myths, misinformation & controversies

Louis shares a rebuttal of the claim that bananas in smoothies negate berry polyphenols. Banana polyphenol oxidase can break down some polyphenols such as epicatechins but does not degrade most anthocyanins, the main blueberry polyphenols, so the effect is likely minor.

No long-lived population ate a carbohydrate-free diet; long-term effects unknown

Jun 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 4 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues that every long-lived population studied includes carbohydrate from vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains or fermented dairy, and that the few carbohydrate-free-eating groups, such as certain Eskimo populations, were not notably long-lived. He says low-carb diets may help short-term but long-term heart-disease effects are speculative, so he suggests including low-GI carbohydrates.
