Understanding the Long-Term Risk of Cardiovascular Disease

A recent study in JACC using the American Heart Association PREVENT equations reveals eye-opening insights into cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk among U.S. adults aged 30 to 79 years.

Paper · Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 202510-Year and 30-Year Risks of Cardiovascular Disease in the U.S. Populationjacc.org

Key findings include:

  • Nearly 3 in 10 adults have either existing CVD or an elevated 10-year predicted risk.
  • Among adults over 65, this rises to over 90%.
  • About 2/3 of middle-aged adults (30-59 years) without CVD already carry an elevated 30-year risk.
  • Men, Black, and Hispanic adults show notably higher risks of total CVD, atherosclerotic CVD (ASCVD), and heart failure (HF).

These findings underscore the critical importance of aggressive lifestyle and pharmacological prevention strategies focused on managing risk factors early—especially in vulnerable populations.

As we continue prioritizing cardiovascular health, this study is a powerful reminder that long-term risk assessment and proactive intervention can save millions of lives and reduce billions in healthcare costs.
