Fat cells shift from lipolysis to autophagy during prolonged fasting, a magazine reports

Louis Nervegna · Jan 04, 2026 · ♥ 13 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fasting & meal timingWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a news article saying fat loss during fasting increasingly runs through autophagy rather than hormonal lipolysis. He suggests this may explain why fasting clears fat from liver and pancreas.

  • Longer fasts shift fat loss from lipolysis to autophagy (per article)
  • Ectopic liver and pancreas fat drives insulin resistance and diabetes (Louis)

New research demonstrates that autophagy dominates fat loss during fasting.

This is not how fat loss typically happens. More gradual fat loss occurs through a hormonal process called lipolysis.

The longer you fast, the more autophagy dominates fat loss compared to lipolysis.

This autophagy mechanism may explain why fasting is so effective at quickly clearing the liver and pancreas of fat. Ectopic fat in these organs is a principle driver of insulin resistance and diabetes.

Quoted:

“Initially, fat loss is triggered by hormones binding to the fat cells, telling them to activate lipolysis and by releasing fat. However, as the fast progresses, the trigger changes away from a predominantly hormonal origin, which begins the handoff to the autophagy system.”

“Large autophagy vesicles can capture thousands of fat molecules at once, making them far more efficient than individual lipolysis enzymes working one molecule at a time.

Without autophagy, there simply aren’t enough enzymes to keep up with demand. The lipolysis system becomes a bottleneck.”

“Traditionally, this [fat loss] process happens through lipolysis, a well-understood mechanism driven by specific enzymes that break down fat.

But researchers examining fasting subjects noticed something strange: the enzymes responsible for lipolysis were diminished, not elevated.

If lipolysis enzymes are required for fat breakdown, why would they decrease during fasting? That’s the puzzle that led scientists to discover an alternative pathway.

The study found that fat cells were ramping up production of proteins linked to autophagy—specifically, a process called lipophagy—which isn’t normally associated with fat breakdown.”

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

New research shows that during fasting, fat cells shift from the traditional lipolysis pathway to autophagy, effectively recycling cellular components to release stored fat more efficiently.

Microscopic imaging revealed autophagic vesicles actively engulfing lipid droplets as fasting progresses, indicating a metabolic handoff that enhances fat breakdown beyond hormonal triggers.

Gene expression studies in human tissue and inhibitor experiments confirm that this non-canonical mechanism operates in humans as well.

The findings deepen our understanding of fasting’s cellular effects and suggest new strategies for optimizing fat loss.
