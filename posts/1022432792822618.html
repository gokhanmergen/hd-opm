New Hope in Reducing Cardiovascular Risk Beyond LDL Cholesterol

Despite advances in lowering LDL cholesterol, many patients still face residual cardiovascular risk—one key contributor being elevated lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] levels, which are largely genetically determined and unaffected by lifestyle or traditional lipid-lowering therapies.

A recent study in NEJM highlights a promising breakthrough: lepodisiran, a small interfering RNA therapy that targets hepatic production of Lp(a).

In a randomized trial with 320 participants, lepodisiran significantly reduced serum Lp(a) concentrations. At the highest 400 mg dose, patients saw a placebo-adjusted reduction of 93.9% from baseline between days 60 to 180, with effects lasting up to a year. The therapy was well tolerated, with only mild injection-site reactions and no serious adverse events linked to the drug.

This could mark a major step forward in closing a critical gap in cardiovascular prevention strategies for those people who are born with genetically-elevated Lp(a).
