❤️ CAN YOU HAVE A HEART ATTACK WITH A CALCIUM SCORE OF ZERO?

Absolutely. And here’s why.

A calcium score of zero is reassuring, but it doesn’t necessarily mean your coronary arteries are free of disease.

🫀 CT CALCIUM SCORE (CAC)

Detects calcified plaque in your coronary arteries.

✅ A score of zero means no detectable coronary calcium.

❌ It does NOT exclude non-calcified plaque.

🫀 CT CORONARY ANGIOGRAM (CTCA)

Uses contrast to visualise your coronary arteries.

✅ Detects both calcified and non-calcified plaque.

✅ Identifies narrowing of the coronary arteries.

WHY DOES THIS MATTER?

Atherosclerosis often begins with non-calcified plaque, which may subsequently become calcified.

And non-calcified plaque can rupture, causing a heart attack.

📊 BUT HERE’S THE IMPORTANT PART.

A calcium score of zero is associated with a very LOW risk of cardiovascular events over the following years.

But LOW risk doesn’t mean NO risk.

THE BOTTOM LINE?

❤️ CAC tells us how much calcified plaque you have.

❤️ CTCA can identify both calcified and non-calcified plaque.

There are no guarantees. Only odds.
