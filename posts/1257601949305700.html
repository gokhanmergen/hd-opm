Healthy lifestyle tied to less dementia despite amyloid and tau in the brain (n=586)

Feb 06, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 7 Brain health & dementia

Louis shares an observational study of 586 people whose brains were examined at autopsy and linked to up to 24 years of lifestyle data. A healthy lifestyle (not smoking, 150+ min/week exercise, light to moderate alcohol, mental stimulation and MIND diet) was associated with protection from cognitive decline even when amyloid or tau pathology was present. He notes dementia and heart disease share lifestyle prevention.

Statins lower testosterone only slightly and often improve erectile function

Jan 03, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 5 Hormones, women's & men's health

Explains that statins reduce total testosterone by a trivial amount in meta-analyses, and may improve erectile function by repairing endothelial function and raising nitric oxide, with ED sometimes an early sign of heart disease. It references large trials and the SAMSON n-of-1 study.

Stiff arteries linked to greater brain nerve fiber injury in older adults (IGNITE trial)

Shares a report that in the multisite IGNITE trial, arterial stiffness was linked to greater injury effects on brain nerve fibers in cognitively unimpaired older adults, summarised by the admin as healthier vessels meaning healthier brains.

Glyphosate: IARC classification and the case for avoiding it

An advocacy-style essay arguing that glyphosate should be avoided. It cites the 2015 IARC classification as probably carcinogenic to humans, based on limited human evidence of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, sufficient animal evidence and strong mechanistic evidence. Not specific to heart disease.

Glyphosate: usage surge, conflicting safety assessments and residues in food

Sep 07, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 7 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

A comprehensive overview of glyphosate use and the regulatory split over its safety. Use rose about 15-fold after GM crops in 1996, and pre-harvest desiccation of oats, wheat and legumes explains high residues in some non-GM foods. IARC and other agencies reached different conclusions on cancer risk.

Metformin's glucose lowering is regulated by Rap1 in brain neurons, mouse study finds

Aug 03, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 15 · 💬 1 Insulin resistance & diabetes

A Baylor College of Medicine study in Science Advances found metformin acts largely through the protein Rap1 in SF1 neurons of the hypothalamus. Mice lacking Rap1 in the brain did not respond, and a tiny dose given into the brain lowered glucose. This is preclinical mouse research.
