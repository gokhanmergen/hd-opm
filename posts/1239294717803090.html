Omega-3 fats may blunt the post-meal inflammatory spike after high saturated fat and sugar

Louis Nervegna · Mar 05, 2026 · ♥ 10 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRPFats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Gut microbiome
AI summary & key facts

Explains that eating increases gut permeability, letting bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS) enter the blood and trigger inflammation, especially after meals high in saturated fat and sugar and low in fiber. Human studies suggest omega-3s reduce this response, though the clinical significance of the spikes is unclear.

  • Meals high in saturated fat/sugar and low in fiber amplify LPS leakage
  • Omega-3s dampen the post-meal LPS response in human studies
  • Clinical significance still unclear

It appears that omega 3 fats can blunt the post-prandial inflammatory spike that occurs when consuming meals high in saturated fat or sugar (and low in fiber).

It’s still unclear what the clinical significance is of these post-prandial inflammatory spikes, but it is very interesting that omega 3 fats seem to be able to block them.

The mechanism seems to be pretty well understood. Every time you eat, your gut lining becomes more permeable. This allows food to be absorbed, but also allows bad things to be absorbed as well, like the lipopolysaccharides that make up the outer membrane of your gut bacteria. These lipopolysaccharides cause inflammation when absorbed into the bloodstream. Meals high in saturated fat and sugar and low in fiber release the lipopolysaccharide from bacteria into the gut. Consuming omega 3 with a meal seems to prevent the transient absorption of these lipopolysaccharides from gut bacteria, and hence lowers the post-prandial spike in inflammation that normally occurs whenever you eat.
