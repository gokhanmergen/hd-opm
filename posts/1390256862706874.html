Vitamin K2 (MK-7) slowed coronary calcium progression in a 2-year randomized trial

Louis Nervegna · Sep 08, 2026 · ♥ 12 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post

A shared summary of a randomized trial in 180 adults with symptomatic coronary artery disease and existing calcium found that 360 mcg/day MK-7 slowed CAC progression versus placebo over two years. It did not remove existing calcium, CAC is a surrogate marker, and the trial was not designed to show fewer heart attacks.

  • 180 adults, 360 mcg/day MK-7 vs placebo for 2 years
  • Median CAC rise 69 Agatston units (placebo) vs 49 (MK-7), about 29% smaller increase
  • Calcium not removed; progression slowed
  • Surrogate endpoint; outcomes trial needed

Research cited

  • Vossen et al. vitamin K2 MK-7 and coronary calcification · RCT · JAMA Cardiology · 2026
Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

Vitamin K2 is often discussed in cardiovascular health because vitamin K is required to activate matrix Gla protein, one of the proteins involved in regulating vascular calcification. A new randomized trial provides direct human evidence that this pathway may have a measurable effect on coronary calcium progression.

Researchers randomized 180 adults with symptomatic coronary artery disease and existing coronary calcium to 360 µg/day of menaquinone-7 (MK-7) or placebo for two years. Coronary calcium increased in both groups, but progression was significantly slower with MK-7. Median CAC score increased by 69 Agatston units with placebo versus 49 with MK-7. Using those median changes descriptively, that is about a 29% smaller increase in the MK-7 group.

The important distinction is that vitamin K2 did not remove existing calcium. The signal was slower progression over time.

And coronary calcium is still a surrogate marker. This trial was not designed to show fewer heart attacks, strokes, or deaths, and the authors specifically note that the clinical meaning of the slower calcification remains uncertain. A larger outcomes trial would be needed before MK-7 could be considered established cardiovascular therapy.

So the finding is promising. Two years of MK-7 altered the trajectory of coronary calcification in people who already had coronary artery disease.

Reference:
Vossen LM, et al. JAMA Cardiol. 2026;11(8):719-726. PMID: 42268593. DOI: 10.1001/jamacardio.2026.1279.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.
