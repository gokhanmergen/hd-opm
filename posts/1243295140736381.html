Methylfolate is not 'natural', and higher blood levels may promote cancer

Louis Nervegna · Mar 11, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 33 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains food folate is polyglutamate-bound and less bioavailable than folic acid, which is less bioavailable than 5-MTHF supplements. Raising methylfolate too high may fuel precancerous cells and shortened lifespan in worms and mice.

  • Bioavailability: food folate < folic acid < methylfolate
  • High folate may fuel growth of existing precancerous lesions
  • Worm and mouse studies link high methylfolate to shorter lifespan

Methylfolate (5MTHF) supplements are not how folate is “naturally” found in food.
It is true that folate found in food is in the methylated form.

BUT the methylated folate found naturally in food is bound to polyglutamate chains that must be cleaved by the enzyme folate conjugase before absorption. This makes natural folate in food much less bioavailable than methylfolate (5MTHF) found in supplements. In fact, natural folate found in food is even less bioavailable than folic acid supplements. And folic acid supplements are in turn less biovailable than methylfolate (5MTHF) supplements.

Methylfolate (5MTHF) is the active form of folate found in the human body. This is true.

The problem is that raising levels of methylfolate too high may promote cancer growth in humans:

↪ Harvard Nutrition Source review of folate: a little is good, too much may be bad

In cancer research, folate has a dual nature. Adequate folate prevents the initiation of cancer by maintaining DNA integrity. However, if a person already has precancerous lesions or tumors, high doses of any folate can fuel the rapid division of those cells.

There is also some research in worms and mice that shows higher blood levels of methylfolate (5MTHF) shortens lifespan:

↪ High-dose methylfolate shortens lifespan in animals; stick to the RDA of folate

↪ Late-life folate restriction reduced biosynthesis without harming healthspan in mice

When you take methylfolate supplements, you raise methylfolate levels in your body more than an equivalent amount of folic acid or folate from food. These methylfolate supplements are extremely potent at raising methylfolate levels in vivo. It’s easy to take too much, which might be dangerous in the long term.

Folic acid supplements (at doses near the RDA) and folate from food have been studied for decades in randomized trials and long-running prospective cohort studies. We know they are safe at RDA level doses. For example, there are a number of papers that have come out of the Harvard Nurses Study that have shown that RDA level folic acid supplements are associated with lower risk of stroke and colon cancer. Other prospective cohort studies have confirmed this:

↪ Harvard Nutrition Source review of folate: a little is good, too much may be bad

A recently published meta-analysis of 45 randomized trials in a total of 96,962 people shows folic acid supplementation reduces the risk of stroke by 15%:

↪ Folic acid supplementation cut stroke risk 15% in a meta-analysis of 45 randomized trials

Folic acid must undergo a multi-step reduction process in the liver (via the enzyme DHFR) before it becomes 5-MTHF, whereas methylfolate bypasses this bottleneck. This makes folic acid less potent than methylfolate. Taking too much folic acid, however, can lead to unmetabolized folic acid circulating in the blood stream, which might itself be problematic. (Emphasis on “might”, because this is still being studied.) It is best to stick with doses close to the RDA.

Personally, I take 400mcg of folic acid every day (the RDA) to get those potential stroke and cancer benefits. If I took 400mcg of methylfolate instead, I would be raising my blood levels of methylfolate higher than I would be comfortable with. And I have no long term data to know whether or not I would get the same stroke and cancer protection from a low dose of methylfolate as with folic acid.

If you’re uncomfortable with folic acid, you can also buy food-grade folate in supplement form, or take lower doses of methylfolate to compensate for its higher potency. Or better yet, just eat foods naturally high in folate.
