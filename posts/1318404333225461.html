Magnesium L-threonate for 6 weeks improved cognitive age ~7.5 years (RCT, n=100)

Louis Nervegna · Jun 14, 2026 · ♥ 29 reactions · 22 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsBrain health & dementiaSleep, stress & social health
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 100 healthy adults aged 18-45 who were unhappy with their sleep. Magnesium L-threonate improved overall cognition, cognitive age, working memory and reaction time, and some subjective but not objective sleep measures. (Louis's post says six months, but the trial and article describe six weeks.)

  • 100 healthy adults aged 18-45 with self-reported poor sleep
  • 2 g/day magnesium L-threonate (145 mg elemental magnesium) vs placebo for 6 weeks
  • Improved overall cognition, cognitive age (~7.5 years), working memory, reaction time
  • Improvements in some subjective, but not objective, sleep measures

Six months of magnesium L-threonate supplementation reduced cognitive age by 7.5 years, and improved sleep quality.

What is remarkable about this study is that these were younger people, age 18-45yo.

Quoted:

This six-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial included 100 healthy adults aged 18-45. All participants reported dissatisfaction with their sleep quality at baseline, making them ideal candidates for testing both cognitive and sleep-related benefits.

Participants received either 2 grams daily of magnesium L-threonate (delivering 145mg elemental magnesium) or placebo for six weeks.

Quoted:

Participants taking magnesium L-threonate showed improvements across multiple cognitive domains, including overall cognition, working memory, and reaction time. Most remarkably, the supplement group demonstrated a reduction in cognitive age by approximately 7.5 years compared to placebo.

Cognitive age refers to how well someone’s brain performs relative to typical age-related standards. A 35-year-old performing at the level of a 27-year-old would show this type of cognitive age reduction.

Working memory—the mental workspace that allows us to hold and manipulate information temporarily—showed particularly strong improvements.

This type of memory proves essential for everyday tasks like following conversations, solving problems on the fly, and learning new information. Declines in working memory represent one of the earliest signs of cognitive aging.

Reaction time improvements also stood out, suggesting enhanced neural processing speed. Faster reaction times indicate more efficient communication between brain cells and better overall cognitive function.

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

A groundbreaking January 2025 study found that six weeks of magnesium L-threonate supplementation turned back participants’ cognitive age by about 7.5 years while significantly improving sleep quality.

This six-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 100 healthy adults used gold-standard cognitive testing and Oura Ring measurements to demonstrate superior brain penetration compared to traditional magnesium forms.

By binding magnesium to threonic acid, this formulation crosses the blood-brain barrier to enhance synaptic plasticity, neurotransmitter regulation, and neuroprotection.

The findings suggest practical benefits for those seeking cognitive and sleep enhancement, with more details and future research directions outlined in the full article.
