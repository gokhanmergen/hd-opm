  • Role of magnesium supplementation in the treatment of depression · RCT · PLoS One · 2017
Shared from Genuinely Healthy · original post

A randomized crossover clinical trial studied 126 adults with mild-to-moderate symptoms of depression. Participants received 248 mg of elemental magnesium per day, provided as magnesium chloride, for 6 weeks. Depression was measured using the PHQ-9 questionnaire.

During magnesium treatment, depression scores improved significantly, with a net difference of about 6 points compared with the control period. Interestingly, the researchers found that the improvement appeared within just 2 weeks of starting magnesium and continued throughout the treatment period.

Magnesium supplementation also improved anxiety symptoms. GAD-7 anxiety scores showed a net improvement of about 4.5 points compared with the control period. The researchers reported that magnesium was generally well tolerated, and most participants said they would use it again in the future.
