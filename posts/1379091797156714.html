Higher fiber intake linked to 53% lower mortality in 3,894 US adults with diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Aug 26, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

A shared summary of a cohort of 3,894 US adults with diabetes found the third quartile of fiber intake had a 53% lower relative risk of death than the lowest (HR 0.47), with about 13% mediated by lower systemic immune inflammation index. It is observational and does not prove fiber extends life by 53%.

Eating Fiber = Longevity Biohacking

FIBER MAY BE THE LEAST SEXY “LONGEVITY HACK,” BUT IT HAS FAR MORE EVIDENCE THAN MOST OF THEM.

A new cohort study examined 3,894 U.S. adults with diabetes and compared dietary fiber intake with subsequent all-cause mortality (PMID: 42499112 ). Compared with participants in the lowest quartile of fiber intake, those in the third quartile had a 53% lower relative risk of death after adjustment for demographics, lifestyle factors and several health conditions (HR: 0.47; 95% CI: 0.37–0.68). Important correction on what I said in the video, it wasn’t just Type I diabetes, it also included people with Type II diabetes

The researchers also estimated that approximately 13% of the association was statistically mediated by a lower systemic immune inflammation index. However, this does not prove that reducing inflammation was the mechanism but it is a possible mechanism

Important context:
This was an observational analysis, fiber intake was estimated through dietary interviews, and residual confounding is always possible. But this aligns with the broader research literature showing people who consume more fiber live longer than people who eat less (PMID: 40651334)

So no, this single study doesn’t prove that fiber will extend your life by 53%. But its findings are consistent with a much larger body of evidence associating higher fiber intake with better cardiometabolic health and lower mortality.
