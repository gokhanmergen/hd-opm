Age raises CVD death risk about 19.5x from 65 to 85, far above diabetes or smoking

Louis Nervegna · Dec 18, 2023 · ♥ 13 reactions · 20 comments · original Facebook post
Cardiovascular disease: the big pictureAging & longevityRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis compares hazard ratios from the SCORE O.P. risk model for people over 65. A hazard ratio of 1.16 per year of age compounds to about 19.5x higher CVD death risk between 65 and 85, far larger than diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure or LDL. The numbers used are for women; men are similar. (The post also writes this as 19,500%, which is a mislabel of 19.5x.)

  • Age HR 1.16/year: (1.16)^20 = ~19.5x from 65 to 85
  • Diabetes: 2.3x
  • Smoking: 1.88x
  • Systolic BP 40 mmHg higher: (1.07)^4 = 1.31x
  • LDL 40 mg/dL (1 mmol/L) higher: 1.13x

Age is by far the biggest risk factor for heart disease.

Let’s compare it to other major risk factors in a quantitative and rigorous way.

This table of hazard ratios for each of the major CVD risk factors is from a risk calculotor which is popular in Europe (called SCORE O.P.). This calculator risk model applies to people over 65, but similar risk calculators apply to middle age and younger people as well.

The hazard ratio of 1.16 for age in the table tells us that for each year of age above 65, your risk of dying from CVD goes up by 1.16x or 16%. This means that if you are 85 then your risk goes up by (1.16)^20 = 19.5x = 19,500%, compared to the average 65yo.

Simply aging 20 years from 65 to 85 raises your risk of dying from CVD by almost 20 times!

Compare this to other major factors (in the table) :

Having diabetes raises your risk by 2.3x.

Smoking raises risk by 1.88x.

Having a systolic blood pressure 40 mmHg above normal raises risk by (1.07)^4 = 1.31x

Increasing total cholesterol by elevating LDL by 40mg/dL (1 mmol/L) raises risk by 1.13x.

(Note that I used numbers from the table for women, in these calculations. The calculations for men are similar.)
