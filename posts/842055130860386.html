Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

Louis Nervegna · Jul 26, 2024 · ♥ 10 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinExercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A dose-response meta-analysis of 69 randomized trials found protein alone does not increase muscle strength without resistance training. With training, strength rose 0.72% per extra 0.1 g/kg/day up to 1.5 g/kg/day, with no further gain; going from the RDA to 1.5 g/kg adds only about 5% more strength.

  • 69 RCTs, mean duration 22 weeks
  • +0.72% strength (95% CI 0.40-1.04%) per 0.1 g/kg/day up to 1.5 g/kg/day
  • No benefit above 1.5 g/kg/day
  • RDA 0.8 to 1.5 g/kg adds about 5% strength with resistance training

How much protein should we consume as we get older to optimize strength and muscle function?

Below is a recently published meta-analysis of 69 randomized controlled trials testing the effects of protein consumption on muscle strength.

Summary- There are no benefits to increased protein consumption on muscle strength unless combined with resistance exercise. There are no benefits to increased protein consumption on muscle strength above 1.5g/kg body weight, even when combined with resistance exercise. Increasing protein consumption from 0.8g/kg (RDA) to 1.5g/kg adds an additional 5% increase in strength when combined with resistance exercise. The average time duration of the studies in this meta-analysis was 22 weeks.

Quoted:

“With resistance training, muscle strength increases at the rate of 0.72% (95% CI 0.40–1.04%) per 0.1 g/kg body weight [BW]/d increase in total protein intake up to 1.5 g/kg BW/d, but no further gains are achieved thereafter.”

“Muscle strength cannot be increased by protein supplementation only without resistance training.”

Note:

It’s important to understand how small the overall benefit of protein consumption is in this meta-analysis. For every 0.1g/kg increase in protein consumption above the RDA up to 1.5g/kg, muscle strength increases by only 0.72%.

Most of the benefit in muscle strength comes from the resistance exercise itself, with the extra protein of 1.5g/kg adding an incremental 5% increase in strength.
