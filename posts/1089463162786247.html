French fries, but not other potato dishes, linked to higher diabetes risk

AI summary & key facts

Louis reports that Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study analysis found french fries raised diabetes risk over 30 years, apparently due to trans fats, while boiled or baked potatoes did not versus the standard diet. Versus whole grains, all white potatoes were linked to higher risk.

  • 30-year follow-up
  • 3 weekly servings of fries raised type 2 diabetes risk 20% (Everyday Health)

French fries are associated with increased diabetes risk.

The latest publication from the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicians Followup Study links french fries with increased risk of diabetes over a 30 year followup period.

The main culprit appears to be trans fats.

When compared to the standard American diet as a whole, french fries raised diabetes risk, but potatoes cooked in other ways did not. However, when compared to healthier foods like whole grains, all forms of white potatoes were associated with higher risk.

Quoted:

“French fries are usually deep-fried at very high temperatures, often in oils, says Dr. Mousavi. During many of the years when the data was collected, that oil may have contained trans fats and other harmful compounds, he says.”

“This combination can lead to inflammation, damage blood vessels, and raise blood sugar levels. On top of that, potatoes themselves have a high glycemic load, meaning they can spike blood sugar quickly, especially when fried and salted,” says Mousavi.

Artificial trans fats were deemed unsafe to eat and banned by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2018.[3] More follow-up is needed to see if modern cooking methods [for frying] would be associated with the same risks, he says.”

“For this research, investigators followed more than 200,000 men and women enrolled in the Nurses’ Health Study, Nurses’ Health Study II, and Health Professionals Follow-up Study. For more than 30 years, the participants regularly responded to dietary questionnaires, detailing how often they ate certain foods, including whole grains and all manner of potatoes.”

“Using information gathered from the studies, the researchers determined that those who ate whole grains instead of french fries lowered their diabetes risk by 19 percent. The choice of whole grains over baked, boiled, or mashed potatoes reduced the risk 4 percent.”
