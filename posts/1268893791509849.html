Visceral fat, via aromatase, likely drives population-level testosterone decline

Shared from Barbell Medicine · original post

Visceral fat contains an enzyme called aromatase. Aromatase converts testosterone to estrogen. The fat stored around your organs does this continuously, at a rate that rises as the fat accumulates.

Every major study documenting the generational testosterone decline adjusted for obesity using BMI. BMI measures weight relative to height. It cannot see changes in visceral fat. Waist circumference tracks visceral fat directly in a way BMI cannot.

Men's waist circumferences have been increasing faster than BMI. The same number on the scale represented more visceral fat as the decades passed, and the obesity adjustments in these studies could not see any of it. As a result, the decline has been labeled as "unexplained".

In 2012, a team at the National Cancer Institute tested archived blood samples from 1988 through 2004 on the same mass spectrometry platform, in the same laboratory. They added waist circumference to the model alongside BMI. After adjusting for age, race, BMI, waist circumference, and lifestyle factors, there was no significant decline in total or free testosterone in the nationally representative sample.

Men who stay lean, active, and free of chronic illness show stable testosterone well into their 70s and 80s in longitudinal data.
