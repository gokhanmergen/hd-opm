Shingles vaccine tied to 46% fewer major cardiac events in high-risk people

Louis Nervegna · Mar 26, 2026 · ♥ 23 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesHeart attack, stroke & proceduresBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes growing evidence that the shingles vaccine protects against dementia, and a new study suggests it also protects the heart. In high-risk people it was associated with 46% fewer major cardiac events and 66% fewer deaths within a year, but this is observational and confounding by healthier behavior is possible.

  • Major cardiac events down 46% within a year
  • Deaths down 66% within a year
  • Observational association, not proof of causation
  • Randomized trials in Alzheimer's expected

Evidence has been accumulating for some time now that the shingles vaccine may protect against dementia. We are likely to see randomized trials testing it as a preventive/treatment for Alzheimer’s in the near future.

There have been multiple past posts about this in the group.

Now new research is also suggesting that the shingles vaccine could protect against heart attacks and cardiovascular-related deaths.

This is not all that surprising given how strong a link there is between cardiovascular disease and dementia. This is not the first study to show this. Previous studies have also suggested that the shingle vaccine lowers risk for CVD.

Please remember that this is an observational study. It only demonstrates an association between shingles vaccination and lower CVD risk. It does not prove causality. For example, it’s possible that people who get the shingles vaccine take better care of their health in general, compared to people who do not (confounding). That could also explain why their risk is lower, as opposed to a direct causal effect from the vaccine itself.

Quoted:

A simple shingles vaccine may secretly be one of the most powerful heart protectors yet.

A shingles vaccine might double as a powerful heart protector. In people already at high risk, it cut major cardiac events by 46% and deaths by an impressive 66% within a year. Scientists think preventing shingles may also stop clot-related complications that can lead to heart attacks and strokes. The effect is so strong, it rivals the benefits of quitting smoking.

The study reviewed data from more than 246,822 adults in the United States diagnosed with atherosclerotic heart disease, which is caused by plaque buildup in the arteries. The results add to growing evidence that the shingles vaccine may offer benefits beyond preventing shingles, including a reduced risk of conditions like heart disease and even dementia

This vaccine has been found over and over again to have cardioprotective effects for reducing heart attack, stroke and death," said Robert Nguyen, MD, a resident physician at the University of California, Riverside and the study's lead author. "Looking at the highest risk population, those with existing cardiovascular disease, these protective effects might be even greater than among the general public."

Earlier research has shown that shingles infections can trigger blood clot formation near the brain and heart, increasing the risk of heart attacks, strokes and venous thromboembolism. By preventing shingles, the vaccine may also help reduce the likelihood of these dangerous clotting events.

Researchers focused on heart-related outcomes occurring between one month and one year after vaccination (or the same timeframe for those who were not vaccinated). Across all measures, vaccinated individuals showed lower risk. They were 46% less likely to experience a major adverse cardiac event and 66% less likely to die from any cause. In addition, their risk of heart attack dropped by 32%, stroke by 25%, and heart failure by 25%.

Nguyen noted that these reductions are substantial and comparable to the benefits seen from quitting smoking. He added that the findings reinforce current recommendations for adults over age 50 to receive the vaccine.
