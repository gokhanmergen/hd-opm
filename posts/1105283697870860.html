Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.

Zoledronic acid cut fractures 35% and all-cause death 28% in hip-fracture patients

Article suggesting bisphosphonates may extend life, noting a NEJM trial of yearly zoledronic acid after hip fracture that reduced new fractures by 35% and deaths from any cause by 28%. It also mentions possible cancer, heart and aging benefits, which remain speculative.

Single dose of donor stem cells raised walking endurance about 20% in frail older adults

Feb 25, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 9 Aging & longevity

Describes a trial of donor bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells in 148 walking adults aged 70-85 with frailty (118 on varying doses, 30 on placebo). The cells lack immune-triggering surface proteins so no immunosuppressants were needed; at nine months the highest dose group walked about 60 m farther in a six-minute walk test than at baseline.

One zoledronate infusion cut vertebral fractures about 44% over 10 years in women 50-60

Jan 18, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 4 · 💬 9 Hormones, women's & men's health

Gokhan shares the NEJM 10-year randomized trial of infrequent zoledronate in early postmenopausal women (50-60 years) with normal-to-low-normal bone density. Zoledronate roughly halved new morphometric vertebral fractures versus placebo, and he calls it a longevity-type drug like statins or aspirin. The trial concerns bone rather than heart outcomes.
