Darker coffee roasts contain fewer polyphenols such as chlorogenic acid

Louis Nervegna · Jan 25, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes that more roasting lowers polyphenols like chlorogenic acid, sharing a post on a Spanish randomized crossover study. In it, lightly roasted, phenolic-rich coffee produced a small improvement in body composition compared with regular roast. Body weight and metabolic syndrome markers did not change.

  • Light roast: ~400 mg hydroxycinnamic acids and 130 mg caffeine per cup vs ~150 mg and 70 mg in regular roast
  • Body fat percentage fell 1.4% and muscle mass percentage rose 0.8%
  • No significant changes in body weight or metabolic syndrome markers
  • Light roast group consumed nearly twice the caffeine

The more you roast the coffee, the lower the polyphenols like chlorogenic acid.

Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

Recent findings from a randomized, controlled crossover study (PMID: 39275165) by the Department of Metabolism and Nutrition, Spanish National Research Council suggest a greater impact of phenolic-rich coffee on body composition compared to regular roast coffee.

Participants consuming lightly roasted coffee ~400 mg of hydroxycinnamic acids per cup and 130 mg of caffeine per cup (compared to ~150 mg hydroxycinnamic acid per cup, 70 mg of caffein per cup in regular roas) experienced:
✅ 1.4% reduction in body fat percentage
✅ 0.8% increase in muscle mass percentage

While both lightly roasted and standard roasted coffee showed benefits, light demonstrated superior results, suggesting it may be a reasonable tool for coffee users to improve metabolic outcomes. Of note, caffeine by itself has shown to support improved body composition in past research. The light roast coffee drinkers [in this case] were consuming nearly twice as much caffeine as those drinking regular roast.

Findings:

  • No significant changes in body weight or metabolic syndrome markers were observed.
  • Positive effects may be linked to chlorogenic acid(s).
  • Moderate coffee consumption (3 cups/day) could complement existing strategies for health improvement, particularly when combined with lifestyle modifications.
