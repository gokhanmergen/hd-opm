Whole-food plant protein beats animal protein for long-term outcomes in Harvard cohorts

Louis Nervegna · Mar 30, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes 30-year follow-up in the Harvard Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study: whole-food plant protein is associated with better outcomes, especially for heart disease. The benefit depends on the animal protein type: plant protein is only slightly better than fish, poultry and fermented dairy but clearly better than red and processed meat.

  • Cohorts tracked for about 30 years from middle age until death
  • Plant protein only slightly better than fish, poultry, fermented dairy
  • Much better than red meat and especially processed meat

Plant Protein vs. Animal Protein?
We now have good long-term data on this question of plant protein vs animal protein from high-quality prospective cohort studies like the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicans Followup Study:

  1. ↪ Protein type matters more than amount: processed meat worst, plant protein best
  1. facebook.com/share/v/1EpXpcu7nc

When hundreds of thousands of people are tracked prospectively for 30 years from middle age until death in the HNS and HPFS, outcomes are better for plant protein (from whole food) than for animal protein. This is especially true for heart disease.

HOWEVER, the benefit depends heavily on the type of animal protein. Plant protein only does a little better than fish, poultry, and fermented dairy. But it does a lot better than red meat and especially processed meat.

Remember the vast majority of the people in the HNS and HPFS are omnivores
