VITAL: vitamin D3 2,000 IU slowed telomere shortening ~140 base pairs over 4 years

Louis Nervegna · Jun 29, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post

Louis shares a summary of a VITAL ancillary study in 1,031 older adults in which daily vitamin D3 2,000 IU preserved leukocyte telomere length versus placebo, roughly 3.5-4.5 years of reduced biological aging. Omega-3 (1 g/day) showed no effect on telomere length. Telomere length is a surrogate marker.

Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

This is the first large RCT showing that vitamin D3 may help preserve biological aging markers.

Recent research (PMID: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2025.05.003) from the VITAL randomized controlled trial found that daily supplementation with 2,000 IU of vitamin D3 over four years significantly slowed leukocyte telomere shortening in older adults, a potential marker of reduced biological aging (see below for limitations.

The study followed 1,031 participants (from a larger cohort of 25,871 adults aged ≥50 years) and measured leukocyte telomere length at baseline, year 2, and year 4.

Findings:

  • Vitamin D3 supplementation preserved 140 base pairs of telomere length over 4 years compared to placebo (11% difference).
  • This corresponds to ~35 base pairs/year preserved, approximating 3.5–4.5 years of reduced biological aging.
  • Omega-3 fatty acid supplementation (1 g/day) showed no significant effect on telomere length at year 2 or year 4.
  • Vitamin D3’s protective effect remained significant after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, and other confounders.

Mechanism (theoretical):

Vitamin D may protect telomere integrity through its roles in reducing oxidative stress, regulating immune responses, and supporting genomic stability (vitamin D affects expression of 2500+ genes), all of which are known contributors to telomere erosion.

limitations of using leukocyte telomere length as a biomarker for aging:

  • Tissue Variability: leukocyte telomeres reflect telomere length in white blood cells, which may not represent telomere length in other tissues.
  • Lifestyle, stress, and acute or chronic conditions like inflammation can impact telomere measurements.
  • Variability in telomere measurement techniques, such as can lead to inconsistencies across studies.
  • Shortened telomeres are associated with aging and disease risk, but the exact causal relationships remain unclear.
