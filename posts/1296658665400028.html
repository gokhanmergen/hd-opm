Lowering triglycerides alone does not shrink plaque: olezarsen cut TG 64%, plaque flat

Louis Nervegna · May 20, 2026 · ♥ 22 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelPlaque, CAC & CT angiographyInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that unless triglycerides exceed about 400-500 mg/dL, drug-lowering them makes little difference to plaque or hard outcomes. In BRIDGE-TIMI 73a olezarsen cut triglycerides 64% with no plaque volume change; VLDL particles are atherogenic but are already counted by apoB.

  • BRIDGE-TIMI 73a: olezarsen lowered TG 64%, plaque volume unchanged
  • Drug-lowering TG matters little below 400-500 mg/dL
  • ApoB already captures VLDL risk
  • Severe hypertriglyceridemia still raises pancreatitis risk

He is right about this.

Unless triglycerides are above 400-500 mg/dL, lowering them with drugs makes little difference on plaque progression or hard outcomes like heart attacks and death.

Multiple large randomized trials have demonstrated this.

Triglycerides themselves are not a primary driver of atherosclerosis.

Triglycerides act mainly as proxy for metabolic health and insulin resistance.

Improving your metabolic health and insulin sensitivity WILL definitely lower your risk, but it is not because of triglycerides per se.

The VLDL particles that carry triglycerides are atherogenic.

HOWEVER, because these VLDL particles are ApoB-tagged, they are already included in measurements of ApoB.

So ApoB alone captures the risk of VLDL, without the need to include a separate measurement of triglycerides.

🚨 Triglycerides dropped 64% and plaque volume did not budge.
That is not a rounding error.
The data says lowering triglycerides alone is not enough.

I am a cardiologist.
I have seen patients obsess over their triglyceride numbers thinking that was the key to reversing heart disease.
Here is what the science actually says.

💓 The BRIDGE-TIMI 73a trial tested olezarsen, an RNA-targeted therapy that slashes triglycerides aggressively.
Triglycerides fell 64%.
Plaque volume did not change.
Not slightly. Not marginally. It did not move.

🔬 Why did this happen?

Triglycerides are a marker of metabolic dysfunction, not the primary driver of arterial plaque.

Plaque builds through LDL particles and ApoB-containing lipoproteins embedding in the arterial wall.

Lowering triglycerides without addressing LDL and ApoB leaves the core mechanism untouched.

Your triglyceride number reflects how your body handles dietary fat and sugar. It is not the engine of atherosclerosis.

❌ Triglycerides alone will not reverse plaque.
❌ A low triglyceride number with high ApoB is still dangerous.
❌ Treating triglycerides while ignoring LDL is treating the wrong target.

The tools with the strongest data are unsexy, free, and require your participation.

⚠️ Does this mean triglycerides do not matter at all?

No. Severely elevated triglycerides raise pancreatitis risk and signal insulin resistance.
But the question is no longer whether triglycerides are elevated.
The question is whether your LDL and ApoB are driving silent plaque progression right now.

🩺 A patient who gets their ApoB below 60 mg/dL, controls blood pressure, and eliminates smoking can halt measurable plaque progression within 12 to 24 months.
That is the difference between a stable coronary artery and one building toward a heart attack.

❤️ Bottom line:
Triglycerides are not the primary driver of plaque. LDL and ApoB are.
This finding comes from a rigorous randomized trial with direct plaque imaging, not survey data.
Know your ApoB. Push your LDL aggressively. Do not get distracted by a single metabolic marker.
64% reduction in triglycerides with zero plaque benefit is the clearest proof we have that target selection matters.
Are you asking your doctor for your ApoB number, or are you still only tracking your triglycerides?
