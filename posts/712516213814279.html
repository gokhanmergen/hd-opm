The longer the lower the better: lifelong LDL lowering and an estimated 60% risk reduction

Gokhan Can · Nov 20, 2023 · ♥ 17 reactions · 21 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelStatins
AI summary & key facts

Argues that the benefit of LDL lowering depends on both how much and how long LDL is reduced. The author, one of the group's admins, is betting on very low-dose statins to hold LDL at 70-100 mg/dL, and estimates about 60% lifetime risk reduction from a 70-point lowering sustained for 30 years. The post also cites Charles Grashow as a case of CAC regression.

  • Estimated about 60% lifetime risk reduction for a 70 mg/dL LDL drop sustained over 30 years (read from a chart)
  • Effects beyond about 80 mg/dL of lowering are unknown
  • Charles Grashow regressed CAC with high-dose statin, ezetimibe, nattokinase and lifestyle

The longer the lower the better

This group has three admins. Two of us (Louis Nervegna and I) are still in our 40ies. And we're betting on some very low dose statins to bring lifetime cardiovascular risk lower. Targeting essentially 70-100mg/dL LDL range --- I like 70 more than 100.

Charles Grashow is the only person I know who actually crushed his CAC (Agatson) score through high dose statin + ezetimibe + nattokinase plus a bunch of other supplements and off label drugs, plus proper lifestyle including exercise. He's the living proof that plaque can be reversed even after it became measurable.

I want to share this very important chart with you. x-axis is roughly how much cholesterol lowering you got (say through lifestyle or drugs you dropped your LDL by 70, then find 70 on the x-axis). Then find the curve roughly matching how long you lowered it. In my case, say, I have another 30 yrs of life expectancy then my expected risk reduction is right in the middle between blue and green line. So I can expect ~60% lifetime risk reduction by taking the lowest dose rosuvastation (5mg) that lowers my LDL by 70 points for 30 yrs.

This chart gives rise to the notion that "The longer the lower the better" for LDL (bad cholesterol). We don't know what happens at the extremes of this, i.e. when you lower, say, by 120 points. For up to ~80 mg/dL reduction we have an idea about what to expect. We also know that the longer you supress your LDL the better for reducing CVD risk.
