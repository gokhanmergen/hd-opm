Olive oil protected against cardiac hypertrophy in aging-model rats

Gokhan Can · Nov 23, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Aging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

In rats given D-galactose to mimic cardiac aging, eight weeks of olive oil reduced cardiac hypertrophy and oxidative stress (MDA), increased antioxidant activity (SOD) and improved expression of heart-health genes. It is an animal study, so human benefit is not shown.

  • Three groups of rats: control, D-galactose, D-galactose plus olive oil (8 weeks)
  • Lower MDA, higher SOD, less hypertrophy

Researchers investigated the protective effects of olive oil against aging-related cardiac problems in rats. The study, published in November 2024, used D-galactose (D-GAL) injections to induce aging-like conditions in the rats' hearts, and then examined how olive oil treatment affected various cardiac markers. The researchers divided the rats into three groups: a control group, a group receiving D-GAL injections, and a group receiving both D-GAL and olive oil treatment over an eight-week period.

The results showed that olive oil provided significant protective effects against aging-related cardiac dysfunction. Specifically, olive oil treatment improved several key markers: it reduced cardiac hypertrophy (enlarged heart tissue), decreased oxidative stress markers (MDA levels), increased antioxidant activity (SOD levels), and enhanced the expression of important genes related to heart health (SIRT1, PGC-1α, and TFAM). The study also found that olive oil helped reduce cardiac cell death (apoptosis) in the aging rats. These findings suggest that olive oil could be a promising dietary component for protecting against age-related heart problems, though the researchers note that additional clinical studies are needed to fully validate these protective effects in humans.
