Top Mediterranean diet adherence linked to 39% lower frailty in Nurses' Health Study

Louis Nervegna · May 25, 2024 · ♥ 13 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes a Nurses' Health Study analysis in women over 60 followed 1990 to 2010. Those with the highest adherence to a traditional Mediterranean-style pattern had a 39% lower risk of frailty than average adherers. Components independently linked to lower frailty included less red and processed meat, lower sodium, higher monounsaturated-to-saturated fat ratio, vegetables and moderate alcohol.

  • Women over 60, diet followed 1990-2010
  • 39% lower frailty risk for top vs average adherence to the Mediterranean-style score
  • Lower red/processed meat, lower sodium, higher MUFA:SFA ratio, vegetables and moderate alcohol were independently associated

Adherence to a traditional Mediterranean diet is associated with lower risk of frailty in the Harvard Nurse’s Study.

The nurses over 60yo who best adhered to the traditional mediterranean dietary pattern of eating over a 20 year period (1990 to 2010) had a 39% reduced risk of frailty compared to average adherence. (The top 3 standard deviations in diet score vs the mean score.)

“Lower consumption of red and processed meat, a lower sodium intake, a higher ratio of monounsaturated to saturated fat, vegetables, and moderate alcohol intake were components of the diet quality scores independently associated with lower risk of frailty.”
