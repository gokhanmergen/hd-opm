High-dose vitamin D with chemo raised complete response 2.3-fold in breast cancer RCT

A 2025 World Journal of Surgery randomized trial of 227 women receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy compared chemotherapy alone with chemo plus 50,000 IU vitamin D3 weekly. Vitamin D was associated with higher odds of pathologic complete response, but it was an adjunct and survival was not studied.

  • 50,000 IU vitamin D3 weekly throughout chemotherapy

Vitamin D didn’t “treat” cancer.
It changed how the body responded to chemotherapy.

A 2025 randomized controlled clinical trial published in the World Journal of Surgery followed 227 women with breast cancer receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy (chemo before surgery).

Participants were randomized to:

  • Chemotherapy alone
  • Chemotherapy plus vitamin D3

The vitamin D protocol:
50,000 IU of vitamin D3 once weekly, given throughout the entire chemotherapy course.

⸻

What outcome did researchers study?

They focused on pathologic complete response (pCR) — meaning no detectable cancer at surgery, a marker strongly linked to better long-term outcomes.

⸻

What did they find?

After adjusting for tumor stage, hormone receptor status, HER2 status, and proliferation markers:

  • 2.33× higher odds of achieving pCR with vitamin D
  • 95% CI: 1.20–4.53
  • Statistically significant (p = 0.013)

In plain language:
Women receiving vitamin D during chemotherapy were more than twice as likely to completely clear detectable tumor tissue by the time of surgery.

⸻

Why this matters

Chemotherapy works by damaging cancer cells.
This trial moves beyond theory and shows a measurable clinical effect in humans—suggesting physiology matters, not just drugs.

⸻

Important limitations (read this)

  • Vitamin D was adjunctive, not a standalone therapy
  • This was not a survival study (yet)
  • The dose was high-dose weekly, not casual daily supplementation
  • Patients were under medical supervision during active cancer treatment

⸻

What this does not mean

❌ Vitamin D does not replace chemotherapy
✅ It suggests that optimizing physiology may improve how treatments work

⸻
