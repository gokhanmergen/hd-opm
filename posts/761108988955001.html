Does a CAC scan change outcomes? Two 2026 randomized trials say not for the average person

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 31 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis asks whether CAC scanning changes whether you have a heart attack; for some it is a wake-up call, but for the average person it does not seem to change outcomes. The shared text cites a Danish trial of 30,000 men (no reduction in death, MI or stroke at seven years, more serious bleeding) and the US CorCal trial of nearly 6,000 people (same event rates as a risk calculator).

New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Aug 31, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 38 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.

Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.

Charles Grashow's protocol to regress coronary calcium over two years

Dec 02, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 7 ↩ 2 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Describes how Charles Grashow responded to worsening coronary calcium in 2020-2021 with an intensive regimen of lifestyle, high-dose nattokinase, LDL-lowering and anti-inflammatory supplements and off-label drugs. His later scans reportedly showed that the trajectory of his coronary disease had reversed. It is a single-patient story, not a trial.

About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.
