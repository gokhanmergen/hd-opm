The AHA recommends Mediterranean and DASH diets, backed by long-running randomized trials

Louis Nervegna · Sep 05, 2025 · ♥ 25 reactions · 29 comments · original Facebook post
Guidelines, policy & public healthMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis rebuts claims that the AHA's dietary guidance was corrupted by industry, noting that the AHA recommends traditional Mediterranean or DASH-style patterns. He points to large RCTs showing plaque reversal and fewer heart attacks, strokes and CVD deaths on those diets.

  • AHA recommends fruits, vegetables, whole grains, mostly plant proteins, fish, low-fat dairy and lean meats
  • Mediterranean and DASH diets supported by multiple large long-running RCTs

It’s an outright LIE that the current dietary recommendations from the American Heart Association (AHA) have been corrupted by the food industry.

The AHA recommends a traditional mediteranean diet or DASH diet.

The scientific evidence for these diets is overwhelming.

Multiple large long-running randomized trials have proven that they can reverse plaque and dramatically lower the risk of a having a heart attack, stroke, or dying of CVD:

↪ Meta-analysis of 4 Mediterranean diet RCTs: MACE down 48%, CVD death down 46%

↪ Six randomized diet trials showed plaque reversal, from Ornish to CORDIOPREV and DISCO

↪ DISCO-CT: DASH diet plus statins reversed soft plaque by about 40 mm3 in a year

You can find the AHA diet recommendations here:

www.heart.orgThe American Heart Association Diet and Lifestyle RecommendationsA healthy diet and lifestyle are the keys to preventing and managing cardiovascular disease.heart.org

Quoted from the AHA website:

Eat an overall healthy dietary pattern that emphasizes

  • A wide variety of fruits and vegetables
  • Whole grains and products made up mostly of whole grains
  • Healthy sources of protein (Mostly plants such as legumes, and nuts; fish and seafood; low-fat or fat-free dairy; and, if you eat meat and poultry, ensure it is lean and unprocessed.)
  • Liquid non-tropical vegetable oils such as canola, corn, olive, soybean and sunflower oils.
  • Minimally processed foods
  • Minimized intake of added sugars
  • Foods prepared with little or no salt
  • Limited or preferably no alcohol intake
