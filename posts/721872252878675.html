Co-author of LMHR study protocol says the design could not detect plaque progression

Louis Nervegna · Dec 10, 2023 · ♥ 4 reactions · 17 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivorePlaque, CAC & CT angiographyLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis paraphrases Dr. Spencer Nadolsky, who helped write the LMHR (keto hypercholesterolemia) study protocol, saying it enrolled only extremely healthy people with no diabetes, prediabetes, atherosclerosis, smoking, overweight or high hs-CRP and perfect blood pressure. Because soft plaque takes years to form even with very high LDL, little progression was likely after one year; enrolling people with existing atherosclerosis would have been more informative. The study's absence of progression should therefore not be read as proof of safety.

  • Participants had no diabetes, prediabetes, atherosclerosis, smoking history; normal BP, not overweight, low hs-CRP
  • Plaque formation takes many years even with high LDL
  • Original aim: monitor plaque in people with LDL-C above 400 mg/dL on keto
  • Better design: enroll people with existing atherosclerosis

One of the doctors who planned and wrote much of the original clinical protocol for the “LMHR study” has serious concerns about how the study was implemented and is now being interpreted.

You can read his full explanation in this Twitter stream. Note that these are full threads. The link is just to the very first tweet in the threads.

I will paraphrase the concern here:

The study only enrolled people who were extremely healthy with no diabetes and no atherosclerosis. People with any history of atherosclerosis were officially EXCLUDED. It takes many years for a very healthy non-diabetic with zero calcification to develop soft plaque and have this transform into calcified plaque, even with very high LDL. The choice of who could enroll made it very unlikely that there would be any detectable atherosclerosis after only one year.

What does extremely healthy mean? In addition to no diabetes or atherosclerosis, the people enrolled in this study had: no prediabetes, perfect blood pressure, not overweight, no history of smokimg, and very low inflammatory markers (hs-CRP).

A better designed study would have enrolled people who already had atherosclerosis and then monitored their progression— because progression of existing atherosclerosis happens much more rapidly.
