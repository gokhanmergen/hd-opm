Stuart Phillips preprint: muscle benefit of protein still caps at 1.6 g/kg

Louis Nervegna · Oct 08, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

Phillips posted a new, not peer-reviewed preprint reviewing the evidence and concluding that the muscle-related benefit of protein still tops out at about 1.6 g/kg per day, and only in people doing resistance training. He argues that most trials report lean mass, about half of which is not muscle, and that women's needs are not different from men's.

  • Ceiling: 1.6 g/kg/day (0.7 g/lb; about 120 g for a 75 kg person)
  • About 3 months of lifting adds 1.1 kg lean mass; extra protein adds about 0.3 kg
  • One trial doubling protein from 1.6 to 3.2 g/kg for 16 weeks showed no significant difference
  • Preprint has not been peer-reviewed

Shared from Stuart Phillips, Ph.D. · original post

It's still 1.6...

I've been told, in careful detail, that my protein number is too low. Fair enough. So I went back through the evidence and wrote it all down. It took a while..

My number is 1.6 g of protein per kilogram of body weight a day (0.7 g per pound, or about 120 g if you weigh 75 kg). After all that reading, it still does it, and there's no data to show otherwise. Literally no data. The entire field is a shambles of numbers that are based on far too many assumptions, many that I have held dear for a number of years

The preprint is here:

Paper · 2026Show me the muscle: how much can protein really add to exercise gains?doi.org

It hasn't been peer-reviewed. I posted it so anyone can check my sources and my arithmetic. The slides (on IG) are the short version, and here's the post that links to the reprint.

Greg Nuckols made the case for more protein carefully, with his work shown. Thank you, Greg. He's right that our 2018 estimate was soft, and we're closer than people think.

Where we differ is what we're measuring. Nearly every trial reports "lean mass," and about half of it isn't muscle. Take it at face value anyway: about three months of lifting adds 1.1 kg and extra protein adds 0.3 on top. One trial doubled protein from 1.6 to 3.2 for 16 weeks. The difference wasn't significant. But, and it's a big one, lean is not muscle mass. People have known this for some time, and it really does matter.

Women do NOT have protein requirements different than men. Menopause does not change this. Lifting is ALWAYS the big stimulus.

Like eating more protein? Fill your boots. My objection is to telling people they NEED it. And if I'm wrong, the preprint describes the trial that would show it.

Until better data is available: lifting bakes the cake and protein adds a (very) thin layer of icing!
