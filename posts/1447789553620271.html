Semaglutide's 3.1-year biological age reversal rests on one DNA clock

In 2025, a company claimed semaglutide reversed biological age by 3.1 years. Is that real?

It started with lizard venom at the Bronx VA in 1992 and moved through Novo Nordisk's albumin trick to Ozempic in 2017.

The outcome trials like LEADER, SELECT and FLOW showed real drops in heart events and deaths.

The 3.1-year number came from a single DNA aging clock measured over 32 weeks and scaled up to a yearly rate. The evoke Alzheimer's trials didn't slow decline. A 2026 study found older female mice on semaglutide lived about 12% longer.

Semaglutide clearly lowers the risk of the diseases that shorten lives. Whether it slows human aging is still open.

Semaglutide extended female mouse lifespan 12%, but a 24% calorie drop likely drove it

Sep 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)

Louis shares Luigi Fontana's take on a Nature study in which semaglutide started at 20 months extended median mouse lifespan by 12% (742 to 834 days). Food intake fell about 24%, so much of the benefit probably reflects calorie restriction. The shared text cautions that it is not evidence of 10 extra years in humans.
