Does eating less protein help you live longer? A review of 350+ studies gives a nuanced answer.

Knopf and Lamming University of Wisconsin-Madison pulled together the evidence on protein and amino acid restriction.

Key insights:

  • Restricting methionine, or the branched-chain amino acids isoleucine and valine, drives many of the metabolic benefits seen in animals
  • The hormone FGF21 is part of the story
  • Benefits in animal models have not been shown to extend human lifespan

What it means for interventions: the field is moving from "less protein" toward which amino acids matter, and human trials are the missing piece.

Older and physically active adults may need more protein, not less, so this is not a case for cutting protein.
