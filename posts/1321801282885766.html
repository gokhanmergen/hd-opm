Tirzepatide raised testosterone more than TRT in men with obesity (pilot, n=83)

Louis Nervegna · Jun 18, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Hormones, women's & men's healthGLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)Weight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a small controlled pilot study in obese men with metabolic hypogonadism. Two months of tirzepatide gave greater weight loss and increases in testosterone, LH, FSH and SHBG while lowering estradiol, compared with no treatment or transdermal testosterone.

  • 83 patients with obesity, mean age 55: tirzepatide (n=28), no treatment (n=30), transdermal testosterone (n=25)
  • Tirzepatide 2.5 mg weekly first month then 5 mg
  • After 2 months, tirzepatide group had larger reductions in weight, waist and fat mass and gains in lean mass
  • Higher LH, FSH, SHBG, total and free testosterone; lower E2 than both comparison groups

A recent small RCT demonstrates weight loss raises testosterone more than TRT, in patients who are obese.

Link to full study:

Paper · Reprod Biol Endocrinol · 2025Short-term impact of tirzepatide on metabolic hypogonadism and body composition in patients with obesity: a controlled pilot study.Conclusion: The results of this study suggest that TZP is effective in improving both metabolic parameters, ED, and gonadal hormone levels in patients with obesity and metabolic hypogonadism. These findings position TZP as a promising treatment for obese patients with functional hypogonadism arising from metabolic-related alterations.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Quoted:

“A total of 83 patients with obesity (mean age 55.3 ± 5.5 years) were included in the study, divided into three groups: Group A (28 patients, mean age 56.3 ± 4.7 years), Group B (30 patients, mean age 55.1 ± 5.2 years), and Group C (25 patients, mean age 54.0 ± 6.5 years).”

“Group A received 2.5 mg of TZP [tirzepatide] weekly for the first month, with the dose increased to 5 mg from the second month; Group B received no pharmacological treatment: Group C received transdermal testosterone.”

“After 2 months, Group A showed significantly greater reductions in body weight, WC [waist circumference], BES score, and fat mass, along with a notable increase in LM [lean mass] and IIEF-5 score compared to Groups B and C. Additionally, Group A exhibited significantly higher serum levels of LH, FSH, SHBG, TT, fT, and bioT, while E2 levels were significantly lower than both Groups B and C.”

Shared from Menno Henselmans · original post

Fat mass produces aromatase, an enzyme that converts testosterone (T) into estrogen. So the more fat you have, the more of your T turns into estrogen. In men, this can even cause obesity-induced hypogonadism (clinically low T) and symptoms of feminization like gynecomastia ('gyno').

A recent study found that significant weight loss in overweight individuals with low T increased T more than T replacement therapy (TRT). And it raised T while reducing rather than further increasing estrogen levels, on top of obviously resulting in better metabolic health outcomes.

TRT is very popular these days (and often used as a euphemism for taking anabolic steroids), but it should be a last resort for medical purposes. Getting a healthy body composition, lifestyle and diet should be the first steps. Often, those are the only steps you need.
