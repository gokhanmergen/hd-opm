Big hsCRP drops on carnivore or vegan diets mostly reflect weight loss and liver fat loss

Louis Nervegna · Oct 10, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRPWeight, visceral & liver fatLow-carb, keto & carnivore
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that when people on carnivore/keto see a large fall in hsCRP, they usually lost a lot of weight and cleared ectopic liver fat, which removes insulin resistance and lowers hsCRP. He notes whole-food plant-based vegan dieters see the same effect, so the shared factor is weight loss, not the diet itself.

  • Liver fat clearance improves insulin resistance and lowers hsCRP
  • Polar-opposite diets (carnivore and vegan) give the same hsCRP drop when weight is lost
  • Louis cites Roy Taylor-type RCTs on liver fat (links in group posts)

In most cases where someone went on a carnivore/keto diet and saw a big drop in hsCRP, they lost a lot of weight and cleared their liver of ectopic fat.

When you clear your liver of fat like this, insulin resistance disappears and hsCRP falls like a rock:

↪ Carnivore, keto and vegan diets reverse diabetes via the same route: weight and fat loss

Read the posts in the group under the tags #roytaylorHD and #liverfatHD. This is proven in large RCT’s.

Any diet can do this. There is nothing magical about a carnivore diet. People on WFPBNO/vegan diets see the same huge drop in hsCRP when they lose a lot of weight and clear their livers of fat— and this is literally the polar opposite diet.

Then the vegans and carnivores fight with each other and claim that the other side is lying. Each claims that their diet magically cured them of all their ailments. In reality, it was all mostly just due to the weight loss (specifically the reduction in liver fat), including the big drop in hsCRP.

The fact that you can achieve the exact same results with two polar opposite diets confirms this. Literally the only thing that these two diets have in common is the major weight loss. They are opposites in every other way.
