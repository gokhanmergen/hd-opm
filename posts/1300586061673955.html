Injecting the missing AIM protein may double cat lifespan by preventing kidney disease

Louis shares a post on Toru Miyazaki's work: cats have a non-functional AIM gene, which stresses the kidneys, and kidney failure is their main cause of death. Louis frames it as each species having its own lifespan-limiting disease (kidney disease in cats, heart disease in humans). Claim is from a social post, not a cited paper.

  • Cats' AIM gene is broken; kidney failure is the leading cause of death
  • Working AIM protein injection claimed to double lifespan

This one gene might dramatically extend lifespan in cats.

Every mammalian species has specific diseases that idiosyncratically shorten their lifespan.

In cats, it is kidney disease.

In humans, it is heart disease.

Shared from Zi Teng Wang · original post

Japanese scientist Dr Toru Miyazaki just developed an injection that can double the lifespan of your cats. Wanna know how it works?

You know how biology is a vibe-slopped broken mess? Like, we humans get scurvy because our vitamin C synthase gene is broken, and that was fine because our ancestors had enough fruit, until we started spending long months at sea eating only carbs and protein and sailors started bleeding from wounds reopening and teeth falling out.

Cats have their own broken genes too. The average housecat lives to about 15, and most cats die of kidney failure. There's this gene called AIM which helps the kidneys work, and the AIM gene is broken in cats. This increases stress on cat kidneys and causes Chronic Kidney Disease.

Dr Miyazaki of Tokyo University thought, what happens if you just give cats an injection of the working AIM protein?

He lost his research funding during the Covid pandemic, but crowdsourced $2m of funding from cat lovers to continue his research.

26 cats recently diagnosed with chronic kidney disease. More than 80% of cats who got AIM injections survived through the next year, and fewer than 20% of untreated cats did.

Dr Miyazaki's team published their results in April 2026, is already applying for veterinary approval, and this may be available by 2027.

They estimate that this drug to help cat kidneys will double the lifespan of the average housecat, from 15 to 30 years.

Thank you to the scientists who did the work, and the cat lovers who crowdfunded it and helped make it happen!
