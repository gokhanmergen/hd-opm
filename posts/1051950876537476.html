Harvard/MGH recruits for a randomized trial of white potatoes and endothelial function

Louis Nervegna · Jun 21, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital trial, recruiting healthy men aged 18 to 40, that studies short-term effects of white potato intake on endothelial function and inflammation. It follows observational links between potatoes, hypertension, diabetes and other CVD risk factors.

  • Recruiting healthy males aged 18-40
  • Screening visit plus four 2-4 hour visits with pre-made meals
  • Tests short-term effects of white potatoes on endothelial function and inflammation

Do plain white potatoes damage endothelial function and promote arterial inflammation?

Scientists at Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital believe that they do, and are conducting a rigorous randomized feeding trial to find out.

Recent studies link white potato consumption to hypertension, diabetes, and other risk factors for CVD. This study will explore the biological mechanisms underlying these associations.

Details of this clinical trial can be found in this post: ↪ Harvard/MGH feeding trial tests whether white potatoes harm endothelial function

Quoted:

“Together, recent publications linking increased potato consumption with hypertension, diabetes and other cardiovascular risk factors, shed doubt upon the Institute of Medicine report citing that insufficient evidence to prove white potatoes have adverse health effects. This has led to our current proposal of assessing the association between potatoes intake and hypertension, and understanding the possible mechanisms underlying this association to provide further evidence that may help with prevention of hypertension and higher risk of cardiovascular disease.”

“We are studying the short term effects of white potato intake on endothelial function and inflammation. In recent years, several changes have been made to government sponsored food programs with respect to potatoes. It was recently found that higher white potato intake was associated with increased blood pressure (hypertension) in three studies. This association was more pronounced in younger individuals. These findings, in light of the recent relaxation of potato restrictions on the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants and Children (WIC), could translate into higher young adult BP in the population and ultimately higher heart disease burden.”

“We hope to investigate the short term effects of white potato intake on endothelial function and inflammation. To accomplish this, we will perform a randomized feeding trial to examine the effects of one additional serving a day of white potatoes compared to one serving a day of non-starchy vegetable on endothelial function (measured by brachial artery ultrasound). We will also investigate the effects of an additional serving of white potatoes on inflammatory biomarkers (C-reactive protein and interleukin-6). Our goal is to assess the association between potato intake and hypertension, and attempt to understand the possible mechanisms underlying this association.”

Note:

These are some of the best nutritional epidemiologists the world. They are well-aware of confounding effects of frying, saturated fat, trans fats, vegetable oils, excess sodium, etc. They are testing plain white potatoes, prepared in simple ways. They know that the study would be useless if they did not do that. If they put salt on the potato when cooking it, then they will put the same amount of salt on the non-starchy vegetable that is being used as the control… and so on. The whole point of this study is to isolate the health effects of white potatoes by themselves from confounders. Removing potential confounders is one of the reasons why you run an RCT in the first place, instead of just relying on observational data.
