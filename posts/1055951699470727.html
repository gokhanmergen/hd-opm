Protein triggers more insulin, so CGM glucose-spike hacks mislead

Louis Nervegna · Jun 27, 2025 · ♥ 16 reactions · 29 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesProtein
AI summary & key facts

Louis criticises a CGM influencer claim that adding protein such as whey to fruit lowers glucose spikes. He argues protein forces larger insulin responses than equivalent carbohydrate calories, so lower glucose does not mean lower metabolic stress.

  • Whey elicits more insulin than equal calories of white bread or glucose
  • Red meat elicits more insulin than oatmeal or white pasta

Most influencers pushing CGM’s don’t know what they’re talking about. Like this guy…

The reason that combining a protein source (like whey protein powder) with fruit lowers blood sugar spikes is because protein forces the pancreas to produce large amounts of insulin.

For example, whey protein forces the pancreas to produce considerably more insulin than an equivalent number of calories from white bread or pure glucose:

↪ Whey protein raised insulin about twice as much as glucose or white bread per calorie

Red meat forces the pancreas to produce more insulin than an equivalent number of calories from oatmeal or white pasta:

↪ Beef and whey raise insulin more than pasta, oatmeal or glucose: use the insulin index

When you force your pancreas to produce a lot of insulin, then your blood sugar will obviously be lower as a result.

This is the same way that sulfonylurea drugs work to control blood sugar spikes in diabetics. They force the pancreas to produce more insulin.

So by combining a protein source like whey powder with fruit, you may be lowering the blood sugar spike— but you are also dramatically increasing the amount of insulin that your pancreas produces.

The end result is HIGHER insulin levels, not lower.

If you don’t know exactly what you are doing, then using a CGM to adjust your food choices like this may actually be RAISING your insulin levels after meals, instead of lowering them. You may be causing more harm than good.
