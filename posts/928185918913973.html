Whey protein is highly insulinogenic; Willett wary of powders and IGF-1 cancer risk

Louis Nervegna · Dec 13, 2024 · ♥ 15 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues processed whey spikes insulin more than glucose or white bread and may raise insulin/IGF-1, which promote cancer cell growth. He cites Walter Willett's 2017 book cautioning that protein supplements add little muscle without exercise and that BCAAs increase IGF-1, which may raise breast and prostate cancer risk.

  • Willett: protein supplements without exercise have only a small effect on muscle
  • BCAAs (leucine, isoleucine, valine) turn up IGF-1
  • IGF-1 excess linked to breast, prostate and probably other cancers per Willett
  • Concern is a hypothesis about long-term cancer risk

Walter Willett shares my concern that processed whey and other processed protein powders may increase the risk for cancer in the long term by dramatically raising levels of insulin/IGF1.

Whey protein powder has an extremely high insulin index. Calorie for calorie, it spikes insulin levels much more than even pure glucose or white bread:

↪ Whey protein raised insulin about twice as much as glucose or white bread per calorie

↪ Beef and whey raise insulin more than pasta, oatmeal or glucose: use the insulin index

There is no food on the planet that raises insulin/IGF1 levels more than processed whey protein. It is probably the most insulinogenic food that we know of. This is why bodybuilders love it. Insulin and IGF1 directly promote growth. The problem is that they also promote growth of cancer cells.

It’s a big red flag when the most distinguished nutritional epidemiologist in the world is seriously worried about something like this.

There is a real possibility that this processed protein shake craze may be causing more harm than good in the long term. People should at least be aware of this.

From p149 of his 2017 book:

“Fast Fact: Be Wary of Protein Supplements—
Athletes, bodybuilders, and others take protein supplements, such as whey protein, to build muscle. Taking protein supplements without exercising has only a small effect on adding muscle and is no replacement for exercise. It's an expensive strategy that isn't any better than a high-protein diet. In addition, trying to rev up muscle growth with protein supplements might be accompanied by a revving up of cancer cells that lurk around for years and don't show up until far down the road (see "High Quality Protein: Is Too Much A Bad Thing?" on page 134).”

From page 134:

“Three essential amino acids- leucine, isoleucine, and valine, the so-called branched-chain amino acids- in high-quality protein turn up production of insulin-like growth factor (IGF-1). This hormone does what its name suggests: stimulates growth. Too much of it, though, increases the risks of developing breast, prostate, and probably other cancers.

Milk and dairy foods are excellent sources of high-quality protein. That's why they are so good for young children. But drinking too much milk throughout life may overstimulate growth.”

Whey protein raised insulin 90% more than white bread despite 57% lower glucose response

May 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 17 Protein

William Wallace shares the Nilsson 2004 trial: in 12 healthy volunteers, whey produced a 90% higher insulin AUC and 57% lower glucose AUC than white bread, tracking plasma branched-chain amino acids. He argues repeated large insulin surges raise IGF1 and mTOR and may have long-term costs, disagreeing with a source that dismisses the concern.

NEJM 2020 dairy review: a mixed bag, best for fermented forms, weak for fractures

Jan 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 21 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis highlights the widely cited 2020 NEJM review on dairy and disease risk. It concludes that high dairy intake is not supported for preventing fractures, is not clearly linked to weight, diabetes or CVD, and may raise prostate and endometrial cancer risk while lowering colorectal cancer risk. Effects depend on the comparison food: dairy fares better than processed red meat or sugary drinks but worse than nuts.

Whey protein raised IGF-1 but not muscle or strength in 24-week RCT of older adults

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 17 Protein

A 24-week placebo-controlled trial raised protein to 1.5 g/kg/day with whey in healthy older adults. It did not improve muscle power, strength, performance or mass, though IGF-1 rose; potassium bicarbonate was also ineffective. Louis argues this means the harms of high IGF-1 without benefit.
