2025 US Dietary Guidelines

We are on the verge of getting new dietary guidelines in the USA.

Sadly, these are likely to be filled with nonsense and misinformation.

We should ask the question:

What would the 2025 dietary guidelines look like if they actually followed the recommendations of the respected scientists on the advisory committee?

It’s easy to answer this question.

Those recommendations were detailed in the “Scientific Report of the 2025 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee”, which is still publicly available. See below:

dietaryguidelines.govScientific Report of the 2025 Guidelines Advisory Committee | Dietary Guidelines for Americansdietaryguidelines.gov

The following is a short summary of that scientific report…

(Note that these recommendations clearly match those of a traditional Mediterranean / DASH diet. This is obvious upon reading them.)

The 2025 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee's report recommends increasing consumption of fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts, whole grains, and plant-based proteins, while reducing intake of red and processed meats, saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars. Key recommendations include reorganizing the protein food group to prioritize plant-based proteins like beans and lentils, encouraging a flexible "Eat Healthy Your Way" approach, and emphasizing plain water and low-fat dairy while limiting sugar-sweetened beverages.

Key dietary recommendations:

  • Increase plant-based proteins: Emphasize beans, peas, lentils, nuts, seeds, and seafood over red and processed meats.
  • Focus on whole foods: Prioritize vegetables, fruits, and whole grains.
  • Reduce red and processed meats: Decrease consumption of red and processed meats, as current intake is at or above recommendations.
  • Limit certain foods: Reduce foods and beverages high in added sugars, saturated fat, and sodium.
  • Reorganize protein group: The report suggests a new structure for the protein foods group, placing beans, peas, and lentils first, followed by nuts, seeds, and soy products, then seafood, and finally meats, poultry, and eggs.
  • Emphasize water: Drink plain water and nutrient-rich beverages like low-fat milk while reducing sugar-sweetened beverages.

Approach and flexibility:

  • "Eat Healthy Your Way" model- The committee proposed a unified and flexible dietary pattern to accommodate a variety of individual preferences and needs.
  • Flexibility: The new approach allows for different dietary patterns to meet the guidelines, as long as they are consistent with the overall recommendations.
  • Focus on overall pattern: The recommendations focus on the entire dietary pattern rather than specific nutrients in isolation.
