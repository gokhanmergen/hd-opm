Man with Alzheimer's gene stayed healthy; Louis speculates heat exposure may help

Louis Nervegna · Feb 15, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an NPR story about a man genetically destined for early Alzheimer's whose brain remained healthy at 75, speculating that decades of heat in navy engine rooms raised protective heat shock proteins. The researchers are unsure of the cause and this is a single case.

  • Heat shock proteins may limit tau spread, per the quoted story
  • Researchers are unsure of the explanation

Your genes are not your destiny.

We can control the expression of genes with diet, exercise, lifestyle, medication, supplements, etc.

In this particular case, he may have accidently protected himself by being exposed to constant heat in his job in the navy. Heat is hormetic and can raise the levels of defensive chemicals (heat shock proteins) in the brain. Sauna might also do this.

Quoted:

“Scientists told Whitney that the high levels of heat shock proteins in his brain might be the result of his life experience.

"I spent 20 years in the Navy, most of it in the hot spots, like the engine rooms of ships—110 degrees is nothing on a ship," he says.

That environment may have caused an increase in heat shock proteins, which were able to limit the spread of tau and prevent the onset of Alzheimer's.

The scientists studying Whitney aren't sure if that's all, or even part, of the explanation. But they are hoping that the paper on Whitney will encourage other researchers to look for answers.”

Shared from NPR · original post

Doug Whitney was supposed to develop Alzheimer's by 50. Now scientists are trying to understand why his brain remains healthy at 75.
