Urolithin A, a pomegranate gut metabolite, improved HFpEF in mice and human heart tissue

Louis Nervegna · Aug 20, 2026 · ♥ 20 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A Science Advances study from King's College London found that urolithin A activates the heart-protective protein PKGIα at cysteine 42, improving heart relaxation, fibrosis and exercise activity in mice with HFpEF and relaxing engineered human heart tissue. About half of people produce urolithin A from pomegranate; human trials are lacking, so benefit for patients is unproven.

  • Mechanistic study in mice and engineered human heart tissue (in vitro)
  • Urolithin A improved relaxation, reduced fibrosis and heart muscle enlargement in HFpEF mice
  • About half of people make urolithin A in the gut after eating pomegranate
  • Human studies are lacking

A new mechanistic study in rodent models and human cells (in vitro) suggests that Urolithin A might help with heart failure.

About half of people produce Urolithin A naturally in their gut, in response to eating pomegranate or drinking pomegranate juice. It might be responsible for some of the purported health benefits of pomegranate juice.

It is possible that pomegranate juice or Urolithin A supplements might help patients with heart failure, although human studies are lacking.

Research cited

  • Targeting PKGIα Cys 42 attenuates cardiac dysfunction in HFpEF · animal · Science Advances · 2026
Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Researchers from King’s College London published the study in Science Advances on August 19, 2026. They investigated whether targeting a specific site on the heart-protective protein PKGIα, called cysteine 42 (Cys42), could improve heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). They found that urolithin A directly activated this pathway, improved heart relaxation, reduced fibrosis and heart muscle enlargement, and increased exercise activity in mice with HFpEF.

The researchers then tested urolithin A in engineered human heart tissue made from human stem-cell-derived heart cells. It improved both contraction and relaxation speed, supporting the same mechanism seen in mice. The study identifies PKGIα Cys42 as a potential new drug target for HFpEF and urolithin A as a promising experimental compound, but it has not yet been shown to treat HFpEF in patients.
