Green Mediterranean diet with walnuts cut liver fat about 39% in an 18-month RCT

Louis Nervegna · Sep 08, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares the DIRECT-PLUS trial in Gut, where a Mediterranean diet enriched with green tea, the aquatic plant Mankai and walnuts and restricted in red and processed meat reduced liver fat most among 294 adults with abdominal obesity. He emphasizes liver fat as a root cause of insulin resistance for most people.

  • 294 adults with abdominal obesity, 18 months
  • All three diets lost liver fat; green-MED dropped an average of 39%
  • Compared with standard counseling and a regular Mediterranean diet

A traditional Mediterranean diet rich in green plant matter and walnuts reduces liver fat by 40%.

This was an 18 month randomized controlled trial.

The diet restricted both processed meat and red meat.

(As we’ve talked about many times in the group, liver fat is the root cause of insulin resistance in most people.)

Here is a link to the full journal paper:

PaperThe BMJ articlegut.bmj.com

Quoted:

“The so-called green Mediterranean diet—which includes daily consumption of green tea and an aquatic plant called Mankai, both of which contain beneficial compounds known as polyphenols—was also found to reduce liver fat more than the other two healthy diets tested during the study.”

“During an 18-month clinical trial, the researchers followed 294 adults with abdominal obesity. They were divided into three groups with different dietary regimens: standard nutritional counseling, a Mediterranean diet, and the green Mediterranean diet.

All three groups lost liver fat, but those in the green Mediterranean diet group had the greatest reduction. They dropped an average of 39% of liver fat compared to a 20% reduction with the traditional Mediterranean diet, and 12% with standard nutritional counseling.”
