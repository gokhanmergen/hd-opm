Nurses' Health Study: 1-3 daily cups of coffee in midlife linked to healthier aging

Louis Nervegna · Jun 02, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 37 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares CNN coverage of Harvard Nurses' Health Study results. Women who drank one to three cups of caffeinated coffee daily in their 50s were more likely to reach older age free of major chronic disease with good cognitive, physical and mental health.

  • 1-3 cups/day caffeinated coffee in the 50s
  • Better odds of healthy aging and freedom from 11 chronic diseases (per CNN summary)

The latest results from the Harvard Nurses Study show that women who regularly consume coffee in middle age, experience healthier aging as they get older, and have lower odds of developing chronic disease in old age.

Quoted:

“Women who drank one to three cups of caffeinated coffee per day in their 50s were more likely to reach older age free from major chronic diseases and with good cognitive, physical, and mental health,” said lead study author Dr. Sara Mahdavi, adjunct professor in the faculty of medicine and department of nutritional sciences at the University of Toronto.

“Researchers analyzed dietary data from more than 47,000 women who were part of the Nurses’ Health Study, according to the research released Monday at the annual meeting of the American Society for Nutrition in Orlando.”

“The women were surveyed in middle age and followed for 30 years to understand their rates of death and disease.”

“In this study, we found that moderate caffeinated coffee consumption during midlife was associated with a higher likelihood of healthy aging 30 years later,” Mahdavi said.
