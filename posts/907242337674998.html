Probiotics might counter statin-linked blood sugar rise via gut microbiota, editorial says

Louis Nervegna · Nov 09, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsGut microbiomeInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an ATVB editorial describing statin-induced gut microbiota changes as a candidate mechanism for statin-associated new-onset type 2 diabetes. The editorial says causality still needs larger sequencing studies. Louis adds that in JUPITER, high-dose rosuvastatin raised A1C by 0.1, bringing diabetes diagnosis about 5.4 weeks earlier over 2 years, a small but real effect.

  • Editorial: statins change gut microbiota, a possible mechanism for new-onset diabetes (SANOD)
  • Causal link unproven; larger prospective studies needed
  • JUPITER: A1C up 0.1 on high-dose rosuvastatin after 2 years
  • Diabetes diagnosed 5.4 weeks earlier on average over 2 years

Avoiding a blood sugar rise on statins might be as simple as taking a good probiotic.

Quoted:

“In this issue of the journal, it is reported that statins induced changes in gut microbiota in a long-term epidemiological setting with a large population-based sample.”

“The functions of gut microbiota include regulation of bile acid metabolism and lipid absorption, inflammatory signals via lipid synthesis, and energy metabolism. Therefore, disruption of these functions, known as dysbiosis, can cause metabolic disorders including the development of T2D (Figure).”

“To elucidate the causal relationship between statin-induced changes in gut microbiota and SANOD risk, further prospective studies on large-scale population-based cohorts are needed to investigate associations using whole or shallow shotgun sequencing. The precise mechanisms as to how each bacterium changes in prevalence upon statin administration and the subsequent risk of SANOD remain to be elucidated.”

Paper · ATVB · 2024Changes in Gut Microbiota After Statin Administration: A New Candidate Mechanism for Statin-Associated New-Onset Type 2 Diabetesahajournals.org

Note: The editorial refers to the JUPITER trial which showed a 0.1 increase in A1C in participants on high dose rosuvastatin after 2 years, compared to controls. The result was that people on the high dose statin were diagnosed with diabetes 5.4 weeks earlier than controls, on average, over this 2 year period. This effect of statins on blood sugar in the trial was small, but real. See post below:

Statins raise type 2 diabetes risk 9-12% relative, a tiny absolute effect for most people

May 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 4 Statins

Louis explains that in roughly 5-year RCTs, statins raise relative risk of new type 2 diabetes by 9-12% (20-36% at very high intensity). In absolute terms this is about 1 in 1000 going to 1.12 in 1000, so low-risk people see little change, while people already on the verge of diabetes may be nudged over the threshold sooner.
