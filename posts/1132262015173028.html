ACC urges measuring hsCRP; statins cut hsCRP about 1 mg/L in a 26-RCT meta-analysis

Louis notes that the latest American College of Cardiology scientific statement recommends measuring and treating hsCRP and points out that statins do this effectively. He reshares a 2022 meta-analysis of 26 RCTs showing statins lowered hs-CRP and CRP in patients with CVD.

  • ACC scientific statement: measure and treat hsCRP
  • 26 studies (3010 patients, 2968 controls): hs-CRP WMD -0.97 mg/L (95% CI -1.26 to -0.68)
  • CRP WMD -3.05 mg/L (95% CI -4.86 to -1.25) in 20 studies
  • For hsCRP of 2 mg/L this is about a 50% reduction

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

For many people with CVD, statins reduce hsCRP by 50% or more.

In a meta-analysis of 26 randomized controlled trials published in 2022, statins reduced hsCRP in patients with CVD by about 1 mg/L, and reduced CRP by about 3 mg/L.

For someone with an hsCRP of 2mg/L (a typical value on the high-end of normal), this translates into a 50% reduction.

Quoted from the paper:

“26 studies were identified (3010 patients and 2968 controls) for hs-CRP and 20 studies (3026 patients and 2968 controls) for CRP. Statins reduced the serum levels of hs-CRP (WMD = −0.97 mg/L; 95% CI: -1.26 to -0.68 mg/L; P < 0.001) and CRP (WMD = −3.05 mg/L; 95% CI: -4.86 to -1.25 mg/L; P < 0.001) in patients with CVDs. Statins decreased the serum levels of hs-CRP in patients receiving both high-intensity and moderate/low-intensity treatments with these drugs. In addition, the duration of treatment longer than 10 weeks decreased hs-CRP levels.”
