Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

If you have an impulse to write “correlation does not equal causation” here, well, don’t worry, I've just called attention to that for you (you’re welcome). Now let's move on.

A recent 6.7-year study (PMID: 38782209) published in The Journal of Nutrition suggests that eating more than one egg per week is associated with a 47% reduced risk of developing Alzheimer’s dementia in older adults. The study included 1,024 participants (~81 years old) from the Rush Memory and Aging Project.

Findings:

✅ >1 egg per week lowered Alzheimer’s dementia risk by 47%.
✅ ≥2 eggs per week was linked to lower Alzheimer's pathology in the brain.
✅ Dietary choline accounted for 39% of the protective effect, supporting brain function.
✅ One large egg provides 150mg of choline, covering 27% of the recommended daily intake (for adult men).

Limitations:

🔹 Observational study. Findings show correlation, not causation.
🔹 Self-reported dietary intake.
🔹 Population-specific—participants were older U.S. adults, limiting generalizability.
🔹 Alzheimer’s is multifactorial. Other lifestyle and genetic factors contribute to risk.

Regular egg consumption may support brain health and reduce Alzheimer’s risk, with choline playing a key neuroprotective role.
