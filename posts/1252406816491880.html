APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Louis Nervegna · Mar 23, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 22 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.

  • Benefit only in APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers
  • Fish, poultry and red meat showed similar benefit
  • Processed meat associated with higher dementia risk in all participants
  • Observational; causality not shown

This is an interesting new observational study, which has attracted a lot of attention online and in the media.

The study suggests that carriers of the APOE4 Alzheimer’s gene variants might see a lower risk of developing dementia if they consume unprocessed meat or fish in their diet. They might also see slower progression, if they already have dementia.

(Specifically those with the APOE 3/4 and 4/4 genotypes.)

No benefit was seen in non-APOE4’s.

Fish and poultry seemed to confer the same benefit as red meat in APOE4’s. There was no advantage to consuming one over the other.

Again, there was no benefit seen in non-APOE4’s.

On the other hand, processed meat was associated with an INCREASED risk of dementia in ALL participants, regardless of APOE4 status.

(And yes, this includes the supposedly “natural” and “uncured” versions of popular processed meats like bacon, sausage, hot dogs, deli meats, etc. that are processed using celery powder instead of sodium nitrite: ↪ 'Uncured' and 'nitrate-free' processed meats can carry as many or more nitrosamines.)

Participants in this study were not having large amounts of meat or fish overall, at least not by American standards. So on the assumption that these results are indeed causal (a huge leap), it would not take much to see the benefit:

  • Total meat (red meat + poultry + processed meat) per day was a mean of 77 grams, or about 2.7 ounces.
  • The mean serving of red meat was about 39 grams/day, or 1.4 oz. in a 2000 calorie diet.
  • APOE 3/4 and 4/4 participants in the highest 20% had a mean of 2.5 oz of red meat per day, while those in lowest 20% had a mean of 0.6 oz per day.
  • Poultry was a mean of 14 grams, or 0.5 oz.

For more information on this study, also see this post:

↪ Yassine: Swedish APOE4 meat study underpowered for dementia but significant for cognition

The part that caught my attention right off the bat was this quote:

“While the outcomes may seem surprising, they align with patterns observed in two large cohort studies, the researchers said.

‘Viewed alongside reinterpreted evidence from the Nurses' Health Study and U.K. Biobank focusing on unprocessed meat, these findings point to a consistent gene-diet interaction, with important implications for public health,’ Norgren and co-authors wrote.

In the U.K. Biobank study of 494,000 participants, unprocessed red meat was inversely associated with dementia (P=0.01), which was driven by APOE4 carriers. In the Nurses' Health Study, supplementary analyses showed an APOE4 interaction (P<0.001) for unprocessed red meat. These patterns were not emphasized in the original publications, Norgren and colleagues noted.”

My note:

So at first glance, there does actually seem to be some precedent for this finding in large prospective cohort studies like the Harvard Nurses Study (although the researchers on the HNS might disagree). It will be interesting to see if this observation holds up to further analysis. Time will tell.

In the meantime, the best evidence we have to prevent dementia with nutrition is the MIND diet. This is what most neurologists now recommend. The studies show that it works regardless of APOE4 status. It encourages fish. It also allows poultry, and even a little red meat. But no processed meat. You can read the posts about it by searching the group for the tag #minddietHD.
