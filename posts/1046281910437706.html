Mediterranean-diet RCT meta-analysis: 48% fewer MACE; WFPBNO only has one small RCT

Louis Nervegna · Jun 13, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a May 2024 meta-analysis of the four large Mediterranean diet RCTs (Lyon, Indo-Mediterranean, PREDIMED, CORDIOPREV) with substantial reductions in cardiovascular events, and argues the only RCT of a whole-food plant-based no-oil diet is Ornish's very small trial, with other WFPBNO evidence observational.

  • Meta-analysis of 4 RCTs vs AHA-style control diets: MACE -48%, MI -38%, stroke -36%, CV mortality -46%
  • Trials: Lyon Diet Heart, Indo-Mediterranean, PREDIMED, CORDIOPREV
  • WFPBNO: one small Ornish RCT; other evidence observational

The whole food plant-based diet with no oil (WFPBNO) can be a very healthy diet, if done correctly.

But this is a science-based group. We do not hyperbolize and exaggerate evidence here. We do NOT make controversial fact-based claims unless we have rigorous evidence to back them up.

If you are following a WFPBNO diet and getting good results, then I congratulate you. You are very welcome to share your experience and opinion (as long as you indicate that it is an opinion).

However, there is only one long-running randomized-controlled trial that has ever tested a WFPBNO diet vs heart disease.

It was a very small RCT run by Dean Ornish in the late 90’s. The results were good, but no better than the 4 very large long-running RCT’s testing a traditional Mediterranean diet. And its very small size makes it less reliable than the large trials:

↪ Ornish trial: lifestyle program cut cardiac events, but plant-based diet was not a cure

↪ Four long-term RCTs show the Mediterranean diet cuts CVD death by 46% in aggregate

↪ Meta-analysis of 4 Mediterranean diet RCTs: MACE down 48%, CVD death down 46%

ALL other long-term studies testing a WFPBNO diet against heart disease have been observational and low on the evidence-hierarchy in medicine, including what Esselstyn has published.

Recently published meta-analysis (May 2024) combining all 4 large long-running randomized controlled trials that tested a traditional Mediterranean diet verses heart disease.

Large long-running RCT’s are the highest quality evidence in medicine. They are at the very top of the evidence hierarchy because they directly prove causality.

  1. ↪ Check where a study sits on the evidence hierarchy before citing it

Summary of results for the Mediterranean diet:

(verses control diets similar to American Heart Association recommendations at the time)

  • MACE events reduced by 48%
  • Heart attacks reduced by 38%
  • Strokes reduced by 36%
  • Cardiovascular mortality reduced by 46%

The 4 RCT’s included in the meta-analysis were: the Lyon Diet Heart Study, the Indo-Mediterranean Diet Heart Study, the PREDIMED Study, and the recent CORDIOPREV Study.

Quoted:

“Our analysis incorporated four RCTs involving a total of 10,054 participants, with an average age of 57 years and a mean follow-up duration ranging from 2 to 7 years. In our pooled analysis, the composite endpoint of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in incidence in participants on MedDiet versus control diet with an OR of 0.52 (95 % CI: 0.32 to 0.84, p = 0.008; I2 = 87 %). Additionally, our study revealed a notable decrease in the incidence of cardiovascular events, both myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke in the the MedDiet group, with an OR of 0.62 (95 % CI: 0.41 to 0.92, p = 0.02; I2 = 56 %) and 0.63 (95 % CI: 0.48 to 0.87, p = 0.002; I2 = 0 %), respectively. However, no statistically significant change in the rate of revascularization was observed, with an OR of 0.74 (95 % CI: 0.30 to 1.27, p = 0.06; I2 = 16 %). Concerning mortality rates, MedDiet significantly reduced the risk of cardiovascular death with an OR of 0.54 (95 % CI: 0.31 to 0.94, p = 0.03; I2 = 55 %), while no significant change was noted in all-cause mortality, with an OR of 0.77 (95 % CI: 0.51 to 1.15, p = 0.20; I2 = 58 %).”

“MedDiet serves as an effective intervention for both primary and secondary prevention of CVD, demonstrating a substantial and long-term impact in reducing the incidence of MACE, MI, stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality while showing no observed effect on all-cause mortality.”
