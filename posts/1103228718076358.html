Fasting triggers autophagy, which recycles damaged cells including cancer cells

Louis Nervegna · Sep 01, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Fasting & meal timingAging & longevityCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that fasting promotes autophagy, where the body recycles damaged tissue first, and argues a balance of catabolism and anabolism supports longevity while constant overeating shortens life. He cites the 2016 Nobel Prize for autophagy; the post offers little direct evidence for fasting effects on cancer.

  • Ohsumi awarded the 2016 Nobel Prize for the discovery of autophagy
  • Constant anabolism through overeating argued to shorten life (opinion)

When you fast, your body catabolizes damaged and unhealthy tissue first. This includes cancer tissue. This process is called autophagy.

Autophagy is an ancient survival mechanism that evolved to keep you alive when food was scarce. Logically, it makes sense for the body to feed on damaged tissue first, before being forced to catabolize healthy tissue.

We need some catabolism to be healthy. Forcing your body into a constant state of anabolism all the time will ultimately shorten your life. This is what overeating does. We need a balance between anabolism and catabolism for longevity.
