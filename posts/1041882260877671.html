Nature study challenges the idea that taurine declines with age

Louis Nervegna · Jun 07, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevitySupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes a Nature paper from Rafael de Cabo's NIA group finding that taurine levels do not decline with age in humans, monkeys or mice, contradicting a high-profile Columbia study. He notes the new data are mostly longitudinal, whereas the Columbia data were largely cross-sectional, and that unchanged levels do not necessarily mean supplementation has no value.

  • Challenges a Columbia study published 2 years earlier
  • New study mostly longitudinal; Columbia study largely cross-sectional
  • Columbia work also included taurine supplementation experiments in mice and primates

Rafael de Cabo’s group at the NIA has just published a paper in Nature challenging the idea that taurine levels decline with age, in humans and other animals like monkeys and mice.

He is claiming that this data implies taurine does not play a major role in aging.

This is a direct challenge to the results of a very high-profile Columbia University study published 2 years ago, which showed the opposite. See the old posts below:

The de Cabo results look more robust because most of the data was collected longitudinally, as opposed to the Columbia paper where a lot of the data was obtained cross-sectionally (at only one point in time).

I have not read the actual paper yet, so I haven’t really formed an opinion here.

However, I would note that just because taurine levels don’t decline with age, does not mean that supplementation has no value. The Columbia paper included a lot of work that was done interventionally. They gave animals taurine— including mice and primates— and observed benefits.

It looks like there is going to be a big fight here, like what happened with resveratrol.

Restoring taurine suppressed NLRP3 activation, and raising taurine in older mice improved grip strength, coordination, some glucose measures and inflammatory markers. It is preclinical research.

A Cell paper from Yale reports that taurine levels increase with age in mice and humans, and boosting taurine further in aged mice increased healthspan and dampened inflammation by regulating the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages.

Mouse taurine doses of 500-1000 mg/kg scale to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg human

Oct 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 7 Aging & longevity

Louis explains that the 2023 Columbia taurine study used 500-1000 mg/kg in mice, and that allometric scaling for faster mouse metabolism converts this to about 2.8-5.7 g for a 70 kg person. He suggests that is the range to test in human trials, since mouse mg/kg doses cannot be applied directly to humans.
