Low-carb versus low-fat debate misses the point: food quality drives heart disease risk

Louis shares a JAMA podcast on a cohort study finding healthy versions of low-carb and low-fat diets give similar heart benefits, while highly processed versions carry higher risk.

  • Healthy low-carb and healthy low-fat diets similar for coronary heart disease risk
  • Processed versions carry significantly higher risk

Why the Low Carb vs Low Fat Debate Misses the Point

It’s the quality of macronutrients that seems to matter most for reducing risk for CVD. Not the proportions.

#hnsHD #hpfsHD #harvardHD #proteinHD #fatHD #carbsHD #lowfatHD #lowcarbHD

Research cited

  • Harvard cohort of low-carb and low-fat diet quality and CHD · cohort · JAMA (news report)
Shared from JAMA · original post

Prioritizing healthy food sources over specific macronutrient ratios may be the most effective strategy for preventing coronary heart disease.

JAMA Medical News Associate Managing Editor Kate Schweitzer spoke with Staff Writer Samantha Anderer about a cohort study which reveals that healthy versions of both low-carb and low-fat diets yield similar heart benefits, while their highly processed counterparts carry significantly higher risks.

Higher plant-to-animal protein ratio linked to lower heart disease risk over 30 years

Jul 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 7 Protein

Louis summarizes Harvard Nurses' and Physicians' follow-up data on over 200,000 people tracked for 30 years. The best results were in those with the highest plant-to-animal protein ratio and the most total plant protein, mainly from replacing red and processed meat.

Protein type matters more than amount: processed meat worst, plant protein most protective

Jun 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Protein

Louis reshares his summary of protein intake and mortality in nearly 300,000 people followed over 30 years in the Nurses' Health Studies and Health Professionals Follow-up Study. Plant protein was protective, fish and poultry slightly so, dairy and eggs neutral, while unprocessed red meat modestly and processed meat very strongly raised mortality.

Whole-food plant protein beats animal protein for long-term outcomes in Harvard cohorts

Mar 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 ↩ 2 Protein

Louis summarizes 30-year follow-up in the Harvard Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study: whole-food plant protein is associated with better outcomes, especially for heart disease. The benefit depends on the animal protein type: plant protein is only slightly better than fish, poultry and fermented dairy but clearly better than red and processed meat.
