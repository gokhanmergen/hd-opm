No long-lived population ate a very low carb diet most of their lives, Louis argues

Louis Nervegna · Jul 17, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis says he has found no long-lived population that ate very low carb over most of life, and that long-term effects are uncertain even though short-term safety is likely.

  • Zero counterexamples found by Louis
  • Short-term safety and possibly benefit not disputed

I’ve talked about this before. It’s nearly impossible to find an example of a long-lived population that ate a very low carb diet over the majority of their lifespan.

It’s not just the “blue zones”. It’s every long-lived population that I can find.

I’m all ears here: If someone knows of a counter-example to this, I’d love to hear about it. Please comment below. But so far, I have found zero.

It’s not that these very low carb diets cannot be healthy. They probably can, if the foods themselves are healthy. They are certainly safe— and maybe even beneficial— in the shorter term. I don’t question that. The problem is that there is legitimate uncertainty as to what they actually do in the very long term. That’s why I don’t follow one myself.

High-carb diet raised insulin yet people ate 689 fewer calories a day in an NIH ward study

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 0 · 💬 3 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Louis shares a post arguing that normal insulin spikes from healthy carbohydrate do not drive hunger or overeating. In a controlled NIH study, 20 adults lived in a research facility on a high-carb low-fat diet (about 75% carbohydrate) and a ketogenic diet (about 10% carbohydrate) for two weeks each. Despite higher glucose and insulin, they ate an average of 689 fewer calories per day on the high-carb diet, contradicting the carbohydrate-insulin model's prediction, although the diets differed in other ways.
