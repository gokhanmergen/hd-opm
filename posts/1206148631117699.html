Human heart muscle cells divide after heart attack, University of Sydney finds

Research cited

  • Cardiomyocyte mitosis in human heart after MI (Univ. of Sydney) · other · Circulation Research · 2026
Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Researchers from the University of Sydney, working with clinicians at the Baird Institute and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital, directly studied living human heart tissue to understand what happens after a heart attack. Using tissue samples taken during bypass surgery, the team—led by Dr. Robert Hume with senior author Professor Sean Lal—examined both damaged and healthy areas of the heart. Their findings, published in Circulation Research (2026), showed clear evidence of cardiomyocyte mitosis, meaning heart muscle cells were actively dividing in humans after myocardial infarction.

This is the first direct proof in living patients that the human heart can regenerate muscle cells after a heart attack, a phenomenon previously seen only in animal models like mice. Importantly, the researchers emphasized that this regeneration is limited and does not prevent scarring or heart failure on its own. However, identifying active cell division in human heart muscle confirms the heart has an intrinsic repair capacity, opening the door to future therapies designed to amplify this natural process and improve recovery after heart attacks.
