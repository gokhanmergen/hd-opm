Semaglutide and other GLP1 drugs raise testosterone 20-30%, in men with large amounts of visceral fat.

In most middle age men, the cause of low testosterone is excess visceral fat. Lose the fat (by any means), and testosterone often normalizes.

The magic weight loss number to see meaningful changes in testosterone is around 7-10%. This is when leptin levels fall low enough to trigger a substantial testosterone increase.

New research shows that losing weight can boost testosterone, with a 10% weight loss raising levels by about 84 ng/dL and bariatric surgery gains averaging 250 ng/dL.

Scientists pinpoint a critical 7–10% weight reduction where leptin drops trigger hormonal shifts.

Popular GLP-1 agonists like semaglutide further amplify testosterone by 20–30% and improve metabolic health.

Early studies suggest these drugs might support male sexual tissues directly.

Experts recommend waiting 3–6 months after weight loss before retesting testosterone.

Men on TRT should monitor and adjust doses as weight changes alter hormone processing.
