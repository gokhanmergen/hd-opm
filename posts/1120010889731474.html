Ezetimibe lowers LDL-C about 18-20% alone and a further 21-30% on top of a statin

Gokhan Can · Sep 24, 2025 · ♥ 16 reactions · 19 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A quick reference on ezetimibe: it blocks cholesterol absorption in the small intestine and is most useful added to a statin. It also modestly lowers apoB and triglycerides and slightly raises HDL.

  • Monotherapy: LDL-C down about 15-25% (average 18-20%)
  • Added to a statin: further 21-30% LDL-C reduction
  • ApoB down roughly 17-20% on monotherapy

LDL Reduction from Ezetimibe

Ezetimibe, when used alone (monotherapy), reduces LDL-C by roughly 15-25%, with studies showing an average reduction of about 18-20%. The primary effectiveness of ezetimibe is in combination with statins, where it can provide an additional 21-30% reduction in LDL-C, leading to greater overall cholesterol reduction.

Ezetimibe Mechanism

  • Ezetimibe works by inhibiting cholesterol absorption in the small intestine.
  • Other Effects: It can also lead to a modest increase in HDL-C and a decrease in triglycerides.

Ezetimibe Monotherapy

  • Efficacy: As a standalone therapy, it can lower LDL-C by approximately 18-20%.
  • Ezetimibe alone can reduce apoB levels by roughly 17-20%.

Ezetimibe with Statins

  • Synergistic Effect: The greatest benefits of ezetimibe are seen when it's added to statin therapy.
  • When combined with a statin, ezetimibe can produce an additional 21% to 30% reduction in LDL-C.
