Meta-analysis of 76 studies finds no link between wine drinking and cancer risk

Louis Nervegna · Dec 03, 2023 · ♥ 3 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a Frontiers in Nutrition systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 epidemiological studies, which found no association between wine consumption and any cancer and a protective trend for pancreatic, skin, lung, brain and overall cancer. He cautions that observational data cannot prove wine is harmless, and advises staying within guidelines of at most 2 drinks a day for men and 1 for women.

  • 76 epidemiological studies
  • No association between wine and any type of cancer
  • Protective trend for pancreatic, skin, lung, brain and general cancer
  • Guideline limits: 2 drinks/day men, 1 for women

Wine consumption has long been a part of the traditional Mediterranean diet— a diet which is known to lower the risk for cancer. Wine has been traditionally consumed in areas of the world where people lived a very long time, like Sardinia and Ikaria, and where cancer rates were very low.

Yet we are led to believe that even small amounts of red wine raise the risk for cancer? Is this really true? What do the studies actually show when wine is examined separately from other forms of alcohol?

A recently published systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 epidemiological studies on wine consumption and cancer attempts to answer this question more rigorously:

“Association between wine consumption and cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis”

“Conclusion: The study findings reveal no association between wine consumption and the risk of developing any type of cancer. Moreover, wine drinking demonstrated a protective trend regarding the risk of developing pancreatic, skin, lung, and brain cancer as well as cancer in general.”

“This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis on this topic, and the aim was to analyze the association between wine consumption and general, upper digestive tract, colorectal, renal, pancreatic, skin, lung, brain, and gynecological cancer.”

NOTE:

This is a meta-analysis of observational studies. Observational studies cannot definitively prove that wine does not raise cancer risk, even when only consumed in mild to moderate amounts. We would need a very long-running randomized controlled trial to do that (unlikely we will ever get one beyond 5 years).

If you choose to consume alcohol, it is very IMPORTANT to follow the official public health guidelines and not exceed 2 drinks per day for men, and 1 drink for women. This includes wine. It is well established that ethanol is a poison in high doses.

Meta-analysis of 4.3 million: moderate wine not linked to higher overall cancer risk

Jan 05, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 17 ↩ 3 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

Louis shares Dr. Brian Lawenda's post on a Frontiers in Nutrition meta-analysis of 73 studies, covering over 4.3 million participants, that found moderate wine consumption (up to one drink/day for women, two for men) was not associated with increased overall cancer risk and might lower some cancer types. The post questions treating wine like beer and spirits.

UNATI: 10,000-person randomized trial of moderate red wine vs reducing alcohol

Aug 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 3 ↩ 1 Alcohol, smoking & vaping

UNATI, led by Martinez-Gonzalez, will randomize about 10,000 drinkers (men 50-70, women 55-75, 3-40 drinks per week) to reduce alcohol or drink moderately (under 7/week women, under 14/week men, red wine with meals) for about 4 years. Outcomes include mortality, cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and dementia.
