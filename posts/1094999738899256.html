CT scan cancer risk must be weighed against the benefit of tracking plaque

Louis Nervegna · Aug 21, 2025 · ♥ 7 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyCancerRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis cautions that tracking plaque with CT scans carries radiation risk, noting CAC scans use much less radiation than CT angiography but predict risk less accurately. He points out the JAMA Internal Medicine projection is observational, subject to confounding and reverse causality.

  • CAC scans: much lower radiation than CTA but less accurate for risk prediction

We have to balance the risks verses the benefits of using CT scans to track arterial plaque.

Note that CAC scans are much lower in radiation than full CT-angiogram scans, but also considerably less accurate in predicting risk.

Also note that this study was observational. These observational studies suffer from confounding and reverse causality.

For example, people who get a lot of CT scans tend to have worse health in general. That’s why they need the scans in the first place. This worse health increases their risk for cancer, independent of the CT scans themselves.

Sep 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The NEJM REACT study used carotid and femoral ultrasound plus coronary CT angiography in 16,808 adults aged 18-70 without known disease. Plaque was present in 57% overall, appeared in young adults, and a zero calcium score often missed early non-calcified plaque. Ten-year risk scores detected very little silent disease, so keeping risk factors favorable throughout life is the priority.
