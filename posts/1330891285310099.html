Statin Side Effects: Rare, But Still Driving Perception

A Duke Health cardiologist told NBC News that concerns about statin side effects are often greater than the evidence supports.

That aligns with new research reported by NBC News, which found serious muscle-related side effects from statins are extremely rare.

💊

NBC NewsSerious statin side effects on muscles are extremely rare, new research confirmsScientists created a tool to help doctors predict a patient’s risk of severe muscle problems from taking a statin.nbcnews.com

Statins are among the most prescribed medications in the world, used by millions of people to lower cholesterol and reduce cardiovascular risk.

Even with decades of data showing statins are safe and effective, fear of side effects still influences whether people start or stay on these medications. That gap between perception and evidence is exactly what large studies like PREVENTABLEtrial are designed to address.

PREVENTABLE is a national, randomized clinical trial studying whether statins improve outcomes in older adults without existing cardiovascular disease—helping clarify both benefits and risks in real-world populations.

Louis cites the STOMP randomized trial in which high-dose atorvastatin had no effect on strength, VO2 max, endurance or physical activity across 14 measures. Muscle pain was about twice as common on atorvastatin, but did not measurably change performance.
