Leucine-rich protein caused atherosclerosis in engineered mice; human relevance unproven

Louis Nervegna · Mar 04, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a video review of a recent paper proposing that leucine-rich protein drives atherosclerosis. The conclusion, which Louis agrees with, is that it occurred in engineered mice fed 46% of calories from protein, is biologically plausible but remains far from proven in humans.

  • Engineered mice fed 46% of calories as leucine-rich protein developed atherosclerosis
  • Human relevance unclear; plausible but far from proven

Does large amounts of dietary protein rich in leucine cause atherosclerosis?

This video is a good summary of the recent paper proposing this hypothesis.

Conclusion of the video: Yes, in engineered mice fed very high amounts of leucine-rich protein (46% of calories from protein). Unclear in humans. It’s biologically plausible, but far from proven at this point. I agree.
