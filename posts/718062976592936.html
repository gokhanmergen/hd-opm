Charles Grashow's protocol to regress coronary calcium over two years

Describes how Charles Grashow responded to worsening coronary calcium in 2020-2021 with an intensive regimen of lifestyle, high-dose nattokinase, LDL-lowering and anti-inflammatory supplements and off-label drugs. His later scans reportedly showed that the trajectory of his coronary disease had reversed. It is a single-patient story, not a trial.

  • June 2020 CT showed minimal calcification; December 2021 showed a worse but still mild score
  • Protocol targeted LDL particles, inflammation, oxidative damage and nitric oxide, including high-dose nattokinase
  • Reported reversal after about two years

Charles's Story

When Charles Grashow received concerning test results in 2020 and 2021 showing developing coronary artery disease (CAD), he decided to take action. Over the next two years, Charles implemented an intensive lifestyle, diet, and supplementation regimen aimed at regressing the plaques in his heart's arteries - and it worked. As evidenced by his latest test results, Charles successfully reversed the trajectory of his CAD.

The Start of Charles's Journey

In June 2020, a heart CT scan delivered Charles's first warning, detecting minimal coronary calcification and enlarged pulmonary arteries. While not yet serious, these were early signs of potential issues. By December 2021, a follow-up scan showed Charles's coronary artery calcium score had worsened and now indicated definite, albeit still mild, CAD.

At this point, Charles faced a choice - proceed as usual down the path of advancing atherosclerosis or work diligently to stop and turn back the clock. Motivated by his belief in science and self experimentation, Charles chose the latter.

The Key Components of Charles's Protocol

Charles put together a multi-prong, evidence-based plan attacking CAD from all angles:

Diet - Charles always had a healthy diet.

Exercise & Weight - Though not overweight, Charles always did his weekly cardiovascular exercise and strength training to improve circulatory health.

Supplements & Off Label Drugs - After extensive research Charles introduced various supplements, mostly targeted at lowering LDL particles (“bad” cholesterol), reducing inflammation and oxidative damage, and boosting nitric oxide production for improved vascular function. He followed a general approach to improve his longevity through off label drugs and supplements. He started taking high dose nattokinase (8k FU). Targeted an LDL of 30 mg/dL, and very low inflammation (HS-CRP of 0.5).

The proof is in the plaque - or lack thereof. A June 2023 CT heart scan showed stunning results...

The Payoff: CAD Reversal Documented

An eagerly awaited CT heart scan in June 2023, two and a half years after starting his intensified protocol, brought Charles definitive proof his approach worked.

Some key takeaways:

  • Charles's coronary artery calcium score, the most telling indicator of atherosclerotic plaque, significantly improved from initial 102 to 32, dropping by over 2/3rds.
  • The radiologist formally classified Charles's status as CAD RADS 1, meaning only minimal evidence of CAD presently with just eccentric calcified segments causing insignificant vessel narrowing.
  • Structurally, Charles's heart and arteries all checked out as normal and within proper limits.

Simply put, Charles achieved regression of his coronary artery disease - the improved state reflected in his scans confirms this remarkable outcome.

While anecdotal and n=1, Charles’s restoration of cardiovascular wellness illuminates the real potential for nutrition, exercise, select supplements and drugs to favorably influence aging and turn around atherosclerosis.

Vitamin K2 MK-7 slowed coronary calcification in a 2-year RCT; soft plaque not measured

Sep 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 11 Supplements & vitamins

A shared post on a new 2-year randomized trial notes that MK-7 reduced progression of coronary calcification versus placebo. It cautions that calcified plaque is generally more stable, soft plaque was not measured, and the trial was not designed to show fewer cardiovascular events.

Why a coronary calcium score can rise as plaque stabilizes on statins

Dec 03, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 28 · 💬 47 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Explains that the Agatston score is not true calcium volume: it multiplies area by a stepwise density factor (1-4), so slightly denser plaque can jump a category and raise the score. Statins turn soft lipid-rich plaque into stable calcified plaque, so a rising CAC on treatment can reflect stabilization rather than failure.

New vitamin K2 trial slowed arterial calcification but did not reduce plaque, video argues

Aug 31, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 38 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Gil Carvalho video on a randomized trial of vitamin K2: calcification progressed more slowly, but total plaque did not fall, the plaque was just less calcified. Carvalho argues calcium stabilises plaque, so slowing calcification without reducing plaque could even be harmful, and no evidence shows K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, so he advises relying on proven therapies, diet and exercise.

The longer the lower the better: lifelong LDL lowering and an estimated 60% risk reduction

Nov 20, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 17 · 💬 21 ↩ 2 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Argues that the benefit of LDL lowering depends on both how much and how long LDL is reduced. The author, one of the group's admins, is betting on very low-dose statins to hold LDL at 70-100 mg/dL, and estimates about 60% lifetime risk reduction from a 70-point lowering sustained for 30 years. The post also cites Charles Grashow as a case of CAC regression.

PCSK9 inhibitors regress plaque without the calcification surge seen with statins

Jan 01, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 44 · 💬 35 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Explains the 'statin paradox' (statins reduce events and plaque but raise coronary calcium as plaque densifies) and argues PCSK9 inhibitors regress plaque without a calcific surge. Cites GLAGOV (evolocumab, LDL 36.6 mg/dL, PAV -0.95% vs +0.05%), PACMAN-AMI (alirocumab triple regression) and Gao 2022 (CAC +2.9% vs +10.6%).

Alirocumab plus statin slowed coronary calcium progression: +2.9% vs +10.6% at 18 months

Jan 01, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 16 · 💬 1 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Summarises a 2022 RCT in CAD patients on moderate statins: alirocumab 75 mg every 2 weeks for 18 months limited CAC progression, with Lp(a) change correlating with CAC change. A retrospective study (Ikegami) found about half the annual CAC progression with PCSK9 inhibitor plus statin; studies are small and larger trials are needed.
