Weekend warrior and regular exercise cut disease risk similarly in a Circulation study

Louis Nervegna · Jan 16, 2025 · ♥ 18 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

A Circulation study (PMID 39324186) found that concentrating exercise on 1-2 days works about as well as spreading it across the week for lowering risk across many disease categories. Total weekly volume of at least 150 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity matters more than distribution.

  • Hypertension risk 23% lower (weekend warrior) vs 28% (regular)
  • Diabetes risk 43% lower (weekend warrior) vs 46% (regular)
  • Lower risk across 16 major disease categories and 200+ diseases
  • At least 150 min/week moderate-to-vigorous activity

The total amount of exercise seems to be more important than how it distributed throughout the week.

  • Weekend warrior vs regular exercise and disease risk (PMID 39324186) · cohort · Circulation · 2024
Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

A study published last month (PMID: 39324186) in Circulation reveals that both weekend warrior (concentrated exercise in 1–2 days) and regular exercise patterns (evenly distributed activity throughout the week) are equally effective in reducing disease risk across 16 major disease categories.

Findings:

  • Lower Risk Across 200+ Diseases: Both patterns significantly decrease the likelihood of developing conditions, including cardiovascular, digestive, mental health, and neurological illnesses.
  • Cardiometabolic Benefits:
  • Hypertension: 23% lower risk (weekend warrior) vs. 28% (regular exercisers).
  • Diabetes: 43% lower risk (weekend warrior) vs. 46% (regular exercisers).

This research suggests that total weekly physical activity volume (≥150 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise) is more critical than how it's distributed.
