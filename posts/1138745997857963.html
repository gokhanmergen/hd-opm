Supplementing with vitamin D2 may actually lower levels of active vitamin D in the body.

Supplementing with vitamin D2 reduced the primary circulating form of vitamin D3 by ~18 nmol/L (7 ng/mL), which is enough to move from sufficient to an insufficient range.

This is due to the fact that vitamin D2 enhances the breakdown of vitamin D3 in the body.
While vitamin D2 comes from plants and vitamin D3 comes from animal sources or sunlight, research consistently shows that D3 raises serum vitamin D levels by 40% or more (10 or more ng/dL) compared to vitamin D2.

At a biological level, vitamin D3 demonstrates clear advantages over D2 for immune function and muscle function.

About half the global population has insufficient vitamin D levels, mainly because sunscreen use, darker skin pigmentation, geographic latitude, and aging significantly reduce the skin’s ability to produce vitamin D3 from sunlight. Consequently, supplementation is often necessary to achieve optimal vitamin D3 status.

If you supplement, choose vitamin D3 over D2. For most adults, 2,000–4,000 IU per day is sufficient to raise plasma vitamin D levels from deficiency into the optimal sufficient range.
