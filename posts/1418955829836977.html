DIABMET-FMT: lean-donor fecal transplant improved insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Oct 12, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

The shared description of the DIABMET-FMT randomized trial says repeated fecal microbiota transplant capsules from lean, metabolically healthy donors improved insulin sensitivity, microbiome diversity and short-chain fatty acid production in adults with type 2 diabetes, without diet, drug or exercise changes. The poster sees it as evidence that the microbiome contributes to diabetes in some people. The details come from the post only and could not be verified here.

  • 124 adults with type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 7.0-10.0%, BMI 27-40) and low gut diversity
  • FMT capsules from lean donors vs autologous placebo, weekly for 6 weeks then monthly for 6 weeks
  • 12-week primary endpoint showed improved HOMA-IR, microbiome diversity and short-chain fatty acid production

Proof that the microbiome plays a role in the development of diabetes, at least in some people.

Shared from Diabetes60 · original post

The DIABMET-FMT trial - a double-blind randomized controlled trial of fecal microbiota transplantation from metabolically healthy lean donors to adults with established Type 2 diabetes and confirmed gut dysbiosis - has published its 12-week primary endpoint data in Cell Host and Microbe in 2025, confirming that repeated FMT from carefully phenotyped lean metabolically healthy donors produces significant improvements in insulin sensitivity, gut microbiome diversity, and short-chain fatty acid production in Type 2 diabetic recipients without changes in diet, medication, or physical activity.

The DIABMET-FMT trial enrolled 124 adults with Type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 7.0 to 10.0 percent, BMI 27 to 40 kg/m2, confirmed low gut microbiome alpha-diversity on baseline sequencing) randomized to receive either FMT capsules from metabolically healthy lean donors (BMI below 23, fasting glucose below 5.0 mmol/L, high gut diversity on sequencing) or autologous FMT capsules as placebo, administered weekly for 6 weeks and then monthly for 6 weeks.

Donor selection was the most rigorously phenotyped in any FMT metabolic trial to date - donors required to meet criteria on gut microbiome composition (minimum 500 species, high Faecalibacterium prausnitzii and Akkermansia muciniphila abundance, low Proteobacteria relative abundance) in addition to standard metabolic health criteria.

At the 12-week primary endpoint: active FMT recipients showed HOMA-IR insulin resistance score improvements averaging 23 percent versus 6 percent in the placebo group. Fasting blood glucose fell by an average of 14 mg/dL in the active group versus 3 mg/dL in placebo. Stool sequencing confirmed significant microbiome diversity increases in the active group with specific expansion of donor-derived Faecalibacterium prausnitzii and Akkermansia muciniphila strains confirmed by strain-level sequencing. Stool SCFA measurement confirmed significant butyrate and propionate increases in the active group at 6 and 12 weeks.

The HbA1c change at 12 weeks - a secondary endpoint - showed a trend toward improvement (0.3 percent reduction in active versus 0.1 percent in placebo) that did not reach statistical significance, consistent with the 3-month HbA1c lag from biological changes occurring at weeks 6 to 12. A 6-month extension is currently following participants.
