Coffee linked to younger epigenetic age and up to 27% lower mortality, per FoundMyFitness

Louis Nervegna · Sep 28, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A FoundMyFitness solocast summary says each extra daily cup is associated with a roughly 0.7-1 year younger epigenetic age, that 3+ cups daily links to 34-41% lower odds of accelerated biological aging, and coffee drinkers show up to 27% lower all-cause mortality. Decaf shows similar benefits.

  • Each daily cup: epigenetic age about 0.7-1 year younger
  • 3+ cups daily: 34-41% lower likelihood of accelerated biological aging
  • Up to 27% lower all-cause mortality vs non-drinkers
  • Similar for decaf, suggesting polyphenols

Quoted:

Coffee may be one of the most overlooked tools for healthier aging

In one study, each additional daily cup was associated with an epigenetic age roughly 0.7–1 years younger

Another analysis linked 3+ cups daily to a 34–41% lower likelihood of accelerated biological aging

Coffee drinkers also show up to 27% lower all-cause mortality risk compared with non-drinkers

Similar longevity benefits appear with decaf, pointing to a potential role for coffee's polyphenols and other compounds
