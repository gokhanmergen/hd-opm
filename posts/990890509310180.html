'Grounding' during sleep is unnatural because primates evolved sleeping off the ground

Louis Nervegna · Mar 23, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 36 comments · original Facebook post
Myths, misinformation & controversiesSleep, stress & social health
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that sleeping grounded (connected to the earth) is not natural, because primates slept in trees for tens of millions of years and wood is an insulator, so a raised bed mirrors natural primate behavior. This is an evolutionary argument and cites no study.

  • Primates evolved for about 100 million years; Homo sapiens 200,000-300,000 years
  • Human DNA is over 98% the same as chimpanzees

“Grounding” yourself when you sleep is extremely unnatural.

Primates sleep in trees, not on the ground. Our primate ancestors slept in trees for tens of millions of years. Wood is an insulator. They were not grounded. We did not evolve to be connected to the earth ground when we slept. If you slept on the ground, you were eaten… and did not live long enough to pass on your genes. If anything, humans evolved NOT to be grounded when we slept.

Primates have been on earth for 100 million years. Homosapiens only evolved from other primates 200,000 to 300,000 years ago. That is 0.2% to 0.3% of our total time on earth as primates. So 99.7% to 99.8% of our evolution as primates occurred before we became Homosapiens. The small portion of our DNA that makes us human is tiny compared to the majority of the DNA that we share with other primates. Our DNA is over 98% the same as chimpanzees who still sleep in trees. Primates evolved for 100 million years to sleep primarily in trees, not in contact with the ground.

When you build a comfortable nest above the ground to sleep in (a bed), you are doing exactly what every other primate naturally does and has done for millions of years. Wood beds are no different from trees, from the perspective of grounding. This is natural for all primates.
