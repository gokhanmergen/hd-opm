Keto-CTA (LMHR): very high LDL keto dieters with plaque kept progressing despite lean

Louis Nervegna · Apr 07, 2025 · ♥ 14 reactions · 23 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivorePlaque, CAC & CT angiographyLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes the newly published one-year prospective Keto-CTA study of lean, metabolically healthy people on keto with very high LDL (mean 254 mg/dL). Those with baseline plaque progressed significantly at similar rates regardless of how high the LDL was, while those with zero plaque were unlikely to develop it within a year. There was no low-LDL comparison group.

  • Mean LDL 254 mg/dL; total plaque volume +0.8% in one year
  • CAC > 100: +2.4% (3x average); 10-year risk > 5%: +1.4% (2x average)
  • Degree of LDL elevation made little difference to progression rate
  • People with zero plaque at baseline were unlikely to develop it in one year
  • Abstract: baseline plaque metrics strongly associated with the change in NCPV

The “LMHR study” (Keto-CTA) was finally published.

This was a 1 year prospective cohort study designed to follow the effects of a keto diet on progression of atherosclerosis in people who are lean and metabolically healthy with very high LDL cholesterol (LMHR).

The study shows that people on keto diets with detectable atherosclerosis and very high LDL continue to lay down significant amounts of plaque and see significant worsening of their atherosclerosis, even if they are very lean and metabolically healthy (LMHR).

The study compares LMHR people who have super high LDL to LMHR people who have just high LDL. The study shows that both groups see significant progression of their atherosclerosis on a keto diet at approximately the same rate, if they have arterial plaque to begin with. The level of high LDL did not seem to make much difference in the rate of progression. Unfortunately, there is no very low LDL group to compare to. Everyone in this study had very high LDL (it was just a matter of degree). The average LDL was 254mg/dL.

If you’re on a keto diet and meet these LMHR criteria, then the study shows that it may not matter much if your LDL is 300mg/dL verses 200mg/dL, for example— but if you have any amount of plaque to start, then this study proves that your atherosclerosis will likely get significantly worse if you continue to follow the keto diet, even if you are lean and metabolically healthy.

Total plaque volume increased by 0.8% on average in this study after one year. (See comments for chart.)

This is bad. This is considerably worse than the average plaque progression of a typical American, i.e. someone who is significantly overweight, sedentary, and eats a standard American diet. This lifestyle leads to the outcome of 20% of people in the USA having a heart attack in their lifetime.

The people in this Keto-CTA study were all lean and metabolically healthy with very few risk factors for heart disease, except high LDL. They were very physically active and health-conscious. Most took supplements commonly thought to be “heart healthy” like vitamin K2, vitamin D, magnesium, fish oil, etc. Many engaged in intermittent fasting and other trendy health practices, in addition to keto. None had ever been officially diagnosed as having heart disease.

Despite all this, they had considerably worse plaque progression than being significantly overweight, sedentary, and just eating the standard American diet.

If you look only at people with CAC scores over 100 at badeline in the LMHR study, they saw plaque volume increase by a whopping 2.4% in one year on the keto diet! (See comments for chart.)

This is 3x worse than the average person in the study (which was already bad).

If you look only at people with 10 year risk greater than 5% on the standard AHA calculator, they saw an increase in plaque volume of 1.4% in one year on keto, regardless of their CAC score at baseline! (See comments for chart.)

This is 2x worse than the average person in the study.

The good news from this study is that if you have ZERO plaque to begin with, then you are unlikely to develop it in only a year if you follow a keto diet and are lean and metabolically healthy (LMHR)— no matter how high your LDL happens to be. That is the silver lining in this study.

Quoted right from the abstract:

“All baseline plaque metrics (coronary artery calcium, NCPV, total plaque score, and percent atheroma volume) were strongly associated with the change in NCPV.”

“Conversely, baseline plaque was associated with plaque progression, supporting the notion that, in this population, plaque begets plaque but ApoB does not.”

In plain English, this study shows that if you have plaque and go on a keto diet, your plaque will likely get significantly worse if you are lean and metabolically healthy— regardless of whether you have super high LDL verses just high LDL.
