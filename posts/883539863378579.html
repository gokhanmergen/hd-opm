Controlled infection study: mild SARS-CoV-2 impaired cognition for a year in 18 adults

Louis Nervegna · Oct 04, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 16 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a post about a Lancet eClinicalMedicine study in which 36 healthy unvaccinated young adults were deliberately inoculated with SARS-CoV-2 and 18 became infected, almost all with mild symptoms. Their cognitive function was measurably impaired during acute illness and at least one year later, though they reported no symptoms. The post is about cognition, not the heart.

  • 36 volunteers inoculated, 18 infected (17 mild, 1 asymptomatic)
  • Cognitive impairment during illness and at least 1 year after
  • No subjective cognitive complaints despite measurable deficits

  • Cognitive effects of experimental SARS-CoV-2 infection (human challenge study) · other · eClinicalMedicine · 2024
Shared from Olafur Pall Olafsson · original post

This is strong evidence that SARS-CoV-2 infections in unvaccinated individuals cause long lasting negative effects on cognitive function, even in healthy young adults that just got a mild infection. If you think you weren't harmed because you are young and healthy or because you just got a mild infection, chances are you're not only wrong but are ironically dumber now than you were back then.

In the study below that was just published, 36 healthy young adult volunteers, that were unvaccinated, were deliberately inoculated with SARS-CoV-2 resulting in 18 of them getting infected. 17 of them got mild symptoms while 1 had no symptoms.

The cognitive function of the infected patients was impaired both during the acute phase of illness but also at least one year after the infection! Note that none of the patients reported having subjective cognitive deficits, but they were still measurable, so just because you didn't notice negative effects doesn't mean you escaped them.
