The Metabolic Edge of Polyunsaturated Fats (PUFA)

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 102 randomized controlled feeding trials (4,220 participants) demonstrates that dietary macronutrient composition significantly impacts glucose-insulin homeostasis, with polyunsaturated fat (PUFA) offering the most consistent metabolic benefits.

The study analyzed isocaloric 5% energy replacements and found that while replacing carbohydrates with saturated fat (SFA) had no significant effect on fasting glucose, substituting carbohydrates with monounsaturated fat (MUFA) significantly reduced HbA1c by 0.09% and HOMA-IR (a marker of insulin resistance) by 2.4%. However, the strongest results were associated with PUFA: replacing carbohydrates with PUFA reduced HbA1c by 0.11% and fasting insulin by 1.6 pmol/L. notably, PUFA was the unique macronutrient capable of improving insulin secretion capacity (+0.5 pmol/L/min) regardless of whether it replaced carbohydrates, SFA, or MUFA. The authors conclude that dietary guidelines should prioritize replacing refined carbohydrates and saturated fats with sources of unsaturated fats, particularly PUFA (such as vegetable oils, nuts, and fish), to optimize glycemic control and mitigate type 2 diabetes risk.

Paper · PLoS Med · 2016Effects of Saturated Fat, Polyunsaturated Fat, Monounsaturated Fat, and Carbohydrate on Glucose-Insulin Homeostasis: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomised Controlled Feeding Trials.Conclusion: This meta-analysis of randomised controlled feeding trials provides evidence that dietary macronutrients have diverse effects on glucose-insulin homeostasis. In comparison to carbohydrate, SFA, or MUFA, most consistent favourable effects were seen with PUFA, which was linked to improved glycaemia, insulin resistance, and insulin secretion capacity.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
