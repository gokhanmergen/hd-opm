Aspartame insulin spikes in mice are small in people, versus whey and red meat

Louis Nervegna · Feb 27, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesMyths, misinformation & controversiesBlood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

Louis responds to a mouse study reported as showing aspartame triggers insulin spikes that build arterial plaque. He agrees persistently high insulin is likely causal in CVD but says aspartame's insulin effect in humans is small, while whey protein and red meat raise insulin much more calorie for calorie.

  • Mouse study: aspartame linked to insulin spikes and arterial plaque
  • Whey raises insulin more than glucose or white bread, calorie for calorie
  • Red meat raises insulin more than white pasta or oatmeal

This study is all over social media.

Here’s the truth.

Yes, persistently elevated insulin is likely causal in CVD and other age-related diseases.

However, the insulin spike from aspartame has been rigorously studied in humans. It is small in the amounts normally consumed.

Guess what is not small:

Calorie for calorie, a whey protein shake raises insulin a lot more than pure glucose or white bread.

↪ Whey protein raised insulin about twice as much as glucose or white bread per calorie

Calorie for calorie, red meat raises insulin more than white pasta or oatmeal (porridge).

↪ Beef and whey raise insulin more than pasta, oatmeal or glucose: use the insulin index

The same influencers who are telling you that a little bit of aspartame causes heart disease because of insulin… are also telling you to guzzle down whey protein shakes and red meat.

That makes zero sense.
