Circulating oxidized LDL tests do not predict CVD risk once ApoB is accounted for

Louis Nervegna · Sep 12, 2024 · ♥ 10 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that blood oxidized LDL tests do not predict cardiovascular disease after adjusting for total ApoB particles, because LDL oxidation that matters occurs within the artery wall, which no commercial test measures. He adds that interventions preventing oxidation of circulating LDL have failed in randomized trials.

  • Association with CVD disappears or shrinks after adjusting for ApoB
  • Most LDL oxidation happens after particles enter the artery wall
  • No commercial test measures oxidation in the arterial wall
  • Antioxidant strategies against circulating LDL oxidation failed in RCTs

Blood tests that measure “oxidized LDL” in circulation do not predict risk for CVD.

Any association disappears or is greatly attenuated after properly accounting for total ApoB particles (which includes LDL).

The vast majority of LDL oxidation happens only after particles have entered the arterial wall. This is where oxidation of LDL is causal, not while the particles are in circulation.

There is currently no commercial test available that can measure LDL oxidation inside the arterial wall where it matters.

Interventions that prevent oxidation of circulating LDL have repeatedly failed to protect against heart disease in randomized controlled trials.
