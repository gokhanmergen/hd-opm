A shingles shot cut major cardiac events 46%.

Not a statin. Not a stent. A vaccine.

New data from the American College of Cardiology's Annual Scientific Session (ACC.26) analyzed 246,822 U.S. adults over 50 with atherosclerotic heart disease. Half had received Shingrix or Zostavax.

Within one year, vaccinated patients had:

  • 46% fewer major cardiac events
  • 66% lower all-cause mortality
  • 32% fewer heart attacks
  • 25% fewer strokes; 25% less heart failure

Likely mechanism: shingles reactivation triggers inflammation and clotting that can rupture unstable plaque. Prevent the virus, blunt the trigger.

Honest caveat: this is a large observational analysis, not a randomized trial — it shows association, not cause. Healthier patients may be more likely to get vaccinated (healthy-user bias). It was presented at a meeting, not yet peer-reviewed.

Still, the signal is consistent with a 2025 European Heart Journal study of 1M+ people that found 23% lower CV events lasting up to 8 years. A cheap upstream lever on inflammation.

If you're over 50 with heart disease and haven't had Shingrix, ask your cardiologist.
