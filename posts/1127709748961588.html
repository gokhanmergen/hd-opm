Prediabetes remission depends on visceral fat loss, not total weight (Nature Medicine)

Louis Nervegna · Oct 04, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

A Nature Medicine analysis found prediabetes can go into remission without overall weight loss if fat is distributed to subcutaneous rather than visceral deposits. Louis stresses that even normal-weight people can have excess liver fat that must be lost to reverse prediabetes.

  • Prediabetes remission possible without total weight loss
  • Weight distributed to subcutaneous rather than visceral depots matters
  • Normal-weight people can still carry too much liver fat

For most people, reversing prediabetes is as simple as losing visceral fat.

Overall weight doesn’t matter that much. Just visceral fat:

Paper · Nat Med · 2025Prevention of type 2 diabetes through prediabetes remission without weight lossClinical practice guidelines recommend defined weight loss goals for the prevention of type 2 diabetes (T2D) in those individuals with increased risk, such as prediabetes. However, achieving prediabetes remission, that is, reaching normal glucose regulation according to American Diabetes Association criteria, is more efficient in preventing T2D than solely reaching weight loss goals. And yes, you can be a normal weight and still have too much visceral fat— particularly, too much fat in the liver. You need to lose this visceral (liver) fat if you truly want to cure the prediabetes.
