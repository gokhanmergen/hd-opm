Nearly 1 in 4 young adults has high blood pressure, raising later dementia risk

Louis Nervegna · Dec 18, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassiumBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a clip (Rhonda Patrick) noting that nearly 1 in 4 young adults has hypertension and that earlier, cumulative exposure to high blood pressure impairs cerebral blood flow and speeds brain atrophy. He links exercise as a protective factor for both heart and brain.

  • Nearly 1 in 4 young adults has high blood pressure
  • Earlier onset and longer cumulative exposure raise long-term risk
  • High BP impairs cerebral blood flow and nutrient delivery, accelerating brain atrophy

Quoted:

Nearly 1 in 4 young adults now has high blood pressure.

That’s deeply concerning. Hypertension isn’t just a risk factor for cardiovascular disease—it also increases the risk of cognitive decline, including dementia and Alzheimer’s disease.

The earlier high blood pressure develops, the greater the long-term risk. Cumulative exposure matters. Chronically elevated blood pressure impairs cerebral blood flow and nutrient delivery to neurons, accelerating brain atrophy and neurodegeneration over time.

This is one of the reasons exercise is so powerful for brain health. What protects the heart also protects the brain.
