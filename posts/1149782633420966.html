Exercise and cancer: evidence is mostly observational and modest

Louis Nervegna · Nov 01, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Medscape article and argues that the evidence that exercise protects against cancer is almost all observational, modest and strong only for a few cancers, with melanoma and prostate cancer possibly showing positive associations.

  • Association strong only in a few cancer types
  • Melanoma and prostate cancer may show modest positive associations

Almost all the evidence that exercise protects against cancer comes from observational studies. Even then, the benefit is modest and the association is only strong in a few types of cancers. Many age-related cancers show no association whatsoever. A few cancers (melanoma and prostate) might actually show a modest positive association with exercise.

The health benefits of exercise are very real, but influencers are doing a disservice to the public by overhyping what it can realistically achieve.

Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.

A Nature Aging multi-omics study compared skeletal muscle from younger and older adults and found that about half of the age-related molecular differences were absent in exercise-trained older adults, who looked more like young muscle. Gene expression for cellular respiration and energy metabolism was preserved.
