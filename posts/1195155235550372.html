Swapping refined starch for resistant starch halved liver fat in 4 months in an RCT

Louis Nervegna · Jan 05, 2026 · ♥ 17 reactions · 24 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatCarbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsGut microbiome
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a randomized trial in which one-for-one replacement of refined starch with resistant starch cut liver fat in half in 4 months. The proposed mechanism is microbiome changes and more short-chain fatty acids.

  • Liver fat cut in half in 4 months
  • Mechanism: increased short-chain fatty acid production via microbiome
  • Liver fat described as the principal driver of insulin resistance

Replacing refined starch with resistant starch one-for-one cuts liver fat in HALF in just 4 months.

These results are from a recent randomized controlled trial.

The mechanism is thought to be related to beneficial changes that occur in the microbiome as a result of consuming resistant starch, particularly an increase in production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFA’s).

As we’ve talked about many times in the group, liver fat is the principle driver of insulin resistance in most people. See this previous post on the topic: ↪ Liver fat is the biggest driver of insulin resistance in most people, per a Harvard expert.

Note that the diabetes drug acarbose essentially turns most starch into resistant starch by blocking the action of the digestive enzyme amylase which breaks down starch in the small intestine. You might be able to replicate these results by taking acarbose. See this post: ↪ Acarbose cut liver fat in a 12-week RCT in women with PCOS, unlike metformin or diet.
