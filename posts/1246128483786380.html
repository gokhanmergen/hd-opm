RED WINE AND ATHEROSCLEROSIS

Higher CAC Score… Better Outcomes?

A 2018 Brazilian study (da Luz et al., Braz J Med Biol Res) followed 200 long-term habitual red wine drinkers and 154 abstainers for 5.5 years, tracking death, heart attack, and need for coronary revascularization (MACE) and discovered a counterintuitive finding- red wine drinkers had higher CAC scores than abstainers, but they experienced significantly fewer cardiac events. Zero heart attacks in drinkers vs. 6 in abstainers. The MACE rate was 0.2 vs. 1.5 per 100 person-years.

When they accounted for confounders, red wine consumption was still independently associated with a 79% reduction in MACE (HR 0.21). Drinkers also had lower CRP, suggesting an anti-inflammatory effect may be part of the story.

The proposed mechanism? Red wine polyphenols may promote plaque stabilization, shifting developing plaques toward a more calcified, less inflamed, and less rupture-prone phenotype. Higher CAC, in this context, may reflect healed or stabilized plaque rather than active, vulnerable disease. This parallels what we see with high-dose statin therapy, where CAC can increase even as cardiovascular events decrease.

Important limitations: This was an observational study, with all male, predominantly healthy, self-selected drinkers. Confounding can’t be fully excluded (drinkers had less diabetes, slightly healthier metabolic profiles). A second CAC scan wasn’t done, so progression couldn’t be tracked. And we can’t separate the polyphenol effect from the alcohol effect or the broader lifestyle.

Bottom line: A higher CAC score doesn’t always mean worse prognosis, context matters a lot. Plaque composition and stability may be as important as plaque burden. This study adds to a growing picture suggesting that certain anti-inflammatory interventions may promote a protective calcification phenotype.

To me, you can certainly skip the alcohol component, which is clearly harmful in other ways, and get similar polyphenol effects via other foods, but they sure seem important one way or another.
