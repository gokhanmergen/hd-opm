Metformin's glucose-lowering effect involves the gut microbiome

Louis Nervegna · Sep 12, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesGut microbiome
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a 2018 review concluding that gut microbiota participate in metformin's glucose-lowering effects in diabetes. Metformin alters gut bacterial composition, but the exact mechanisms still need to be worked out.

  • Metformin enhances growth of bacteria such as Akkermansia muciniphila
  • Gut dysbiosis has been linked to type 2 diabetes

Metformin encourages the growth of beneficial bacteria in the gut, which lower blood sugar.

Quoted:

“It is now clear that gut microbiota participates to the glucose-lowering effects of metformin in the context of diabetes. Further work is now needed to determine the exact mechanisms of action of the drug and to understand by which processes metformin is able to enhance the growth of some bacteria exhibiting beneficial effects for the host.”

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.
