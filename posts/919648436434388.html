Review: moderate coffee intake linked to lower mortality and age-related disease

Gokhan Can · Nov 29, 2024 · ♥ 17 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A review of coffee and human aging reports that regular moderate coffee consumption is associated with lower all-cause mortality and fewer cardiovascular, stroke and cancer cases. Coffee and its components (caffeine, chlorogenic acids) appear to support stress adaptation and aging mechanisms.

  • Moderate coffee reduces all-cause mortality
  • Lower risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke and cancer
  • Caffeine and chlorogenic acids influence stress-adaptation pathways

Impact of coffee intake on human aging: Epidemiology and cellular mechanisms

  • The regular consumption of moderate doses of coffee reduces all-cause mortality
  • Coffee intake reduces age-associated diseases (cardiovascular, stroke, cancer)
  • Coffee and its main components (caffeine, chlorogenic acids) control stress adaptation
  • Coffee intake preserves the main biological mechanisms of aging

"This pleasurable lifestyle factor should be enjoyed as a ‘treasure trove of benefits tailored for the golden years’."

Summarizes an individual-participant meta-analysis from the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists showing that about five years of statin therapy did not change new cancer diagnoses or cancer deaths. This is reassuring evidence against fears that lowering LDL promotes cancer.

Statins work as well in women as men: 27-trial CTT meta-analysis of 174,000 participants

Dec 19, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 11 Statins

Louis rebuts the claim that statins are less effective in women using the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' meta-analysis of 27 randomized trials. Per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction, proportional reductions in major vascular events and all-cause mortality were similar in women and men, with no adverse effect on cancer or non-CV mortality. Trials averaged 4.9 years, so longer-term benefit is likely larger.

About three cups of coffee a day tracks with lowest cardiometabolic risk

Jul 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis discusses a UK cohort of 188,000 adults where moderate coffee drinkers (about three cups) had roughly 48% lower risk of developing two or more of type 2 diabetes, coronary disease and stroke. He cautions that most coffee research is observational, so the exact optimum is uncertain.

Caffeine activates AMPK in yeast cells, which may explain coffee's longevity links

Jun 26, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 11 · 💬 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A Queen Mary University of London study in Microbial Cell found that caffeine acts via AMPK, the cellular fuel gauge, influencing growth, DNA repair and stress response. It was done in fission yeast, so human relevance is unproven.
