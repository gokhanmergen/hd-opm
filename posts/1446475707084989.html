Olive oil has the strongest trial evidence, but canola and soybean oil also have support

Louis Nervegna · Nov 10, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that olive oil is the only fat tested in multiple large randomized outcome trials (CORDIOPREV, PREDIMED, Lyon). Lyon also tested canola oil with olive oil and saw the largest total mortality reduction in a cardiology prevention trial, and the Indo-Mediterranean study (about 1,000 people) tested soybean oil. Harvard cohorts link canola or soybean oil in place of butter to lower cancer, CV and total mortality.

  • Olive oil: CORDIOPREV, PREDIMED, Lyon Heart Study
  • Lyon (canola + olive oil): largest total mortality reduction in a prevention trial
  • Indo-Mediterranean Heart Study: about 1,000 people, soybean oil, fewer heart attacks and strokes
  • Cohorts: canola or soybean oil instead of butter linked to lower cancer, CV and total mortality

Olive oil is the only fat source that has been rigorously tested in multiple large randomized outcome trials focused on heart disease, e.g. CORDIOPREV, PREDIMED, and the Lyon Heart Study:

↪ Olive oil is the only fat tested in several large trials: Lyon, PREDIMED and CORDIOPREV

↪ Meta-analysis of 4 Mediterranean diet RCTs: MACE down 48%, CVD death down 46%

However, the Lyon Heart Study also tested canola oil in combination with olive oil and achieved the largest reduction in total mortality ever reported in a cardiology prevention trial:

↪ Lyon Diet Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut cardiac death and MI 73% post-heart attack

And the Indo-Mediterranean Heart Study was a fairly large randomized outcome trial (~1000 people) that tested soybean oil in exclusion of olive oil. It achieved reductions in heart attacks and strokes comparable to CORDIOPREV, PREDIMED, and Lyon:

Paper · The LancetThe Lancet articlethelancet.com

So while the evidence for canola oil and soybean oil from large randomized outcome trials is not nearly as strong as olive oil, it does exist.

What we do have is an enormous amount of data from long-running prospective cohort studies on canola oil and soybean oil, which is overwhelmingly positive. Walter Willett aludes to this in the video clip below.

Take the data from the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicians Followup Study, for example. In tracking the diets of hundreds of thousands of participants from midlife to old age over a 30-year long period, use of canola oil or soybean oil in place of butter was associated with major reductions in cancer mortality, cardiovascular mortality, and total mortality:

↪ Replacing butter with plant oils linked to 17% lower mortality in 221,054 US adults

Quoted from the original post:

Olive oil gets all the glory in the Blue Zones conversation, but Dr. Walter Willett makes the case for widening the circle.

His take: olive oil earns its reputation, the flavor, the history, the data from the longest-lived populations we've studied. But soybean and canola oil deserve a seat at the table too. The distinction that matters is Omega-3 vs. Omega-6. Corn and sunflower oils bring Omega-6 but skip the Omega-3. Soybean oil brings both.

The takeaway isn't "switch oils." It's that most liquid plant oils are doing more for you than you think, and the real upgrade is swapping out saturated fats for any of these unsaturated options.
