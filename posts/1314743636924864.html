New statin use linked to less frailty in older veterans; STAREE will test it

Louis Nervegna · Jun 10, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares the observational VA/Mass General Brigham study (JACC) reporting that older US veterans starting statins developed less frailty over time. He notes two large randomized trials, PREVENTABLE and STAREE, are testing statins in the elderly and tracking frailty, with STAREE expected to report within months.

  • Observational study of nearly 1,000,000 veterans (per Joel Kahn summary); new statin use lowered risk of incident frailty
  • PREVENTABLE and STAREE are randomized trials of statins in older adults, both tracking frailty
  • Needs replication; observational design cannot prove causality

Statins may protect against frailty in the elderly.

There are currently two large randomized trials testing whether statins extend lifespan in the elderly, PREVENTABLE and STAREE. Both are also tracking frailty.

The STAREE trial has completed and is going to report results in just a few months. So we will get a rigorous answer on whether statins truly prevent frailty very shortly.

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.

STAREE statin trial in adults over 70 is finished; results due in second half of 2026

May 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 6 ↩ 1 Statins

Louis reposts details of STAREE: a placebo-controlled trial of atorvastatin 40 mg in about 10,000 healthy Australians aged 70+, with disability-free survival (including dementia-free) as the primary outcome. The trial website says follow-up has ended and results are expected in the second half of 2026.
