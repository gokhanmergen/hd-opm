Blending bananas with berries does not cancel the berries' benefits

Louis Nervegna · Jul 24, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Myths, misinformation & controversiesNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a rebuttal of the claim that bananas in smoothies negate berry polyphenols. Banana polyphenol oxidase can break down some polyphenols such as epicatechins but does not degrade most anthocyanins, the main blueberry polyphenols, so the effect is likely minor.

  • It does not degrade most anthocyanins

Shared from Dr. Matthew Nagra, ND · original post

No, blending bananas with your berries does not cancel out the benefits of the berries. This is a great example of how people with credentials like @foundmyfitness can create unnecessary fear around foods on social media.

It is true that the bananas contain polyphenol oxidase (PPO) and that that can break down certain polyphenols like epicatechins, but that doesn’t mean all the benefits go out the window. For example, that enzyme doesn’t degrade most anthocyanins, which are the polyphenols most often associated with blueberries. So you’re not losing those benefits.

That alone makes this sort of fearmongering ridiculous, but it’s even worse.

As my friend @gilcarvalho.md pointed out on X, one of the main reasons that the levels of certain polyphenols was so much lower in people who consumed the banana/berry smoothie compared to just the berry smoothie was that they let the smoothie sit there for 30 to 60 minutes before consuming it! Over that time, the PPO enzyme is breaking down those specific polyphenols. However, levels were much higher in people who consumed the smoothie right after it was made, which I’d wager is when most of us consume our smoothies. Don’t get me wrong, levels were still lower than with the berry smoothie, but the difference wasn’t as striking.

So no, you don’t have to toss out the bananas. If the texture and flavour of adding bananas to your smoothies helps you get more fruit in, that’s a good thing. We don’t need to be creating fear around adding such a healthy food.
