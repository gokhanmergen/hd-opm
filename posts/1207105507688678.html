Dates spike blood sugar briefly but do not worsen long-term glycemic control

Louis Nervegna · Jan 22, 2026 · ♥ 21 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes dates, despite high sugar, do not seem to harm longer-term glucose control or insulin resistance, may modestly improve them and appear to lower LDL, with a short-term spike. He plans to use dates as a sweetener.

  • No negative effect on longer-term glycemic markers
  • May modestly improve insulin resistance and lower LDL
  • Short-term blood sugar spike

Amazingly, dates don’t seem to negatively impact longer-term markers of glycemic control or insulin resistance, despite being very high in sugar.

In fact, they might even modestly improve them. And they seem to lower LDL cholesterol.

They do cause a short term blood sugar spike. (The video is a little misleading about this.) But longer term, they seem to have no deleterious effect on markers of glucose control and insulin sensitivity.

I am planning to use whole dates more often as a natural sweetener.

High blood xylitol linked to 57% higher MACE risk in observational cohorts (ESC 2026)

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A study presented at ESC Congress 2026 analyzed 17,710 participants from CLSA and EPIC-Norfolk. Highest-quartile xylitol levels were associated with 57% higher MACE risk over six years in CLSA and 18% higher over 30 years in EPIC-Norfolk, with a dose-response pattern. It is observational and cannot show xylitol causes events.
