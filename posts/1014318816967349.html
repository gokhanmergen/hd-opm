Vitamin D lowered depression symptoms dose-dependently in meta-analysis of 31 RCTs

Louis Nervegna · Apr 25, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

Examine.com summary of a dose-response meta-analysis of 31 RCTs: vitamin D reduced depressive symptoms, with most benefit near 5,000 IU/day and benefit seen only in people already depressed and not vitamin D sufficient. Most trials were under six months. Off the cardiovascular focus.

  • 31 RCTs
  • Most benefit at about 5,000 IU/day; effect unclear above 8,000 IU/day
  • No benefit in people with levels above 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L)
  • Most trials shorter than 6 months; UL is 4,000 IU/day

Research cited

  • Vitamin D and depression dose-response meta-analysis · meta-analysis
Shared from Examine.com · original post

A meta-analysis of 31 randomized controlled trials found that supplemental vitamin D reduced depression symptoms in a dose-dependent way — higher doses led to greater improvements.

The study used a method developed by Swedish researchers in 2016 to combine data from trials with different vitamin D dosages. This allowed them to create a dose-response curve estimating how much benefit each dosage provided.

Most of the benefit was observed at about 5,000 IU of vitamin D per day. However, the data became too messy to confirm whether doses above 8,000 IU per day had any effect.

The benefits of vitamin D were only observed in people with existing depression. Additionally, no benefits were seen in participants who already had sufficient vitamin D levels (above 30 ng/mL or 75 nmol/L). This highlights the importance of considering vitamin D supplementation on an individual basis.

Most of the included studies had a duration of less than six months, so the long-term effects of vitamin D remain unclear. This is important because the Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) for vitamin D is 4,000 IU per day, and taking more than that for longer than six months could increase the risk of adverse side effects.
