Statins for all could halve heart attacks yet add only 10-20 days to life over 5 years

Louis Nervegna · May 14, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsHeart attack, stroke & proceduresHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis works through why universal statin use could cut lifetime heart attack and stroke risk from about 20% to 10% while extending average lifespan by only days over 5 years: only about 10% of heart attacks and strokes are fatal, and only 1 in 5 US deaths are from them, so the reduction in overall deaths is about 50 times smaller. His arithmetic suggests a 0.022% to 0.094% reduction in overall deaths over 5 years. Note that this is a population-averaged back-of-envelope calculation.

  • Statin RCTs of about 5 years: 1.1% to 4.7% absolute reduction in heart attack/stroke
  • Lifetime absolute risk of heart attack or stroke about 20% for the average American
  • About 10% of heart attacks/strokes are fatal; 1 in 5 US deaths are from them, so a factor of 50
  • Implied overall death reduction of 0.022% to 0.094% over 5 years

If everyone in the USA took a statin long term, it would cut the number of heart attacks and strokes in half. But over a 5 year period, this would only translate into adding 10-20 days to the average persons lifespan.

Here’s the math:

Statins have been shown to reduce absolute risk of having a heart attack or stroke by anywhere from 1.1% to 4.7% in randomized controlled trials lasting about 5 years.

This is actually very good because the lifetime absolute risk of a heart attack or stroke is only 20% for the average American. So lowering it by 1.1% to 4.7% in only 5 years is a big deal. If you keep this up long term, the average American can reduce their lifetime absolute risk of heart attack or stroke from 20% to 10%. That would cut the number of heart attacks and strokes in the USA in half.

↪ A 1.1-4.7% absolute risk reduction from statins is large against a 20% lifetime risk

So is all of this consistent with the claim that statins would only extend the average person’s life by 10-20 days over a 5 year period?

Yes it is. Here is why.

Because only about 10% of people who have a heart attack or stroke actually die from it. And only 1 in 5 deaths in the USA are from heart attacks and strokes. This means that the reduction in overall deaths would be a factor of 10*5 = 50 less than the reduction in heart attacks and strokes. So you would only prevent somewhere between 1.1%/50=0.022% to and 4.7%/50=0.094% of overall deaths in a 5 year period. That percentage reduction in overall deaths is tiny. It would translate into the average person living only a few more days over that same 5 year period.
