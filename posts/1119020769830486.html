Louis shares a chart showing US heart disease death rates fell from over 500 per 100,000 in 1950 to almost 100 in 2020, a larger decline than for any other major disease. He argues the timing correlates with the introduction of statins and ever-lower LDL targets, so that heart disease has become a manageable chronic disease when treated aggressively.

  • Over 500 deaths per 100,000 in 1950 to almost 100 in 2020
  • Larger drop than any other major disease
  • Timing correlates with statins and lower LDL targets (correlation, not proof)

Death rates from heart disease have fallen by almost 5x since the 1950’s.

We’ve gone from over 500 deaths per 100,000 in 1950 down to almost 100 in 2020:

↪ US heart disease death rate fell about 5-fold, from over 500 to almost 100 per 100,000

The drops strongly correlate with the introduction of cholesterol lowering drugs, and with the steady reduction in LDL targets over time:

↪ Heart disease deaths fell almost 5x since 1950 as LDL targets tightened

No other disease has seen such a massive decrease in death rates, during that timeframe. Nothing else is even close, as the chart shows.
