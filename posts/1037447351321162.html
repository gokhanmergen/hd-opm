Framingham: lower small dense LDL linked to lower Alzheimer's risk

Louis Nervegna · May 31, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

A Medical Xpress report on the Framingham Heart Study found lower small dense LDL cholesterol and higher fat-transport markers were associated with reduced Alzheimer's disease risk, pointing to a complex relationship between blood lipids and brain health.

  • Lower small dense LDL associated with lower Alzheimer's risk
  • Higher fat-transport markers also associated with reduced risk

This new finding comes from the famous Framingham Heart Study.

Lower levels of small dense LDL cholesterol and higher levels of fat-transport markers in the blood are associated with reduced risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease. These findings highlight the complex relationship between blood lipids and brain health, suggesting new directions for dementia risk assessment and prevention.

High LDL does not protect the brain: 2024 Lancet Commission lists LDL as top dementia risk

Dec 15, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 38 Brain health & dementia

Louis counters the claim that high blood LDL protects against dementia. The 2024 Lancet Commission lists LDL cholesterol as the biggest modifiable dementia risk factor, accounting for 7% of cases among 14 factors that together account for 45%. He adds circulating cholesterol does not cross the blood-brain barrier; the brain makes its own.
