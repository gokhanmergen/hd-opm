AI summary & key facts

In the FLOWPROMOTE randomized trial (JAMA Cardiology), 40 mg atorvastatin shrank total plaque by about 14% over 18 months, while 40 mg rosuvastatin plus 10 mg ezetimibe shrank it by about 21%, measured with before-and-after coronary CT angiography. A shared summary adds that 104 newly diagnosed coronary disease patients took part, LDL fell to about 43 in the combination arm, and soft plaque fell about 28%. The trial was small and measured plaque, not heart attacks.

Dangerous, rupture-prone heart plaque shrank in 98% of patients on this combo. 🫀

A new trial in JAMA Cardiology (FLOWPROMOTE, published Oct 30) followed 104 people newly diagnosed with coronary artery disease for 18 months.

Half took a standard statin. Half took high-dose rosuvastatin plus ezetimibe.

In the combo group, LDL dropped to around 43. Total plaque went down about 21%, and the soft, dangerous plaque went down about 28%. Plaque shrank in both groups, and the lower LDL went, the more it shrank.

What this study can’t tell us yet: it was small, it measured plaque on CT scans instead of heart attacks, and everyone was just starting treatment. Still, it fits years of research showing lower LDL means less plaque and lower risk.

Your move: know your LDL number, and ask your doctor what your target should be.
