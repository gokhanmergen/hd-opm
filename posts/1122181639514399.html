Lowering LDL about 80 mg/dL for 52 years could cut lifetime heart attack risk 20% to 4%

Louis Nervegna · Sep 27, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Mendelian randomization studies suggest a lifelong LDL reduction of about 80 mg/dL lowers heart attack risk by about 80% in relative terms. Assuming a 20% lifetime absolute risk, as for the typical American, that drops to about 4%.

  • About 80 mg/dL lower LDL over ~52 years
  • Relative risk reduction around 80%
  • Typical lifetime absolute heart attack risk about 20% (1 in 5 Americans)
  • Math: 100% - 0.8 x 20% = 4%

How much can you lower your LIFETIME risk of a heart attack with LDL lowering therapy?

————-

Mendelian randomization studies provide an answer to this question:

Dropping LDL by about 80mg/dL for a period of around 52 years, reduces the typical lifetime absolute risk of a heart attack from 20% all the way down to 4%.

This corresponds to a relative risk reduction of 80% as shown in the blue line on the chart below. Under the assumption that the average person’s lifetime absolute risk of a heart attack is about 20%, this translates to a reduction in absolute risk down to 4%. (The math: 100% - 0.8*20% = 4%)

(The typical American has about a 20% lifetime absolute risk of a heart attack, because 1 in 5 Americans will have a heart attack during their life.)

See these posts for more details on converting relative risk to absolute risk, and vice-versa:

↪ LDL down 39 mg/dL: 20%+ fewer heart attacks in RCTs, 30%+ in cohorts, 50%+ genetically

↪ Statin absolute risk reduction is cumulative: 1-2% in trials, far more over a lifetime

As for living a lot longer, you are going to have to majorly reduce your risk of other diseases like cancer in order to do that. You can’t expect lowering LDL alone to accomplish that. You will need diet and lifestyle to do that.
