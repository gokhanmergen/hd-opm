The traditional Mediterranean diet was a cheap peasant diet of about 90% plant foods

Louis Nervegna · Dec 02, 2025 · ♥ 19 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis argues the traditional Mediterranean diet was mostly cheap plant food (legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains) with about 10% seafood, yogurt, cheese, poultry and eggs, and rare unprocessed red meat. He says people ate this for cost and taste, and that the modern Mediterranean diet has drifted toward the standard American diet and is no longer healthy.

  • Roughly 90% healthy plant foods
  • Protein mainly from legumes, nuts, seeds and whole grains
  • Fermented dairy preserved milk; red meat was a special occasion food
  • Modern Mediterranean eating is claimed to resemble the standard American diet

The traditional Mediterranean diet was a peasant diet.

They ate almost 90% healthy plant foods, and got most of their protein from legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains. The other 10% was seafood, yogurt, and some cheese, poultry, eggs. On special occasions, they had unprocessed red meat. Fancy processed meats like prosciutto were not a part of this traditional peasant Mediterranean diet. They were eaten rarely, if ever.

The reason for all this was cost.

Plant food was cheap. Olives were everywhere, so the oil was easy to make from scratch by simply pressing them mechanically. Seafood was cheap. Fermenting dairy into yogurt and cheese preserved it for much longer, so they could eat it well after the animals stopped lactating. Killing the animals for red meat was not economical because they could get far more food year round by fermenting the milk.

They chose these foods because they were cheap and tasted good. Not because they were healthy.

They got lucky by eating this way. As the population got wealthier, the Mediterranean diet got less and less healthy. Today, it is not much different from the standard American diet. The modern Mediterranean diet is NOT healthy.
