Low-dose lithium shows neuroprotective signals and lower dementia risk in a review

Louis Nervegna · Jun 11, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a JAMA Psychiatry narrative review concluding lithium has neuroprotective effects across cellular, imaging and epidemiologic studies. Low-dose lithium below psychiatric doses preserves neurons and gray matter, cumulative exposure is associated with lower dementia risk, and early trials in mild cognitive impairment look favorable.

  • Lithium upregulates anti-apoptotic proteins, boosts neurotrophic factors, inhibits tau-related kinases, stabilizes mitochondria
  • Low doses below standard psychiatric dosing preserve neuronal viability and gray matter
  • Cumulative exposure associated with reduced dementia risk; trials are early

Shared from JAMA Psychiatry · original post

📝 Narrative Review: #Lithium, a long-standing mood stabilizer, demonstrates convergent neuroprotective and neurotrophic effects relevant to #Dementia and #AlzheimerDisease.

Evidence across cellular, preclinical, human #neuroimaging, and #epidemiologic studies indicates that lithium upregulates anti-apoptotic proteins, boosts neurotrophic factors, inhibits tau-relevant kinase pathways, and stabilizes #mitochondrial function.

Low-dose lithium, which is below standard #psychiatric dosing, preserves neuronal viability and gray matter, and cumulative exposure is associated with reduced dementia risk.

Early clinical trials in mild cognitive impairment suggest favorable biomarker and cognitive outcomes, supporting prospective studies of low-dose lithium as a disease-modifying treatment.
