The brain makes its own cholesterol and does not use cholesterol from the blood

Louis Nervegna · Jul 01, 2026 · ♥ 18 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaLDL, ApoB & lipid panelMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis corrects a common online claim: the blood-brain barrier prevents the brain from using circulating cholesterol, so the brain synthesizes what it needs. The shared post adds that diet affects brain health mainly through blood vessels, and that saturated and trans fats raise LDL while unsaturated fats lower it.

  • Blood-brain barrier prevents use of blood cholesterol by the brain
  • Saturated and trans fats raise LDL particles; unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, seeds) lower them

If I had a nickel for every person online who insists that their brain uses cholesterol from their blood…

It’s just not true. Your brain makes its own cholesterol. The blood brain barrier prevents your brain from using cholesterol circulating in the blood.

#bbbHD #braincholesterolHD

Shared from The Brain Docs · original post

“Eat less fat.” “Eat more fat.” No wonder everyone’s confused!

The truth is: your body doesn’t see “fat” as one thing. Saturated and trans fats push LDL particles up. Unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, seeds) bring them down. That’s about as settled as nutrition science gets.

Here’s the part that surprises people: your brain makes all the cholesterol it needs on its own. It doesn’t pull any from your blood. So what your diet is really shaping isn’t the brain directly. It’s the blood vessels that keep it running. And midlife is the window where that matters most.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).
