Cocoa compound theobromine linked to slower biological aging in two European cohorts

Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Scientists at King’s College London have identified a natural cocoa compound, theobromine, as being linked to slower biological aging. By analyzing blood samples from more than 1,600 participants across two European cohorts (TwinsUK and KORA), researchers found that individuals with higher levels of theobromine tended to appear biologically younger than their actual age. Biological age was measured using DNA methylation markers and telomere length, both of which reflect how well the body is functioning rather than how many years a person has lived.

The study showed that theobromine stood out among other cocoa- and coffee-related compounds, as it was the only one consistently associated with slower epigenetic aging. While theobromine is commonly known for being toxic to dogs, in humans it has been linked to potential health benefits such as reduced heart disease risk. Researchers caution, however, that the findings do not suggest eating more dark chocolate as a shortcut to longevity, since chocolate also contains sugar and fat. Instead, the results highlight how everyday dietary compounds may influence gene regulation and aging, opening new directions for future aging and nutrition research.

Cocoa flavanols lower blood pressure and cut cardiovascular deaths 27% in COSMOS

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 3 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

Louis shares a summary of cocoa flavanol evidence. An updated meta-analysis of 25 RCTs showed modest drops in blood pressure and improved flow-mediated dilation. The COSMOS trial gave 500 mg/day of flavanols to 21,442 older adults for a median 3.6 years, with total CV events down 10% (within chance) and CV deaths down 27%. A 2026 severity-ranked reanalysis favored cocoa.
