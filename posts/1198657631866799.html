Baby aspirin reduced liver fat in a small 6-month randomized trial of MASLD patients

Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

According to a 2024 randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial, adults with metabolic dysfunction–associated fatty liver disease who took low-dose aspirin (81 mg once daily) for 6 months showed a measurable reduction in liver fat. Liver fat was assessed using magnetic resonance spectroscopy, a direct and objective imaging method. On average, participants in the aspirin group experienced about a 10% reduction in hepatic fat content, while the placebo group showed little to no improvement.

The study also found that the response was not uniform across all participants. About 42.5% of people taking aspirin achieved a clinically meaningful reduction of at least 30% in liver fat, compared with 12.5% in the placebo group. The researchers noted that aspirin’s effect is likely related to its anti-inflammatory action and impact on platelet-mediated liver inflammation, but they emphasized that aspirin is not a cure and should be considered an adjunct approach that requires further validation in larger and longer studies.
