Heart attack clots form on ruptured unstable plaque, not in plaque-free arteries

Louis argues that although most heart attacks are caused by clots, clots form when unstable plaque ruptures, so people with near-zero plaque have near-zero risk of clot-caused heart attacks. Blood thinners like aspirin are proven to lower risk in people with considerable plaque but are unlikely to help, and may harm, those with little or no plaque.

Clots and Heart Attacks

Most heart attacks are caused by clots.

But people with arteries clear of plaque do not magically get heart attacks because clots form out of nowhere.

This idea— promoted by quacks and charlatans on social media— is complete nonsense.

Clots form when unstable plaque ruptures.

It is the unstable plaque that is ultimately responsible for the heart attack.

Many imaging studies have proven that people with near-zero plaque have near-zero risk of heart attacks caused by clots.

It is very rare for a heart attack caused by a clot to occur in a person with no arterial plaque.

If you have considerable plaque, then taking a blood thinner like aspirin is proven to lower risk of heart attacks and strokes in large randomized controlled trials. This is why people at high risk are routinely prescribed blood thinners, while people at low risk (who have little plaque) are not. People with little to no plaque are very unlikely to benefit from blood thinners like aspirin. For people with no plaque, the risk of blood thinners far outweighs any potential benefit.

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.
