Vasomotion: capillary sphincters constantly reroute blood flow

Louis Nervegna · Sep 24, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

A popular-science post explaining that tiny precapillary sphincters in the smallest vessels open and close rhythmically, deciding which capillary beds receive blood. This reroutes blood to active muscle or the gut after a meal without conscious control.

  • Precapillary sphincters contract and relax continuously
  • Reroutes blood to where it is needed, e.g. exercised muscle or stomach after a meal

#vasomotionHD

Shared from Humans Are Weird · original post

Right now, without any input from you, thousands of tiny valves inside your blood vessels are opening and closing in a constant, silent rhythm. 🩸

It is called vasomotion, and it happens in the smallest blood vessels in your body, the capillaries. Tiny rings of muscle called precapillary sphincters contract and relax over and over, deciding moment to moment which capillary beds get a rush of fresh blood and which ones get skipped for now. It is how your body reroutes blood on the fly, sending more to a muscle you just used, or to your stomach after a meal, without you ever consciously directing a single drop.

What gets me about this one is the sheer scale of it. You have an estimated 25,000 miles of blood vessels inside you, and a huge portion of the smallest ones are being turned on and off like this every few seconds, all day, every day, completely below the level of anything you could ever feel or notice. It is one of those systems built entirely to run without your permission or awareness, and it never once takes a break. 🫀

Did you know your blood flow was being redirected like this constantly, or is this the first time vasomotion has crossed your radar?
