Higher CRP is more likely with obesity, smoking, female sex, no statin, high LDL-C and TG

Louis Nervegna · Apr 09, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

A shared study identifies characteristics associated with higher CRP levels, which could help interpret inflammation testing.

  • Higher CRP: overweight/obesity, current smokers, women, no statins, higher LDL-C and TG

People with the following characteristics are more likely to have higher CRP levels:

  • with overweight or obesity
  • current smokers
  • women
  • not receiving statins
  • with higher levels of LDL-C and triglycerides

Meta-analysis: statins cut arterial wall inflammation on PET-CT, high-intensity most

Oct 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 Statins

Louis highlights a 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 clinical trials (373 subjects) using 18F-FDG PET-CT, a marker of arterial wall inflammation. Statins markedly reduced uptake (target-to-background ratio), significantly so for high-intensity therapy and non-significantly for low-to-moderate intensity.

Statins, especially with ezetimibe, lower Lp-PLA2, a plaque-specific inflammation marker

Louis explains that Lp-PLA2 reflects inflammation inside atherosclerotic plaque (unlike systemic hsCRP) and is recognised as a CVD risk factor. A quoted review reports pravastatin, atorvastatin, rosuvastatin and simvastatin significantly lower it, with ezetimibe added lowering it further.
