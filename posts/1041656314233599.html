Satirical list of types of people who oppose statins

A polemic describing ten stereotypes of statin critics. It is opinion with no studies cited.

10 Types of People Who Oppose Statins, and Why That's Misguided

  1. The Misinformed Layperson

Lacks a full understanding of the medical evidence or relies on cherry-picked data that confirms personal biases. Often unaware of the overwhelming body of research supporting statin use.

  1. The Financially Invested Influencer or Doctor

Some doctors and online personalities profit from promoting alternative health narratives. For example, low-carb advocates may oppose statins because their dietary advice tends to raise LDL levels, making statin rejection essential to protect their brand and income.

  1. The Echo Chamber Resident

Relies heavily on a narrow range of online sources, particularly contrarian influencers or fringe physicians, without exposure to consensus opinions from actual cardiovascular experts.

  1. The “Cholesterol Is Essential” Believer

Misunderstands cholesterol metabolism, thinking the body must obtain it from blood. In reality, nearly all cells can synthesize their own cholesterol. High levels of circulating LDL is not essential for hormone production or general health.

  1. The Natural Fallacy Advocate

Argues, “Cholesterol is natural, so it must be good.” But nature also gives us cancer, aging, and genetic diseases. LDL cholesterol, while biologically useful, is harmful in excess — a classic case of antagonistic pleiotropy.

  1. The “High Cholesterol Equals Longevity” Misinterpreter

Confuses correlation with causation, citing studies where people with high cholesterol live longer, often ignoring confounders like reverse causality or selection bias in older populations.

  1. The “Low Cholesterol Is Risky” Person

Claims that low cholesterol leads to early death, failing to distinguish between naturally low cholesterol and low levels caused by aging or serious illness — again confusing correlation with causation.

  1. The Side Effect Exaggerator

Overstates the prevalence or severity of statin side effects. In reality, most people tolerate statins well, and switching to a different statin usually resolves any issues.

  1. The Conspiracy Believer

Insists that all clinical trials are manipulated or fraudulent. Rejects decades of independent research in favor of unproven internet theories.

  1. The Pseudo-Intellectual Contrarian

Opposing mainstream science provides a sense of superiority or “insider knowledge.” For some, rejecting statins is less about evidence, more about ego, an identity or a brand.
