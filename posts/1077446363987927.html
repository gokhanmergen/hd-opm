About 7,000 steps a day captures most of the mortality benefit of walking

Research cited

Shared from Science Sphere · original post

Forget the 10,000-step myth—just 7000 steps a day can cut your risk of early death by nearly 50%, according to a sweeping global study led by the University of Sydney.

Analyzing data from 57 studies across 10 countries, researchers found that walking 7000 steps a day offers most of the health benefits traditionally associated with the 10,000-step goal—including significant reductions in heart disease, type 2 diabetes, dementia, and depression. In fact, the risk of death dropped 47% at 7000 steps—with only modest gains beyond that point.

The message? Progress beats perfection. Even increasing your daily steps from 2000 to 4000 shows measurable health benefits. For people who find traditional fitness targets daunting, this study offers a more realistic, achievable goal—one that could transform public health guidelines worldwide.
