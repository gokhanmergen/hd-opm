Observational, randomized and genetic evidence all argue against statins causing dementia

Louis Nervegna · Nov 01, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a new observational study linking statin use to lower dementia risk and lists the wider evidence in the group: observational studies as a whole suggest protection, randomized trials (including in people over 70) show no increase in dementia, trials of statins as Alzheimer's treatment are neutral, and Mendelian randomization suggests lower dementia risk with statin-pathway variants. It is a curated index of earlier group posts.

  • Randomized trials show no increase in dementia, including in over-70s
  • Alzheimer's treatment trials with statins were neutral
  • Mendelian randomization points to lower dementia risk

Yet another observational study associating statin use with lower risk of dementia.

While you can certainly cherry pick single observational studies showing the opposite, the observational research as a whole suggests a protective effect:

  1. ↪ Statin use linked to 20% lower dementia and 32% lower Alzheimer's risk in meta-analysis
  1. ↪ Statin users had 14% lower dementia risk in a meta-analysis of 7 million people
  1. ↪ Analysis of 125 million statin users links statins to lower Alzheimer's risk and mortality
  1. ↪ Statins do not cause dementia or cognitive decline and may be protective in cohorts
  1. ↪ Statins for 3+ years linked to 63% lower dementia risk in a 55-study meta-analysis

Randomized trials have also shown no increase in dementia risk in people randomized to statins, as compared to placebo. This is true even in people over 70yo:

  1. facebook.com/share/r/1B9ifpApuJ
  1. ↪ STAREE: statins reduced events 30% in healthy adults 70+, NNT 37, no dementia increase

Randomized trials testing statins as treatments for Alzheimer’s show a neutral effect. They don’t make the Alzheimer’s worse. Nor do they make it better:

  1. ↪ Two RCTs found statins neither helped nor harmed Alzheimer's disease progression

People born with genes that affect the same cholesterol pathways as statins have dramatically lower risk of dementia. Mendelian randomization studies provide convincing evidence for this:

  1. ↪ Statin- and ezetimibe-like gene variants linked to 76-82% lower dementia risk per 40 mg/dL

Louis highlights a Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis of about 1 million people showing lifelong lower non-HDL cholesterol via statin-like (HMGCR) and ezetimibe-like (NPC1L1) variants was associated with 76% and 82% lower dementia per ~40 mg/dL drop. CETP variants showed a 70% reduction.

A systematic review of observational studies found statin use associated with 14% lower all-cause dementia and 18% lower Alzheimer's. Louis corrects that these were cohort and case-control studies, not RCTs.

Analysis of 125 million statin users links statins to lower Alzheimer's risk and mortality

A shared summary of a large observational analysis finding statin therapy associated with reduced Alzheimer's dementia risk and lower all-cause mortality, slightly stronger for hydrophilic statins such as rosuvastatin. It controlled for demographics, comorbidities and labs but is observational.

Two RCTs found statins neither helped nor harmed Alzheimer's disease progression

Jun 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 5 ↩ 3 Statins

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as a treatment for established Alzheimer's: simvastatin in 406 people with normal lipids over 18 months, and atorvastatin 80 mg (LEADe) in people already on donepezil over 72 weeks. Both were neutral on cognition and safe.

Statins for 3+ years linked to 63% lower dementia risk in a 55-study meta-analysis

Feb 12, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 20 ↩ 1 Statins

Louis shares a news report of a Brazilian meta-analysis of 55 observational studies with about 7 million patients. Staying on statins for at least three years was associated with 63% lower dementia risk; the STAREE and PREVENTABLE trials should clarify causality.

Statin use linked to 20% lower dementia and 32% lower Alzheimer's risk in meta-analysis

Oct 22, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 1 ↩ 3 Statins

Louis cites a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies. Statin use was linked to a lower risk of dementia (36 studies) and Alzheimer's disease (21 studies), which the authors say confirms no neurocognitive risk and hints at a possible benefit. They call for dedicated randomized trials.
