HDL and ApoA-I raising therapies repeatedly failed, so HDL is probably not directly causal

Louis Nervegna · Mar 11, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelInsulin resistance & diabetesPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs
AI summary & key facts

Louis reviews why HDL or ApoA-I is probably not causal for heart disease. CETP inhibitor trials failed (ILLUMINATE with torcetrapib was stopped for harm; dal-OUTCOMES with dalcetrapib and ACCELERATE with evacetrapib showed no benefit), as did fibrate and niacin trials and CSL112 infusions, and Mendelian randomization agrees. He proposes HDL and ApoA-I mainly act as a proxy for insulin sensitivity, which is the real causal factor.

  • ILLUMINATE (torcetrapib): terminated early for more events and deaths
  • dal-OUTCOMES (dalcetrapib): HDL-C and ApoA-I up about 30%, no reduction in CV death, MI or stroke
  • ACCELERATE (evacetrapib): ApoA-I up over 100%, no significant MACE reduction, stopped early
  • Fibrate, niacin and CSL112 (native ApoA-I infusion) trials also failed; Mendelian randomization shows no benefit

Does HDL (ApoA-1) by itself play a major causal role in heart disease?

The answer from randomized controlled trials in humans is likely NO.

ApoA-1 is the main protein component of HDL in blood. Many studies have shown an association between lower levels of ApoA-1 (HDL) and higher risk of heart disease. The question is whether or not this association is actually causal?

Three major clinical trials testing drugs designed to raise HDL and apolipoprotein A-I (ApoA-I) levels in an attempt to reduce cardiovascular disease failed:

  1. ILLUMINATE Trial (torcetrapib)

Torcetrapib raised ApoA-I and HDL cholesterol levels substantially.

However, the trial was terminated early due to increased risk of cardiovascular events and mortality in the treatment group.

  1. dal-OUTCOMES Trial (dalcetrapib)

Dalcetrapib modestly raised ApoA-I and HDL-C levels by 30%.

But it did not reduce the risk of cardiovascular death, heart attack, or stroke compared to placebo.

  1. ACCELERATE Trial (evacetrapib)

Evacetrapib raised ApoA-I levels by over 100%. However, it did not significantly reduce major adverse cardiovascular events compared to placebo.

The trial was terminated early.

Other major trials also failed:

It’s not just the three CETP inhibitors mentioned above that have failed. Large trials that raised HDL and ApoA-1 by adding fibrates or niacin to standard of care failed.

Large trials of direct ApoA-1 therapies have also failed, e.g. CSL112. The recent failure of CSL112 in phase 3 was probably the nail in the coffin for ApoA-1 as directly causal. They literally gave the patients native ApoA-1 isolated from human plasma (CSL112) and it still failed.

↪ CSL112 ApoA-I infusion failed in phase 3 AEGIS-II, supporting HDL as marker not cause

The same result occurs in Mendelian randomization studies. Genes that predispose people to higher HDL or ApoA-1 (without increasing insulin sensitivity) do not lower lifetime risk.

Summary:

Interventions to raise ApoA-1 and HDL have repeatedly failed to improve clinical outcomes in high-risk patient populations. The same result occurs in Mendelian randomization studies. This likely refutes the hypothesis that simply increasing circulating ApoA-I or HDL levels is sufficient by itself for cardiovascular risk reduction.

A possible explanation for the inverse association of ApoA-1 with heart disease is that ApoA-1 is an indirect marker (or proxy) for insulin sensitivity— and it is actually insulin sensitivity that is causal, as opposed to ApoA-1 itself. Higher levels of ApoA-1 likely reflect better insulin sensitivity, and thus lower risk for heart disease. Interventions that raise ApoA-1 or HDL without improving insulin sensitivity (like the drugs used in the clinical trials discussed above) are unlikely to lower risk for heart disease.

Why insulin resistance lowers HDL, and why improving insulin sensitivity beats chasing HDL

Louis lists six mechanisms by which insulin resistance lowers HDL: impaired HDL maturation (lower LCAT), reduced apoA-I production, faster HDL catabolism (hepatic lipase, CETP), altered HDL composition and function, higher triglycerides making small dense HDL, and inflammation and oxidative stress. He concludes the best way to raise HDL is improving insulin sensitivity (lose visceral fat, exercise, cut refined starch and sugar), since insulin resistance is causal in heart disease while large RCTs suggest HDL itself is probably not.
