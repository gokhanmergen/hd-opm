Statins started within a year of type 2 diabetes tied to 15% lower dementia risk

Louis shares a Danish observational study of over 132,000 patients with type 2 diabetes; starting statins within a year was tied to 15% lower dementia risk over a decade, driven by vascular dementia. He notes it hints at starting early, as the STAREE trial showed no effect when starting in old age.

  • 132,000+ patients diagnosed 2006-2019 in Denmark
  • Statins within 1 year: 15% lower dementia risk over a decade
  • Delaying up to 5 years: 10% reduction
  • Benefit mainly vascular dementia, not Alzheimer's

This is yet another observational study suggesting a protective effect— or at least a neutral effect— of statins on dementia risk.

In this case, the benefit was seen mostly in risk for vascular dementia.

TIMING MATTERS: Starting statins within a year of a type 2 diabetes diagnosis was tied to a 15% lower risk of dementia over a decade.

Researchers tracked more than 132,000 patients in Denmark diagnosed between 2006 and 2019, finding that even those who delayed statins by up to five years still saw a 10% reduction in risk.

The benefit was driven specifically by lower rates of vascular dementia — not Alzheimer's — which researchers say makes biological sense because statins lower cholesterol, reduce inflammation and protect blood vessels in the brain.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
