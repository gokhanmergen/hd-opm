Obicetrapib improved Alzheimer's biomarkers, most in APOE4/4 carriers (BROADWAY substudy)

Louis Nervegna · May 23, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a pre-specified BROADWAY substudy (1,535 patients) in which obicetrapib improved five Alzheimer's biomarkers, with the largest benefit in APOE4/4 homozygotes. Caveats are that these are biomarkers, not cognitive outcomes, in a subgroup needing replication.

  • 1,535 patients; APOE4/4 had the largest benefit
  • Biomarkers: p-tau217, Abeta42/40, p-tau217/Abeta ratio, GFAP, NfL
  • Placebo-adjusted benefits 13.67 to 22.65%
  • GFAP -6.39% on obicetrapib vs +8.85% on placebo

The cholesterol drug obicetrapib may lower the risk of Alzheimer’s in APOE4/4 carriers.

This is exciting news because people with APOE4/4 are typically told there is no treatment that can lower their high risk.

This class of cholesterol drugs, CETP inhibitors, has not worked well against CVD so far. But the results with Alzheimer’s look very promising.

Quoted:

If you are APOE4/4 and you've been told genetics is destiny for 20 years — read this.

Pre-specified BROADWAY substudy. 1,535 patients. APOE4/4 homozygotes had the LARGEST benefit of any subgroup. All five biomarkers moved correctly — p-tau217, Abeta42/40, p-tau217/Abeta ratio, GFAP, NfL. Placebo-adjusted benefits 13.67 to 22.65 percent. GFAP alone: -6.39% on obicetrapib vs +8.85% on placebo. Davidson and colleagues wrote: "first demonstration of an oral intervention capable of reducing both beta-amyloid and tau pathology biomarkers in ApoE4 carriers." This is education, not medical advice. Caveats: biomarker not cognitive outcome, subgroup is 1,535-total slice, needs replication. I got emotional reading this paper.

The European Commission approved obicetrapib, a low-dose oral CETP inhibitor, on 21 September 2026 in two versions: obicetrapib 10 mg alone and combined with ezetimibe 10 mg. Phase 3 trials reported LDL-C reductions of about 40% alone and about 50% with ezetimibe versus placebo. The poster says the drug has shown promise for both cardiovascular disease and Alzheimer's.
