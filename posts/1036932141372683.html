KETO-CTA: why Louis thinks the flawed paper should stay published

Louis Nervegna · May 30, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivorePlaque, CAC & CT angiographyLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis responds to the KETO-CTA controversy, saying the paper should not be retracted because the data are valuable and public. He thinks criticism targeted the write-up, not the methodology, and that it is the only CT-angiography study on lean, metabolically healthy keto and carnivore dieters.

  • Authors released the full dataset
  • Criticisms mainly of the write-up, not methods (Louis's view)
  • Only CTA study in lean people with high HDL and low triglycerides on keto/carnivore diets

The MEDSCAPE review that Tom Dayspring is referring to can be found at this link:

MedscapeNo, KETO-CTA Study Did Not Upend Causal Evidence on ASCVDIs there really no link between high LDL-C and atherosclerosis in lean-mass hyperresponders on a keto diet? The data are nowhere close to supporting the authors' outsized claims, writes John Mandrola.medscape.com

For the record, I don’t think that this paper should be retracted at this point. The data is too valuable. The authors have been very open about releasing the full dataset publicly. JACC has published some of the letters to the editor legitimately criticizing the paper. The authors have admitted many of their mistakes, and are now being much more responsible about the claims they are making about the paper on social media.

The public criticism has focused mainly on the write-up of the study, which had some serious flaws— not on the methodology of the study itself, which was sound. The methodology and execution of the study are not in question.

Given the lack of any other studies on this topic, we very much need this study and its data. Right now, it is the only existing study using CT-angiography that tells us how keto and carnivore diets affect risk for heart disease, in people who are lean and “metabolically heathy” with high HDL and low triglycerides.
