About one cup of blueberries a day: best human evidence is for vascular function

Oct 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A podcast excerpt says one cup (150-180 g) of fresh or frozen blueberries daily matches the dose in most positive human studies. The clearest benefit is better endothelial function, with possible memory benefits in at-risk older adults and glucose benefits when metabolism is strained. Randomized trials have not shown they prevent dementia, diabetes, heart attacks or strokes.

Video: immunoglobulin G buildup in fat tissue drives insulin resistance and aging

Feb 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 Insulin resistance & diabetes

Gokhan summarizes a Mike Lustgarten video on research showing IgG accumulates in fat tissue with age and obesity, impairs insulin signaling, promotes inflammation and fibrosis, and is reduced by calorie restriction. Blocking IgG recycling through the FcRn receptor in mice improved metabolic health and extended lifespan. He calls it potentially Nobel-worthy 'if it holds up'.

Gokhan argues that obesity is not merely a willpower issue. He cites twin-study heritability of 40-80%, BMI heritability of 47-80%, and the higher obesity rate of children with two obese parents, plus weight setpoint theory, while noting lifestyle still matters.
