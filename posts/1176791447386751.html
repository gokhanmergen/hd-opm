JACC review: guidelines favor whole-food plant-based diets for CVD and cancer prevention

Louis Nervegna · Dec 08, 2025 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsCancerGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a September 2025 JACC: CardioOncology state-of-the-art review noting that AHA, ACC, ACS, NCCN and WCRF/AICR guidelines all recommend a predominantly whole-food plant-based diet, as does the EAT-Lancet consensus. Patterns like Mediterranean and DASH are included, emphasizing fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds and olive oil while minimizing red meat.

  • Mediterranean and DASH diets count as plant-forward patterns with moderate fish, poultry, dairy

Quoted:

“Current guidelines from the American Heart Association, the American College of Cardiology, the American Cancer Society, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, and the World Cancer Research Fund/American Institute for Cancer Research all recommend a predominantly whole-food plant-based diet for the prevention of CVD and cancer (see Table 1). Similarly, global consensus from the EAT-Lancet Commission, which included representatives from 16 countries, recommended a plant-forward diet as the optimal diet for human health and environmental sustainability. Although a strict whole-food plant-based diet excludes any food from animal sources, other predominantly whole-food plant-based eating patterns such as the Mediterranean diet and the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension diet include fish, lean poultry, and low-fat or fermented dairy foods in moderation. These diets share an emphasis on adequate consumption of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and monounsaturated fat sources (such as olive oil and avocado), while minimizing red meat (defined as any mammalian meat) and highly processed foods, including processed meats, refined carbohydrates, and sugar-sweetened beverages.”

“Cardiovascular disease (CVD) and cancer remain the leading causes of mortality in the United States, where poor diet has surpassed smoking as the leading risk factor for death, and life expectancy has hit a plateau as CVD mortality has stagnated over the past decade. Although the pathophysiology of CVD and cancer is complex and multifactorial, lifestyle factors including diet often contribute significantly to their pathogenesis. There is a wealth of observational data as well as emerging trial data supporting the benefits of a predominantly whole-food plant-based diet in the prevention of CVD and cancer.”

“CVD and cancer share diet-related risk factors, including obesity, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes. Red meat and ultraprocessed foods are associated with increased risks of CVD and cancer, likely mediated by shared pathways including chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, metabolic dysregulation, and gut dysbiosis. Multiple guidelines recommend a whole-food plant-centered diet, given the growing evidence for its effectiveness in primary and secondary prevention of CVD and cancer.”
