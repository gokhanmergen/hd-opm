Over 30 randomized trials find omega-6 linoleic acid does not raise inflammation markers

AI summary & key facts

The poster says more than 30 human randomized trials tested whether omega-6 fat raises inflammatory markers and consistently found it does not, with some showing anti-inflammatory effects. He links a discussion with Harvard's Walter Willett on seed oils and heart health.

  • 30+ human RCTs tested whether linoleic acid increases inflammatory markers
  • Consistently no increase; some showed anti-inflammatory effects

Seed oils are not inflammatory
There have been over 30 randomized controlled trials in humans testing the hypothesis that omega 6 fat (linoleic acid) increases inflammatory markers. These trials have consistently found that this does NOT actually happen in humans:

↪ 30 RCTs show higher linoleic acid intake does not raise inflammatory markers

In fact, some of the trials actually showed an anti-inflammatory effect.

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
