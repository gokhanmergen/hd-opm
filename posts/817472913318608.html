Whether TMAO directly causes heart disease is unresolved; gut bacteria and insulin matter

Louis Nervegna · Jun 10, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Gut microbiomeKidney healthBlood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

The poster shares a Kevin Klatt video concluding that the science on TMAO causality is complicated and uncertain. He summarizes that gut bacteria turn choline, carnitine and betaine into TMA, the liver converts TMA to TMAO in an insulin-dependent step, kidneys clear it, and fish raise it transiently despite lower heart risk.

  • TMAO first linked to atherosclerosis by Hazen's group (2011/2012)
  • Liver converts TMA to TMAO; insulin needed
  • Kidney function lowers TMAO
  • Fish transiently raise TMAO yet associate with lower CVD risk

Does TMAO actually cause heart disease?

Or is it just a non-causal marker of disease risk?

What does the latest science show?

This video does a good job of addressing these questions.

In a nutshell, the latest science on this is complicated— and there is still not a solid answer to the question of whether or not TMAO is directly causal. For the time being, we have to live with uncertainty on this.

—-

Some background info:

TMAO is a natural chemical present in the blood that might directly damage arteries.

Scientists have been debating this for years now, since its possible atherogenicity was first discovered by Stanley Hazen’s group at the Cleveland Clinic in 2012.

Your liver produces TMAO from TMA. Insulin is required for this conversion to take place. The more TMA and insulin available, the more TMAO is produced. Reducing either your insulin levels or your TMA levels lowers TMAO.

TMA is produced by bad bacteria in the gut. They feed on dietary choline, carnitine, betaine (TMG), and in turn produce TMA. Reducing consumption of these nutrients will reduce TMAO. Killing the bad bacteria with certain plant molecules (e.g. berberine, allicin, resveratrol, etc.) will also lower TMAO.

TMAO is found directly in some foods like fish. Eating these foods will transiently raise your TMAO level in the blood. This is paradoxical because many studies show fish consumption is associated with lower risk of heart disease.

Your kidneys filter out excess TMAO. The better your kidney function, the lower your TMAO.
