Metformin reduced muscle atrophy and fibrosis during bed rest in older adults

Louis Nervegna · Aug 22, 2024 · ♥ 10 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

A small randomized trial from the University of Utah found that older adults taking metformin during bed rest had less muscle atrophy and less fibrosis during recovery. Muscle biopsies showed fewer markers of cellular senescence, linking the effect to senescence.

  • Less muscle atrophy during bed rest with metformin
  • Less muscle fibrosis in recovery
  • Fewer cellular senescence markers in biopsies
  • Small randomized trial in non-diabetics

A small randomized controlled trial demonstrated that metformin protects against muscle loss and sarcopenia in elderly non-diabetics.

Quoted:

We saw two things in our study,” Drummond says. “When participants took Metformin during a bed rest, they had less muscle atrophy. During the recovery period, their muscles also had less fibrosis or excessive collagen. That build-up can make it harder for the muscle to properly function.”

Tying these results to senescence, the research team examined muscle biopsies from study participants. They found that the participants who took Metformin had fewer markers of cellular senescence.

“This is the first paper that has made the direct connection between a therapy targeting cellular senescence and improved muscle recovery following disuse in aging,” says lead author Jonathan Petrocelli, Ph.D. He explains that metformin helps muscle cells better remodel and repair tissue during periods of recovery after inactivity.

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.

Whey protein raised IGF-1 but not muscle or strength in 24-week RCT of older adults

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 17 Protein

A 24-week placebo-controlled trial raised protein to 1.5 g/kg/day with whey in healthy older adults. It did not improve muscle power, strength, performance or mass, though IGF-1 rose; potassium bicarbonate was also ineffective. Louis argues this means the harms of high IGF-1 without benefit.
