Your body makes DGLA from linoleic acid, linking seed oils to a possible senolytic effect

Louis Nervegna · Nov 04, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis points out that the body makes DGLA from the omega-6 linoleic acid in seed oils and that this senolytic mechanism might explain some effects of GLA supplements. The shared Bryan Johnson post summarizes research in which DGLA killed zombie (senescent) cells in mice through ferroptosis. Low-linoleic-acid diets reduce DGLA production.

  • DGLA is synthesized from linoleic acid via GLA
  • The step from LA to GLA is a slow bottleneck
  • DGLA killed senescent cells by ferroptosis while sparing healthy cells (mice)

Guess what your body makes DGLA from?

Linoleic acid.

Yep, seed oils.

I posted about the early stages of this research on DGLA as a senolytic 5 years ago in the rapamycin group: facebook.com/share/p/1MN6VPxELS.

This senolytic mechanism might also explain some of the purported health benefits from GLA supplements, particularly the anti-inflammatory effects.

From Gemini AI:

A diet low in linoleic acid (LA) generally reduces DGLA levels in the body. Because linoleic acid is an essential fatty acid that the human body cannot produce on its own, it serves as the foundational building block for the entire downstream omega-6 pathway.

When you significantly restrict your dietary intake of linoleic acid, it impacts DGLA levels through several cascading mechanisms:

  • Depleted Precursor Supply: With less linoleic acid entering the system, the body has fewer raw materials to convert into γ-linolenic acid (GLA), which directly limits the amount of GLA available to be elongated into DGLA.
  • The Δ6-Desaturase Bottleneck: The conversion of linoleic acid to GLA relies on the enzyme Δ6-desaturase. This step is already inherently slow and acts as a strict bottleneck in the pathway. Restricting the initial substrate (LA) makes it even harder for the body to maintain optimal downstream DGLA levels.
  • Competing Pathways: If overall linoleic acid is low, the body may prioritize mobilizing existing downstream fatty acids or altering enzyme activity to maintain homeostasis, ultimately drawing down the tissue stores of intermediate fats like DGLA.

Exceptions to the Rule

While a low-LA diet decreases DGLA produced naturally by the body, you can bypass this decline by supplementing downstream directly. Consuming oils rich in GLA—such as borage oil, evening primrose oil, or black currant seed oil—allows the body to generate DGLA directly, skipping the linoleic acid step and the slow Δ6-desaturase enzyme entirely.

Shared from Bryan Johnson · original post

Zombie cells may have a kill switch

As we age, some cells stop dividing but refuse to die.

They're called “zombie” (senescent) cells. They pile up and inflame the tissue around them which is bad for you body.

This research group figured out how to kill them in mice.

They found that these zombie cells run low on one fatty acid: DGLA. So they added it back causing many of the
zombie cells to die while the healthy cells survived.

They died by ferroptosis where the cell’s outer membrane oxidizes and breaks.

Would be interesting if replicated in humans.
