Twinkie diet shows metabolic gains come from fat loss, not food type

Louis uses the Mark Haub 'Twinkie diet' case, where a professor lost 27 pounds in 10 weeks on junk food at 1,800 kcal/day, to argue that metabolic improvement follows loss of liver and visceral fat. He does not recommend such a diet.

  • 27 lb lost in 10 weeks at 1,800 kcal/day
  • LDL -20%, HDL +20%, triglycerides -39%
  • Liver fat loss is argued to be the key driver

I’ve posted about this case before…

Any crazy diet can improve metabolic health, if it leads to a reduction in liver fat (and visceral fat in general).

The accumulation of ectopic fat in the liver is the root cause of insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome. This is proven in large randomized trials. See these past posts:

Obviously, eating nothing but junk food is not an advisable diet for long term health. But it vividly demonstrates the point.

Think about this the next time someone tells you that they reversed their diabetes by eating a magic diet consisting of nothing but ribeye steaks or nothing but raw living plant foods.

What they’re forgetting to mention is that the magic diet only worked after losing 30+ lbs.

It was the fat loss in the liver, duh.

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

A Kansas State nutrition professor lost 27 pounds in 10 weeks by eating mainly Twinkies and Doritos while capping calories at 1,800 per day.

Shockingly, his LDL cholesterol dropped 20%, HDL rose 20%, and triglycerides fell 39% despite the junk-food diet.

The experiment underscores that calorie control drives weight loss and metabolic gains, no matter the food source.

Yet critics warn about missing nutrients and unknown long-term health risks without whole grains, fruits, and veggies.
