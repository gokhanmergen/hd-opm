STAREE statin trial in adults over 70 is finished; results due in second half of 2026

Louis Nervegna · May 18, 2026 · ♥ 16 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis reposts details of STAREE: a placebo-controlled trial of atorvastatin 40 mg in about 10,000 healthy Australians aged 70+, with disability-free survival (including dementia-free) as the primary outcome. The trial website says follow-up has ended and results are expected in the second half of 2026.

  • About 10,000 adults aged 70+, atorvastatin 40 mg vs placebo
  • Primary outcome includes disability-free survival
  • Results expected second half of 2026

Do statins extend lifespan of the elderly?
Do they protect against dementia, cognitive decline, and cardiovascular disease in people over 70?

We will soon have a definitive answer.

The STAREE trial (Statins in Reducing Events in the Elderly) is a very large randomized, placebo-controlled study investigating whether starting statins (atorvastatin 40mg) in 10,000 healthy adults aged 70+ prolongs independent, disability-free survival. They are particularly emphasizing disability-free survival which includes absence of dementia.

monash.eduLink to monash.edumonash.edu

Conducted across Australia, the trial specifically examines the effects of statins on preventing dementia, cognitive decline, and cardiovascular disease in healthy older people.

The trial is complete. They are analyzing the data right now. Results will be reported in just a few months.

The primary outcome is disability-free survival, which is an umbrella outcome that covers the effects of all major diseases including the aging process itself.

Trial Details

Population: Nearly 10,000 community-dwelling adults aged 70 and older without a prior history of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or dementia.

Method: Participants are randomly assigned to take either 40 mg of atorvastatin or a placebo daily.

Goals: The study evaluates co-primary outcomes of prolonging disability-free survival (living without dementia or physical dependency) and reducing major cardiovascular events (heart attacks, strokes, and cardiovascular death).

Current Status: The active intervention and follow-up phase of the trial have concluded, and the data is being prepared for analysis. The primary results are expected in the second half of 2026.

Note: The researchers have purposely chosen atorvastatin for this study because of its ability to cross the blood brain barrier and potentially protect the brain. And because of its ability to easily get inside other kinds of tissue (such as early stage cancers). It is lipophilic, unlike many other statins.

Quoted:

“Statins in Reducing Events in the Elderly (STAREE) will be the first randomised controlled trial (RCT) of statin therapy in an apparently healthy cohort of older adults living independently in the community and will provide the evidence required to determine whether statin therapy should be taken routinely by healthy people aged 70 years and over.”

“Questions about statins that need answering with specific reference to older adults:

  • Can statins prevent a first heart attack or stroke?
  • Can statins prevent dementia?
  • Can statins improve healthy life expectancy?
  • Do statins impact on day to day physical function?
  • Are the frequency and/or severity of adverse reactions to statins higher?
  • Is the risk of developing diabetes higher?
  • Is statin therapy cost effective in healthy older individuals?”

A Vital Human summary of STAREE: 9,971 adults averaging about 75 years, atorvastatin 40 mg versus placebo for a median 5.9 years. Major CV events fell 30% (HR 0.70) with an NNT of 37, but no significant difference in overall mortality or disability-free survival and no increase in dementia.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
