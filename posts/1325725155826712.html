Vitamin K2 slowed coronary calcium only when started early; two late-stage trials failed

Louis Nervegna · Jun 23, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographySupplements & vitaminsBlood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a summary of three RCTs of vitamin K2 (MK-7) on arterial calcification. K2 lowered the inactive MGP marker in all three, but calcification slowed only in the most recent trial, which enrolled people with early coronary disease; two trials in established aortic valve or dialysis calcification showed no change.

  • Circulation 2022: 720 mcg/day MK-7 plus 1000 IU D3 for two years in established aortic valve calcification; valve calcium unchanged (275 vs 292 Agatston)
  • Valkyrie, JASN 2020: 2000 mcg MK-7 three times weekly in hemodialysis patients; coronary, aortic and valvular calcium unchanged
  • VitaK-CAC, JAMA Cardiology 2026: 360 mcg/day MK-7 for two years, CAC 50-400; progression slowed
  • The effective trial used the lowest dose; disease stage likely mattered
  • No controlled trial has shown D3 without K2 forces calcium into arteries

A nice summary of the 3 major RCT’s testing vitamin K2 as an intervention to slow arterial calcification.

The first 2 trials failed. The latest succeeded.

The trial that worked intervened early before significant calcification occurred. The two trials that failed intervened late after significant calcification had already formed and hardened.

Quoted:

Vitamin K2 did exactly what its mechanism predicts in all three trials. The arteries only moved in one of them - the most recent one.

K2 is the cofactor for gamma-glutamyl carboxylase, the enzyme that carboxylates matrix Gla protein (MGP). Carboxylated MGP binds calcium and keeps it out of the arterial wall. Uncarboxylated, it can't. Every trial below confirmed K2 was active by measuring the inactive form, dp-ucMGP, falling.

Circulation, 2022: 720 mcg/day MK-7 plus 1000 IU D3, two years, in patients with established aortic valve calcification. Marker fell. Valve calcium unchanged, 275 vs 292 Agatston.

Valkyrie, JASN 2020: 2000 mcg MK-7 three times weekly in hemodialysis patients with extensive calcification. Marker improved. Coronary, aortic, and valvular calcium all unchanged.

VitaK-CAC, JAMA Cardiology 2026: 360 mcg/day MK-7, two years, in early coronary disease, CAC 50 to 400 Agatston. Progression slowed.

The trial that worked used the lowest dose of the three. It was disease stage. Once calcium mineralizes into hydroxyapatite in the wall, activating MGP probably cant pull it back out. Caught early, while plaque is still soft, MGP might slow the transition.

The claim that started this, that D3 without supplemental K2 forces calcium into your arteries, has never been shown in a controlled trial.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

Vitamin K2 MK-7 slowed coronary calcification in a 2-year RCT; soft plaque not measured

Sep 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 11 Supplements & vitamins

A shared post on a new 2-year randomized trial notes that MK-7 reduced progression of coronary calcification versus placebo. It cautions that calcified plaque is generally more stable, soft plaque was not measured, and the trial was not designed to show fewer cardiovascular events.

K2 plus D3 slowed coronary calcium progression about 21% in a 2-year trial

Sep 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 14 Supplements & vitamins

In 398 adults with severe coronary calcification, two years of 720 mcg vitamin K2 (MK-7) plus 1,000 IU vitamin D3 daily slowed calcium-score progression versus placebo. However, total plaque volume rose similarly in both groups among the 143 assessed, so the trial does not show plaque reversal or fewer heart attacks.

Vitamin K2 activates matrix Gla protein to limit arterial calcification, review says

Sep 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 21 Supplements & vitamins

A 2021 narrative review in Open Heart describes vitamin K2 as a regulator of calcium in the cardiovascular system by activating matrix Gla protein (MGP). Low vitamin K status leaves more inactive MGP, which has been linked to arterial stiffness and vascular calcification. It is a narrative review, not outcome trial evidence.

Vitamin K2 (MK-7) slowed coronary calcium progression in a 2-year randomized trial

Sep 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary of a randomized trial in 180 adults with symptomatic coronary artery disease and existing calcium found that 360 mcg/day MK-7 slowed CAC progression versus placebo over two years. It did not remove existing calcium, CAC is a surrogate marker, and the trial was not designed to show fewer heart attacks.
