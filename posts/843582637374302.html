NMR reports state LDL particle size adds no risk information once LDL-P is accounted for

Louis Nervegna · Jul 29, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

The post points out that NMR lipid reports themselves say small LDL-P and LDL size are associated with CVD risk but not after total LDL particle number is taken into account. ApoB is an equivalent measure of particle number, so large particles do not protect against high LDL-P or ApoB.

  • Report quote: 'Small LDL-P and LDL Size are associated with CVD risk, but not after LDL-P is taken into account'
  • ApoB is equivalent to LDL-P as a particle count

On NMR cholesterol tests for particle size, it literally says right on the report that LDL particle size does not matter.

They literally tell you right on the report that LDL particle size does not affect CVD risk once corrected for total number of LDL particles.

The report literally tells you that the only thing that matters for risk is the total number of LDL particles (LDL-P), not the size.

(Note that ApoB is an equivalent way to measure the number of LDL particles.)

Quoted from the report:

“Small LDL-P and LDL Size are associated with CVD risk, but not after LDL-P is taken into account.”

(They are putting this warning directly on the report so you can’t win a lawsuit against them after getting this test and then having a heart attack. They are explicitly warning you and your physician that studies have shown that having large particle size on this test will not protect you from high LDL-P or high Apo-B.)

LDL-C vs LDL-P vs ApoB: particle number predicts risk better than cholesterol content

LDL-C measures cholesterol inside LDL particles while LDL-P counts the particles; they can be discordant (low LDL-C with many small particles), and particle number predicts risk better. Particle size adds little once number is accounted for, and ApoB, which counts all atherogenic particles, is the best test.
