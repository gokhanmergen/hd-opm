Low-dose lithium carbonate failed to slow cognitive decline in mild cognitive impairment

Louis Nervegna · Mar 02, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

A JAMA Neurology RCT found low-dose lithium did not significantly delay cognitive decline or change imaging and plasma biomarkers over 2 years in older adults with MCI, though it was safe. Louis notes the trial used lithium carbonate, whereas Yankner's Harvard work suggests lithium orotate is the active form in Alzheimer's models.

  • Low-dose lithium, 2-year RCT in MCI
  • No significant effect on cognition, neuroimaging or plasma biomarkers
  • Safe and well tolerated
  • Poster's caveat: carbonate vs orotate form

Low dose lithium carbonate failed to delay progression of cognitive decline in patients with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) likely to progress to Alzheimer’s.

The results of this RCT were just published.

My note:

It’s very possible that they used the wrong form of lithium in this trial. We now know from Bruce Yankner’s recently published work at Harvard that it is the orotate form of lithium that has significant activity in Alzheimer’s models, not the carbonate form. Yankner predicted that the carbonate form would not work well. (Refer to comments section for more on this.)

Among older adults with mild cognitive impairment, low-dose lithium did not significantly delay cognitive decline or affect neuroimaging and plasma biomarker outcomes over 2 years. Treatment was safe and well tolerated.
