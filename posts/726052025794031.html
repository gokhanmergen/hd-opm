WHAT IS METHYLGLYOXAL? HOW DOES IT ENHANCE AGES PRODUCTION AND DRIVE AGING AND ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE? WHAT CAN BE DONE TO REDUCE METHYLGLOXAL?

I wanted to share my thoughts about methylglyoxal and advanced glycation Endproducts (AGEs) which remain understudied despite their importance in driving aging and age-related diseases like diabetes and Alzheimer’s. The reason this is critical is that methylglyoxal (MGO) is highly reactive and the major byproduct of glycolysis in every cell. Because of its ability to modify arginines, lysines, DNA, and lipids non-enzymatically - it influences almost all hallmarks of aging negatively including proteostasis, inflammation, senescence, DNA damage, and mitochondrial function.

Despite this evidence, for over 3-4 decades very few labs have worked on it largely because it’s hard to measure except by a few labs who use mass spec-based methods, and the tools to reduce it have been sparse. A major setback in the field was when a clinical trial on aminoguanidine had to be discontinued in humans because of toxicity despite promising animal data.

I was fascinated by this question because of its potential significance and ability to explain the reason why sugar and glycolysis are toxic. Most long-lived interventions like calorie restriction induce an anti-Warburg effect - they switch metabolism from sugar utilization to fat utilization.

In our first study from my lab at the Buck Institute, we found that the TRPA1 channel is a sensor of methylglyoxal and enhances the production of glyoxalases that detoxify methylglyoxal. Unfortunately, with age and hyperglycemia our ability to detoxify methylglyoxal declines.

We also discovered that alpha lipoic acid could activate the TRPA1 channel to activate NRF2 to increase glutathione and glyoxalases to detoxify methylglyoxal.

Then my lab embarked on a screen to enhance the neuronal protection due to lipoic acid and we only chose to go forward with supplements as we wanted a faster path to the clinic and the safety and efficacy of the supplements were much higher than the drug-like compounds. We found that a combination of lipoic acid, nicotinamide, piperine, pyridoxine, and thiamine acted in synergy to reduce methylglyoxal and AGEs. We call this this combination GLYLO as it reduces glycation. We could show that even if each of the components was used at a fifth of the dose it was much more effective in lowering methylglyoxal and AGEs.

Next, we tried this combination in mice and were surprised to see that not only did this combination extend lifespan even in old mice (starting at 24 months) but it also improved insulin sensitivity and reduced preference and intake of sugar. It also rescued the phenotypes in the leptin receptor-deficient obese mouse
