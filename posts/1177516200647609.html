Niacin inhibited vascular calcification in mice via SIRT1/SIRT6 activation

Louis Nervegna · Dec 09, 2025 · ♥ 18 reactions · 21 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumSupplements & vitaminsKidney health
AI summary & key facts

Louis flags a new mouse study (a rarity for the group) showing niacin reduced smooth-muscle osteogenic differentiation and CKD/vitamin D3-induced vascular calcification, by maintaining SIRT1 and SIRT6 and autophagy. He notes other B3 forms also raise NAD, which activates sirtuins; this is preclinical only.

  • Niacin reduced VSMC osteogenic differentiation
  • Relieved CKD and vitamin D3-induced calcification in C57BL/6J mice
  • Mechanism: maintains SIRT1 and SIRT6 and boosts autophagy flux
  • Louis: other B3 forms (NAM, NR, NMN) also raise NAD

I don’t ordinarily post about mouse studies in this group, but this one is particularly interesting:

This was just published.

Niacin inhibits vascular calcification in an (artificially engineered) mouse model of atherosclerosis.

The mechanism involves activation of the sirtuin genes SIRT1/SIRT6, likely as a direct result of increasing cellular levels of NAD.

Quoted:

“Our data showed that niacin treatment remarkably reduced VSMC osteogenic differentiation. Moreover, niacin treatment alleviated CKD and vitamin D3-induced vascular calcification in C57BL/6J mice. Mechanistically, we for the first time demonstrated that niacin inhibited vascular calcification via maintaining both Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) and Sirtuin 6 (SIRT6) levels. Further, we verified that niacin increased SIRT1 and SIRT6-mediated autophagy flux in VSMC. Our findings reveal that niacin exerts anti-calcification effect via maintaining both SIRT1 and SIRT6, providing novel therapeutic strategies in the treatment of vascular calcification.”

My note:

All forms of vitamin B3 have been shown to increase cellular levels of NAD— which is what actually activates the SIRT1/SIRT6 genes— not just niacin. This includes nicotinamide (niacinamide), nicotinamide riboside (NR), and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), in addition to nicotinic acid (niacin). It’s possible that you would see this same anti-calcification effect with the other forms of vitamin B3 as well, given the mechanisms involved. These other forms tend to better tolerated than niacin.

Again, this was a mouse study in an artificially engineered model of atherosclerosis. It’s unclear whether or not this anti-calcification effect from niacin would actually translate to humans.

Note that niacin failed to improve outcomes in two very large well done randomized-controlled trials where it was combined with maximum-prescribed doses of statins. Side effects were significantly higher in the groups taking niacin in conjunction with the high dose statins, as compared to the statins alone.

For this reason, niacin (nicotinic acid) should not be combined with high dose statins.
