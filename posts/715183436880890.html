Subbotin's hypothesis: coronary atherosclerosis starts with endothelial dysfunction

Gokhan Can · Nov 26, 2023 · ♥ 9 reactions · 24 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Shares a talk by Dr. Vladimir Subbotin proposing that endothelial injury leads to intimal thickening, lipid deposition and then inflammation and plaque. The author's view is that this does not contradict current LDL-lowering strategies, which can co-exist with measures protecting the endothelium.

  • Hypothesis: endothelial injury precedes intimal thickening and lipid deposits
  • Author: LDL lowering and endothelial health strategies are compatible

Dr. Vladimir Subbotin puts forth a hypothesis that coronary atherosclerosis begins with dysfunction of the endothelial cells lining the arteries. He argues that endothelial injury leads to intimal thickening and encroachment, enabling lipid deposits to form within the deeper arterial layers. This sets off inflammation and plaque development.

(In my opinion, the presented data doesn't contradict with the core prevention strategies we're employing today. LDL lowering can co-exist with strategies that maintain a healthy endothelium.)
