PREDIMED: more olive oil linked to 48% lower CVD mortality; each 10 g/day cut CVD 10%

Louis Nervegna · Jun 13, 2025 · ♥ 14 reactions · 42 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis reports a PREDIMED analysis in which participants with the highest olive oil intake had much lower cardiovascular mortality, with a clear dose-response relationship. It was a primary prevention cohort, and most olive oil consumed in the intervention group was extra virgin.

  • Highest baseline olive oil intake: 48% lower CV mortality (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.29-0.93)
  • Each additional 10 g/day EVOO: CVD risk -10%, CV mortality -7%
  • Primary prevention, 5-year trial
  • All-cause mortality effect did not reach significance

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

Higher consumption of olive oil in the PREDIMED randomized controlled trial was associated with significantly lower cardiovascular mortality.

Most of the olive oil consumed by the intervention group was as EVOO.

The risk of dying of CVD was cut by almost 50% in the participants consuming the most olive oil.

There was a clear dose-effect relationship: Every extra 10g of olive oil consumed per day (roughly one tablespoon) cut the risk of dying from CVD during the 5 year trial by 7%.

Note that this was a primary prevention trial. None of the participants had been diagnosed with CVD prior to the start of the trial. Every extra 10g of olive oil per day cut the risk of developing CVD during the trial by 10%.

PREDIMED also showed that olive oil reduced all-cause mortality, but the results did not reach the typical cutoff used for statistical significance in medical studies. See comments for more on all-cause mortality in this study.

Quoted:

“Higher baseline total olive oil consumption was associated with 48% (HR: 0.52; 95% CI: 0.29 to 0.93) reduced risk of cardiovascular mortality. For each 10 g/d increase in extra-virgin olive oil consumption, cardiovascular disease and mortality risk decreased by 10% and 7%, respectively. No significant associations were found for cancer and all-cause mortality. The associations between cardiovascular events and extra virgin olive oil intake were significant in the Mediterranean diet intervention groups and not in the control group.”

“Conclusion - Olive oil consumption, specifically the extra-virgin variety, is associated with reduced risks of cardiovascular disease and mortality in individuals at high cardiovascular risk.”
