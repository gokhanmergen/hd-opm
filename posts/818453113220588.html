Omega-6:omega-3 ratio does not predict heart disease; nuts and seeds link to lower risk

Louis Nervegna · Jun 12, 2024 · ♥ 21 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

The poster rejects claims that pistachios, almonds, cashews, pumpkin seeds and similar foods are harmful because of a high omega-6:omega-3 ratio. He says these foods are associated with lower heart disease and diabetes risk (possibly excepting very high coconut intake) and the ratio is useless for predicting risk.

  • Nuts and seeds associated with lower CVD and diabetes risk
  • Omega-6:omega-3 ratio has not predicted risk in studies (per poster)
  • Possible exception: very high coconut intake

Pistachios, brazil nuts, pumpkin seeds, cashews, almonds, pecans, and whole coconuts are all going to kill you because of their high omega 6 to omega 3 ratio.

Really??

Look up the actual studies on these particular foods. They are all associated with lower risk of heart disease and diabetes— with the possible exception of very high amounts of coconut— and have multiple other health benefits.

This omega 6 to omega 3 ratio is useless for predicting risk of heart disease or diabetes. It doesn’t work. We know this from many studies now.
