ApoB cannot be calculated from a lipid panel but is roughly 0.7 times non-HDL cholesterol

Louis explains that ApoB counts particles while non-HDL cholesterol (total minus HDL) measures the cholesterol they carry, so non-HDL is only a rough proxy. A crude estimate is ApoB approximately 0.7 x non-HDL-C, but a direct ApoB test is more accurate.

Lp(a) and triglycerides are ApoB particles, so accepting them but not LDL is odd

Jul 21, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 11 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

The poster points out that keto and carnivore influencers who deny LDL/ApoB causality but accept Lp(a) and triglycerides as causal are contradicting themselves: Lp(a) and VLDL (measured as triglycerides) are ApoB-tagged particles just like LDL. Most triglyceride is carried by VLDL, which differs little from LDL.

Olezarsen cut triglycerides 64% but did not change plaque in ESSENCE-TIMI 73b imaging

Mar 31, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The ESSENCE-TIMI 73b imaging study found olezarsen lowered triglycerides about 64% and remnant cholesterol about 72%, with ApoB down only about 16%, yet 12-month CCTA showed no difference in plaque. Louis reads it as showing TG lowering alone, without treating insulin resistance, does not slow plaque.

Lowering triglycerides alone does not shrink plaque: olezarsen cut TG 64%, plaque flat

May 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 12 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that unless triglycerides exceed about 400-500 mg/dL, drug-lowering them makes little difference to plaque or hard outcomes. In BRIDGE-TIMI 73a olezarsen cut triglycerides 64% with no plaque volume change; VLDL particles are atherogenic but are already counted by apoB.

High HDL tracks with lower risk in UK Biobank, but HDL-raising drugs have failed

Oct 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 3 ↩ 1 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that a viral UK Biobank graph shows higher HDL linked to lower ASCVD risk at any LDL/ApoB, as dozens of earlier observational studies have, though the effect size here is larger. Every randomized HDL-raising drug trial failed on hard outcomes, and Mendelian randomization shows no lifetime benefit from genes raising HDL, so HDL is thought to be a proxy for insulin resistance, which is better measured directly.
