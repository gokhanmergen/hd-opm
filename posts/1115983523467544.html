Vegans with insulin resistance should target liver fat; white potatoes are not health food

Louis Nervegna · Sep 19, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 17 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis advises vegans with insulin resistance, high blood sugar or high triglycerides that the main cause is excess visceral and liver fat, even in lean people. He also cautions against white potatoes, citing a Harvard trial testing whether they harm arterial endothelium, and prefers sweet potatoes.

  • Main cause of insulin resistance in most people is excess visceral, especially liver, fat
  • Lean people can still have enough liver fat to cause insulin resistance
  • Harvard trial testing plain white potatoes and endothelial function
  • Sweet potatoes suggested as a better choice

Advice for people on vegan diets who still have problems with insulin resistance and blood sugar, including high triglycerides

The main cause of insulin resistance in most people (including vegans) is excess visceral fat, particularly fat in the liver. Even people who are relatively lean can still have enough fat in the liver to cause insulin resistance:

↪ Liver fat is the biggest driver of insulin resistance in most people, per a Harvard expert

↪ Liver fat as low as 1.85% is linked to insulin resistance (Yale)

↪ Roy Taylor trial: clearing liver and pancreas fat via 8-week low-calorie diet reversed T2D

There are many past posts in the group on this topic of liver fat and insulin resistance, and what to do about it. You can read them by searching the group for the tags #liverfatHD and #roytaylorHD.

White potatoes are not health food. There is a vast medical literature showing that white potatoes are just as harmful as refined sugar and refined starch:

↪ Harvard: white potatoes act like refined starch, tied to weight gain and diabetes

↪ Willett: refined starch and sugar (~40% of US calories) drive heart disease and diabetes

It’s likely that plain white potatoes (no fat or toppings of any kind) directly damage the endothelial lining of arteries. There is now a clinical trial at Harvard that is testing this hypothesis:

↪ Harvard/MGH recruits for a randomized trial of white potatoes and endothelial function

↪ Harvard/MGH feeding trial tests whether white potatoes harm endothelial function

Sweet potatoes are a better choice, especially the purple ones that are consumed in Japan. Sweet potatoes are not really potatoes. Sweet potatoes are a root like carrots and beets, and genetically unrelated to white potatoes which are tubers:

↪ Sweet potatoes are unrelated to white potatoes and not linked to weight gain or diabetes

Any grain-based starch needs to be in the form of 100% whole grains. These should be eaten as intact as possible. You should not over-consume whole grains in the form of finely ground powders for baking, because doing so makes it harder to lose liver fat and visceral fat in general:

↪ One-year RCT: low-carb and whole-food high-carb diets removed liver and visceral fat alike

↪ Two tricks to lower glucose and insulin spikes from whole grains

Any sugar needs to be eaten only in the form of whole or blended fruit with all the fiber still included. Studies show that blending whole fruit actually lowers the glycenic index:

↪ Blending fruit into smoothies does not spike blood sugar more than whole fruit

If you are vegan, I strongly suggest that you adopt a strict plant-based traditional Mediterranean diet. This diet will dramatically improve issues with insulin resistance and dramatically lower risk of having a heart attack or stroke. Many studies have proven this. You can read past posts in this group on the mediterranean diet by searching the group for the tags #mediterraneanrctHD and #mediterraneanHD. It is easy to do a vegan version of the traditional mediterrabean diet, since the diet is mostly plant-based anyway.

Why insulin resistance lowers HDL, and why improving insulin sensitivity beats chasing HDL

Louis lists six mechanisms by which insulin resistance lowers HDL: impaired HDL maturation (lower LCAT), reduced apoA-I production, faster HDL catabolism (hepatic lipase, CETP), altered HDL composition and function, higher triglycerides making small dense HDL, and inflammation and oxidative stress. He concludes the best way to raise HDL is improving insulin sensitivity (lose visceral fat, exercise, cut refined starch and sugar), since insulin resistance is causal in heart disease while large RCTs suggest HDL itself is probably not.
