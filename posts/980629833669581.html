Harvard: white potatoes act like refined starch, tied to weight gain and diabetes

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares Harvard's Nutrition Source page arguing potatoes should not count as a vegetable because of their rapid digestion. Quoted evidence includes a 2011 cohort analysis where French fry and potato intake increased long-term weight gain and a study linking potatoes to diabetes risk in women. Louis notes the quotes come from an older version of the web page.

  • Mozaffarian 2011: about 120,000 people followed up to 20 years
  • Increased French fries or baked/mashed potatoes: extra 3.4 and 1.3 lb per 4 years
  • Replacing potatoes with whole grains could lower diabetes risk in women
  • Over 40% of calories in the standard American diet come from refined starch/sugar and potatoes

White potatoes may be as dangerous as refined starch and refined sugar.

The Harvard School of Public Health has been arguing for years that white potatoes should be reclassified as a refined grain instead of a vegetable, because of the evidence linking them with heart disease, diabetes, and obesity.

At present, over 40% of the calories in the standard American diet come from refined starch/sugar and white potatoes. Now that trans fats have been removed from the food supply, this enormous intake of refined carbohydrate may represent the single biggest cause of disease and suffering in western diets:

↪ Willett: refined starch and sugar (~40% of US calories) drive heart disease and diabetes

Quoted:

[Edit - January 2026 - The quotes below come from the old webpage. The page has since been extensively rewritten, so you will no longer find these exact quotes in the new article.]

“Over the long term, diets high in potatoes and similarly rapidly-digested, high carbohydrate foods can contribute to obesity, diabetes, and heart disease. [5-9]

Potatoes seem to be a particular culprit for weight gain and diabetes:

A 2011 study by Mozaffarian et al. that tracked the diet and lifestyle habits of 120,000 men and women for up to 20 years looked at how small food-choice changes contributed to weight gain over time.

People who increased their consumption of French fries and baked or mashed potatoes gained more weight over time—an extra 3.4 and 1.3 pounds every four years, respectively. [7]

People who decreased their intake of these foods gained less weight, as did people who increased their intake of other vegetables.

A similar long-term study found that high potato and French fry intakes were linked to a greater risk of diabetes in women, and that replacing potatoes with whole grains could lower diabetes risk. [9]”
