Telomerase and telomere length may be important in arterial and endothelial aging

Louis Nervegna · Feb 07, 2026 · ♥ 16 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes telomerase may prove important in heart disease, though it is early. Telomere length limits how many times arterial wall cells, especially endothelium, can divide before senescence; telomerase extends telomeres. Shared Nobel Prize post on the discovery by Greider and Blackburn.

  • Telomere length controls cell division capacity before senescence
  • Telomerase lengthens telomeres
  • Active area of research, too early to tell

Telomerase may turn out to be very important in heart disease. This is an active area of research. It’s still too early to tell.

The length of a cell’s telomeres control how many times it can divide before it dies or becomes senescent. For example, this is true of the cells in the arterial wall, and especially the endothelium. The enzyme telomerase lengthens telomeres.

#telomeraseHD #telomeresHD #endotheliumHD

Shared from Nobel Prize · original post

At the age of 23, before she’d even earned her PhD, Carol Greider made the discovery that would earn her the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Along with her supervisor and co-laureate Elizabeth Blackburn, she discovered the enzyme telomerase. At each end of a chromosome lies a protective "cap" called a telomere – telomerase is the enzyme that creates it.
