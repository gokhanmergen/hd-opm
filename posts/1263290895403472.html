Five days of overeating ultra-processed food raises liver fat without much weight gain

AI summary & key facts

In healthy young men, 1,200-1,500 extra daily calories from refined carbs, saturated fat and processed food for five days increased liver and visceral fat with barely any weight gain and made brain insulin response impaired.

  • 5 days of overfeeding, 1,200-1,500 kcal/day extra
  • Liver fat rose with minimal weight gain
  • Brain insulin resistance observed

Just 5 days of overeating refined carbs, saturated fat, and ultra-processed food is enough to significantly increase liver fat.

This occurs even in the absence of increasing overall body fat.

As we’ve talked about many times in the group, excess liver fat is a main cause of insulin resistance: ↪ Liver fat is the biggest driver of insulin resistance in most people, per a Harvard expert.

Just five days of overeating ultra-processed food can impair your brain’s insulin response and increase visceral fat

In healthy young men, adding 1,200–1,500 extra calories per day from refined carbs, saturated fat, and processed foods caused fat to accumulate around the liver with barely any weight gain

At the same time, their brains became insulin resistant, weakening satiety signals and disrupting how the body decides to store incoming energy

That creates a vicious cycle: you feel less full, overeat more easily, and increasingly store calories as visceral fat (the inflammatory, metabolically harmful fat around your organs)

Why insulin resistance lowers HDL, and why improving insulin sensitivity beats chasing HDL

Mar 21, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 ↩ 10 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis lists six mechanisms by which insulin resistance lowers HDL: impaired HDL maturation (lower LCAT), reduced apoA-I production, faster HDL catabolism (hepatic lipase, CETP), altered HDL composition and function, higher triglycerides making small dense HDL, and inflammation and oxidative stress. He concludes the best way to raise HDL is improving insulin sensitivity (lose visceral fat, exercise, cut refined starch and sugar), since insulin resistance is causal in heart disease while large RCTs suggest HDL itself is probably not.
