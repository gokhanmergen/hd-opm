Animal study: creatine may help diet-related fatty liver but not alcohol-related

Louis Nervegna · May 17, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Life Sciences 2022 animal study suggesting creatine may help fatty liver from poor diet but possibly not alcohol-related. He notes it needs human replication.

  • Animal study, Life Sciences 2022
  • Possibly unhelpful in alcohol-related fatty liver

It’s just an animal study, but could be a big deal if replicated in humans.

See this previous post for more information on the relationship between liver fat and insulin resistance:

↪ Roy Taylor trials: 15 kg weight loss cleared liver and pancreas fat, reversing diabetes

We now know that ectopic fat accumulation in the liver is the major cause of insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome in humans. The best way to remove this liver fat is weight loss.

Research cited

  • Creatine and fatty liver (animal study) · animal · Life Sciences · 2022
Shared from Ergo-Log Webmagazine · original post

If you have a fatty liver due to unhealthy eating and lack of exercise, supplementing with creatine may very well be a solution. But if you have a fatty liver due to chronic alcohol abuse, you may be better off not taking creatine. This is suggested by an animal study published in Life Sciences in 2022.
