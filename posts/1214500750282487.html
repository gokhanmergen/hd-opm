Mendelian randomization: longer telomeres linked to less coronary calcification

Louis Nervegna · Feb 01, 2026 · ♥ 13 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a January 2026 MR study suggesting longer telomere length causally protects against coronary artery calcification. He ties this to replicative senescence of artery wall cells in atherosclerosis.

  • Genetically longer telomeres linked to less arterial calcification
  • One of the first studies hinting telomere length causally protects against CAC

A new Mendelian randomization study hints that telomeres play a major causal role in coronary artery calcification (CAC).

People who are genetically prone to having longer telomeres as they age experience less calcification of their arteries as they get older. This relationship appears to be causal.

It’s long been known that replicative senescence of cells in the artery wall plays a major role in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis, particularly in the endothelium. Telomere shortening is the main cause of this replicative senescence.

This new study is one of the first to hint that increasing telomere length (TL) can causally protect against atheroscleosis and arterial calcification.

Interestingly, this same Mendelian randomization study examined whether a genetic propensity towards lower epigetic age or towards better oral/dental health were protective. They were not. Only telomere length seemed to causally protect against coronary artery calcification as people got older.

Note that certain interventions that we have discussed in the group before are known to increase telomerase or protect telomeres. These include statins, pomegranate juice, and the herb gotu kola (centella asiatica), to name just a few. Each of these has some degree of evidence for a protective effect against atherosclerosis, with statins obviously being the strongest by far. There is also evidence that heart-healthy diets like the Ornish diet or the traditional Mediterranean / DASH diet can increase levels of telomerase and shorten the rate of telomere shortening.

You can search the group for the tags #telomeresHD and #telomeraseHD to learn more about this topic, including the interventions mentioned above.

It is important to remember that Mendelian randomization is fundamentally observational in nature. It can only hint at causality. However, it is more effective at predicting causality than any other type of observational study. Only randomized controlled trials can truly prove causality.

Quoted:

“While CAC is closely linked to aging, its potential as a marker of biological aging remains uncertain. Telomere length (TL), epigenetic aging markers, and dental deterioration parameters have been proposed as indicators of biological aging. However, the extent to which these biological aging indicators are associated with CAC remains unclear.”

“Genetically determined longer TL [telomere length] was significantly associated with lower CAC levels (IVW p < 0.001), supporting TL as a protective factor against atherosclerosis progression (as measured by CAC). Other analyses confirmed the robustness of this finding (MR-Egger p = 0.03, WME p = 0.004). A three-sample MR analysis provided further evidence for this association (IVW p < 0.01). However, neither epigenetic aging markers nor dental deterioration parameters exhibited a significant causal relationship with CAC.”

“This study supports an inverse causal relationship between TL and CAC, reinforcing CAC as a biomarker of biological aging. Epigenetic aging markers and dental deterioration parameters were not significantly linked to CAC. Future studies should explore additional aging-related traits and refine the genetic instruments for epigenetic aging and dental health to further elucidate their potential roles in vascular aging.”
