Glaucoma neurorecovery review: stressed retinal neurons may recover; vitamin B3 candidate

👁️ Glaucoma damage has long been seen as permanent. But some eye researchers argue that stressed nerve cells in the eye may be able to recover some of their function.

Glaucoma harms the optic nerve, often due to high eye pressure. It is a leading cause of irreversible blindness.

In a review, a team led by Professor Jonathan Crowston describes an idea called neurorecovery. Nerve cells in the retina may stop working for a while before they die, and some may bounce back.

In mice, the team briefly raised eye pressure and tracked retinal signals. Function dropped quickly, then slowly returned.

Older mice recovered much more slowly. Exercise and diet changed how fast and how fully the mice recovered.

The researchers then tested the idea in people. In a Melbourne trial of 57 glaucoma patients, high dose nicotinamide, a form of vitamin B3, was taken alongside usual eye drops for 12 weeks.

Compared with a placebo, it led to a small but significant improvement in retinal nerve signals and in some vision test scores.

A second small trial at Columbia University in the US, which combined nicotinamide with pyruvate, also found some improvement in vision tests.

The authors say recovery could be boosted with lifestyle changes or medicines, not just by lowering eye pressure.

There are important caveats. Both human trials were small and lasted only about three months. Only around a quarter of patients improved by a clear margin.

Some mouse findings are unpublished, and mouse results may not apply to people.

It is still unknown whether these short term gains slow glaucoma over years. Larger trials are under way.

The doses used were far higher than in normal supplements. Talk to your eye doctor before trying them, and never stop your eye drops.

📃 RESEARCH PAPER
📌 Flora Hui et al., "Improvement in inner retinal function in glaucoma with nicotinamide (vitamin B3) supplementation: A crossover randomized clinical trial," Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology.

Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.
