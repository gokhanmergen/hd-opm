JAMA: daily caffeinated coffee after cardioversion linked to lower AF recurrence

Louis Nervegna · Nov 09, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

JAMA's research summary reports that adults with atrial fibrillation who drank at least one cup of caffeinated coffee daily after successful cardioversion had lower recurrence than those who abstained. Hospitalization, heart failure and stroke rates were similar.

AHA: coffee drinkers with treated AFib were 39% less likely to have a recurrence

Nov 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Heart rhythm & heart failure

The American Heart Association summarizes the DECAF trial: adults treated for AFib who drank at least one cup of coffee daily for 6 months had 39% fewer recurrences than those who abstained. The 200-person trial was published in JAMA, though some people still find caffeine triggers AFib symptoms.

DECAF trial: continuing coffee cut AF recurrence after cardioversion, 47% vs 64%

Nov 11, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 2 Heart rhythm & heart failure

A shared summary of the DECAF randomized trial in 200 habitual coffee drinkers with persistent atrial fibrillation or flutter after cardioversion. Those who kept drinking caffeinated coffee had fewer recurrences over 6 months than those who abstained, with no safety concerns, though the result does not justify starting coffee.

High-dose omega-3 raises AFib risk in high-CVD-risk people, not at low doses (2026)

Sep 16, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 12 Heart rhythm & heart failure

Louis warns that the atrial fibrillation risk from high-dose omega-3 is real. A 2021 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (81,210 people) found higher AF risk, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs (114,592 people) confirmed it at higher doses in people at high CVD risk, but not in those without high risk, and not at low doses in either group.

Daily Mail egg-AFib headline is wrong: the study linked TMAO, not eggs, to AFib

Jul 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis corrects a Daily Mail headline claiming eggs raise AFib risk. The Cleveland Clinic study associated blood TMAO with AFib, not egg intake, and the same group had earlier shown in a human trial that eggs do not raise TMAO.

Cleveland Clinic links gut-bacteria byproduct TMAO to atrial fibrillation risk

Jul 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 · 💬 1 ↩ 2 Heart rhythm & heart failure

Cleveland Clinic researchers analyzed blood samples from over 5,000 people undergoing heart procedures and found TMAO, made by gut bacteria from nutrients in red meat and other animal products, was linked to higher AFib risk. Cell experiments suggested TMAO may alter the heart's electrical environment. Louis frames it as TMAO possibly raising AFib risk.
