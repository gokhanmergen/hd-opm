DECAF trial: continuing coffee cut AF recurrence after cardioversion, 47% vs 64%

A shared summary of the DECAF randomized trial in 200 habitual coffee drinkers with persistent atrial fibrillation or flutter after cardioversion. Those who kept drinking caffeinated coffee had fewer recurrences over 6 months than those who abstained, with no safety concerns, though the result does not justify starting coffee.

  • 200 habitual coffee drinkers (~7 cups/week) randomized for 6 months
  • AF/flutter recurred in 47% of coffee drinkers vs 64% of abstainers

  • No safety concerns reported

Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

For decades, patients with AF have been told to avoid coffee. A new randomized trial suggests that advice may be outdated.

The DECAF Trial (PMID: 41206802)
200 habitual coffee drinkers with persistent AF or atrial flutter were randomized for 6 months after electrical cardioversion to:

☕ Continue ≥1 cup of caffeinated coffee/day
🚫 Abstain from all coffee and caffeine

Results:
AF or flutter recurred in 47% of coffee drinkers vs 64% of abstainers (a 39% lower hazard of recurrence).
Importantly, no safety concerns were reported.

Why this is actually relevant:
These were long-term, heavy consumers (~7 cups/week) with existing rhythm disorders. The study doesn’t justify starting or increasing caffeine intake, especially for those who’ve had adverse responses.

Possible reasons coffee helped:

  • Adenosine-receptor blockade reduces atrial excitability.
  • Anti-inflammatory effects from coffee polyphenols.
  • Mild catecholamine activation may improve autonomic balance and encourage greater daily movement, which itself lowers AF risk.
  • Abstainers may have replaced coffee with higher-sugar drinks, worsening metabolic stress.

For habitual drinkers with AF, moderate coffee intake appears physiologically neutral or even protective. Guidance should be individualized, but the old blanket ban on coffee is losing scientific support.

Louis warns that the atrial fibrillation risk from high-dose omega-3 is real. A 2021 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (81,210 people) found higher AF risk, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs (114,592 people) confirmed it at higher doses in people at high CVD risk, but not in those without high risk, and not at low doses in either group.
