Ornish trial: lifestyle program cut cardiac events, but plant-based diet was not a cure

Louis Nervegna · Jun 24, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 20 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

In Ornish's small 1998 randomized trial, patients on the diet and lifestyle program had significantly fewer cardiac events over 5 years than controls (25 events in 28 patients vs 45 in 20 controls). Even among 28 who adhered, there were still 2 heart attacks, 8 angioplasties, 2 bypasses, 23 hospitalizations and 2 deaths, so the diet was not a cure.

  • 25 events in 28 intervention patients vs 45 in 20 controls (RR 2.47, 95% CI 1.48-4.20)
  • Intervention group: 2 MIs, 8 angioplasties, 2 bypasses, 23 hospitalizations, 2 deaths
  • Angioplasties, hospitalizations and total events significant; MI, bypass and deaths not
  • Comprehensive lifestyle program, not diet alone

Below are the results of Dean Ornish’s small randomized controlled trial testing a WFPBNO diet verses CVD (published in 1998).

Note that this was a comprehensive lifestyle program that included other positive lifestyle changes besides just diet.

People on the diet and lifestyle program had considerably fewer cardiac events than controls over a 5 year period (p<0.001).

However, the study clearly shows that a WFPBNO diet was NOT a cure for CVD.

Of the 28 people who faithfully stuck with the WFPBNO diet and lifestyle program for 5 years, there were still:

2 heart attacks (myocardial infarction),
8 angioplasties,
2 bypass surgeries,
23 cardiac hospitalizations,
2 deaths!

Some of these numbers are better than controls, but only angioplasties, cardiac hospitalizations, and total events were statistically significant when compared to controls (p<0.05). Heart attacks, bypass surgeries, and deaths were not. In fact, there were 2 deaths in the diet group and only 1 in the control group. This is obviously NOT a cure.
