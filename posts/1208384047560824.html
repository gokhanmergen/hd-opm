Chronic ibuprofen and NSAID use raises heart attack, stroke, kidney and BP risks

Louis Nervegna · Jan 24, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinnersHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis agrees ibuprofen is hard on kidneys and adds that chronic NSAID use can worsen heart failure, raise blood pressure and bleeding risk, and raise heart attack and stroke risk (aspirin excluded). Occasional use is fine.

  • Chronic NSAIDs raise heart attack and stroke risk (not aspirin)
  • Can exacerbate heart failure and hypertension
  • May lower risk of certain cancers

He is right. Ibuprofen is very rough on the kidneys. It can also exacerbate heart failure, and raise blood pressure in people with hypertension. It increases risk of bleeding, and can make ulcers worse.

What he forgets to mention is that chronic use of NDAIDS like ibuprofen also raises the risk for heart attacks and strokes. (This does not include aspirin.)

On the positive side, chronic use of ibuprofen and other NSAIDS may reduce risk of certain cancers.

Ibuprofen is perfectly safe for occasional use, but the long term side effects of chronic use need to be very seriously considered.

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.
