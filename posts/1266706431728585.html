Whey raises insulin more than white bread or glucose; all macronutrients stimulate insulin

Louis Nervegna · Apr 12, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesProtein
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that every macronutrient triggers insulin, carbohydrates usually the most and fastest, but some proteins like whey raise insulin more than carbohydrate calorie for calorie, contradicting influencer claims.

  • Whey raises insulin more than white bread or glucose per calorie

All macronutrients cause insulin secretion.

Carbohydrates usually produce the largest and fastest response.

Protein also stimulates a large insulin response. Some proteins raise insulin even more than carbohydrate, which is a fact that many influencers conveniently ignore.

For example, whey protein raises insulin more than white bread or pure glucose, when comparing calorie for calorie:

Paper · Nutr Metab (Lond) · 2012The insulinogenic effect of whey protein is partially mediated by a direct effect of amino acids and GIP on β-cells.Conclusion: Compared with white wheat bread meal, whey causes an increase of postprandial insulin, plasma amino acids, GIP and GLP-1 responses. The in vitro data suggest that whey protein exerts its insulinogenic effect by preferential elevation of the plasma concentrations of certain amino acids, GIP and GLP-1.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

↪ Whey protein raised insulin about twice as much as glucose or white bread per calorie

Beef raises insulin more than oatmeal (porridge) or white pasta, when comparing calorie for calorie:

Paper · Am J Clin Nutr · 1997An insulin index of foods: the insulin demand generated by 1000-kJ portions of common foods.The aim of this study was to systematically compare postprandial insulin responses to isoenergetic 1000-kJ (240-kcal) portions of several common foods. Correlations with nutrient content were determined. Thirty-eight foods separated into six food categories (fruit, bakery products, snacks, carbohydrate-rich foods, protein-rich foods, and breakfast cereals) were fed to groups of 11-13 healthy subjects. Fat usually provokes much less insulin, not because it is "free food," but because dietary fat is readily and easily stored. It requires relatively little metabolic handling compared with carbohydrate or protein.

The amount that particular foods raise insulin is quantified by the “insulin index”. This has been extensively studied. See the Wikipedia article on the subject, which includes a table of various foods:
