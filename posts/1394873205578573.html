Urolithin A looks promising for heart failure in animals but is untested in humans

Louis Nervegna · Sep 14, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Heart rhythm & heart failureSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis says urolithin A is a compound to watch for heart failure based on animal evidence, but it has not been properly tested in humans with the condition. Small human trials in healthy people suggest improved muscle function and exercise capacity; the shared post mentions 500-1,000 mg daily and mitophagy effects.

  • Animal evidence suggests benefit in heart failure
  • Not tested in humans with heart failure
  • Human trials at 500-1,000 mg/day in healthy people: muscle and mitochondrial markers improved

Urolithin A might be promising for heart failure. It has not been properly tested for this indication in humans yet, but preclinical evidence in animals suggests a benefit.

It is something to keep on your radar screen.

The few human trials that have been done suggest improvements in muscle function and exercise capacity in healthy individuals (without heart failure).

Shared from FoundMyFitness · original post

At 500–1,000 mg daily, urolithin A is showing early human evidence of benefits for mitochondrial markers and muscle performance

It targets mitophagy, the process cells use to remove damaged mitochondria, the structures that produce energy

In middle-aged adults with low fitness, 500 mg daily for four months increased muscle markers involved in mitochondrial cleanup and energy production

In a small trial of resistance-trained men, 1,000 mg daily for eight weeks improved static strength and repetitions before fatigue compared with placebo, but did not significantly improve the maximum weight they could lift
