Statins, bempedoic acid, fibrates and ezetimibe lower hsCRP; PCSK9 inhibitors do not

Louis Nervegna · Jun 12, 2026 · ♥ 21 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes a meta-analysis of randomized trials of lipid-lowering therapies and CRP. Statins, bempedoic acid, ezetimibe, fibrates and omega-3 all reduced hsCRP, whereas PCSK9 inhibitors seemed to raise it modestly. He suggests high LDL is itself inflammatory when it oxidizes in the arterial wall.

  • Average hsCRP reduction: statins 17.3%, bempedoic acid 20%, fibrates 10.3%, ezetimibe 3.2%, fish oil about 12.8%
  • Meta-analysis concluded statins, bempedoic acid, ezetimibe and omega-3 reduce CRP independently of LDL-C reduction
  • PCSK9 inhibitors seem to modestly increase hsCRP

Statins, bempedoic acid, ezetimibe, and fibrates, all lower inflammation and hsCRP.

Multiple trials have proven this.

Here are the average amounts by which they lower hsCRP:

Statins - 17.3%
Bempedoic acid - 20%
Ezetimibe - 3.2%
Fibrates - 10.3%

(For comparison, fish oil lowers hsCRP by about 12.8%.)

Part of the reason why they all do this is because high LDL itself can be inflammatory. When LDL gets inside the arterial wall and oxidizes, it attracts immune cells which generate inflammation. This raises levels of hsCRP and other inflammatory markers.

The only major exception is PCSK9 inhibitors, which actually seem to modestly increase hsCRP.
