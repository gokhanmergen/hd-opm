Zero-oil vs olive-oil diet trial was calorie restriction; LDL differences not significant

Louis Nervegna · Sep 26, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 42 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis critiques a trial claimed to show a no-oil diet lowers LDL more than a diet with 4 tablespoons of olive oil. He says LDL and ApoB results were not statistically significant overall, and the no-oil group ate only 1338 kcal per day, over 400 kcal less than the olive oil group. He concludes it was a short-term calorie restriction study, not an oil versus no-oil comparison.

  • Overall LDL and ApoB results not statistically significant
  • No-oil group ate 1338 kcal/day, over 400 fewer than olive oil group
  • Olive oil arm used 4 tablespoons daily

This study attracted a lot of attention. We discussed it previously in the group.

The claim was that a diet with zero oil lowers LDL more than a diet that includes 4 tablespoons of olive oil per day.

First off, the overall results for both LDL and ApoB in the trial were not statistically significant. What the study actually showed is that practicing calorie restriction causes LDL to trend lower than not practicing calorie restriction. I say “trend” because there was no statistical significance. The no oil group was only consuming 1338 calories per day, which put them in a huge calorie deficit. The olive oil group was consuming over 400 calories more per day, which put them much closer to energy balance.

This was fundamentally a short term calorie restriction study. Not a study about oil vs no oil.
