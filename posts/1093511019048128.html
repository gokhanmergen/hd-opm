About 10% of people with a zero calcium score still have significant soft plaque

Nov 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis notes that roughly 10% of patients with a CAC of zero have significant soft plaque, and shares a primer explaining why a zero score does not exclude heart disease. CAC detects only calcified plaque while CT coronary angiography shows both calcified and non-calcified plaque and narrowing. A zero score still means very low event risk over the following years, but low is not none.

About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.

REACT: silent atherosclerosis found in 57% of 16,808 adults, 7-9% of 18-29-year-olds

Sep 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The NEJM REACT study used carotid and femoral ultrasound plus coronary CT angiography in 16,808 adults aged 18-70 without known disease. Plaque was present in 57% overall, appeared in young adults, and a zero calcium score often missed early non-calcified plaque. Ten-year risk scores detected very little silent disease, so keeping risk factors favorable throughout life is the priority.

A CAC score of zero does not rule out atherosclerosis, especially under age 45

Jun 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 · 💬 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains that a zero calcium score can coexist with visible calcium specks or with non-calcified soft plaque, particularly in people under 45. Because soft plaque takes 10-15 years to calcify, a 45-year-old with significant soft plaque might not show calcium until about age 60.

High Lp(a) with a CAC score of zero still means low 10-year event risk (JACC)

May 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 10 Lp(a)

Louis shares a JACC finding that elevated Lp(a) raises relative risk across CAC strata, but among people with CAC of 0 absolute event rates stay low even when Lp(a) is high. It matches earlier MESA data and supports CAC scoring in people with high Lp(a).

Louis shares a cardiologist's warning that a zero coronary calcium score can falsely reassure because non-calcified plaque may be present, especially in younger people and women.
