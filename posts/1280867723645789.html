LDL works like speed in crashes: lowering it reduces risk without guaranteeing safety

Louis Nervegna · May 01, 2026 · ♥ 13 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis uses a driving analogy: slower drivers crash less but not never, just as lower LDL lowers risk without blanket protection, and other risk factors must also be addressed.

People who drive at 50 mph on the highway are a lot less likely to get into a serious car accident than people who drive at 100 mph.

Yet… lots of people who drive at 50 mph still get into serious accidents.

Does this mean that speed is not a major cause of car accidents? No, of course not.

Lowering your speed just lowers your risk. That’s all that it does. It doesn’t give you blanket protection against car crashes.

This is how LDL cholesterol works in heart disease.

Lots of other factors affect your chances of getting into a car accident besides your speed. You have to address all those other factors, as well, to have the best chances of staying safe.

If you’re a terrible driver, then you will still tend to get into serious accidents at 50 mph, even though your chances are a lot lower than at 100 mph.

In this case, you probably need to lower your speed even more to get sufficient protection.

The bottom line is that lowering your speed is a proven way to lower your risk of an accident, regardless of your skill level or what car you drive.

Speed is directly CAUSAL in car accidents.

That’s why we have laws to limit speed while driving.

High speed is not a guarantee that a crash WILL happen, but it increases the chances in the long run. You’re asking for trouble in the longer term if you’re always driving at 100 mph, regardless of how skilled a driver you are. There will be some people who can get away with this, but most will pay for it in the long term.

No matter what other serious risk factors you have for getting into accidents and no matter how bad a driver you actually are, you can be almost certain that lowering your speed will lower your risk of a serious accident in the long term. It’s not blanket protection, but it will make you safer in the long run.

How much you need to lower your speed to get adequate protection depends on your other risk factors. Terrible drivers with old cars that are in bad shape should probably be driving very slow. Good drivers with new cars can safely drive at higher speeds.

It’s nearly impossible to get into a serious accident if you’re sitting in your driveway going 0 mph. Speed is basically a necessary condition for a serious accident to occur. It’s not sufficient by itself, but it is necessary.

Other risk factors for car accidents don’t behave this way. Having bad tires is not necessary for a serious car accident to occur. Being a bad driver is not necessary.

However, it is absolutely necessary to actually leave the parked position in your driveway to get into a serious accident. If your car stays parked at 0 mph in your driveway for your entire life, it is virtually impossible for a serious car accident to ever occur.

LDL behaves the same way. It is likely a necessary condition for heart disease to occur. But it is not sufficient by itself in the vast majority of cases (with possible exceptions like extreme genetic hypercholesterolemia). The reason we know that LDL is not sufficient by itself is because there are well-documented examples of people with very high LDL for their entire lives who do not develop any clinically significant heart disease.

The other risk factors for heart disease besides LDL do not behave this way. They cause heart disease, just like bad tires cause car accidents, but they are not necessary for heart disease to occur. Heart disease can still occur without them. This is what makes LDL different.

Note:

Technically what I mean here when I say “LDL” is all ApoB tagged lipoproteins. LDL makes up the majority. The other lipoproteins are less important. Intervention trials testing whether lowering these other lipoproteins reduces risk of hard outcomes like heart attacks, stroke, death, have been mostly disappointing, except when values are extreme. Intervention trials involving LDL, on the other hand, have been consistently successful. That’s why the emphasis here is on LDL.
