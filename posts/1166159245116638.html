Targeting Oxidized LDL: New CAR-T Therapy Reduces Arterial Plaque by 70%

A preclinical study led by researchers at the University of Pennsylvania has demonstrated that CAR T cell therapy—traditionally a cancer treatment—can be effectively adapted to treat atherosclerosis by targeting inflammation. By engineering regulatory T cells (Tregs) to recognize and suppress oxidized LDL (OxLDL), the specific molecule that drives arterial inflammation, the team achieved a 70% reduction in plaque buildup in mice prone to high cholesterol. Unlike standard CAR T therapies that destroy cells, these engineered Tregs work by dampening the local immune response, offering a potential new therapeutic avenue for heart disease patients who remain at risk despite using cholesterol-lowering medications.

Microplastics raised plaque 63% in male mice, with no change in cholesterol or weight

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

Summarises a UC Riverside mouse study: atherosclerosis-prone mice given 10 mg/kg/day microplastics for nine weeks. Males had 63% more plaque in the aortic root and 624% more in the brachiocephalic artery; females had no significant increase. Weight and cholesterol were unchanged, suggesting direct endothelial damage and inflammation. Admin suggests high fiber and Finnish sauna as defenses (not from the study).
