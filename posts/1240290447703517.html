Metformin and UDCA did not help people who already had long COVID (396-patient trial)

Research cited

  • Metformin and UDCA for established long COVID · RCT
Shared from Patrik Andersson · original post

A new clinical study shows that two well-known medications, metformin, which is used for diabetes, and UDCA, which is used for liver disease, do not help treat long COVID symptoms when given after the symptoms have already developed.

Previous research has shown that metformin can reduce the risk of developing long COVID if it is taken early during the acute infection. Therefore, researchers wanted to investigate whether metformin or UDCA could also relieve symptoms in people who already had long COVID.

The study included 396 adults with persistent symptoms after COVID-19. They were randomly assigned to receive either metformin, UDCA, or a placebo for two weeks. The trial was double-blind, meaning that neither the participants nor the researchers knew who received which treatment. After eight weeks, there was no difference between the groups. Those who received the medications did not recover faster and did not experience greater symptom relief than those who received placebo.

The researchers conclude that these medications are not a solution for people who have already developed long COVID. The findings show that there are still no proven effective treatments and that more research is needed to develop targeted therapies, especially those addressing immune system disturbances believed to underlie the condition.
