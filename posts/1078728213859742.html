Gut-microbe D-lactate raises blood sugar and liver fat; an oral 'trap' helped obese mice

Gokhan Can · Jul 30, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Gut microbiomeInsulin resistance & diabetesWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Canadian researchers found D-lactate made by gut bacteria signals the liver to produce excess glucose and fat in obese mice and humans. An oral polymer trap binding it in the gut improved glucose control, insulin resistance and liver inflammation in mice without changing diet or weight.

  • Oral 'gut substrate trap' improved blood sugar and reduced liver damage in obese mice
  • Effects occurred without changes in diet or body weight

In a new study, Canadian scientists discovered that a molecule called D-lactate, produced by gut bacteria, contributes to high blood sugar and fatty liver disease in obese mice and humans. This microbial-produced fuel enters the bloodstream and signals the liver to create excess glucose and fat, acting as a previously unknown branch of the classic Cori cycle. To combat this, the researchers developed a novel "gut substrate trap," a safe, oral polymer that binds to D-lactate in the intestines, preventing its absorption. When given to obese mice, this trap significantly improved blood sugar control, reduced insulin resistance, and lessened liver inflammation and damage, all without altering diet or body weight, opening a potential new avenue for treating metabolic diseases.
