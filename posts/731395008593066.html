High-dose astaxanthin may risk crystalline maculopathy; stick to 4-12 mg studied as safe

Louis Nervegna · Dec 28, 2023 · ♥ 11 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis warns that high-dose astaxanthin could potentially cause crystalline maculopathy and vision loss, as seen with high-dose lutein and canthaxanthin, which are chemically similar xanthophylls. He advises choosing doses shown safe in human studies, such as 4-12 mg. The astaxanthin risk is inferred from related compounds, not directly shown.

  • Safe human-study doses cited: 4-12 mg astaxanthin
  • Lutein high-dose: crystalline maculopathy case report (JAMA Ophthalmol 2016)
  • Canthaxanthin causes crystalline retinopathy and vision loss at high doses (case report 2013; 2020 imaging study)

Taking high doses of astaxanthin is potentially dangerous.

It might cause crystalline maculopathy— like too much lutein can— which is very serious and could cause loss of eyesight. Both astaxanthin and lutein are xanthophylls, which are chemically very similar.

This is why you should choose dosages proven safe in human studies (e.g. 4mg - 12mg).

Canthaxanthin— another xanthophyll compound very similar astaxanthin— is well known to cause retinopathy and vision loss in high doses.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.
