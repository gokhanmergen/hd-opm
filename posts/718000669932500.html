Hibiscus IMPROVES BLOOD FLOW

Paper · Nutrients · 2019Acute Effects of Hibiscus Sabdariffa Calyces on Postprandial Blood Pressure, Vascular Function, Blood Lipids, Biomarkers of Insulin Resistance and Inflammation in Humans.Conclusion: The extract of HSC improved postprandial vascular function and may be a useful dietary strategy to reduce endothelial dysfunction and CVD risk, although this requires confirmation.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Consumption of hibiscus extract increased plasma total nitrites/nitrates relative to baseline over 4 hours (significant effect, p=0.016). Additionally, urinary nitrates were significantly higher with HSC versus control at 4 hours (p=0.048). This suggests increased nitric oxide production, which vasodilates blood vessels.

Acute hibiscus extract consumption significantly increased flow-mediated dilation (FMD) of the brachial artery by 2.29 percentage points at 2 hours and 2.02 percentage points at 4 hours relative to baseline and water control (p<0.001). This indicates improved vascular endothelial function after HSC consumption.

Plasma total antioxidant capacity was significantly higher in the 0-2 hour period after intake of hibiscus extract relative to control (p=0.026).

HIBISCUS LOWERS BLOOD PRESSURE

Seventeen randomized trials were included. Hibiscus exerted stronger effects on systolic BP (−7.10 mmHg ;P = 0.02) than placebo, with the magnitude of reduction greatest in those with elevated BP at baseline. Hibiscus induced reductions to BP similar to that resulting from medication. Hibiscus also significantly lowered levels of low-density lipoprotein compared with other teas and placebo (−6.76 mg/dL; P = 0.05).

HIBISCONE C INHIBITS PI3K

The phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase (PI3K) pathway regulates cellular metabolism and is upregulated in many cancers, making it an attractive chemotherapeutic target. We demonstrate that hibiscone C competitively inhibits PI3K activity in intact cells, slows proliferation, and induces cell death. Inhibited PI3K resulted in +10% mice lifespan in one study.
