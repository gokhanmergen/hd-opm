Vitamin D overdose caused fatal hypercalcemia in an 89-year-old, UK coroner warns

Louis notes severe hypercalcemia can cause cardiac arrest, coma or kidney failure. The shared story reports a UK coroner's warning after an 89-year-old died of vitamin D toxicity after nine months of supplements.

Shared from Facts Maverick · original post

A coroner in the United Kingdom has issued a serious warning about the dangers of excessive supplement use following the tragic death of an 89-year-old man named David Mitchener. Mitchener was admitted to the hospital in after suffering from hypercalcemia, a condition where high calcium levels in the body weaken the bones and damage the kidneys and heart. He passed away ten days later, with tests revealing his vitamin D levels were at the maximum limit recordable by the laboratory.

The investigation revealed that Mitchener had been taking vitamin D supplements for at least nine months prior to his death. The excessive intake led to vitamin D toxicity, which caused the fatal buildup of calcium in his system. In his official report, the coroner raised concerns that vitamin supplements often lack clear warnings about the risks of severe side effects or death from overdose.

The post summarises a video describing an NEJM study of 16,000 asymptomatic people showing plaque accumulates first in the neck and leg arteries, during the 20s to 40s, before the heart.
