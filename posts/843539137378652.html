New AHA risk calculator could mean 107,000 more heart attacks and strokes in 10 years

Louis Nervegna · Jul 29, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Guidelines, policy & public healthStatinsRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

A STAT news share on the debate over the AHA's updated risk calculator, which would classify many people as lower risk and make fewer eligible for statins or blood pressure drugs. A new study estimates up to 107,000 more heart attacks and strokes over 10 years if millions lose eligibility.

  • Estimate: 107,000 more heart attacks and strokes over 10 years
  • New calculator predicts fewer statin prescriptions than the old one
  • Expert panels must decide whether to change guidelines

There’s a big debate brewing about the new risk calculator that the AHA has released.

Expert panels are going to have to decide whether or not official guidelines for prescribing statins and other drugs should be changed based on this new risk model.

You can find a link to that calculator here:

facebook.com/share/p/NKpf9FxH9AkkPTPj

A previous journal paper predicted that using the new calculator will result in far fewer people being prescribed statins, because their risk will be calculated as lower than with the old version of the calculator:

facebook.com/share/p/3NxtBFaapgdeYC4W

Now this new paper predicts that this could have serious negative consequences.

Quoted:

“If millions of Americans no longer qualify for a statin or a blood pressure medication based on a new calculator updated to better predict their risk, that could lead to 107,000 more heart attacks and strokes over 10 years, a new study estimates.”

This is a complicated issue.
