YELLOW-III 'melting butter' sign: lipid core shrinks and fibrous cap thickens in 6 months

Louis Nervegna · Mar 22, 2026 · ♥ 25 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares Evan Levine's post and explains that YELLOW-III, using near-infrared imaging, showed a major drop in plaque volume driven by the soft lipid core and a major rise in fibrous cap thickness within 6 months of statin plus PCSK9 inhibitor therapy.

  • Reduction in total plaque volume driven by lipid core
  • Fibrous cap thickness increased in 6 months

Melting away plaque— the “melting butter sign”.

New results from the YELLOW-III study.

Note that these results are discussed (in detail) in a separate post here: ↪ YELLOW-III: statin plus evolocumab thickened fibrous caps over 30% in 6 months.

In just 6 months timeframe, this study showed a major reduction in total plaque volume (PAV), driven primarily by reduction of the soft lipid core (the “butter”) inside plaques. The study also showed a major increase in fibrous cap thickness (FCT) in just 6 months, which is known to stabilize existing plaque.

About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.
