Louis shares a BoxLife Magazine article about a YouTuber who ate a Mediterranean-style day in Greece and then a typical American fast-food day, reporting steady energy and good digestion versus fatigue and bloating. It is a personal experience story with no study behind it.

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

Fitness YouTuber Will Tennyson compared a day on the Mediterranean diet with a typical American menu to see how food quality affects energy, digestion, and wellbeing.

From creamy Greek yogurt and olive oil–fried zucchini to iced lattes and fast-food fries, his taste buds and body endured polar opposites.

He experienced steady energy, smooth digestion, and community dining in Greece versus fatigue, bloating, and mental fog back home.

The surprising findings reveal why ingredient quality and lifestyle pace matter as much as calories.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.

Lyon Diet Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut heart attacks and CV deaths 73%

Sep 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis argues that LDL and diet are largely independent risk factors, citing the Lyon Heart Study in which both groups had similar cholesterol but the Mediterranean-diet group fared far better than the AHA-diet group. His takeaway is to eat healthily whatever your LDL. The claim that no drug matches this effect is his opinion.

Mediterranean diet linked to higher mitochondrial microproteins humanin and SHMOOSE

Mar 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A USC study reported by Medical Xpress found that people closely following a Mediterranean diet had higher levels of the mitochondrial microproteins humanin and SHMOOSE, both linked to protection against cardiovascular disease and neurodegeneration. Foods like olive oil, fish and legumes may influence aging at a cellular level.
