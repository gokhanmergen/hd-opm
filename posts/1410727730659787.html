Two slices of deli meat a day linked to 44% higher dementia risk in a new review

Louis Nervegna · Oct 02, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 15 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyBrain health & dementiaMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

A review in the Annual Review of Nutrition reports that about two slices of processed meat daily is linked to 44% higher dementia risk and 52% higher Alzheimer's risk, drawing on dozens of studies including nearly 500,000 UK participants. Replacing 10% of ultra-processed food calories with unprocessed food was linked to 19% lower dementia risk. The poster says processed meat has the worst hazard ratios of any food category apart from trans fats.

  • Two slices of deli meat daily: 44% higher dementia risk and 52% higher Alzheimer's risk
  • Includes a UK study of nearly 500,000 people
  • Replacing 10% of ultra-processed calories with unprocessed foods: 19% lower dementia risk

Yet another study linking processed meat with poor health outcomes. This time it is dementia.

Processed meats have the worst hazard ratios of any food category, with the only exception being trans fats. They likely raise mortality more than any other source of protein: facebook.com/share/v/18NzYS1pLQ.

APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.
