An underappreciated cause of side effects from statins and other drugs is accidently combining them with foods or supplements that raise blood levels of the drug.

The main issue with combining statins (or any other drug) with supplements is what is called a “cytochrome p450 interaction”.

The cytochrome p450 system is what filters out foreign substances in your intestines and liver, to protect you from being poisoned.

What this interaction means is that the supplements might change how much of the statin (or other drug) that you absorb in your intestines and how fast your liver excretes the statin. The classic example of this is taking a statin and grapefruit juice at the same time. The juice will cause you to absorb a lot more of the statin in your intestine and excrete it slower in your liver. So effectively, you get a higher dose.

Therefore, you may have to adjust the dose of the statin (or other drug) to compensate for the interaction based on your level of LDL. You can avoid this to some degree by separating when you take the statin from when you take the supplements. That’s what I try to do myself, since I also take a lot of supplements. It’s not perfect though. I’m sure there is still a p450 interaction in my case. But I just adjust my dose of statin until I’m happy with my LDL, so the interaction really does not matter.

The most likely supplements to cause cytochrome p450 interactions with drugs are herbs and botanical compounds.

Edit: As Kate Shore points out in the comments below, the cytochrome p450 interaction between grapefruit juice and statins occurs only with certain statins, not all of them. See her comment for a list.
