Vitamin D from sun self-limits, but 20,000 IU/day supplements risk hypercalcemia

When you get vitamin D from the sun, there is a natural negative feedback mechanism present in your body which stops production once blood levels get high enough. This is a built-in safety mechanism.

(This is the same kind of mechanism by which your thermostat turns off the heat in your house once the temperature gets high enough.)

There is no such negative feeback mechanism when you take supplements, so you have to be very careful about the dose.

If you take 20,000IU of vitamin D3 every single day for many months or years, then you run a real risk of your blood levels getting high enough to cause hypercalcemia, which can be very serious. This can cause a sudden cardiac arrest, among many other problems (including calcification of arteries and other tissue).

It is impossible to do this from sun exposure— no matter how much sun you get.
