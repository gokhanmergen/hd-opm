Anticholinergic drugs such as antihistamines and benzodiazepines linked to dementia risk

Louis Nervegna · Jun 29, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

The shared article discusses antihistamines and other anticholinergic or sedating drugs (e.g. Benadryl, Tylenol PM, benzodiazepines, Ambien) that reduce acetylcholine signalling and correlate with higher dementia risk. Evidence is observational and relevance to heart health is indirect.

  • Anticholinergic medications correlate with increased dementia risk
  • Benzodiazepines also correlate

#dementiaHD #alzheimersHD #antihistaminesHD #benadrylHD #anticholinergicsHD #benzodiazepinesHD #bredesenHD #ambienHD

Shared from Dale Bredesen, MD · original post

A recent article from Women's Health highlights concerns about antihistamines and dementia risk. Many commonly used medications, including some antihistamines, can negatively impact cognition by downregulating acetylcholine, a key neurotransmitter vital for memory and learning.

We have long warned that anticholinergic medications correlate with an increased risk of dementia, as do benzodiazepines. This includes drugs like Benadryl, Tylenol PM, and Advil PM, as well as some sleep medications like Ambien. Newer drugs like Claritin and Zyrtec, which don’t cross the blood-brain barrier as much, are likely better.
