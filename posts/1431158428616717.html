Can the foods you eat actually slow brain shrinkage as you age? A randomized controlled trial suggests that certain dietary patterns may help preserve brain structure, particularly after age 50.

Researchers assigned nearly 300 participants to follow one of three diets for 18 months: standard healthy dietary guidelines, a Mediterranean diet, or a modified Green Mediterranean diet containing additional green tea and mankai (duckweed).

Researchers measured hippocampal occupancy score (HOC), a marker of brain shrinkage that reflects changes in the hippocampus, a region important for memory. Interestingly, this measure tends to decline more rapidly after age 50.

The results? Only the Green Mediterranean group showed no statistically significant decline in HOC over the 18 months.

However, when researchers focused specifically on participants over 50, both Mediterranean diets outperformed the standard healthy diet, with no statistically significant difference between the two Mediterranean approaches.

Researchers also identified three foods associated with greater preservation of brain structure: walnuts, green tea, and mankai.

Importantly, these findings concern brain structure rather than actual dementia prevention, and all groups also participated in physical activity routines.

How to Use This: Consider adopting a Mediterranean-style diet rich in vegetables, fish, and nuts, while limiting processed foods. Adding walnuts and green tea may offer additional benefits.
