Claim that blending fruit causes bigger glucose spikes is untrue, per Louis

Louis Nervegna · Dec 03, 2025 · ♥ 24 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis states that blending fruit does not cause a larger glucose spike than whole fruit and can lower or leave unchanged the glycemic index, criticizing Glucose Goddess and Gary Brecka's claims. The post links earlier group posts but cites no new study.

  • Blending can lower the glucose spike or leave it unchanged
  • Proposed mechanism: sugar binds more tightly to soluble fiber

It is untrue that blending fruit causes a bigger glucose spike than eating the fruit whole.

In fact, blending fruit can actually lower the glucose spike.

Blending fruit can cause the sugar to bind more tightly to the soluble fiber, making it absorb more slowly in the digestive tract.

Blending fruit can actually LOWER the glycemic index.

But often, it just stays the same…

Louis shares a rebuttal of the claim that bananas in smoothies negate berry polyphenols. Banana polyphenol oxidase can break down some polyphenols such as epicatechins but does not degrade most anthocyanins, the main blueberry polyphenols, so the effect is likely minor.
