Cheese umbrella review: modestly lower mortality, CVD, stroke and diabetes risk

Gokhan Can · Aug 06, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyFats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)
AI summary & key facts

An umbrella review of 54 meta-analyses found cheese consumption associated with lower risk of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular outcomes, type 2 diabetes, fractures and dementia, with moderate evidence quality. Gokhan flags this as interesting given the group's usual caution on saturated fat.

  • All-cause mortality RR 0.95; CVD death 0.93; CVD 0.92; CHD 0.92; stroke 0.93
  • Type 2 diabetes RR 0.93; fractures 0.90; dementia 0.81; ER-negative breast cancer 0.89
  • Moderate evidence quality; no clear link for cancer mortality, hypertension or prostate cancer

Can Cheese be Healthy?

(I guess we tend to be generally against saturated fat around here. But, here's some interesting research)

We reviewed 54 meta-analyses and found that cheese consumption was linked to lower risk of all-cause mortality (RR=0.95), cardiovascular death (0.93), CVD (0.92), CHD (0.92), stroke (0.93), ER− breast cancer (0.89), type 2 diabetes (0.93), fractures (0.90), and dementia (0.81). Other outcomes showed no clear associations. Evidence quality was moderate for these inverse links and for null results in cancer mortality, hypertension, and prostate cancer. Overall, cheese appears neutral to modestly beneficial for health.

Paper · Adv Nutr · 2023Cheese consumption and multiple health outcomes: an umbrella review and updated meta-analysis of prospective studies.This umbrella review aims to provide a systematic and comprehensive overview of current evidence from prospective studies on the diverse health effects of cheese consumption. We searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library to identify meta-analyses/pooled analyses of prospective studies examining the association between cheese consumption and major health outcomes from inception to August 31, 2022. Statins neither cause nor cure cancer: meta-analyses of randomized trials show no effect

Dec 31, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 4 Statins

Reviews the evidence on statins and cancer. A 2012 meta-analysis of 134,537 participants in 22 RCTs found identical cancer incidence (1.4% per year) and mortality (0.5% per year) in statin users and non-users, and a 2023 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found the same. Anticancer effects in specific contexts remain preclinical or suggestive.
