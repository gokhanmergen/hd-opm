Louis disputes claims that high protein intake builds more muscle

Louis Nervegna · May 22, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Protein
AI summary & key facts

Louis says protein above 1.6 g/kg does nothing for muscle, citing a creator who cut protein 25% and added muscle while visceral fat fell. It is an anecdote plus links to group posts.

  • Claim: no benefit above 1.6 g/kg
  • Anecdote: 25% protein cut, muscle gain

I had a good laugh after watching this video.

Study after study shows that protein intakes above 1.6g/kg do absolutely nothing for building muscle, even when combined with intense weight lifting:

↪ Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

↪ Sports science says 1 g/lb protein is excessive; extra protein needs resistance training

This guy spent two years of his life repeating this experiment. Guess what he learned?

It does nothing.

————-

He cut his overall protein intake by 25%— down to 1.6g/kg — by reducing animal foods.

The result?

He added muscle, while dramatically reducing visceral fat. His bloodwork significantly improved. All while being in a slight calorie deficit.

Sports science says 1 g/lb protein is excessive; extra protein needs resistance training

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 19 ↩ 4 Protein

The poster says sports-science consensus is that muscle protein synthesis maxes out around 1.6 g/kg in natural bodybuilders, so 2.2 g/kg is wasteful. Extra protein above the RDA builds strength or muscle only when combined with resistance training, per dozens of randomized trials.

Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 6 ↩ 11 Protein

A dose-response meta-analysis of 69 randomized trials found protein alone does not increase muscle strength without resistance training. With training, strength rose 0.72% per extra 0.1 g/kg/day up to 1.5 g/kg/day, with no further gain; going from the RDA to 1.5 g/kg adds only about 5% more strength.
