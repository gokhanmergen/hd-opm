Monkey metformin dose of 20 mg/kg scales to only about 450 mg in a 70 kg human

Louis Nervegna · Sep 13, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis calculates that the cynomolgus monkey aging study used 20 mg/kg metformin daily for 40 months. Using the FDA allometric scaling factor of 3.1, this is about 6.45 mg/kg, or roughly 450 mg for a 70 kg person, about a quarter of the 2 g typical diabetic dose.

  • 20 mg/kg/day in monkeys for 40 months
  • FDA scaling factor 3.1 gives 6.45 mg/kg
  • About 452 mg for a 70 kg human, roughly 1/4 of 2 g diabetic dose

The paper that showed metformin slowed aging of cynomolgus monkeys used a dose of 20mg/kg in the monkeys.

After allometric scaling, this corresponds to a human dose of about 450mg for a 70kg human.

So they used a fairly low dose.
(About 1/4 of the 2g dose typically used in diabetics.)

See math below.

Quoted from the full paper:

“Monkeys were fed three meals and had free access to water. Along with breakfast, the O-Met group was treated every day with 20 mg/kg metformin administered through drinking water over the duration of the study (40 months).”

The FDA conversion guide suggests an allometric scaling factor of 3.1 to convert from cynomolgus monkeys to humans. (See table below.)

20mg/kg / 3.1 = 6.45mg/kg

For a 70kg human, this would be a dose of

(6.45mg/kg)(70kg) = 452mg .
