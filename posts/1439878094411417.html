Shared from Vita Pulse · original post

A study published in Nature Neuroscience identified a mechanism that may disrupt the brain’s waste-clearing system in Alzheimer’s disease. Working with mice, researchers found that amyloid-beta increased calcium activity in astrocytes, the support cells that help regulate fluid movement through brain tissue. This heightened activity stimulated cholesterol production inside the cells. Excess cholesterol then encouraged the removal and breakdown of AQP4 water channels from their normal locations around blood vessels. With fewer properly positioned channels, the glymphatic system became less effective at clearing waste. The findings reveal a potential feedback loop in which amyloid accumulation further weakens the brain’s cleanup process.

Researchers improved waste clearance and memory performance in the mice by calming astrocyte activity, reducing cholesterol synthesis, or administering atorvastatin, a cholesterol-lowering medicine. These results suggest a possible research target for protecting fluid transport during early Alzheimer’s-like changes. However, the treatment findings come from animal experiments and do not establish an effective therapy for people. Cholesterol produced within brain cells is regulated differently from cholesterol circulating in the blood, so the results should not be interpreted as evidence that dietary cholesterol directly blocks brain clearance. Human studies are needed, and patients should not start, stop or change statins based on this research.

Source: Zhang Z et al., Nature Neuroscience (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02261-9

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
