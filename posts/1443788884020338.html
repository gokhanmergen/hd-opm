Even light alcohol raises breast cancer risk, but high folate erased it in Nurses' Study

Louis Nervegna · Nov 07, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Walter Willett clip noting wine, beer and spirits all raise breast cancer risk at under one drink a day, replicated in over 100 studies. Louis adds that in the Harvard Nurses' Study the excess risk disappeared with higher folate intake or folic acid supplementation, so folate may matter for those who drink.

  • Risk seen at under one drink a day; replicated in over 100 studies
  • Wine, beer and spirits carried roughly the same risk per drink
  • In Nurses' Health Study, excess risk vanished with higher folate intake

Even mild to moderate alcohol consumption raises the risk of breast cancer.

One thing that he forgets to mention here is that the increased risk in breast cancer from mild to moderate alcohol consumption in the Harvard Nurses Study disappears with higher dietary intake of folate (or with folic acid supplementation): ↪ Harvard Nutrition Source review of folate: a little is good, too much may be bad. So if you do choose to drink, paying attention to your folate intake might be important.

Quoted from the original post:

Breast cancer and alcohol: the link isn't theoretical anymore.

Dr. Walter Willett's team saw it early. When the first follow-up questionnaires came back, wine, beer, and spirits all stood out as risk factors for breast cancer, even at less than one drink a day. The finding held up, and it's since been replicated in over 100 studies. At this point, it's an accepted cause-and-effect relationship, not a correlation to debate.

What's striking is that it didn't matter which kind of alcohol: wine, beer, and distilled spirits carried roughly the same risk per drink. There's no "safer" category to hide in.

This isn't about guilt or giving up joy, it's about making an informed choice with real data behind it. In the Blue Zones, alcohol (when present) is social, moderate, and often wine shared over a meal with people you love, not a daily habit driven by stress or routine.
