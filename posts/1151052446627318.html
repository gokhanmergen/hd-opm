Nurses' Health Study: plant protein, not animal protein, linked to lower frailty risk

Louis Nervegna · Nov 02, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 19 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites a 2022 Nurses' Health Study analysis in which higher plant protein intake was associated with lower frailty risk, while animal and dairy protein were not. He adds that protein alone does not build muscle without resistance exercise.

  • Substituting 5% of energy from plant for animal protein: 38% lower frailty risk
  • For dairy protein 32% lower; non-dairy animal protein 42% lower
  • Resistance exercise is needed to build strength

Eating more high-quality animal protein in middle age and onward does absolutely nothing to protect against frailty and sarcopenia in old age.

Plant protein, on the other hand, is protective.

These results come from the Harvard Nurses Study.

Dozens of well-done randomized controlled trials have proven that protein by itself does not increase muscle strength. To increase muscle strength, you must do resistance exercise: ↪ Meta-analysis of 69 RCTs: protein strength gains plateau at 1.5 g/kg and need lifting

Protein intake and risk of frailty among older women in the Harvard Nurses' Health Study - March 2022

“Conclusions
A higher intake of plant protein, but not animal or dairy protein, was associated with a lower risk of frailty. Substitution of plant protein for animal protein, especially non-dairy animal protein, was associated with lower risk of frailty.”

“Substituting 5% of energy from plant protein intake at the expense of animal protein, dairy protein, or non-dairy animal protein was associated with 38% (29%, 47%), 32% (21%, 42%), and 42% (33%, 50%) reduced risk of frailty.”

Paper · J cachexia sarcopenia muscle · 2022Protein intake and risk of frailty among older women in the Nurses' Health StudyBackground There is evidence that an overall healthy diet is associated with lower risk of frailty. However, the effect of diet composition, specifically the role of protein intake on frailty, is mostly unclear. The aim of this study was to evaluate the intake of protein, including total, plant, animal, and dairy protein, in relation to frailty incidence in a large cohort of older women.
