LDL particle size does not predict CVD risk once particle number (ApoB) is counted

AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that LDL particle size is not associated with cardiovascular risk after adjusting for the total number of LDL particles, measured by LDL-P or ApoB. High LDL or ApoB with large particles is unlikely to be protective. Size can still hint at insulin sensitivity, but better tests exist.

  • Particle number (LDL-P or ApoB) matters, not size
  • Large 'fluffy' LDL is unlikely to protect if ApoB is high
  • Size remains a rough marker of insulin sensitivity

LDL particle size is not associated with risk for CVD, after the total number of LDL particles is properly adjusted for.

The number of LDL particles is measured by LDL-P or ApoB.

In simple terms, if you have high LDL or ApoB (indicating a large number of LDL particles) having a larger LDL particle size is unlikely to protect you.

Particle size is still useful as a measure of insulin sensitivity and glycemic control, but there are more accurate tests for this.
