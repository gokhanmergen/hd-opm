CETP inhibitor obicetrapib slowed p-tau217 accumulation in the BROADWAY analysis

Louis Nervegna · Sep 05, 2025 · ♥ 7 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis notes that HDL-raising drugs failed for heart disease but may have value for Alzheimer's. In a secondary analysis of the BROADWAY trial presented at AAIC, the CETP inhibitor obicetrapib slowed rise of the Alzheimer's biomarker p-tau217 versus placebo.

  • Obicetrapib blocks conversion of HDL to LDL (CETP inhibition)
  • Prespecified secondary analysis of BROADWAY, a CV-focused trial
  • Obicetrapib significantly changed p-tau progression vs placebo (per quoted report)

Drugs to raise HDL failed miserably in clinical trials for heart disease.

But they may have value in treating Alzheimer’s.

Quoted:

“This was from a prespecified secondary analysis of data from a cardiovascular-focused trial of obicetrapib called BROADWAY, primary results from which were reported in May. The drug works by blocking conversion of high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol into the low-density form (LDL). CETP inhibitors were all the rage a few years ago as a novel approach to cholesterol management, but poor results with the first wave of candidates didn't meet expectations.”

“But research on the interplay of cholesterol metabolism and Alzheimer's disease led the company to keep an eye on obicetrapib's effects on Alzheimer's disease biomarkers. "Lipid dysregulation is very important for Alzheimer's disease, it's an important risk factor, especially with APOE4," he told attendees. "Obicetrapib actually has the ability to significantly change p-tau progression over time as compared with placebo."

“The investigators' belief, Scheltens said, is that these effects are not a consequence of diminished HDL-LDL conversion, but rather some type of direct action on Alzheimer's pathology.”

Shared from Dale Bredesen, MD · original post

A heart drug slowed the accumulation of phosphorylated tau protein-217 (p-Tau 217) in those with cardiovascular disease in a new trial. Those familiar with BrainScan will recognize p-Tau 217 as one of the biomarkers tested to detect cognitive decline. While the drug (obicetrapib) targets cholesterol metabolism, much of the benefit may simply come from improved cardiovascular health. Link to medpagetoday.com (medpagetoday.com)

Louis shares a pre-specified BROADWAY substudy (1,535 patients) in which obicetrapib improved five Alzheimer's biomarkers, with the largest benefit in APOE4/4 homozygotes. Caveats are that these are biomarkers, not cognitive outcomes, in a subgroup needing replication.

Obicetrapib gets first approval worldwide in the EU, lowering LDL up to 40-50%

Sep 21, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 5 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

The European Commission approved obicetrapib, a low-dose oral CETP inhibitor, on 21 September 2026 in two versions: obicetrapib 10 mg alone and combined with ezetimibe 10 mg. Phase 3 trials reported LDL-C reductions of about 40% alone and about 50% with ezetimibe versus placebo. The poster says the drug has shown promise for both cardiovascular disease and Alzheimer's.

CETP inhibitors might help prevent Alzheimer's and Lewy body dementia in APOE4 carriers

Louis shares a breakdown of why a lipid drug class could affect the brain: APOE is the brain's main cholesterol carrier, and a 2024 Mendelian randomization found the CETP benefit strongest in APOE4 carriers. It cites genetic and biomarker evidence, but this is early and not proof of clinical benefit.

Statin- and ezetimibe-like gene variants linked to 76-82% lower dementia risk per 40 mg/dL

Aug 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

Louis highlights a Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis of about 1 million people showing lifelong lower non-HDL cholesterol via statin-like (HMGCR) and ezetimibe-like (NPC1L1) variants was associated with 76% and 82% lower dementia per ~40 mg/dL drop. CETP variants showed a 70% reduction.
