Life expectancy in Hong Kong is one of the highest in the world.

They live on average 86 years, compared to Americans who live 76.

What do these people actually eat?

Turns out there was a rigorous study in 2011 to assess exactly what people in Hong Kong eat:

cfs.gov.hkThe first Hong Kong Total Diet Study (1st HKTDS)cfs.gov.hk

The report from that study can be found here:

cfs.gov.hk1st HKTDS Report e.pdfcfs.gov.hk

Who funded the study? It was the government in Hong Kong. They wanted to understand exactly what people in the city were eating, especially the amount of nutrients and the exposure to toxic substances.

As the photo from from the study report (below) shows:

Cereal grains make up the vast majority of the daily diet by weight (491g).

Next is vegetables (177g).

Then fruit (147g).

Meat, poultry, and game are fourth (113g).
For reference, 100g of meat is about the size of a deck of cards or the palm of your hand.

Fish and seafood are fifth (71g).

Dairy and eggs are low. So are legumes, nuts, and seeds.

Sugars, confectionary, snack foods, and added fats and oils are very low.

There is mild to moderate alcohol consumption.

There is also a catch-all category of “mixed dishes” composed of a mixture of the various food groups which comprises a major portion of their diet (222g).

Quoted from the website of the study (first link above):

“The study covered majority of food consumed by the Hong Kong population, in which a TDS food list containing 150 different food items was developed, based on the food consumption data of the Hong Kong Population-based Food Consumption Survey (FCS). The FCS was a survey on 5,008 Hong Kong people aged 20-84 using two non-consecutive 24-hour dietary intake (24-hr recall) questionnaires supplemented by a food frequency questionnaire, and revealed that over 1,400 food items were consumed by the Hong Kong people. Foods were selected according to the following criteria: (a) food commonly consumed by the population; and (b) food that is likely to contain high concentration of concerned substances even its consumption is low.”
