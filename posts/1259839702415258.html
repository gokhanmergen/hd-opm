Statins cut deaths 31% in people 80+, showing absolute benefit depends on baseline risk

Gokhan Can · Apr 02, 2026 · ♥ 12 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Quotes Dr Carvalho on a new analysis in people aged 80 or older: statin users had a 31% lower relative risk of dying and, as stated, a 17% lower absolute risk. The key principle is that absolute benefit depends on control-group risk, so it looks small in young people over the short term but grows with time, age or illness.

  • 31% relative risk reduction in mortality in those 80+
  • 17% absolute risk reduction (as quoted)
  • Absolute benefit scales with control-group risk

"Statins reduce heart attacks, strokes and deaths.

But is the absolute reduction small?

A new analysis looked at people ≥80yo

Those on statins had a 31% lower risk of dying (relative risk reduction) and the absolute risk was 17% lower.

This illustrates a key principle.

Absolute risk hinges on the risk of the control group

In younger populations it always seems small over the SHORT TERM

Because there isn´t much to prevent in the 1st place

Over time (or in an older or sicker population) the benefit size balloons..."

Even a cure for heart disease would show only 2-5% absolute risk reduction short-term

Nov 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 7 Statins

Louis explains that over a few years the absolute risk of a heart attack is only about 2-5%, so no intervention, even a complete cure, can cut absolute risk by more than that in a short trial. A 1-2% absolute reduction from statins or from diet and lifestyle trials is therefore a meaningful result.
