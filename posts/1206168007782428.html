Pomegranate juice may reverse carotid IMT, but larger trials are lacking, says Louis

Louis Nervegna · Jan 21, 2026 · ♥ 37 reactions · 53 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Substack about a 2004 pomegranate study, calling the author eccentric but saying pomegranate polyphenols look promising. He proposes telomerase activation as a mechanism and notes larger trials were never done.

  • Possible carotid IMT reversal in a 2004 study
  • No larger definitive studies

The guy who wrote this article is kind of a nut job. I hesitate to even share it. But there is a grain of truth to it imo.

I do believe that pomegranate juice and pomegranate polyphenols, in particular, are promising interventions. It is sad that larger studies have never been done to prove definitively whether or not they really work. We just don’t know unfortunately.

I’ve been drinking pomegranate juice and/or taking pomegranate supplements for over 15 years now, and plan to continue doing so.

My note:

There is a small biotech company in Nevada called Sierra Sciences that discovered that pomegranate extract is a potent activator of the enzyme telomerase in human fibroblast skin cells. This science was never formally published, but was disclosed in patents. I’ve heard the founder discuss it many times.

In my opinion, this provides a plausible biological mechanism for the (possible) reversal of carotid IMT from pomegranate juice. Replicative senescence of cells in arteries (particularly the endothelium) plays a major role in atherosclerosis as we get older, and the enzyme telomerase specifically prevents this from occurring.

It is interesting to note that statins have also been shown to modestly activate this same telomerase enzyme in arteries. This is known to be one of their pleiotropic effects in CVD. (See comments for more on this.)
