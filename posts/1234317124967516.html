Meta-analysis of 34 RCTs: taurine supplements improved several cardiometabolic markers

Louis Nervegna · Feb 26, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

A meta-analysis of 34 randomized trials in 1,394 participants found oral taurine improved multiple cardiometabolic risk factors compared with placebo or control. Specific markers are not listed in the post.

  • 34 RCTs, 1,394 participants
  • Improvements in several cardiometabolic risk factors

In this recently published meta-analysis of 34 randomized controlled trials in 1,394 participants, the dietary supplement taurine improved several markers of cardiometabolic health, compared to a placebo or another control.

Taurine 3 g/day lowered insulin, HOMA-IR and inflammation in type 2 diabetes RCT

Jul 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 15 Insulin resistance & diabetes

A 120-person RCT gave 3 g taurine daily with a low-calorie diet for 8 weeks. Insulin, HOMA-IR, TNF, hs-CRP and MDA fell, but lipids, HbA1c and fasting glucose did not improve.

BRIDGE trial: Mediterranean diet cut weight and visceral fat but did not improve cognition

In a 14-month randomized trial of older, mainly African American adults with obesity, an eight-month Mediterranean diet intervention improved diet quality, weight and visceral fat, but no cognitive benefit appeared versus control.

Higher fiber intake linked to 53% lower mortality in 3,894 US adults with diabetes

Aug 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

A shared summary of a cohort of 3,894 US adults with diabetes found the third quartile of fiber intake had a 53% lower relative risk of death than the lowest (HR 0.47), with about 13% mediated by lower systemic immune inflammation index. It is observational and does not prove fiber extends life by 53%.

Low-insulinemic and Planetary Health diets tied to less weight gain at menopause

Jun 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 2 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis shares results from the Harvard Nurses' Health Study published in JAMA Network Open. Among over 38,000 women followed 12 years around menopause, a low-insulinemic dietary pattern and the Planetary Health Diet were most associated with less weight gain and lower obesity risk.

Luigi Fontana, reacting to a 2026 JAMA editorial on lipid lowering, argues that statins, ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors now drive LDL very low, but many patients still have visceral fat, insulin resistance, fatty liver and inflammation.
