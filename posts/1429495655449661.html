Statin dose-response: the lowest dose gives the biggest percentage LDL drop per mg

Louis Nervegna · Oct 23, 2026 · ♥ 19 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Tom Dayspring slide showing diminishing returns with statin dose: 10 mg atorvastatin lowers LDL about 37%, with 20 mg adding 6%, 40 mg adding 5% and 80 mg adding 3%. His advice is to lower LDL with lifestyle first, then use the lowest effective statin dose, taking a low dose being better than quitting.

  • Atorvastatin 10 mg: 37%; 20 mg: 43%; 40 mg: 48%; 80 mg: 51%
  • Each doubling adds only 6%, 5%, then 3%

The biggest percentage drop per mg in
LDL from statins comes from the lowest prescribed dose. As you increase the dose, the percentage drop increases by smaller and smaller increments. So you get the biggest bang for the buck from the lowest dose. It’s much better to take the lowest dose than quit entirely.

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

I stole this slide from one of Tom Dayspring’s talks:

The biggest percentage drop per mg in LDL from statins comes from the lowest prescribed dose.

As you increase the dose, the percentage drop increases by smaller and smaller increments.

— For example, 10mg of Atorvastatin drops LDL by 37% on average.

— Switching from 10mg to 20mg adds only an extra 6% drop, for a total of 43%.

— Switching from 20mg to 40mg adds only an extra 5% drop, for a total of 48%.

— Switching from 40mg to 80mg adds only an extra 3% drop, for a total of 51%.

To borrow a term from economics, you get decreasing returns to scale.

The moral of the story is that it makes sense to lower your LDL as much as possible with diet, exercise, lifestyle (and perhaps supplements) first. Then handle the rest with the lowest dose statin possible. This maximizes the percentage benefit, and minimizes the chances of side effects.

The exact LDL that you should target depends on your level of risk. The higher your risk, the lower you should consider going.
