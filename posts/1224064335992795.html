Harvard: macronutrient quality, not carb-versus-fat ratio, determines heart disease risk

Louis Nervegna · Feb 13, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsCarbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsLow-carb, keto & carnivore
AI summary & key facts

Harvard Chan study finds low-carb and low-fat diets are tied to lower heart risk when rich in quality plant foods and low in animal products.

  • Quality matters more than quantity
  • Overall healthy eating pattern recommended

In the long term, it’s the quality of the individual macronutrients (fat, carbs, protein) that seems to matter most for heart health. Not the proportions.

Research cited

  • Low-carb and low-fat diet quality and CHD · cohort · Harvard Chan
Shared from Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health · original post

Findings from a new study led by researchers at Harvard Chan School suggest that it’s the quality—not quantity—of macronutrients that makes a difference for heart health, debunking the myth that modulating carbohydrate and fat intake alone is inherently beneficial.

“For clinicians, dietitians, and patients, our study suggests that promoting an overall healthy eating pattern, rather than strict macronutrient restriction, should be a central strategy for the primary prevention of heart disease,” said corresponding author Qi Sun, associate professor in the Departments of Nutrition and Epidemiology.
