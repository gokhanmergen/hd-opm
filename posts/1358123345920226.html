There is a mistaken idea that lactate is bad for you, even in physiological levels.

(For example, Peter Attia has repeatedly made the claim that metformin is bad because it modestly raises lactate levels in healthy people. This is nonsense.)

The truth is that physiological levels of lactate have health benefits.

Exercise is the best way to gain these benefits of lactate. (Metformin is another way.)

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

A groundbreaking Nature Communications study reveals vigorous exercise is four to ten times more potent than moderate activity for reducing all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease deaths, and type 2 diabetes risk.

One minute of vigorous activity equates to four minutes for overall mortality, 7.8 minutes for cardiovascular benefits, and about 9.4 minutes for diabetes prevention.

These dramatic differences arise from lactate signaling that boosts GLUT4 transporters and PGC-1α driven mitochondrial biogenesis.

Distributed brief vigorous bouts throughout the day optimize glucose control beyond single workouts.

While moderate exercise still matters, short intense sessions can deliver outsized health returns when time is limited, hinting at transformative workout strategies described in the full article.
