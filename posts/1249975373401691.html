Red meat tied to lower mortality only in frail underweight over-80s (Chinese case-control)

Louis Nervegna · Mar 20, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 18 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis comments on an AJCN 2026 nested case-control study of Chinese adults 80+: non-meat eaters were less likely to reach 100, but only if underweight and frail. He cautions that case-control studies are low on the evidence hierarchy and may not be causal, and suggests protein and IGF-1 may matter in frail old age.

  • Association seen only in frail, underweight people over 80
  • Fish, egg or dairy eaters were as likely as meat eaters to reach 100
  • Case-control design; causality not established

Some commentary on a recent observational study from China tracking red meat consumption in the elderly:

First, a caveat -
This was a case-control study. These are low on the evidence hierarchy in medicine: ↪ Evidence hierarchy: RCTs and long prospective cohorts sit at the top. None of this may actually be causal. Even the association might not be real.

The study showed that in frail underweight people over 80yo, red meat consumption was associated with a small reduction in mortality. There was no reduction seen in normal weight individuals of the same age.

A similar association with lower mortality was seen in people of the same age who did not consume red meat, but instead consumed fish, eggs, or dairy.

Quoted:

“Avoiding meat might slightly lower the odds of reaching 100 — but only for frail, underweight seniors. In very old age, staying strong and maintaining muscle matters more than long-term disease prevention. Older adults who included fish, eggs, or dairy were just as likely to become centenarians as meat eaters, suggesting that key nutrients may make the difference. The takeaway: nutrition needs change dramatically with age.”

“Non-meat eaters over 80 were less likely to reach 100 — but only if they were underweight. In later life, getting enough protein and key nutrients may matter more than sticking strictly to a plant-based diet.”

What might explain this association?
(assuming it is real)

In old people (e.g. >80yo) who are experiencing frailty and sarcopenia, IGF1 levels often drop excessively low. This contributes to wasting and weakness, which increases mortality. Being chronically underweight at this age is a proxy for this occurring. Red meat is very effective at raising IGF1. So are fish, eggs, and dairy. This is a case where red meat might actually be beneficial. There is some biological plausibility here.

For the rest of us, high IGF1 probably causes more harm than good.

This video clip makes an excellent point:

The diet that is best for preventing disease for the majority of our lives is not necessarily the best diet for keeping us alive when we get very old. In old age, the strategy changes from prevention to palliation.
