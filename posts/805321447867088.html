CAC score may be a simple measure of overall biological aging

Louis Nervegna · May 18, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an article by Dr. Joel Kahn describing a study that proposes the coronary artery calcium score as a broad marker of biological aging, not just heart disease. The quoted authors note CAC is widely available, takes seconds and often costs $100 or less.

  • CAC is quick, needs no injections and often costs $100 or less (as quoted)
  • Authors say CAC may be the most available, cost-effective way to assess aging throughout the body

The CAC scan— which has now become standard in cardiology for measuring arterial calcification— might actually be a good measure of overall “biological age”.

——

“The study results highlight CACS as a simple measure broadly associated with biological aging, with clinical and research implications for estimating the physical and physiological aging trajectory of older individuals.

The CACS is widely available at hospitals and imaging centers, involves no injections, takes just a few seconds, and often costs $100 or less.

These data indicate that the CACS may be the most available and cost-effective method of not only measuring cardiac aging, but assessing aging throughout the body.”

Why a coronary calcium score can rise as plaque stabilizes on statins

Dec 03, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 28 · 💬 47 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Explains that the Agatston score is not true calcium volume: it multiplies area by a stepwise density factor (1-4), so slightly denser plaque can jump a category and raise the score. Statins turn soft lipid-rich plaque into stable calcified plaque, so a rising CAC on treatment can reflect stabilization rather than failure.

Centella asiatica and pine bark extract: small trials on arterial plaque stability

Nov 17, 2023 · Gokhan Can ♥ 8 ↩ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Summarizes small trials of Centella asiatica and French maritime pine bark for arterial plaque, including a 1-year study of 60 patients where soft plaque did not grow compared with a 23% increase on placebo. A 3-year study of over 300 patients reported plaque progression in 5.3% with the combination versus over 20% in controls.
