Shared from Amazing Science Facts · original post

A clinical trial led by the Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, building on Salk Institute research, used a synthetic vitamin D analog called paricalcitol to strip away the protective barrier surrounding metastatic pancreatic tumours, one of the deadliest and most treatment-resistant cancers. In the 36-patient trial, partial tumour destruction responses jumped from just 9 percent with standard chemotherapy to 42 percent when the vitamin D drug was added. The analog reprograms the dense defensive tissue around the tumour to open up, allowing chemotherapy to flood into the cancer cells. Researchers say it offers genuine new hope against a cancer that has resisted progress for decades.
