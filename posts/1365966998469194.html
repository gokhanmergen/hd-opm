UNATI: 10,000-person randomized trial of moderate red wine vs reducing alcohol

Louis Nervegna · Aug 10, 2026 · ♥ 15 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

UNATI, led by Martinez-Gonzalez, will randomize about 10,000 drinkers (men 50-70, women 55-75, 3-40 drinks per week) to reduce alcohol or drink moderately (under 7/week women, under 14/week men, red wine with meals) for about 4 years. Outcomes include mortality, cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and dementia.

  • ~10,000 participants
  • Group 1 reduce; Group 2 moderate (<7 drinks/wk women, <14 men)
  • Outcomes: mortality, CVD, cancer, cirrhosis, diabetes, dementia

UNATI - The first large long-term randomized trial to test how mild-to-moderate alcohol consumption (red wine) affects risk for major diseases like heart disease, diabetes, dementia, and cancer:

Quoted from the website:

Today, there is much confusion about the health effects of alcohol consumption. While some recommendations state that "there is no safe level of consumption," other programs of study suggest that moderate drinking may even be associated with lower mortality compared to no drinking at all.

However, many of these programs of study have limitations, the most important of which is that they are not randomized trials. This may influence their results and create confusion about whether [moderate] drinking is healthy.

To answer this controversy more clearly, the UNATI study (University of Navarra Alumni Trialist Initiative), a randomized clinicalessay that will follow for 4 years about 10,000 people (men aged 50 to 70 and women aged 55 to 75) who drink between 3 and 40 drinks per week.

Participants will be randomly divided into two groups. Throughout those 4 years, they will receive annual medical check-ups and continuous counseling by a health professional (4 times a year) according to one of these guidelines:

Group 1: Reduce alcohol consumption.

Group 2: Consume in moderation: less than 7 drinks per week in women and less than 14 in men, avoiding excesses, prioritizing red wine, only with meals and spread throughout the week.

Objective

To assess how alcohol consumption influences the development of a range of serious health problems, such as mortality, cardiovascular disease, cancer, cirrhosis, subject 2 diabetes, depression, dementia, serious injuries or infections requiring hospitalization.

This study could provide for the first time a clear answer based on solid scientific evidence to a core topic for public health: what is really the impact of moderate alcohol consumption?
