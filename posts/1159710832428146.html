FOURIER stroke analysis: LDL below 40 mg/dL cut major events ~31% and recurrent stroke 27%

Gokhan Can · Nov 13, 2025 · ♥ 26 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Summarises a Circulation analysis of 5,200+ patients with prior ischemic stroke from FOURIER and its extension: lower achieved LDL-C meant fewer events. Versus LDL >=70 mg/dL, below 40 mg/dL had ~31% fewer major CV events and 27% fewer recurrent strokes, and even <20 mg/dL showed no excess hemorrhagic stroke.

  • 5,200+ patients with prior ischemic stroke
  • LDL <40 vs >=70 mg/dL: ~31% fewer MACE, 27% fewer recurrent strokes
  • No increase in hemorrhagic stroke, even <20 mg/dL

A recent analysis of over 5,200 patients with a prior ischemic stroke found that achieving very low LDL-cholesterol levels significantly reduces the risk of future cardiovascular events and recurrent strokes. The study, which analyzed data from the FOURIER trial and its extension, demonstrated a consistent trend: the lower the LDL-C, the lower the incidence of adverse events. Compared to patients with an LDL-C of 70 mg/dL or higher, those who achieved levels below 40 mg/dL had an approximately 31% lower rate of major cardiovascular events and a 27% lower rate of recurrent stroke. Published in Circulation, the research also noted that these very low LDL levels (even below 20 mg/dL) were not associated with an increased risk of hemorrhagic stroke, supporting the potential benefit of more intensive cholesterol-lowering therapies in this high-risk population.
