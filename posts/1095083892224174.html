AI summary & key facts

Louis compares the Keto-CTA study of lean metabolically healthy keto dieters with very high LDL against the Nature-CT group (standard American diet, normal LDL), using the same scanners, Cleerly software, age (55) and principal investigator. Plaque progression was 3.8x worse in the keto group, which he argues undermines the claim that metabolic health protects against high LDL.

  • Plaque progression 3.8x worse in Keto-CTA than Nature-CT
  • Both groups age 55 on average, same CT scanners, Cleerly software and PI (Budoff)

The Keto-CTA (LMHR) study showed plaque progression 3.8x worse than in a comparable healthy group eating the standard American diet with normal LDL:

facebook.com/share/v/1FJtCmQdfK

↪ Keto-CTA: metabolic health and large LDL particles gave no protection from plaque

The exact same CT-scanners were used in both groups. And the exact same Cleerly software was used for analysis in both groups. The average age in both groups was the same (55yo), and the principal investigator in both trials was the same (Mathew Budoff).

It was an apples to apples comparison.

If you are going to re-analyze one study with different software from Cleerly, then you need to re-analyze the other one as well— so you can directly compare them.

Hypothetically: If the results improve by 50% in both studies after using new software, then the ratio of Keto-CTA being 3.8x worse than Nature-CT will not change. It would still show that a carnivore-style keto diet with very high LDL is 3.8x worse than the standard American diet with normal LDL, in terms of plaque progression.

Keto-CTA hyper-responders progressed soft plaque 3.8x faster than Nature-CT controls

Apr 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 1 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis compares Keto-CTA (keto diet, LMHR) with the Nature-CT study, both from Budoff's UCLA group using CCTA and Cleerly software, with similar age (~55), no diabetes and no statins. After one year non-calcified plaque volume rose 18.8 mm3 in LMHR versus 4.9 mm3 in the Nature-CT standard-diet group. Budoff reportedly agrees it is a good comparison and plans a paper comparing them.
