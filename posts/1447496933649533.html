Over 100 randomized trials show polyunsaturated seed-oil fats improve insulin sensitivity

Louis Nervegna · Nov 11, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis states that over 100 human randomized trials in aggregate show polyunsaturated fats improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control. The shared FoundMyFitness post adds that these omega-6 fats can lower LDL and have not been shown to raise inflammation in trials, but repeatedly reheated oil forms harmful oxidation products.

  • Over 100 RCTs show PUFA improves insulin sensitivity and glycemic control
  • Omega-6 trials also show lower LDL
  • No increase in inflammation in randomized human trials
  • Repeatedly heated or reused oil creates harmful oxidation products (also applies to beef tallow)

Most people do not realize that there are over 100 randomized controlled trials in humans which show in aggregate that polyunsaturated fats (from seed oils) improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control.

Seed oils don’t deserve their bad reputation

Dozens of randomized controlled trials show that their omega-6 fats can improve insulin sensitivity, blood sugar control, and lower LDL cholesterol

Randomized human trials haven’t shown these fats to increase inflammation, and some evidence suggests they may help reduce it

But how the oil is used matters: repeatedly burning and reusing it can create harmful oxidation products, a problem that also applies to beef tallow

Layne Norton rebuts seed-oil claims with human evidence on inflammation, LDL and oxidation

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.

Louis summarizes a meta-analysis of randomized feeding trials showing polyunsaturated fat, mostly omega-6 from vegetable oils, lowers fasting glucose and A1C and improves insulin resistance and insulin secretion. Monounsaturated fat did well but less so, and saturated fat showed no appreciable benefit.
