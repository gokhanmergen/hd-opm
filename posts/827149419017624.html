Oral PCSK9 inhibitor AZD0780 cut LDL at least 30% in phase 1, 78% with rosuvastatin

Gokhan Can · Jun 28, 2024 · ♥ 17 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A phase 1 trial presented at EAS 2024 found AZD0780, the first oral small-molecule PCSK9 inhibitor, cut LDL by at least 30% in 35 participants, and a subanalysis suggested a 78% reduction with rosuvastatin. Specialists question the early data.

  • 35 participants with elevated LDL
  • At least 30% LDL reduction
  • 78% LDL reduction combined with rosuvastatin (subanalysis)

A preliminary phase 1 trial of AZD0780, the first oral small molecule PCSK9 inhibitor, has shown promising results in lowering LDL cholesterol levels. The study, presented at the European Atherosclerosis Society (EAS) 2024 conference, found that AZD0780 reduced LDL cholesterol by at least 30% in 35 participants with elevated LDL. A subanalysis within the trial suggested a potential synergistic effect when AZD0780 was combined with the statin rosuvastatin, resulting in a 78% reduction in LDL cholesterol levels. Rick Vega, Director of Research at AstraZeneca, stated that these findings indicate a fixed-dose combination of AZD0780 with a statin could be an attractive first-line treatment option.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).
