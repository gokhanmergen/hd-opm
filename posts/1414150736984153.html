About one cup of blueberries a day: best human evidence is for vascular function

Louis Nervegna · Oct 06, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsBlood vessels, circulation & endotheliumBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

A podcast excerpt says one cup (150-180 g) of fresh or frozen blueberries daily matches the dose in most positive human studies. The clearest benefit is better endothelial function, with possible memory benefits in at-risk older adults and glucose benefits when metabolism is strained. Randomized trials have not shown they prevent dementia, diabetes, heart attacks or strokes.

  • Dose: about 1 cup (150-180 g) fresh or frozen blueberries per day
  • Clearest benefit: improved endothelial function
  • Selected memory benefits in older adults with cognitive vulnerability
  • No randomized trial proof of preventing dementia, diabetes, heart attack or stroke

Quoted:

About one cup of whole blueberries per day is the dose that overlaps most consistently with the positive human evidence.

The clearest benefit is vascular. Blueberries improve endothelial function, the ability of arteries to relax and deliver more blood when tissues need it.

Studies also suggest selected memory benefits in older adults with cognitive vulnerability. Improvements in insulin sensitivity or post-meal glucose handling appear more likely when metabolism is already under strain. Findings for gut health and exercise recovery are promising but less consistent.

Randomized trials have not shown that blueberries prevent dementia, diabetes, heart attacks, or strokes. But one cup (roughly 150 to 180 grams) of fresh or frozen blueberries per day is a practical, evidence-based target with benefits extending well beyond a simple “antioxidant” effect.

Daily multivitamin slowed cognitive aging by about two years in a COSMOS meta-analysis

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 10 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a summary of COSMOS cognition results: in the main analysis of 573 adults 60+, a daily multivitamin modestly improved overall cognition and episodic memory over two years. Pooling with two earlier COSMOS cognition studies (more than 5,000 older adults) gave clear benefits, estimated at about two years of slower cognitive aging. Effects were modest, it is unclear which nutrient matters, dementia prevention was not tested, and the manufacturer donated tablets. He adds that this was an RDA-level multivitamin (Centrum Silver).
