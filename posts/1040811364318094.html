With a CAC score of zero, high LDL still deserves treatment over a 30-40 year horizon

Gokhan Can · Jun 05, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Q&A: a zero CAC is good news but only a current snapshot. LDL drives plaque over decades, and waiting for calcium to appear means significant plaque has already formed, so proactive LDL lowering still protects against future events.

  • LDL management gives long-term (30-40 year) protection that CAC zero alone does not

Question:

I'm a middle-aged adult with a CAC score of zero, should I still be concerned about high LDL cholesterol?

Best answer:

A zero CAC score is good news for middle-aged adults, indicating no detectable calcified plaque. However, this is just a current snapshot and shouldn't be the sole basis for your long-term heart health strategy.

LDL cholesterol is a primary driver of plaque buildup in arteries over many years. Therefore, managing elevated LDL offers better long-term protection against cardiovascular disease (over the next 30-40 years) than relying on a zero CAC score alone.

Waiting for calcium to appear on a CAC scan means significant plaque has already formed. A proactive approach to lower high LDL, even with a current CAC score of zero, provides the best chance to prevent future heart attacks and strokes.

The key insight is that CAC scoring captures established disease, while LDL management prevents disease from developing in the first place.

About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.
