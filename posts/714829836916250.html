Evolving LDL-C management (JAHA 2023): risk categories, thresholds and treatment goals

Gokhan Can · Nov 25, 2023 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Summarizes a 2023 JAHA review of personalized LDL-C management, giving risk categories from low to very high and the LDL goals for each. High-risk patients should reduce LDL at least 50% to below 70 mg/dL, while very-high-risk patients should aim below 55 mg/dL, or below 40 after recurrent events on statins.

  • High risk: 10-year ASCVD risk of 20% or more; at least 50% LDL-C reduction, goal below 70 mg/dL
  • Very high risk: at least 50% reduction, goal below 55 mg/dL, or below 40 mg/dL for recurrent events on statins
  • Borderline/intermediate risk is 5-20% 10-year ASCVD risk

Evolving Management of Low‐Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (2023)

(The linked document is an excellent overview of current best practices. Please read the relevant sections to understand what is optimal in your specific situation. Sharing a summary down below.)

Risk Categories:

  • Low risk: 10-year ASCVD risk <5%
  • Borderline/Intermediate risk: 10-year ASCVD risk 5-20%
  • High risk: 10-year ASCVD risk ≥20% or other high-risk conditions
  • Very high risk: established ASCVD, diabetes with target organ damage, severe CKD, or unequivocal ASCVD on imaging

LDL-C Treatment Thresholds/Goals:

  • High risk: ≥50% LDL-C reduction from baseline; goal <70 mg/dL
  • Very high risk: ≥50% LDL-C reduction from baseline; goal <55 mg/dL or <40 mg/dL for recurrent events on statins

Treatment Recommendations:

  • Statins first-line for both high and very high risk
  • Consider addition of ezetimibe and/or PCSK9 inhibitor in very high risk if ≥50% LDL-C reduction or <55-70 mg/dL goal not achieved on max tolerated statins
  • Consider newer agents like bempedoic acid or inclisiran for high/very high risk if additional LDL-C lowering needed

The guidelines emphasize early risk assessment and treatment to prevent first and recurrent ASCVD events across a continuum of risk. Combination therapy is key for high risk patients needing significant LDL-C lowering.

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way.
