Shared from Dr Erickson · original post

Glaucoma damages retinal ganglion cells—the neurons that carry visual information from the eye into the brain through the optic nerve.

In a randomized Phase 2 trial, Columbia University researchers tested high-dose nicotinamide, a form of vitamin B3, together with pyruvate in patients receiving treatment for open-angle glaucoma. After approximately two months, the supplement group showed significantly more improving locations during visual-field testing than the placebo group.

The findings suggest that supporting cellular energy metabolism may temporarily enhance the function of stressed retinal ganglion cells. However, the study did not demonstrate complete optic-nerve restoration or prove that the supplements prevent long-term glaucoma progression.

Only 32 participants completed this small trial, and the doses were considerably higher than those found in ordinary supplements. Patients should not attempt this combination without guidance from an ophthalmologist, especially because it does not replace prescribed pressure-lowering treatment.

Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.
