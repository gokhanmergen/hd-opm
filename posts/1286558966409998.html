Adventist cohort: 1-3 eggs a month tied to 17% lower Alzheimer's risk, 5+ a week 27%

Louis Nervegna · May 08, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares news of the new Adventist Health Study finding that regular egg eating in people 65+ was associated with lower Alzheimer's risk, and notes the phosphatidylcholine in eggs does not raise TMAO. It adds to earlier studies with similar signals.

  • 1-3 eggs/month: 17% lower risk
  • 5+ eggs/week: 27% lower risk

Eggs might protect against Alzheimer’s.

This is the latest finding from the Adventists Health Study, in Loma Linda California.

ScienceDailyEating eggs could cut Alzheimer’s risk by 27%Eating eggs might do more than just start your day—it could help protect your brain. Researchers found that people 65 and older who eat eggs regularly have a significantly lower risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease, with daily or near-daily consumption linked to up to a 27% reduction. Even modest egg intake showed benefits, suggesting that small dietary…sciencedaily.com

These results were just published.

It didn’t take much to see a benefit. Eating just 1-3 eggs per month was associated with a 17% reduction in risk. Eating 5 or more eggs per week was associated with a 27% reduction.

This is not the first study to link egg consumption with lower risk of Alzheimer’s. See these previous posts:

Note that the form of choline in eggs (phosphatidylcholine) does not raise levels of TMAO:

↪ Eggs and phosphatidylcholine did not raise TMAO in an RCT, unlike choline bitartrate

Quoted:

The study found that people who ate at least one egg for five or more days each week had up to a 27% lower risk of being diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease.

"Compared to never eating eggs, eating at least five eggs per week can decrease risk of Alzheimer's," said Joan Sabaté, MD, DrPH, a professor at Loma Linda University School of Public Health and the study's principal investigator.

Even smaller amounts of egg consumption were associated with benefits. Eating eggs just 1 to 3 times per month was linked to a 17% reduction in risk, while those who ate eggs 2 to 4 times per week saw about a 20% lower risk, Sabaté said.

Eggs contain several nutrients that are important for the brain, Sabaté said. They are a rich source of choline, which the body uses to produce compounds such as acetylcholine and phosphatidylcholine that are essential for memory and communication between brain cells.

Eggs also provide lutein and zeaxanthin, which are carotenoids that build up in brain tissue and have been linked to better cognitive performance and lower levels of oxidative stress. In addition, eggs contain omega-3 fatty acids, and the yolk is especially high in phospholipids, making up nearly 30% of total egg lipids. These compounds play a key role in how neurotransmitter receptors function.

“Research supports eggs as part of a healthy diet," said Jisoo Oh, DrPH, MPH, an associate professor of epidemiology at Loma Linda University School of Public Health and the study's lead author. "Seventh-day Adventists do eat a healthier diet than the general public, and we want people to focus on overall health along with this knowledge about the benefit of eggs."

Adventist Health Study-2: eggs tied to 17-27% lower Alzheimer's incidence in 39,498 adults

May 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 3 Meat, eggs & dairy

A summary of a Loma Linda study following 39,498 adults aged 65+ for 15.3 years, linking Adventist Health Study-2 diet records to Medicare diagnoses. 2,858 developed Alzheimer's; eggs 1-3 times per month, 2-4 per week and 5+ per week were associated with 17%, 20% and 27% lower incidence versus never-eaters.

Adventist Health Study: nuts and beans protected against dementia as much as eggs

Adventist study: vegans 85+ had higher neurological death risk than non-vegetarians

Aug 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 8 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Among AHS-2 participants (about 88,000, over 12,000 deaths), vegans aged 85 and older had higher risk of death from stroke (17%), dementia (13%) and Parkinson's (37%) than healthy non-vegetarians, while pesco-vegetarians had the lowest risk. The comparison was against very healthy non-vegetarians, not the standard American diet.

Eating eggs weekly linked to lower Alzheimer's dementia risk in older adults

Rush study: more than 1 egg per week linked to 47% lower Alzheimer's dementia risk

Mar 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 13 ↩ 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis shares a summary of a 6.7-year Journal of Nutrition study of 1,024 older adults (about 81 years) finding that eating more than one egg per week was associated with 47% lower Alzheimer's dementia risk, with choline explaining 39% of the effect. He notes the egg choline form (phosphatidylcholine) does not raise TMAO. The study is observational with self-reported diet.
