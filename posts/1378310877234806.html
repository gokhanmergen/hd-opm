100 mg vitamin B6 reduced depression symptoms in a new randomized trial; B12 failed

AI summary & key facts

Louis notes a new RCT reports that 100 mg vitamin B6 significantly reduced depression symptoms while B12 did not. The same authors found in 2022 that B6 reduces anxiety. He notes depression and anxiety are CVD risk factors and warns of nerve toxicity with prolonged high doses.

  • 100 mg B6 reduced depression symptoms; B12 had no effect
  • 2022 trial by same authors: B6 reduced anxiety
  • Warning: prolonged high doses can cause peripheral neuropathy

A newly published randomized trial reports that 100mg of vitamin B6 significantly reduces symptoms of depression.

Vitamin B12 was also tested in the trial, but failed.

A previously published randomized trial in 2022 by the same authors showed that 100mg of vitamin B6 also reduces symptoms of anxiety.

Note that depression and anxiety are major risk factors for CVD.

Warning:

There can be side effects to high doses of B6 taken for prolonged periods of time. The most serious being peripheral neuropathy. For most people, this would not occur at a dose of 100mg, but it could for some. There were no side effects reported in this trial. However, the trial was short in duration.

Quoted:

A daily high-dose Vitamin B6 supplement could significantly reduce symptoms of depression in people who already experience them, new research from the University of Reading has found.

The double-blind randomised trial, published this month in the journal Depression and Anxiety, asked participants to take a 100mg Vitamin B6 tablet every day for a month. Those who reported mild or more severe depression at the start showed a reduction in symptoms compared to those given a placebo or Vitamin B12.

The findings are the first from a randomised controlled trial to demonstrate the potential of Vitamin B6 as a treatment for depression, and build on a substantial body of epidemiological evidence linking Vitamin B6 status with depression risk.

Dr David Field, lead author from the School of Psychology and Clinical Language Sciences at the University of Reading, said: "Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions in the world, but many patients choose not to take existing drug treatments either due to the side effects or because they find them ineffective.”

“Vitamin B6 plays a crucial role in the production of several chemical messengers in the brain, including serotonin and GABA, both of which are central to mood regulation. An increase in these neurotransmitters is the most likely reason why we found that taking a regular high dose of B6 for a month produced a significant reduction in depression symptoms in people who were experiencing them.”

"This is a promising result that we believe justifies large-scale clinical trials to properly evaluate Vitamin B6 as a treatment for depression, either on its own or alongside existing therapies. While no side effects were reported by participants in this study, any trials would also monitor any potential side effects.”

The study follows on from research published in 2022, also led by Dr Field, which found that high-dose Vitamin B6 reduced anxiety symptoms. In this latest study, a total of 268 participants were randomly assigned to receive either Vitamin B6, Vitamin B12 or a placebo for a month. Participants were divided into those who showed mild or more severe symptoms of depression, anxiety or stress at the start of the study, and those who did not.

Those with depression symptoms at the start showed a large reduction compared to the other two groups. The effect was also seen, to a lesser degree, in participants whose scores fell just below the clinical threshold for mild depression.
