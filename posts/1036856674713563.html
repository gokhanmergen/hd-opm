  • Loyal claims lowering IGF1 extends dog lifespan

Genetic interventions to lower IGF1 (or its equivalent) extend lifespan in most animal species tested. The pet longevity company Loyal is claiming to have evidence that lowering IGF1 in adulthood extends lifespan in dogs as well. IGF1 directly drives mTOR. Rapamycin works in part by lowering IGF1 signaling.

facebook.com/share/r/1CfJ8KnCDg

facebook.com/share/p/1CyEUpEhbW

The longest lab mouse ever to live was a transgenic mouse with silenced IGF1. It lived twice as long as controls.

facebook.com/share/p/1EDgYHBt1R

A similar mutation occurs naturally in humans producing a disease called Laron syndrome. These people have very low IGF1 from birth, just like the mice. They almost never develop cancer or diabetes, and rates of heart disease and Alzheimer’s are low. No one with Laron Syndrome has ever been documented to have died from cancer.

In Nir Barzilai’s studies of centenarians, the factor that seems to be most responsible for their longevity is genes that keep IGF1 low throughout their lives.
