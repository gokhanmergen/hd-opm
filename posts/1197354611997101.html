Dropping numeric alcohol limits from US dietary guidelines is a mistake, argues Louis

Louis Nervegna · Jan 08, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis criticizes the new US guidelines for saying only 'limit alcoholic beverages' instead of the previous 1 drink/day for women and 2 for men. He explains the old limits were set where non-cardiovascular risks, especially cancer, begin to rise significantly.

  • Previous limits: 1 drink/day (women), 2 drinks/day (men)
  • Breast cancer risk rises significantly beyond one drink/day for women
  • New guidelines: 'limit alcoholic beverages' without numbers

I think this change is very bad.

The hard limits set on daily alcohol consumption in the previous dietary guidelines were chosen carefully for very specific reasons.

They were set at 1 serving/day for women, and 2 servings/day for men.

Removing these limits is a bad idea. This gives people the impression that it’s ok to consume more.

These were chosen because they are the levels at which risk for non-cardiovascular related diseases begins to rise significantly, particularly cancer:

The Nutrition Source - Harvard Chan SchoolAlcohol • The Nutrition SourceThroughout the 10,000 or so years that humans have been drinking fermented beverages, they’ve also been arguing about their merits and demerits. The debatenutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu

For example, breast cancer risk in women begins to rise significantly beyond one serving of alcohol per day. That’s specifically why this limit was chosen for women.

While there might be cardiovascular benefits to mild-to-moderate alcohol consumption, exceeding these carefully chosen limits probably cancels-out any possible benefits with increased risk from other diseases.

You can read the latest scientific statement from the American Heart Association on alcohol use and CVD here: ↪ AHA statement: low alcohol shows no risk to possible benefit, heavy drinking harms.

Consuming large amounts of alcohol is unequivocally harmful.

New federal dietary guidelines announced by the Trump administration on Wednesday include a dramatic change on alcohol consumption.

The new Dietary Guidelines instruct Americans to "limit alcoholic beverages," a change from previous guidance that suggested U.S. adults ages 21 and over limit consumption to two drinks or fewer per day for men and one drink or less per day for women.
