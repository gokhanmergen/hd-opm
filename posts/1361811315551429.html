Shared from Keck School of Medicine of USC · original post

A hormone made in a small organ behind your breastbone could hold the key to helping older adults respond better to cancer treatment.

Keck School of Medicine of USC researchers have received a five-year, $3.2 million grant from the National Institutes of Health to study thymulin, a hormone produced by the thymus that keeps chronic inflammation, sometimes called "inflammaging," under control as we age.

The team, led by Fumito Ito, MD, PhD, previously found that thymulin reduced inflammation and improved survival in older mice with cancer. Now they will test whether it can strengthen immunotherapies like anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 treatments and help slow metastatic cancer, with the goal of developing therapies that work better for the older patients who make up most cancer cases.
