Eight lifestyle factors linked to 87% lower mortality in 276,132 veterans

Louis reshares a post summarizing a Harvard and VA study of 276,132 people tracking eight lifestyle factors. Each added factor reduced mortality about 19%, and having all eight versus none was linked to 87% lower mortality, with a linear dose-response.

  • 8 factors: physical activity, never smoking, stress management, no opioid use, healthy nutrition, alcohol limits, restorative sleep, social connection

Here’s the caption:

What if the most powerful medicine available didn’t come in a pill?
A Harvard and VA study of 276,132 people tracked 8 lifestyle factors — physical activity, never smoking, stress management, no opioid use, healthy nutrition, alcohol limits, restorative sleep, and social connection — and followed them for over a million person-years.
The findings are extraordinary.
Each additional lifestyle factor reduced mortality risk by roughly 19%. Adopting all 8 compared with none was associated with an 87% lower risk of death. In terms of life expectancy, that translated to 24 extra years for men and 20.5 extra years for women — at age 40.
And the dose-response was linear. Every single factor added benefit. There was no threshold, no point of diminishing returns. The more you adopt, the more years you gain.
When they looked at which factors drove the most preventable deaths at a population level, three stood out: physical inactivity (responsible for 39.9% of attributable deaths), ever smoking (23.8%), and poor nutrition (13.8%). Together, these three accounted for the majority of preventable mortality in the entire cohort.
This doesn’t mean you need to overhaul everything at once. The data is clear — start with one. Then another. The benefit accumulates with every change you make.
