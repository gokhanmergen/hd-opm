Low LDL linked to old-age mortality because of reverse causality, not harm from low LDL

Louis Nervegna · Nov 02, 2024 · ♥ 19 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a short video clip explaining that diseases of old age cause metabolic dysregulation that lowers LDL as a side effect. This reverse causality creates the apparent link between low LDL and higher mortality in the elderly. The low LDL does not cause the disease.

  • Illness lowers LDL, creating a spurious association with mortality
  • Described as repeatedly studied and well understood

In less than a minute, he correctly explains the connection between low LDL cholesterol and higher mortality in old age.

This has been repeatedly studied, and it is now well understood: Diseases in old age that kill people cause metabolic disregulation which lowers LDL as a side effect. This is called reverse causality. The low LDL does not cause the disease— it’s the reverse— the disease causes the low LDL. Reverse causality creates an association between low LDL and higher mortality in old age.
