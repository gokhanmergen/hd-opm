Excess visceral fat can cause fatigue, brain fog and cravings

Louis Nervegna · Jul 17, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an interview clip explaining how visceral fat releases inflammatory molecules that keep the immune system activated, which is energy-expensive. He argues diets feel better when they reduce visceral fat.

  • High visceral fat linked to about 44% higher cancer risk
  • Roughly double the risk of dying early
  • Shrinks with calorie deficit, HIIT, sleep, stress control

A nice explanation of how too much visceral fat can cause “mystery symptoms” like fatigue, brain fog, energy crashes, and cravings.

So many people suffer from these symptoms, without realizing that they can be directly caused by the excess fat that they are carrying around in their abdomens.

This is why people can go on radically different diets, yet still end up feeling much better… The common denominator is the loss of visceral fat.

For example, a carnivore diet and a vegan are the polar opposite of one another, but they can both make you feel better if you lose a significant amount of weight. The long term effects might be very different, but in the short term, any diet that leads to significant loss of visceral fat can make you feel better.

The important thing is to find a healthy diet that works for you— a diet that allows you to keep your visceral fat low. If you need medication to help with keeping the weight off, that’s ok too. Everybody is different.

Visceral fat may be one of the most underrated contributors to chronic fatigue, brain fog, and cravings

This deep fat sits around organs like the liver and releases inflammatory molecules that keep the immune system activated

That immune activation is energy-expensive, which can show up as fatigue, brain fog, energy crashes, and cravings

High visceral fat is also linked to ~44% higher cancer risk and roughly double the risk of dying early

The good news: it's often one of the first fat stores to shrink with a calorie deficit, high-intensity exercise, better sleep, and stress control
