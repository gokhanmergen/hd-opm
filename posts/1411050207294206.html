High vitamin B12 in a multivitamin did not raise disease risk in a 14-year Harvard trial

Louis Nervegna · Oct 02, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCancer
AI summary & key facts

The poster argues it is unlikely high B12 intake causes cancer or clonal disease. The Physicians' Health Study multivitamin trial, which contained 10 times the RDA of B12, found no increase in major disease over 14 years and an 8% lower overall cancer risk. Nurses' and Physicians' cohorts also found no B12-disease link, except combined B12 and B6 with hip fracture, which vanished for B12 alone.

  • Physicians' Health Study RCT: over 14,600 participants, 14 years, multivitamin with 10x RDA of B12
  • 8% lower overall cancer risk; no increased risk for any major disease
  • Harvard cohorts: B12 intake not associated with disease; B12 plus B6 and hip fracture signal disappeared for B12 alone

Does high vitamin B12 intake causally increase risk for cancer or other related diseases (e.g. clonal diseases) ?

I think this is unlikely.

We have a large rigorously done 14-year-long randomized trial that tested multivitamin supplementation as part of the Harvard Physicians Health Study. The multivitamin contained 10x the RDA of vitamin B12.

The other components in the multivitamin were a lot closer to the RDA. Only the B12 was 10x. (See comments for a breakdown of what else was in the multivitamin.)

Not only was there no increase in risk for any major disease over the 14 year period, the multivitamin with 10x RDA of B12 actually reduced overall cancer risk by 8%.

In addition to the 14 year randomized trial, they looked at B12 intake pretty carefully in both the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicians Followup Study. They were unable to associate it with any disease. The only possible exception was combined B12 and B6 being associated with higher risk of hip fracture— but that disappeared when looking at B12 alone.

A very large long-running RCT at Harvard Medical School showed a solid 8% reduction in cancer risk from a basic RDA level multivitamin.

Paper · JAMAJAMA articlejamanetwork.com

This clinical trial involved over 14,600 people and ran for 14 years. The participants were all well-off doctors in the Harvard medical system in the Boston area, so they were extremely well nourished and unlikely deficient in anything major. Yet there was still an 8% reduction in cancer.

This is about as high quality of an RCT as you are ever going to find in medicine.

In light of this trial, the claim that low dose multivitamins increase risk for cancer is patently ridiculous.

The exact ingredients used in the multivitamin can be found in this post: ↪ COSMOS multivitamin matches Centrum-type RDA doses except B12 at 10x the RDA. All the ingredients were close to the RDA, except for vitamin B12 which was 10x the RDA.
