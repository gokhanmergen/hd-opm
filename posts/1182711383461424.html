Long-distance running may raise risk of advanced colon adenomas in a small ASCO 2025 study

Louis Nervegna · Dec 17, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Medscape report of a small prospective study presented at ASCO 2025 suggesting intensive long-distance running may be a risk for advanced colon adenomas. He uses it to argue that intense exercise should be viewed like any intervention, with proven benefits and possible risks.

  • Small prospective study presented at ASCO 2025
  • Intensive long-distance running linked to advanced adenomas of the colon
  • Authors call for refined screening in runners

While studies have consistently shown that long distance runners have lower all cause mortality, there may be downsides with regards to specific diseases.

We need to start thinking of intense exercise just like any other intervention. It has undeniable proven benefits, but may also have some risks. It is important to understand what those risks might be, and not pretend that they do not exist.

Research cited

  • Long-distance running and advanced colon adenomas (ASCO 2025) · cohort · 2025
Shared from Medscape · original post

Intensive long-distance running could be a risk for advanced adenomas (AAs) of the colon, a small prospective study reported this summer at the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) 2025.

Refined screening strategies for this running population are therefore warranted, and pathologic and epidemiologic evaluations should explore causation and ancillary risk factors in this unique population, according to Timothy L. Cannon, MD, oncologist at Inova Schar Cancer Institute in Fairfax, Virginia, and colleagues. Marathon Runners May Have Higher Colon Cancer Risk (Medscape)
