Soybean oil 30 g/day lowered arachidonic acid in a 2026 trial; 30+ RCTs: not inflammatory

Louis Nervegna · Oct 22, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Inflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis agrees with a post saying seed oils are not inflammatory, noting over 30 human randomized trials show no inflammation increase and some show anti-inflammatory effects. The shared summary describes a 2026 trial of 10 adults on 30 g soybean oil daily for four weeks, where red blood cell arachidonic acid fell significantly and IL-6 trended down.

  • 10 adults, 30 g soybean oil daily for 4 weeks
  • Arachidonic acid decreased significantly; IL-6 trended down
  • Linoleic acid to arachidonic acid conversion about 0.2%

He is right about this.

Over 30 randomized trials in humans have shown that seed oils are not inflammatory: ↪ 30 RCTs show higher linoleic acid intake does not raise inflammatory markers.

Seed oils are quietly inflaming your arteries, the story goes.

A 2026 trial fed 10 adults 30g of soybean oil a day for four weeks.

Red blood cell arachidonic acid, the thing linoleic acid is supposed to spike, didn't rise.

It dropped. Significantly.

IL-6 trended down too.

The conversion rate from linoleic acid to arachidonic acid in your body is about 0.2 percent.

That's not a metabolic pathway to disease. That's a rounding error with a marketing budget.
