AHA PREVENT calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equations for ages 30-79

Jan 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 21 ↩ 12 Risk assessment & lab tests

The AHA updated its cardiovascular risk calculator, PREVENT, for adults aged 30-79 without known CVD. It uses newer, larger and more diverse data than the 2013 Pooled Cohort Equations and includes kidney and metabolic disease, giving 10- and 30-year risk of both ASCVD and heart failure. Louis warns it will underestimate risk in people with prior heart attack/stroke, significant atherosclerosis or a high calcium score.

Adding CAC to the PREVENT risk calculator only modestly improves accuracy

Aug 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains that adding CAC score to the AHA PREVENT-ASCVD equations improves prediction only modestly in US adults 45-79, suggesting standard risk factors already capture much of what CAC reflects. He notes that a very high CAC should still be treated as high risk.

Adding Lp(a) does not improve PREVENT 10-year risk prediction, but personalizes risk

Aug 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 6 Lp(a)

Louis quotes a JAMA Cardiology piece noting many biomarkers are associated with ASCVD but add little predictive accuracy to risk equations. The editorial adds that measuring Lp(a) can still personalize risk because it is a causal factor.

PREVENT 10-year risk estimates also predict subclinical atherosclerosis (JAHA)

Jun 13, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 3 Risk assessment & lab tests

Louis points to the AHA PREVENT calculator, shared from Luigi Fontana's summary of a JAHA study. The PREVENT 10-year risk estimates predict not only heart attack and stroke but also subclinical atherosclerosis seen on imaging, helping flag silent disease early. PREVENT applies to adults aged 30 to 79.

JACC PREVENT analysis: nearly 3 in 10 US adults have CVD or elevated 10-year risk

Jun 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Risk assessment & lab tests

A shared summary of a JACC study applying the AHA PREVENT equations to US adults aged 30 to 79, showing high burden of existing or predicted cardiovascular disease, higher risk in men, Black and Hispanic adults, and an elevated 30-year risk in about two-thirds of middle-aged adults without CVD.
