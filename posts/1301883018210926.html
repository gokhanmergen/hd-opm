These recent results are from the Adventists Health Study. This is now the fifth large prospective cohort study to associate egg consumption with lower risk of dementia.

But here is what the headlines missed:

In this same study, nuts and beans were associated with just as large a protective effect as eggs!

My takeaway from this: If you like eggs, substitute them in place of less healthy foods like red and processed meat. Not in place of healthy foods like nuts and beans… or you may lose any possible benefit.

Eggs don’t protect your brain. Your overall diet does.

A 15-year study of nearly 40,000 older adults found that people who ate eggs regularly had up to 27% lower Alzheimer’s risk.

Headlines ran with it. But the same study showed that swapping eggs for nuts or beans gave the same result: roughly 12 almonds or a third of a cup of lentils matched the protection.

That’s a problem for the “it’s the choline” story. A large egg has ~150 mg of choline. Those nuts and beans? Almost none. If choline were doing the work, foods without it couldn’t replicate the outcome.

The real signal isn’t one food, it’s the whole pattern: vegetables, berries, beans, whole grains, nuts, olive oil, omega-3s. That’s what the strongest evidence supports.

Adventist study: vegans 85+ had higher neurological death risk than non-vegetarians

Aug 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 8 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Among AHS-2 participants (about 88,000, over 12,000 deaths), vegans aged 85 and older had higher risk of death from stroke (17%), dementia (13%) and Parkinson's (37%) than healthy non-vegetarians, while pesco-vegetarians had the lowest risk. The comparison was against very healthy non-vegetarians, not the standard American diet.
