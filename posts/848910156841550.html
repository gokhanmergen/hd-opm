Botanical stem-cell mobilizer reportedly normalized heart function in 10 HF patients

Louis Nervegna · Aug 08, 2024 · ♥ 10 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post
Heart rhythm & heart failureSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

The poster describes a small trial of a botanical blend said to stimulate bone marrow to release stem cells, with an interview claim that all 10 congestive heart failure patients returned to normal heart function after 6 months. The poster flags this as promising but unverified; it rests on an interview claim, not published data.

  • Claim: all 10 CHF patients normalized at 6 months (interview, ~54:00)
  • Trial uses 3x recommended dose
  • Recommended dose said to release ~10 million stem cells/day

They are running a very small human clinical trial on this supplement for congestive heart failure right now.

It looks promising.

He claims in the interview that all 10 congestive heart failure patients in the trial were returned to normal heart function after only 6 months (minute 54:00). If really true, this is remarkable.

This is not stem cell injections.

This is a blend of botanicals that stimulates your own bone marrow to release more stem cells into circulation.

Supposedly, the recommended dose releases 10 million new stem cells per day. (The trial is using 3x the recommended dose.) Compare this to a single injection which contains a couple hundred million stem cells, given all at once.

Single dose of donor stem cells raised walking endurance about 20% in frail older adults

Feb 25, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 9 Aging & longevity

Describes a trial of donor bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells in 148 walking adults aged 70-85 with frailty (118 on varying doses, 30 on placebo). The cells lack immune-triggering surface proteins so no immunosuppressants were needed; at nine months the highest dose group walked about 60 m farther in a six-minute walk test than at baseline.

Mutated blood stem cells (CHIP) may be a major cause of heart disease, doubling MI risk

Apr 08, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 13 Inflammation & CRP

Louis explains clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP): age-related mutations in bone-marrow stem cells yield white blood cells that drive inflammation and plaque growth, and the same mutations link to leukemia. A quoted report says CHIP carriers had a 54% higher chance of dying within a decade, from heart attacks and strokes, and mouse transplants of CHIP-mutated cells produced fast-growing, inflamed plaques. Louis credits Gokhan Can for pointing him to this.
