88% of US coronary heart disease deaths were attributable to modifiable risk factors

Louis Nervegna · Aug 05, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A JAMA Cardiology analysis of US IHD deaths 1990-2023 attributed 88% to modifiable risk factors, led by high blood pressure, diet and high LDL. Death rates fell mainly through better treatment, while weight and blood sugar worsened.

  • 88% attributable to modifiable risk factors
  • Top: blood pressure, diet, LDL
  • Rising: weight, blood sugar

90% of early heart deaths are preventable.

It’s a tragedy that the modifiable risk factors causing these early deaths are not being properly addressed.

Research cited

  • Modifiable Risk Factors and Attributable IHD Mortality in US States, 1990-2023 (GBD) · cross-sectional · JAMA Cardiology · 2026
Shared from Dr Paddy Barrett · original post

A study published this week analysed US coronary heart disease deaths from 1990 to 2023. The finding: 88% of those deaths were attributable to modifiable risk factors — led by high blood pressure, diet, and high LDL cholesterol. The uncomfortable part? Death rates fell mostly because treatment improved, not because we got healthier — and two controllable risks, weight and blood sugar, have been rising.
