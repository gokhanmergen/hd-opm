Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

K2 plus D3 slowed coronary calcium progression about 21% in a 2-year trial

Sep 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 14 Supplements & vitamins

In 398 adults with severe coronary calcification, two years of 720 mcg vitamin K2 (MK-7) plus 1,000 IU vitamin D3 daily slowed calcium-score progression versus placebo. However, total plaque volume rose similarly in both groups among the 143 assessed, so the trial does not show plaque reversal or fewer heart attacks.

Vitamin K2 activates matrix Gla protein to limit arterial calcification, review says

Sep 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 28 · 💬 21 Supplements & vitamins

A 2021 narrative review in Open Heart describes vitamin K2 as a regulator of calcium in the cardiovascular system by activating matrix Gla protein (MGP). Low vitamin K status leaves more inactive MGP, which has been linked to arterial stiffness and vascular calcification. It is a narrative review, not outcome trial evidence.

Vitamin K2 (MK-7) slowed coronary calcium progression in a 2-year randomized trial

Sep 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary of a randomized trial in 180 adults with symptomatic coronary artery disease and existing calcium found that 360 mcg/day MK-7 slowed CAC progression versus placebo over two years. It did not remove existing calcium, CAC is a surrogate marker, and the trial was not designed to show fewer heart attacks.

Vitamin K2 MK-7 slowed coronary calcification in a 2-year RCT; soft plaque not measured

Sep 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 11 Supplements & vitamins

A shared post on a new 2-year randomized trial notes that MK-7 reduced progression of coronary calcification versus placebo. It cautions that calcified plaque is generally more stable, soft plaque was not measured, and the trial was not designed to show fewer cardiovascular events.
