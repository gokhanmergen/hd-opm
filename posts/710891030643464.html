Why low LDL-C correlates with higher mortality: causation versus correlation

Gokhan Can · Nov 17, 2023 · ♥ 5 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Points to a video on how ignoring the difference between correlation and causation can mislead, and argues that the association of low LDL-C with higher mortality is a marker of illnesses such as cancer or liver disease rather than a cause. Also links a paper from the author's cardiologist on causal roles of LDL-C, HDL, triglycerides and inflammation.

  • Low LDL-C correlating with mortality is attributed to underlying disease (cancer, liver disease), not causation

Trying to do statistics without a deeper understanding of causation and correlation can be harmful to your health. This guy explains it in plain language: Don't be fooled by RATIOS like Triglycerides:HDL-C (Nutrition Made Simple!)

Alternatively, you can check out this paper written by my (Stanford) cardiologist to understand how different factors (LDL-C, HDL, Trigs, Inflammation) causatively affect heart disease: Google Drive file (drive.google.com)

Dietary fiber as a longevity intervention: 15-30% lower mortality at 25-29 g/day

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 23 · 💬 12 Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains

Long summary arguing fiber is a bioactive longevity tool. The WHO-commissioned Reynolds 2019 meta-analysis found 15-30% lower all-cause mortality at 25-29 g/day, with benefits continuing per extra 8 g; newer NHANES data suggest diminishing returns past ~30 g/day. It describes mechanisms including butyrate-driven epigenetic effects and gut barrier reinforcement against toxins such as microplastics.

CHIP (clonal hematopoiesis): a little-known risk factor for heart disease and stroke

Apr 08, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Risk assessment & lab tests

Explains clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential: blood cells carrying acquired mutations that raise the risk of blood cancers, coronary disease and stroke. It becomes more common with age, can be detected with a next-generation sequencing panel, and has no specific treatment yet. The post notes testosterone therapy and anything strongly stimulating bone marrow may help CHIP expand.

Louis shares Dr. Brian Lawenda's critique of an observational Israeli study of over 83,000 women.
