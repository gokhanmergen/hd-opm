Vitamin D2 supplements may lower vitamin D3 levels, a Surrey study finds

⚠️ Think twice before you pick your daily vitamin D supplement—new science reveals a crucial difference you might be missing.

A major study by the University of Surrey, the John Innes Centre, and the Quadram Institute BioScience found that taking vitamin D2, a common supplement, actually lowers the body's levels of vitamin D3—the form most effective for immune and overall health.

While many take vitamin D to strengthen bones and immunity, the investigation shows that D2 supplementation can reduce D3 concentrations below those seen in people not taking any supplements at all.

Vitamin D is available primarily in two forms: D2 and D3. The body naturally produces D3 in response to sunlight, and it is proven to be more efficient in boosting and maintaining healthy vitamin D levels. In this systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials, participants receiving D2 saw a decline in their D3, highlighting a negative interaction that hadn't been widely recognized before.

Researchers urge that, especially in times with limited sun exposure, vitamin D3 is likely the safer and more effective supplement choice for most people. This discovery is prompting experts to recommend plant-based D3 options and calls for health guidelines to be revisited to prioritize public wellness.
