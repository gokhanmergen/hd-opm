BITTER FINDING: A popular sweetener was linked to a 57% higher risk of heart attack and stroke.

Researchers tracked more than 17,700 participants across two large study cohorts and found that those with the highest blood levels of xylitol faced significantly greater cardiovascular danger — even after adjusting for age, obesity, diabetes and other risk factors.

The study, presented at the ESC Cardiology Congress in Germany, also found xylitol may enhance the ability of platelets to form clots.

High blood xylitol linked to 57% higher MACE risk in observational cohorts (ESC 2026)

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A study presented at ESC Congress 2026 analyzed 17,710 participants from CLSA and EPIC-Norfolk. Highest-quartile xylitol levels were associated with 57% higher MACE risk over six years in CLSA and 18% higher over 30 years in EPIC-Norfolk, with a dose-response pattern. It is observational and cannot show xylitol causes events.

Lipoprotein(a) explained: 80-90% genetic, stable for life, a risk factor beyond LDL

Oct 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 8 Lp(a)

Explainer on Lp(a): an LDL-like particle with apo(a) attached, 80-90% genetically determined, fully expressed by 1-2 years of age, and elevated in about 1 in 5 US individuals (1.4 billion globally). Levels above 50 mg/dL or 125 nmol/L raise ASCVD risk, possibly via impaired fibrinolysis and cholesterol deposition, and may occur with normal lipid panels.
