Louis: omega-6 does not cause inflammation in trials; omega-3:6 ratio is not predictive

Louis Nervegna · Jun 17, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Inflammation & CRPMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

A summary opinion post stating that controlled feeding studies and RCTs consistently show omega-6 fats do not cause inflammation, and that the omega-3 to omega-6 ratio does not predict heart disease or diabetes. It directs readers to group tags for the underlying posts and cites no specific study.

  • Claim: omega-6 fats do not cause inflammation in controlled trials
  • Claim: omega-3:6 ratio does not predict CVD or diabetes

Dozens of controlled feeding studies and randomized controlled trials have tested the hypothesis that omega 6 fats cause inflammation.

They consistently show that omega 6 fats do NOT cause inflammation. Some have shown an anti-inflammatory effect.

You can read many of the posts on this subject in the group by clicking the tag #inflammation6HD.

The omega 3 to 6 ratio does not predict risk for either heart disease or diabetes. Many studies have examined this, and it does not work. You can read the posts in the group discussing the research on this by clicking the tag #3to6ratioHD.

Louis shares a Johns Hopkins piece citing the 2019 Marklund pooled analysis of over 68,000 participants in 30 cohorts across 13 countries. The association is observational, which the author notes.
