Mild mitochondrial stress from statins is hormetic, not harmful

Louis Nervegna · Nov 17, 2025 · ♥ 25 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that mild mitochondrial toxicity from statins, metformin, exercise and calorie restriction is mitohormesis, which is beneficial at low doses. He says influencer claims that statins damage mitochondria misrepresent this.

  • Mitohormesis proposed as part of statin pleiotropic benefits
  • Dose determines toxic versus beneficial

I’m getting really sick of this whole “statins are toxic to mitochondria” nonsense. For the vast majority of people, the mild toxicity that statins have in mitochondria is hormetic. It is believed to be one of the explanations for the pleiotropic benefits of statins. Everyone needs to take a minute to google the terms “hormesis” and “mitohormesis”. Influencers are taking something that is probably very good for you and trying to turn it into a bad thing. The key is the dose. The same substance can be very good for you at low doses— via hormesis— and also outright toxic to you at very high doses. It is important to differentiate these two extreme cases. Normal doses of statins are hormetic in mitochondria for the vast majority of people. If you’re not getting any genuine side effects from statins (e.g. myopathy), it is ridiculous to worry about them “harming your mitochondria”. The opposite is probably true in this case.

Please understand that just because something is mildly toxic to mitochondria does not mean that it is bad for you. In fact, it often means that it is good for you!

The term for this is mitohormesis. This is how metformin and statins both work. It is also how exercise and calorie restriction work, as well as many different kinds of plant molecules (e.g. urolithin A from pomegranites). They all work by creating hormetic stress in mitochondria, i.e. mitohormesis.

The key is the dose…

The same substance that can be very good for you at low doses— via hormesis— can also be outright toxic to you at very high doses.

We need to get past this oversimplified idea that substances are always bad or good. When it comes to substances that work by hormetic mechanisms like statins and metformin, the dose is what matters. Normal doses of statins and metformin are hormetic in mitochondria.
