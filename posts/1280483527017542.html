Sarcopenia is fat replacing shrinking muscle fibers; resistance training best prevents it

Louis Nervegna · Apr 30, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a visual of sarcopenia, with muscle fibers shrinking and fat replacing muscle, stating resistance training is the most effective prevention.

  • Fiber shrinkage, wider gaps, fat replacement

This is what sarcopenia in the elderly actually looks like.

Resistance training is the only proven way to truly prevent this. Other things can help a little, but strength training is by far the most effective.

This is what muscle loss actually looks like.

On the left is healthy muscle.
On the right is sarcopenia.

At first, the change is subtle.
Muscle fibers begin to shrink.

Over time, they become smaller.
And less densely packed.

The space between fibers increases.
And fat begins to replace muscle.

This is not just “losing strength.”
It’s a structural change.

The muscle becomes weaker.
And less efficient.

This can happen with aging.
Or with inactivity.

That difference you’re seeing isn’t just visual.
It directly affects function.

Less muscle means less power.
And less support for your body.

Athletes in their 70s and 80s have leg muscle density similar to athletes in their 40s

May 16, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 5 Exercise & fitness

Louis shares University of Pittsburgh MRI findings in 40 high-level recreational athletes aged 40-81 training 4-5 times per week. Muscle density and strength held steady with consistent activity, suggesting much age-related decline is due to inactivity. Sedentary adults may lose up to 8% of muscle per decade after 40.

Whey protein raised IGF-1 but not muscle or strength in 24-week RCT of older adults

Jul 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 17 Protein

A 24-week placebo-controlled trial raised protein to 1.5 g/kg/day with whey in healthy older adults. It did not improve muscle power, strength, performance or mass, though IGF-1 rose; potassium bicarbonate was also ineffective. Louis argues this means the harms of high IGF-1 without benefit.

Nearly 70 trials show protein alone does not increase strength; training does the work

Mar 19, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 5 Protein

Louis states that randomized trials in aggregate show protein alone does not raise muscle strength unless combined with resistance exercise, and that Americans already eat more than enough protein to build muscle. He shares FoundMyFitness saying training is the main driver of adaptation.

Older veterans starting statins were less likely to develop frailty

Jun 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 3 ↩ 1 Statins

Louis shares a large observational study in the European Heart Journal where older US veterans who initiated statins had significantly lower frailty risk over time. Authors suggest anti-inflammatory effects may slow functional decline but emphasize randomized trials are needed.

Red meat tied to lower mortality only in frail underweight over-80s (Chinese case-control)

Mar 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 18 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis comments on an AJCN 2026 nested case-control study of Chinese adults 80+: non-meat eaters were less likely to reach 100, but only if underweight and frail. He cautions that case-control studies are low on the evidence hierarchy and may not be causal, and suggests protein and IGF-1 may matter in frail old age.
