Most people track cholesterol and blood sugar to monitor heart health—but one of the most dangerous predictors of heart attacks is almost never discussed: fat around the heart.

Known as epicardial fat, this visceral layer physically compresses the heart and drives inflammation—yet it rarely shows up in standard bloodwork.

Dr. Sean O’Mara argues that structural fat, visible on MRI, is a far more reliable marker of disease than insulin or cholesterol levels.

He shares why facial puffiness may offer an early clue—and how this fat is not only trackable, but reversible with the right lifestyle changes.

Visceral fat had the strongest inflammatory protein signature of any trait in VITAL/COSMOS

Nov 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 2 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Louis shares a cross-sectional proteomics analysis of 896 adults from VITAL and COSMOS. Visceral fat measured by DXA was associated with 221 circulating proteins, more than blood pressure, HDL, triglycerides or insulin resistance. It cannot prove causation, but suggests visceral fat is a meaningful target for reducing chronic inflammation.

Waist size predicted future disability better than 9 other aging and fitness measures

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 5 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Whitehall II data on 4,593 UK adults (average age 65) followed for about 11 years compared 10 standard measures, including walking speed, chair rises, balance, grip strength, lung function and BMI. Waist size predicted disability, and severe disability, best, and adding other tests improved prediction only slightly. Waist reflects visceral fat, which drives inflammation, insulin resistance and liver fat.

Green Mediterranean diet cut visceral fat about 14% in an 18-month randomized trial

Oct 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A shared summary of an 18-month RCT comparing a healthy diet, a Mediterranean diet and a Green Mediterranean diet enriched with green tea and Wolffia globosa (mankai duckweed). The Green Mediterranean diet reduced visceral fat by about 14%, the largest effect of the three arms. Visceral fat drives inflammation and metabolic dysfunction.
