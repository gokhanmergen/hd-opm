Evidence hierarchy: RCTs and prospective cohorts are the best basis for health decisions

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an evidence hierarchy graphic and urges readers to ask where a cited study falls on it, since lower-tier evidence is often used to rebut higher-tier. RCTs can prove causality and prospective cohorts show long-term outcomes such as over 30 years, and a combination of both is best.

  • RCTs can prove causality
  • Prospective cohorts can follow people for decades
  • Ask where a study sits on the hierarchy before trusting it

A reminder that randomized controlled trials and prospective cohort studies are at the top of the evidence hierarchy in medicine.

Randomized controlled trials can prove causality.

Prospective cohort studies can look at what happens over very long periods of time, e.g. 30 years.

A combination of both is best. This gives us information anout causality plus information about the long term.

This is the kind of quality evidence that you should demand before making any important health or medical decision.

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

Everyone reading medical studies should understand this hierarchy of evidence.

A common technique used by quacks and charlatans— or people who just don’t know what they are talking about— is to cite studies from the bottom of this hierarchy to try to refute studies at the top.

This technique is used all the time in health groups on Facebook. The first question you should ask when you see someone post a study is: Where on this hierarchy does the study fall?
