FDA approves tirzepatide for CV risk reduction in type 2 diabetes after SURPASS-CVOT

Louis Nervegna · Aug 28, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide)Heart attack, stroke & proceduresInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares that the FDA approved tirzepatide to lower heart attack and stroke risk in type 2 diabetes, based on SURPASS-CVOT. In 13,299 adults with established heart disease, tirzepatide was noninferior to dulaglutide for MACE (12.2% vs 13.1%, HR 0.92), without formally reaching superiority.

  • 13,299 adults with T2D and established heart disease
  • Tirzepatide up to 15 mg vs dulaglutide 1.5 mg weekly, median 4 years
  • MACE 12.2% vs 13.1%, HR 0.92; noninferiority met (P=0.003), superiority not reached (P=0.09)
  • Safety consistent with GLP-1 class, mainly GI effects

The FDA just approved tirzepatide to lower the risk of heart attack and stroke in people with type 2 diabetes.

This approval is based on strong results from the SURPASS-CVOT clinical trial.

The approval rests on SURPASS-CVOT, the first heart outcome trial to test an incretin drug against another active cardioprotective drug instead of placebo.

The trial

  • 13,299 adults with type 2 diabetes + established heart disease
  • Tirzepatide (up to 15mg) vs. dulaglutide (Trulicity) 1.5mg, weekly
  • Double-blind, noninferiority design, median 4 years follow-up
  • High-risk pop: avg. age 64, ~⅔ had coronary disease, ~20% prior stroke, on top of standard heart/diabetes meds

Headline result

  • MACE (heart death, heart attack, or stroke): 12.2% tirzepatide vs. 13.1% dulaglutide
  • HR 0.92 (95.3% CI 0.83–1.01)

✅ Noninferiority met (P=0.003)
❌ Superiority not formally reached (P=0.09), but every MACE component trended in tirzepatide’s favor

Why noninferiority still matters

  • Dulaglutide already has proven heart benefit → matching it implies tirzepatide also beats placebo

Supporting data

  • Broader 6-point cardiorenal outcome: 3.7% absolute risk reduction, NNT 27, favoring tirzepatide
  • Also lower all-cause mortality & 4-point MACE vs. dulaglutide
  • Indirect vs. placebo (via REWIND data): MACE HR 0.72, CV death/HF HR 0.70, all-cause death HR 0.61
  • Earlier signals: SURPASS-4 (HR 0.74) & 7-trial meta-analysis (HR 0.80)
  • Real-world 2026 cohort: MACE HR 0.68 vs. sitagliptin proxy

Safety: consistent with the #GLP class — mainly more GI effects (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting)

Bottom line: Tirzepatide now has robust evidence for heart protection in T2D + ASCVD, not just glucose and weight benefits.
