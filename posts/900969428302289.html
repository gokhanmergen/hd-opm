The US eats more high-quality protein than anywhere, yet heart disease and frailty persist

Louis Nervegna · Oct 30, 2024 · ♥ 18 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues the standard American diet supplies the most total and animal protein of any major region, yet the US has high rates of heart disease, cancer, sarcopenia and frailty. He concludes there is no protein emergency in the US. This is an ecological argument without cited studies.

  • US and Canada eat the most animal protein and least plant protein globally
  • Rates of heart disease, cancer, sarcopenia and frailty among the highest

The standard American diet contains more high-quality protein than any other major diet on earth.

In the USA and Canada, we eat more animal protein and less plant protein than any other region of the world.

Thanks to all the high-quality animal protein that we consume, we are at the very top in terms of total protein consumption, as well.

Our rates of heart disease and cancer are some of the highest in the world, and so are our rates of sarcopenia and frailty in old age. All of this high-quality protein seems to have done very little, if anything, to improve our health.

We don’t have a protein emergency in the USA. You almost certainly get more than enough high-quality protein if you eat like the average American.

Six lines of evidence favor whole-food plant protein over red and processed meat

Louis recommends an article by group member Mert Erogul MD on protein and aging. It argues six lines of evidence converge: growth-signaling genetics, IGF-1 biomarker data, rodent protein restriction through FGF21 and mTORC1, the roles of methionine, isoleucine and valine, short-term human trials, and epidemiology showing source matters more than amount. Plant protein from whole foods is favored.

Louis shares an Ageproof article by group member Mert Erogul MD arguing that genetics, IGF-1 biomarkers, rodent experiments, amino-acid decomposition, short-term human trials and epidemiology converge: methionine, isoleucine and valine, not leucine or total protein, are implicated. Louis summarizes Harvard Nurses' and Physicians' follow-up data on over 200,000 people tracked for 30 years. The best results were in those with the highest plant-to-animal protein ratio and the most total plant protein, mainly from replacing red and processed meat.

Whole-food plant protein beats animal protein for long-term outcomes in Harvard cohorts

Louis summarizes 30-year follow-up in the Harvard Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study: whole-food plant protein is associated with better outcomes, especially for heart disease. The benefit depends on the animal protein type: plant protein is only slightly better than fish, poultry and fermented dairy but clearly better than red and processed meat.
