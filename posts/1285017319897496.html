  • Effects of GH surges known for decades

Chronic elevation of growth hormone is dangerous
The symptoms that Bryan Johnson describes here are all classic textbook responses to a growth hormone surge. This stuff has been known for decades. This is not new, in any way.

Growth hormone raises blood glucose by acting as a counter regulatory agent to insulin. It triggers the liver to produce more glucose while simultaneously making muscles and fat cells less sensitive to insulin, which prevents them from absorbing sugar from the bloodstream. It forces the body to break down fats for fuel instead of carbs, which ensures glucose remains available in the blood, essentially prioritizing sugar for the brain and other vital functions.

All of this happens because growth hormone surges are a key part of the body’s stress response. When you’re being hunted by a pack of hungry animals for days, this kind of big surge (pulsatile release) of growth hormone helps keep you alive. These aren’t side effects. This is what nature intended growth hormone to do.

This is not the main function of growth hormone, of course. But it is an important one.

Chronic elevation of growth hormone, on the other hand, causes major health issues. Just like you see with chronic elevation of other hormones that surge during the stress response.

All his symptoms are exactly what you’d expect to see in someone experiencing the stress response of a big growth hormone surge. This is what happens to people with acromegaly (chronically high growth hormone), except for their entire lives. If left untreated, these people live considerably shortened lifespans.

Intentionally doing this to yourself in the long term is stupid. You don’t need an n=1 experiment to learn this the hard way. It’s been well-known for decades.
