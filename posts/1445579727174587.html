Willett: seed oils lower LDL and do not raise inflammation in dozens of randomized trials

Louis Nervegna · Nov 09, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Myths, misinformation & controversiesInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Walter Willett clip arguing the 'seed oils are toxic' claim does not hold up. Unless partially hydrogenated or heated to extremes, seed oils are good for you, lower LDL, and dozens of randomized studies show no increase in inflammation.

  • Seed oils lower LDL (evidence since the 1950s and 60s)
  • Dozens of randomized trials show no increase in inflammation
  • Concerns apply to partially hydrogenated oils or extreme heat

Quoted from the original post:

Seed oils are not the villain the internet made them out to be.

Walter Willett breaks down why the "seed oils are toxic" claim doesn't hold up against the data: when they're not partially hydrogenated or pushed to extreme heat, seed oils are genuinely good for you. We've had the evidence since the 1950s and 60s showing they lower LDL, the cholesterol you actually want to bring down.

And the inflammation fear? There was a theoretical case for it, so scientists tested it. Dozens of randomized studies later, the answer is consistent: seed oils do not increase inflammation. The literature isn't mixed on this one.
