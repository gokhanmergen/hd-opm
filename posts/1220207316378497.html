Raw milk exosomes activate mTOR and IGF1; fermenting milk destroys them

Louis Nervegna · Feb 08, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a review arguing milk acts as a signaling system that activates mTORC1 for neonatal growth, via exosomes and miRNAs. He notes fermentation (yogurt, cheese) destroys these exosomes and pasteurization helps less.

  • Raw milk contains exosomes and extracellular vesicles that activate mTOR and IGF1
  • Fermentation destroys them; pasteurization and homogenization help less

The raw milk of mammals contains exosomes and other extracellular vesicles that directly activate pathological growth pathways like mTOR and IGF1:

Paper · Nutr J · 2013Milk is not just food but most likely a genetic transfection system activating mTORC1 signaling for postnatal growthlink.springer.com

Fermenting the milk, e.g. to make yogurt or cheese, destroys these exosomes. Pasteurization and homogenization also help, but not as much:

Previously, we reported that miRNAs from pasteurized, store-bought bovine milk have biological activity in humans. Here, we assessed the effects of milk processing, storage, somatic cell content, and handling by consumers on the degradation of miRNAs in milk; we also quantified miRNAs in dairy products. Quoted:

“This review presents milk as a materno-neonatal relay system functioning by transfer of preferential amino acids, which increase plasma levels of glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), insulin, growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) for mTORC1 activation. Importantly, milk exosomes, which regularly contain microRNA-21, most likely represent a genetic transfection system enhancing mTORC1-driven metabolic processes. Whereas human breast milk is the ideal food for infants allowing appropriate postnatal growth and species-specific metabolic programming, persistent high milk signaling during adolescence and adulthood by continued cow´s milk consumption may promote mTORC1-driven diseases of civilization.”

Quoted:

"When milk was fermented to produce yogurt, miRNA [microRNA] concentrations decreased to levels much lower than in milk (this study).

We speculate that the decrease was due to the lysis of exosomes during fermentation and the large amounts of RNases produced by microbes."

This research may partially explain why the long-term epidemiology on fermented dairy in humans is consistently better than non-fermented dairy. Yogurt, cheese, and other fermented milk products are mostly free of these pathological growth-promoting substances.

It might also explain some of the short term benefits that people allegedly report from drinking raw milk. These same mTOR and IGF1 pathways are known to have short term benefits on reproductive fitness which trade-off with longevity in the long term.

The mTOR and IGF1 growth pathways shorten lifespan in virtually every animal species ever tested. They have short term benefits on reproductive fitness, but they shorten lifespan in the long term. Inhibiting these pathological growth pathways with genetic interventions, calorie restriction, fasting, and drugs like rapamycin consistently extends lifespan in animal models, including mammals.

We know that there is often a tradeoff between optimizing reproductive fitness in the short term and optimizing longevity in the long term. This is the classic theory of antagonistic pleiotropy.

You can learn more about these topics by searching the group for the tags #mtorHD, #igf1HD, and #antagonisticpleiotropyHD.

There's a reason that bodybuilders have success building muscle with milk and protein supplements made from milk. Nature designed milk to activate growth pathways like mTOR and IGF-1. It is the perfect food to make mammals grow.
