Low omega-3 index (<4%) linked to mortality risk comparable to smoking in Framingham data

Supplements & vitaminsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a clip summarising observational Framingham Heart Study data on the omega-3 index and notes the research is mostly observational but compelling. The shared text claims a low omega-3 index carries mortality risk similar to smoking and that raising it to about 8% could add roughly five years of life expectancy.

  • Omega-3 index under 4% associated with mortality risk comparable to smoking
  • Raising the index from low to ~8% associated with about 5 years longer life expectancy
  • Nearly 90% of Americans reportedly fall short of ~250 mg/day EPA+DHA
  • Evidence is mostly observational

The research she is describing is mostly observational, but it is compelling nonetheless.

Some of it comes from the famous Framingham Heart Study, arguably the highest-quality long-term prospective cohort study ever conducted in cardiology.
