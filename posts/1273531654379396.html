ACE inhibitors cut all-cause mortality 10% in RCT meta-analysis; ARBs showed none

Louis Nervegna · Apr 21, 2026 · ♥ 10 reactions · 28 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassium
AI summary & key facts

A summary of the evidence on ACE inhibitors versus ARBs for all-cause mortality. RCT meta-analyses favor ACE inhibitors, while observational and head-to-head data are mixed or neutral, so the question is not settled. ARBs are better tolerated (less cough and angioedema).

  • 2012 meta-analysis of 20 RCTs (~159,000 hypertensive patients): ACE inhibitors HR 0.90 for all-cause mortality; ARBs HR 0.99
  • Cochrane meta-analysis of 9 head-to-head RCTs (~11,000 patients): RR 0.98, no mortality difference
  • UK Biobank/China target trial emulation: 5-year mortality 3.45% (ACEi) vs 3.04% (ARB), HR 1.13
  • Proposed ACEi mechanism: reduced bradykinin degradation

ACE inhibitors vs ARB’s: Which lowers all-cause mortality more?
This post is copied from the rapamycin group. Gokhan Can was the author. The original can be found here: facebook.com/share/p/1CmmPzi2M5.

RCT-BASED META-ANALYSES (FAVOR ACE INHIBITORS)

The strongest signal comes from a landmark 2012 meta-analysis of 20 RCTs involving ~159,000 hypertensive patients published in the European Heart Journal. ACE inhibitors were associated with a significant 10% reduction in all-cause mortality (HR 0.90, 95% Cl 0.84-0.97, p = 0.004), whereas ARBs showed no mortality reduction (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.94-1.04, p = 0. The difference between the two classes was statistically significant (p = 0.036 for heterogeneity).

This has been replicated in parallel placebo-controlled meta-analyses. In one such analysis, ACE inhibitors reduced all-cause mortality by 11% while ARBs showed no benefit at all. A 2017 meta-analysis by Salvador et al. and a 2018 analysis by Xie et al. both supported ACE inhibitors having higher efficacy in reducing all-cause mortality compared with ARBs.

The proposed mechanism is that ACE inhibitors decrease the degradation of bradykinin, leading to nitric oxide and prostaglandin release and additional vasodilation— effects that ARBs do not produce.

OBSERVATIONAL / REAL-WORLD DATA (MIXED OR FAVOR ARBS)

A recent large target trial emulation study using the UK Biobank and China Renal Data System flipped the script. After propensity score matching, 5-year all-cause mortality was 3.45% with ACE inhibitors vs. 3.04% with ARBs (HR 1.13, 95% Cl 1.07-1.19), suggesting ACE inhibitor initiation was associated with higher mortality.

However, a large multinational cohort study across 8 databases found no significant difference in effectiveness outcomes between the two classes, though the confidence intervals could not rule out a moderate difference.

HEAD-TO-HEAD RCTS

A Cochrane meta-analysis of 9 head-to-head randomized trials including ~11,000 patients found no difference in all-cause mortality between the two classes (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.88-1.10). The problem is that direct comparison RCTs are few and generally underpowered for mortality endpoints.

ВОТТОМ LINE

The weight of RCT evidence tends to favor ACE inhibitors for all-cause mortality, particularly in hypertension and high-CV-risk populations. ARBs are better tolerated (fewer cough/angioedema side effects), which may explain why observational studies— where adherence matters more— sometimes show equivalent or even favorable ARB results. Current guidelines treat them as largely interchangeable first-line agents, but the mortality question remains genuinely unsettled, and some experts argue the two classes should not be considered equivalent.
