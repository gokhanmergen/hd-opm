Exercise beat metformin for diabetes prevention, cutting incidence 39%

Louis Nervegna · Aug 13, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

A quoted summary says the largest diabetes prevention study found lifestyle intervention with 150 minutes weekly of moderate exercise cut diabetes incidence 39%, surpassing metformin. Over 20 years, the reduction was 24% vs 17% for metformin.

  • 150 min/week moderate exercise cut diabetes incidence 39%
  • 20-year follow-up: 24% reduction vs 17% (metformin)
  • 3.5 vs 2.5 years of added diabetes-free survival

Quoted:

Exercise outperforms medication in diabetes management.

The largest study ever on diabetes prevention showed a lifestyle intervention with 150 minutes of weekly moderate-intensity exercise cut diabetes incidence by 39%, surpassing Metformin’s effect.

A 20-year follow-up revealed a 24% reduction in diabetes incidence and a 3.5-year increase in diabetes-free survival for the exercise group, compared to 17% and 2.5 years for Metformin.

This shouldn’t be surprising. Even light exercise significantly improves blood glucose control and mitochondrial function, making movement unmatched for metabolic health.

Note that a more recent study with metformin has shown a much stronger preventative effect:

RCTs show skipping breakfast does not cause weight gain and cuts about 260 calories a day

Oct 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 1 Fasting & meal timing

Observational studies link skipping breakfast to weight gain, but a 2019 BMJ review pooling 13 randomized trials found no evidence that eating breakfast aids weight loss or that skipping it causes weight gain. Breakfast skippers ate about 260 fewer calories per day overall. The possible downside is poorer glycemic control later in the day in people with diabetes or prediabetes.
