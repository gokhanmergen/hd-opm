Excess body fat can cut testosterone by up to half a reference range; fat loss restores it

Louis Nervegna · Mar 25, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Hormones, women's & men's healthWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis posts that overweight and obesity can lower testosterone by as much as half a reference range (about 350 ng/dL) and that losing visceral fat often returns levels to normal. The shared FoundMyFitness post explains that extra fat raises aromatization of testosterone to estrogen, which signals the brain to reduce testosterone production.

  • Drop can be about 350 ng/dL (half of the 300-1000 ng/dL range)
  • Fat tissue converts testosterone to estrogen via aromatization
  • Visceral fat loss often normalizes levels

Being overweight or obese drops your testosterone levels dramatically.

The drop can be as much as half a reference range, e.g. 350 ng/dL.

(The normal reference range for total testosterone in men is 300 to 1000 ng/dL. Half of this is [1000-300]/2 = 350 ng/dL.)

Lose the excess visceral fat… and levels often return to normal.

Shared from FoundMyFitness · original post

Being overweight or obese directly reduces your testosterone levels, potentially by as much as half a reference range

Excess fat tissue increases aromatization, the process where testosterone is converted into estrogen

Elevated estrogen signals the brain to dial back testosterone production, resulting in significantly lower testosterone levels

Losing fat (particularly visceral fat) can dramatically restore hormonal balance and boost testosterone to healthy, optimal levels
