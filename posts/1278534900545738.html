Research cited

  • SAMSON · RCT
Shared from Barbell Medicine · original post

In the SAMSON trial, 60 patients took statins, placebo, and nothing in rotating periods and rated their symptoms throughout. Ninety percent of the symptom burden attributed to statins also occurred on placebo; ten percent was pharmacological.

In the ASCOT data, side effect reports jumped 41% when patients learned they were on statins rather than placebo. The expectation of side effects produces them.

This is the nocebo effect. The muscle aches and fatigue are real, generated by the brain in response to the expectation of harm rather than the drug's chemistry. Patients who stop a statin because of these symptoms cannot distinguish a pharmacological effect from an expectation effect, because from the inside they feel identical.

Reported muscle symptoms on statins range from 1% to 25% depending on definition. SAMSON puts the pharmacological contribution at roughly 10%. Actual muscle damage, confirmed by objective testing, is rarer still.

Millions of people have stopped a medication with documented cardiovascular mortality benefit because of symptoms that are real but not drug-caused.
