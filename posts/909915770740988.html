Heart disease deaths fell almost 5x since 1950 as LDL targets tightened

Louis Nervegna · Nov 13, 2024 · ♥ 13 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelStatinsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes US heart disease death rates dropped from over 500 to almost 100 per 100,000 since the 1950s, with timing correlating with statins and lower LDL targets. He quotes a group member's AI-style summary relating MI mortality eras to guideline LDL goals and trials like 4S, PROVE-IT and IMPROVE-IT, with the caveat that correlation is not causation.

  • Death rates from over 500 to almost 100 per 100,000 since the 1950s
  • Guideline LDL targets fell from <130 (1985) to <55-70 mg/dL very-high-risk (2019)
  • Cited: 4S (30% MI mortality reduction), PROVE-IT TIMI 22, IMPROVE-IT (2% absolute risk reduction)
  • Summary lists confounders such as lifestyle and improved medical therapy

Annual death rates from heart disease have fallen by almost 5x since the 1950’s.

It used to be that over 500 out every 100,000 people in the USA died of heart disease each year. Now that number is down to almost 100.

The timing of the drops in death rates correlate strongly with the introduction of statins and the steadily more aggressive targets for lowering LDL.

This comment is from our group member, Dr. Adrian Mondry MD.

————-

Here's a correlation between published mortality rates for myocardial infarction (MI) and recommended LDL levels based on guidelines:

Mortality Rates for Myocardial Infarction:

  • 1980-2000: 300-400 deaths per 100,000 population (pre-statin era)
  • 2000-2010: 150-250 deaths per 100,000 population (early statin era)
  • 2010-2020: 50-150 deaths per 100,000 population (contemporary statin era)

Recommended LDL Levels:

  • 1985: National Cholesterol Education Program (NCEP) recommends LDL < 130 mg/dL
  • 2001: NCEP ATP II recommends LDL < 100 mg/dL
  • 2013: American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) recommends LDL < 70-100 mg/dL for high-risk individuals
  • 2019: European Society of Cardiology/European Atherosclerosis Society (ESC/EAS) recommends LDL < 55-70 mg/dL for very high-risk individuals

Correlation:

  • As recommended LDL levels decreased, mortality rates for MI also decreased.
  • The most significant decline in mortality rates occurred between 2000-2010, coinciding with widespread statin adoption.
  • Contemporary guidelines recommending more aggressive LDL lowering (< 55-70 mg/dL) may contribute to further reductions in MI mortality.

Studies:

  • The Scandinavian Simvastatin Survival Study (1994): demonstrated 30% reduction in MI mortality with LDL lowering
  • The PROVE-IT TIMI 22 trial (2004): showed 16% reduction in MI mortality with intensive LDL lowering
  • The IMPROVE-IT trial (2015): demonstrated 2% absolute risk reduction in MI with ezetimibe + simvastatin

Guideline Evolution:

  • 2020 ACC/AHA Guideline Update: emphasizes personalized risk assessment and LDL lowering
  • 2021 ESC/EAS Guideline Update: recommends LDL < 50 mg/dL for extremely high-risk individuals

Limitations:

  • Correlation does not imply causation.
  • Other factors (e.g., lifestyle changes, improved medical therapy) contribute to declining MI mortality.
