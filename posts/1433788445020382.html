“LDL research is incomplete and flawed.”

Well, we have over 200 prospective cohort studies, genetic studies, and randomized controlled trials involving over 2 million participants, 20 million person-years of follow-up, and 150,000+ cardiovascular events.

The evidence consistently shows a dose-dependent relationship between LDL-cholesterol and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. And yet, he doesn’t actually explain how this research is supposedly flawed.

Then comes the claim that “the higher your LDL, the longer you live.”

This ignores something we’ve understood for decades: illnesses like cancer, infections, and certain liver diseases can lower LDL. That means low LDL can sometimes be a consequence of illness, rather than the cause of increased mortality.

But when we look at people who genetically have lower LDL throughout their lives, or those who lower it through medication, we see reduced cardiovascular risk.
My friend @SimonHill challenged Malhotra in the comments, but rather than address his criticisms, Malhotra simply replied, “Nonsense.” When Simon pushed back further, Malhotra accused him of projecting.

It can be difficult to know who to trust on social media, but here are a couple red flags:

  1. Dismissing the scientific consensus without extraordinary evidence.
  2. Refusing to engage with criticisms.
