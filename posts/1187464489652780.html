Low vitamin C reduced fat burning during exercise, and 500 mg fixed it in a small trial

Louis Nervegna · Dec 24, 2025 · ♥ 24 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post

A 2006 trial found young adults with marginal vitamin C status oxidized less fat and felt more fatigue; supplementation with 500 mg resolved it.

  • 500 mg vitamin C
  • About 25% less fat burned in low-vitamin-C group (shared summary)
  • Vitamin C is needed to make carnitine

This was a fascinating small clinical trial. Low blood levels of vitamin C may impede fat loss and induce fatigue during exercise. The issue was resolved in the trial by simply supplementing with 500mg.

Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

The findings come from a 2006 study published in Nutrition & Metabolism by Johnston, Corte, and Swan, which examined young adults with low (marginal) vitamin C levels. The researchers found that individuals with low vitamin C burned 25% less fat and experienced greater fatigue. This happened because vitamin C is needed to produce carnitine, a compound that helps the body convert fat into energy. When vitamin C levels are low, fat metabolism becomes less efficient.

In the same study, participants with low vitamin C were given 500 mg of vitamin C per day for 8 weeks. After supplementation, their fat burning increased up to four times, and fatigue was reduced. This shows that 500 mg/day was beneficial for restoring low vitamin C levels and improving fat metabolism, especially in people who were deficient. The study does not suggest that everyone needs 500 mg daily, but it supports that this dose is effective and safe for correcting low vitamin C status.
