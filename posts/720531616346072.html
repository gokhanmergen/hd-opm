Opinion: aging, not lifestyle, is the greatest risk factor for cardiovascular disease

Gokhan Can · Dec 07, 2023 · ♥ 30 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Argues that even people with perfect lifestyles are vulnerable to CVD, cancer and dementia because aging is the biggest risk factor, and that technology will ultimately be needed to address aging itself. It cites no studies.

Some folks believe that living a perfectly healthy lifestyle will prevent cardiovascular disease (CVD). However, the unfortunate reality is that aging is the greatest risk factor for CVD, cancer, dementia, and other age-related diseases. Even those who exercise regularly, eat well, and avoid unhealthy habits are still vulnerable to these diseases as they get older.

Aging causes gradual cellular damage over decades that eventually manifests itself as disease. Lifestyle choices only modestly reduce risk -- they do not address the root cause of aging itself. No purely natural intervention can stop the aging process. At best, healthy living might delay onset of disease.

Ultimately, technology and medicine are our best hope to comprehensively address aging as the foundation of chronic disease. Drugs, treatments, and other innovations that directly target and repair age-related cellular damage could potentially extend healthy lifespans.

I personally take all the drugs I'm taking because evidence links them to reduced damage and increased longevity. Technology is the only solution here. If you think vegan/keto/mediterrenean etc or exercise will do, I'd like to break that bubble.
