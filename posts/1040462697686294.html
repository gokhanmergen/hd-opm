Adding Lp(a) does not improve the accuracy of the PREVENT risk calculator

Louis Nervegna · Jun 04, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Lp(a)Risk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a JAMA Cardiology editorial on a model that added Lp(a) to the AHA risk calculator. Adding Lp(a) did not improve prediction accuracy, so it is unlikely to be added soon, though measuring it can still personalize risk because Lp(a) is a causal factor.

  • Adding Lp(a) did not improve prediction in the PREVENT equations
  • Lp(a) is a causal factor, so measurement can still personalize risk

A new model has been developed that adds Lp(a) to the standard AHA risk calculator that physicians use to determine if patients should be placed on cholesterol-lowering medication.

AHA PREVENT calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equations for ages 30-79

Jan 25, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 21 ↩ 12 Risk assessment & lab tests

The AHA updated its cardiovascular risk calculator, PREVENT, for adults aged 30-79 without known CVD. It uses newer, larger and more diverse data than the 2013 Pooled Cohort Equations and includes kidney and metabolic disease, giving 10- and 30-year risk of both ASCVD and heart failure. Louis warns it will underestimate risk in people with prior heart attack/stroke, significant atherosclerosis or a high calcium score.

Adding CAC to the PREVENT risk calculator only modestly improves accuracy

Aug 26, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 6 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis explains that adding CAC score to the AHA PREVENT-ASCVD equations improves prediction only modestly in US adults 45-79, suggesting standard risk factors already capture much of what CAC reflects. He notes that a very high CAC should still be treated as high risk.

Louis notes that the AHA's PREVENT risk calculator performs well in Hispanic and Asian populations. The shared JAMA summary says it accurately predicted CVD, ASCVD and heart failure events across diverse patient groups.
