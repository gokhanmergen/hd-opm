Statin plaque regression RCTs: SATURN, REVERSAL, ASTEROID, COSMOS, JAPAN-ACS

Louis Nervegna · Jan 18, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 29 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyStatins
AI summary & key facts

Louis lists human trials in which statins reversed or halted atheroma growth measured as percent atheroma volume (PAV). High-dose atorvastatin and rosuvastatin regressed PAV in SATURN and ASTEROID, while REVERSAL showed halted progression versus pravastatin. COSMOS added canakinumab to rosuvastatin, and PROSPECT and JAPAN-ACS showed lower progression on statins.

  • SATURN: atorva 80 mg PAV -0.99% vs -0.31% with 10 mg, 24 months, n=1039
  • REVERSAL: atorva 80 mg +0.4% vs pravastatin 40 mg +2.7%, 18 months, n=502
  • ASTEROID: rosuvastatin 40 mg PAV -0.98% from baseline, 24 months, n=507
  • COSMOS: rosuvastatin 40 mg PAV -0.95% vs -0.05% in 52 weeks, n=418
  • PROSPECT: plaque progression 24.6% on statins vs 38.5% not, 3.4 years; JAPAN-ACS: -5.1% vs -0.7%, n=252

Here is a list of some of the randomized controlled trials in humans that showed reversal of plaque using statins:

(PAV stands for Percent Atheroma Volume.)

The SATURN trial (Nicholls et al., 2011) showed high-dose atorvastatin (80mg) regressed PAV by -0.99% compared to -0.31% with low-dose atorvastatin (10mg) over 24 months in 1039 patients (p=0.001).

The REVERSAL trial (Nissen et al., 2004) found high-dose atorvastatin (80mg) halted progression of PAV (0.4% increase) compared to low-dose pravastatin (40mg; +2.7% increase) over 18 months in 502 patients (p=0.02).

The ASTEROID trial (Nissen et al., 2006) showed high-dose rosuvastatin (40mg) regressed PAV by -0.98% compared to baseline over 24 months in 507 patients (p<0.001).

The COSMOS trial (Puri et al., 2016) found rosuvastatin (40mg) + canakinumab (anti-inflammatory) regressed PAV by -0.95% compared to -0.05% with placebo over 52 weeks in 418 patients (p<0.001).

The PROSPECT trial (Stone et al., 2011) showed patients on statins had a lower rate of plaque progression (24.6%) compared to those not on statins (38.5%) over 3.4 years (p=0.008).

The JAPAN-ACS trial (Hiro et al., 2009) found strong statins reduced plaque volume by -5.1% compared to -0.7% with non-statin therapy over 8-12 months in 252 patients (p=0.015).
