Lower lifetime LDL exposure means lower CVD risk across genetic variants and drugs

Louis Nervegna · Jun 01, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelMyths, misinformation & controversiesFats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)
AI summary & key facts

Quoted post arguing LDL is causal: genetic variants, statins, PCSK9 inhibitors and ezetimibe all show a log-linear, consistent relation between lower LDL and lower cardiovascular risk regardless of how LDL is lowered. It rebuts claims that LDL does not matter.

  • Relationship is log-linear and consistent across mechanisms
  • Cites mendelian randomization data (PMID 23083789)

Quoted:

LDL cholesterol is one of the most well-established causal risk factors in all of medicine.

And this graph is exactly why.

In my latest podcast episode on seed oils I explain why LDL matters. Anti-seed oil psychos will try to convince you LDL doesn’t matter but the DATA IS CLEAR

Different genetic variants. Different mechanisms. Same result:
Lower lifetime LDL exposure = lower cardiovascular disease risk (PMID: 23083789).

Not kinda.
Not “maybe.”
Not “only if inflammation is high.”
Not “only if LDL is oxidized.”
Not “only if your triglycerides are high.”

The relationship is log-linear and remarkably consistent. That means regardless of HOW LDL is lowered, reducing exposure reduces risk by the SAME amount (~50-55% reduction in CVD risk per 1mmol lifetime reduction in LDL). Statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, ezetimibe, genetics… same pattern.

If you still think LDL cholesterol has nothing to do with cardiovascular disease after looking at this data, then honestly, there’s probably no amount of evidence that will convince you. This is what causality looks like in human data.
And no, saying “but cholesterol is important for hormones/cell membranes/brain function” is not an argument against this. Oxygen is essential for life too, but too much of the wrong thing in the wrong place can still kill you and your body can make plenty of the cholesterol you need with VERY LITTLE cholesterol intake

Statin absolute risk reduction is small over years but can exceed 10% over a lifetime

Nov 10, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 25 ↩ 2 Statins

Louis explains that short statin trials show only 1-2% absolute risk reduction because baseline risk over a few years is only 2-5%, the same as diet and exercise trials. Over a lifetime, with a 20% baseline risk of heart attack, cohort and Mendelian randomization data suggest cumulative reductions of 10% or more.

Statin absolute risk reduction is cumulative: 1-2% in trials, far more over a lifetime

Sep 22, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 30 · 💬 2 ↩ 10 Statins

Louis counters the claim that statin trials show only 1-2% absolute risk reduction: trials last about 5 years when baseline risk is just 2-5%, whereas lifetime heart attack risk is about 20%. Cohort and Mendelian randomization data suggest long-term statin use can cut this to under 10%.

A low-dose statin could roughly halve lifetime heart attack risk if taken for decades

Feb 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 29 · 💬 47 Statins

Louis explains a graph combining RCT, cohort and Mendelian randomization data: an LDL reduction of 39 mg/dL (reachable with a very low dose statin) lowers heart attack risk more the longer it is sustained. Translated to absolute risk, a roughly 20% lifetime risk could fall to about 10%, and the rest requires diet, lifestyle and other therapies.
