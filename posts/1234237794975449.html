Statins and cholesterol drugs in the brain: why they do not starve neurons of cholesterol

Hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin don’t cross the blood brain barrier very well. So they have little effect on cholesterol production in the brain.

Other drugs like PCSK9 inhibitors, ezetimibe, bempedoic acid, etc. have ZERO effect on cholesterol production in the brain. They only affect cholesterol levels in the blood, which does not cross the blood brain barrier. The brain makes all its own cholesterol internally, completely separate from what’s in the blood. The blood brain barrier does not allow cholesterol in the blood to enter the brain:

↪ Hashtag-only image post on brain cholesterol, PCSK9 and dementia

So what about lipophilic statins like atorvastatin? These do get into the brain. They do cross the blood brain barrier. But there is promising research that shows these lipophilic statins may prevent Alzheimer’s:

↪ Statin use linked to 20% lower dementia and 32% lower Alzheimer's risk in meta-analysis

In fact, the research is so promising that there is now a giant 20,000 person randomized trial testing whether atorvastatin can prevent Alzheimer’s in older people. We should get the results in a few years:

↪ PREVENTABLE: atorvastatin 40 mg vs placebo up to 5 years in ~20,000 adults 75+

However we already know from two previous trials that lipophilic statins do not make existing Alzheimer’s worse (nor do they seem to help unfortunately):

↪ Two RCTs found statins neither helped nor harmed Alzheimer's disease progression

High LDL in midlife is now recognized as the biggest modifiable risk factor for dementia (2024 Lancet Commission Report). It is estimated to be responsible for 7% of dementia cases in the USA:

↪ Lancet Commission 2024: high LDL and hearing loss each account for 7% of dementia cases

Louis states that PCSK9 inhibitors lower cholesterol only in circulation and not in the brain, because circulating cholesterol cannot cross the blood-brain barrier and the brain produces its own. He frames this as basic physiology countering claims about brain harm from lower LDL.

Louis highlights a Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis of about 1 million people showing lifelong lower non-HDL cholesterol via statin-like (HMGCR) and ezetimibe-like (NPC1L1) variants was associated with 76% and 82% lower dementia per ~40 mg/dL drop. CETP variants showed a 70% reduction.

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as a treatment for established Alzheimer's: simvastatin in 406 people with normal lipids over 18 months, and atorvastatin 80 mg (LEADe) in people already on donepezil over 72 weeks. Both were neutral on cognition and safe.

Louis summarises two randomized trials of statins as treatment for existing Alzheimer's disease, one with simvastatin and one with atorvastatin. Neither slowed progression, and neither worsened it. This concerns treating established AD, not preventing it.

PREVENTABLE: 20,000-person trial of atorvastatin 40 mg to prevent dementia over age 75

Jun 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 5 Statins

Louis describes the ongoing PREVENTABLE trial, which randomizes about 20,000 community-dwelling adults aged 75+ to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo to test whether statins prevent dementia. Atorvastatin was chosen because it is lipophilic and crosses the blood-brain barrier; the trial began in 2020 and is still recruiting.
