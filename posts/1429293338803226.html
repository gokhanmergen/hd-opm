Dariush Mozaffarian: human evidence shows seed oils are not harmful

Louis Nervegna · Oct 23, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Inflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

A video quote from Tufts professor Dariush Mozaffarian says randomized trials, long-term observational studies and biomarker studies show seed oils lower glucose, LDL and insulin, are not pro-inflammatory and reduce heart attack risk. The post says the omega-6 fear sounds convincing biochemically but does not hold up in people.

  • Seed oils lower glucose and insulin and improve insulin sensitivity
  • Lower LDL and not pro-inflammatory in human studies

“There’s been randomized controlled trials. There’s been long term observational studies. There’s been blood biomarker studies. There’s been all kinds of experiments. All showing health benefits. These [seed] oils lower glucose, lower insulin, improve insulin sensitivity, raise the good cholesterol, lower the bad cholesterol. They’re not pro-inflammatory. Reduce risk of heart attacks. There’s really good evidence that they’re not bad for us, actually. I think people shouldn’t worry about seed oils as a problem.” -Professor Dariush Mazaffarian, Tufts.

en.wikipedia.orgDariush Mozaffarian - Wikipediaen.wikipedia.org

Quoted from the original post:

Seed oils are not the enemy. Dr. Dariush Mozaffarian breaks down the three "partial truths" behind the anti-seed-oil myth, and why actual human data (randomized trials, long-term studies, blood biomarkers) tells a different story: lower glucose, better insulin sensitivity, improved cholesterol, and reduced heart attack risk.

The biochemistry behind the omega-6 fear sounds convincing on paper. It just doesn't hold up when you test it in real people.

This is exactly the kind of nuance we love digging into on this show: not every "current craze" is grounded in evidence, and sometimes the boring, well-studied answer is the right one.
