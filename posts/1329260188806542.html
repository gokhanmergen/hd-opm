Heart attacks peak in the early morning; bedtime dosing of BP drugs may help

Louis Nervegna · Jun 27, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Heart attack, stroke & proceduresBlood pressure, salt & potassiumAspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that most heart attacks occur shortly after waking, when cortisol surges, vessels tighten and blood pressure spikes. He suggests taking blood pressure medication, aspirin and statins at bedtime, and corrects his post to say people are found dead in the 'bathroom' rather than 'toilet', since defecation syncope is much rarer than a standard heart attack.

  • Highest heart-attack risk is early morning after waking
  • Cortisol surge, vessel tightening and blood pressure spike drive the risk
  • Skipped or inconsistent medication timing weakens the morning defense
  • Defecation syncope is a different and much less common event than a typical heart attack

This is one of the main reasons why older people are often found dead on the toilet. That’s the first place most people go upon waking in the morning.

——————-

Most heart attacks happen early in the morning, shortly after waking.

This is why it can be a good idea to take blood pressure medication, aspirin, statins, etc. at bedtime.

This keeps blood pressure low, blood thin, and plaque stable at the time when you are most vulnerable.

Quoted:

Most people think heart attacks strike randomly.

But your highest risk is in the early morning hours — while you’re still in bed.

That’s when stress hormones like cortisol surge, blood vessels tighten, and blood pressure spikes.

For someone with untreated high blood pressure or unstable plaques, that’s a dangerous combination.

And here’s what most people miss — if you skip doses, if you take meds at inconsistent times, your body’s defenses aren’t ready for that morning surge.

Sometimes, the first symptom… is the last. If you take blood pressure medicine, when do you take it — morning or night? And has your doctor ever explained why?

Correction to the post:

It should really say “bathroom” instead of “toilet”, as pertains to where people are often found dead.

This is to avoid any confusion with the type of cardiac arrest that can be caused by defecation syncope, when straining to use the toilet specifically. This is very different from a regular heart attack, and much less common.

When people are found dead in the bathroom, it is usually from a regular heart attack. Not from a cardiac arrest caused by defecation syncope. The latter can certainly happen, especially in frail/elderly people, but it is a lot less common than a regular heart attack.

As Ed Endicott explains in his comment on the post, taking blood pressure medication at night can actually increase the risk for a defecation syncope related cardiac arrest. So this is not a good idea for anyone who might be at higher risk of this occurring. Again, this mostly concerns people who are frail/elderly. For the vast majority, the main concern is a regular heart attack in the morning after waking.
