A recent study demonstrates that CGM’s are not accurate for estimating A1C in people without diabetes.

The estimated A1C from the CGM differed by a whopping 0.6% from the actual laboratory measured A1C.

This is a huge difference: For example, it would be the difference between an A1C of 5.1% (a good value) and 5.7% (a pre-diabetic value).

Quoted:

“In a study published in Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics, researchers found a discordance between glucose management indicator (GMI), a CGM metric that estimates HbA1c, and laboratory-measured HbA1c. In an analysis of data from 153 children and adults without diabetes who wore a masked CGM for 10 days, the cohort’s mean GMI was 0.59% higher than laboratory-measured HbA1c.”

“Based on the findings from our study, it would not be appropriate to use GMI as a substitute for HbA1c in people without diabetes,” Shah told Healio. “GMI will overestimate HbA1c by about 0.6%, and it is possible that it may misclassify many people without diabetes as prediabetes.”
