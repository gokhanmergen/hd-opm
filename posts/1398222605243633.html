Mediterranean-like macronutrient mix lowered estimated biological age in 4 weeks

Louis Nervegna · Sep 18, 2026 · ♥ 11 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A University of Sydney study in 104 adults aged 65-75 found three diet groups reduced estimated biological age after four weeks, with the strongest evidence for an omnivorous diet of 28-29% fat, 14% protein and 53% carbohydrate. Biological age was estimated from 20 biomarkers including cholesterol, insulin and C-reactive protein.

Quoted:

Scientists studying 104 older adults found that three groups showed reductions in estimated biological age after following different diets for just four weeks. The strongest statistical evidence came from participants who ate an omnivorous diet containing less fat [28-29%] and more carbohydrates [53%].

The findings, published in Aging Cell, offer an interesting glimpse into how quickly the body may respond to changes in nutrition. They also raise a bigger question: can the food we eat actually influence the aging process?

For the University of Sydney study, researchers examined 20 biomarkers to calculate an estimated biological age score. These included cholesterol, insulin and C-reactive protein.

Two groups followed omnivorous diets, meaning their protein came equally from animal and plant sources. The other two followed semi-vegetarian diets, with 70% of their protein coming from plants.

Within both categories, participants followed
either a higher-fat, lower-carbohydrate diet or a lower-fat, higher-carbohydrate diet.

This created four distinct groups.

Participants following the omnivorous high-fat diet did not show a meaningful change in their calculated biological age. This group also followed the diet most similar to what participants had been eating before the study began.

The other three groups showed reductions in estimated biological age based on their biomarker profiles.

The strongest statistical evidence appeared in the omnivorous high-carbohydrate [53%] group.

Participants in this group consumed approximately 14% of their energy from protein, 28-29% from fat and 53% from carbohydrates.

What makes the research noteworthy is the timeframe. Changes in the biological age estimates appeared after only 28 days, suggesting that some measurable responses to diet may occur faster than many people expect.

Whether those changes are meaningful over the long term remains unanswered.

My note:

The overall result here was that either lowering fat to moderate levels (~30%) or lowering the ratio of animal to plant protein reduces markers of biological age, in about a month’s time.
