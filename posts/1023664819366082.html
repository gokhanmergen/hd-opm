Beef and whey raise insulin more than equal-calorie oatmeal, pasta or bread

Louis Nervegna · May 09, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 41 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesProteinCarbs, fiber, sugar & whole grains
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that CGMs measure glucose, not insulin, and that some proteins provoke as much or more insulin than carbohydrates. Citing the 1997 insulin index paper and a 2012 whey study, he shows beef raises insulin more than pasta or oatmeal calorie for calorie, and whey more than glucose or white bread, so swapping protein for carbs may raise post-meal insulin.

  • Beef raises insulin more than equal calories of white pasta or oatmeal
  • Whey raises insulin more than equal calories of glucose or white bread
  • CGMs do not measure insulin
  • Insulin index (1000-kJ portions) is the tool to compare foods

CGM’s measure glucose, not insulin.

Many proteins raise insulin, as much or more than carbs— even though they have very little effect on glucose.

Blindly substituting protein for carbs may actually have the unwanted effect of raising insulin after a meal, depending on what substitutions you make.

For example, substituting red meat for an equivalent amout of calories from oatmeal will raise insulin after a meal.

Likewise, substituting whey protein for an equivalent amount of calories from whole wheat bread will raise insulin after a meal.

Some proteins raise insulin levels even more than carbs.

Beef raises insulin more than an equivalent number of calories from white pasta or oatmeal (porridge), when comparing calorie for calorie:

Paper · Am J Clin Nutr · 1997An insulin index of foods: the insulin demand generated by 1000-kJ portions of common foods.The aim of this study was to systematically compare postprandial insulin responses to isoenergetic 1000-kJ (240-kcal) portions of several common foods. Correlations with nutrient content were determined. Thirty-eight foods separated into six food categories (fruit, bakery products, snacks, carbohydrate-rich foods, protein-rich foods, and breakfast cereals) were fed to groups of 11-13 healthy subjects. Finger-prick…pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Whey protein raises insulin more than an equivalent number of calories from pure glucose or white bread, when comparing calorie for calorie:

Paper · Nutr Metab (Lond) · 2012The insulinogenic effect of whey protein is partially mediated by a direct effect of amino acids and GIP on β-cells.Conclusion: Compared with white wheat bread meal, whey causes an increase of postprandial insulin, plasma amino acids, GIP and GLP-1 responses. The in vitro data suggest that whey protein exerts its insulinogenic effect by preferential elevation of the plasma concentrations of certain amino acids, GIP and GLP-1.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Social media influencers have been misleading people about this for years.

If you really want to understand how much a particular food raises your levels of insulin, learn about the insulin index (also called the insulinogenic index).

Google “insulin index”.
