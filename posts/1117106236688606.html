It’s foolish to play games and try to guess which risk factors for heart disease are more important than others.

Even the best scientists in the world have not figured this out yet. Nobody has the correct answer to this question.

As mentioned many times, AGING is the biggest risk factor of all for heart disease, by far. More than 10 times greater than any other known risk factor. Every day, you are getting older and this is working against you:

↪ Age is the biggest risk factor for heart disease; geroscience targets aging itself

↪ Age raises CVD death risk about 19.5x from 65 to 85, far above diabetes or smoking

↪ Aging is the biggest risk factor for 9 of the top 10 causes of death in the USA

↪ JACC statement on geroscience: why aging drives CVD and how to slow cardiovascular aging

The rational approach is to target ALL known risk factors for heart disease as aggressively as possible (including the aging process itself)— and not try to guess. This means using all weapons available: diet, exercise, supplements, drugs, etc.

This is is the philosophy of this group:

Age raises CVD death risk about 19.5x from 65 to 85, far above diabetes or smoking

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 20 ↩ 5 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

Louis compares hazard ratios from the SCORE O.P. risk model for people over 65. A hazard ratio of 1.16 per year of age compounds to about 19.5x higher CVD death risk between 65 and 85, far larger than diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure or LDL. The numbers used are for women; men are similar. (The post also writes this as 19,500%, which is a mislabel of 19.5x.)

JACC statement on geroscience: why aging drives CVD and how to slow cardiovascular aging

Dec 16, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 ↩ 2 Aging & longevity

Louis calls the JACC Scientific Statement on geroscience the best review so far of why age is the largest CVD risk factor. It argues aging is a common root of chronic disease, explaining multimorbidity, frailty and poorer CVD prognosis in older adults, and describes gerotherapeutic approaches, some already used in CVD care, that could transform management.

REACT in NEJM: 57% of adults have atherosclerosis and age is the biggest risk factor

Aug 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 19 · 💬 4 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Louis shares the REACT study in NEJM of more than 16,000 adults aged 18-70 without known CVD, imaged by vascular ultrasound and CT. About 57% had plaque, rising from 1 in 13 in their 20s to about 9 in 10 in their 60s. Men developed silent disease 5-10 years earlier than women. He concludes age is the biggest risk factor.
