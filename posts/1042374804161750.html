A longtime vegan's heart attack at 70: plant-based diets cut risk but don't remove it

Louis Nervegna · Jun 07, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

The post shares a commentary by a former Vegetarian Society (Singapore) president who suffered a heart attack at 70 after 40 years of veganism. Louis's takeaway is that a well-done plant-based diet can be very healthy but is not a magic cure for heart disease; genetics, age and other factors still matter.

  • Heart attack at age 70 in 2022 after 40+ years vegan
  • Plant-based diet lowers but does not eliminate heart disease, diabetes and obesity risk

It can be a very healthy diet if done correctly, but it is not a magic cure for heart disease.

Shared from Robin Ong · original post

The article, written by George Jacobs, a former president of the Vegetarian Society (Singapore), recounts his personal experience of adopting a vegan diet for over 40 years, initially motivated by concerns about meat production's impact on world hunger. Despite his commitment to veganism and a relatively healthy lifestyle, Jacobs suffered a heart attack at age 70 in 2022. He acknowledges that while a plant-based diet can reduce health risks—such as lowering the chances of heart disease, diabetes, and obesity—it does not eliminate them entirely. Factors like genetics, age, and other lifestyle elements also play significant roles in health outcomes. Jacobs notes his rising weight and elevated LDL cholesterol levels as potential contributors to his condition. He remains dedicated to veganism for ethical and environmental reasons, emphasizing that no diet is a complete shield against health issues, and encourages ongoing health monitoring and nutritional awareness, such as ensuring adequate vitamin B12 intake for vegans.
