Fasting insulin mainly reflects liver fat-driven insulin resistance

Louis Nervegna · Apr 22, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that elevated fasting insulin is mostly a compensatory response to hepatic insulin resistance from liver fat, with muscle fat contributing too.

  • Liver fat is main driver
  • Muscle insulin resistance from intramuscular fat

“Elevated fasting insulin doesn't magically occur. It is a compensatory response to insulin resistance of adipose and muscle tissues.”

True, but incomplete. Even more important is specifically the ectopic fat that accumulates in the liver. We now know that this is the main driver. Elevated fasting insulin is mainly a compensatory response to insulin resistance in the liver, caused by too much liver fat.

Adipose tissue and muscle contribute, but to a lesser extent. The major reason why muscle tissue becomes insulin resistant in the first place is because of accumulation of too much fat within the muscle itself (the same way a steak gets marbled), similar to what happens in the liver.

Losing body fat reverses insulin resistance mainly because the liver is the first place where most people lose fat. Losing weight clears the liver of fat.
