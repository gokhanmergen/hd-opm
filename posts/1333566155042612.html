Pomegranate and plaque: claims exceed the evidence, and the best trial showed no benefit

Louis Nervegna · Jul 02, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis, who drinks low-sugar pomegranate juice himself, pushes back on viral claims that pomegranate cuts arterial plaque by 30%. He says the highest-quality trial found no benefit, so it should not replace proven therapies.

  • Highest-quality trial testing pomegranate for plaque showed no benefit
  • Advice: if used, combine with proven treatments rather than replace them

I am a big fan of pomegranate, and drink the juice myself (the low sugar version). It probably does have some health benefits.

But many of the claims go way beyond the evidence.

Maybe it does have some value in preventing (or even reversing) plaque, but the totality of evidence to date does NOT support this. In fact, the highest quality trial testing this hypothesis showed no benefit.

Betting your life on this is a big mistake.

If you’re going to do it (like I do), then do it in combination with things that are proven to work. Not in place of them.
