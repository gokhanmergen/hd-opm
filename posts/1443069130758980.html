Preview of HORIZON (Lp(a)) and ZEUS (IL-6): improved bloodwork is not enough

Louis Nervegna · Nov 06, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRPLp(a)How to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis announces an upcoming discussion of two failed cardiology trials and shares a post arguing treatments should be judged on outcomes, not just lab numbers. In ZEUS, IL-6 and hsCRP fell but the combined risk of heart attack, stroke or CV death was not significantly reduced.

  • ZEUS lowered IL-6 and hsCRP without significant reduction in MACE
  • CANTOS cited as reference (PMID 28845751)

Tomorrow we are going to learn the details behind two major cardiology outcome trials that failed:

HORIZON targeting Lp(a)
ZEUS targeting IL-6 and hsCRP

Quoted from the original post:

This is why you should want to see more than improved bloodwork before calling a treatment a success.

In ZEUS, a drug lowered inflammation markers [IL-6 & hsCRP] in people with cardiovascular and kidney disease, but didn’t significantly reduce their combined risk of heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death.

That distinction matters well beyond this trial. In health and longevity, we spend a lot of time tracking numbers. The harder question is whether changing those numbers actually improves someone’s health.

I’m watching Saturday’s (tomorrow's) American Heart Association presentation closely. The headline results are already public, but the presentation will provide more clarity.

Pelacarsen (Lp(a)HORIZON) and ziltivekimab (ZEUS) cut their targets but not heart events

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Shared summary of two NEJM trials. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) sharply but event rates were 12.3% vs 12.1% on placebo, and ziltivekimab cut CRP and IL-6 but events were 17.6% vs 17.7%. The post notes prior biomarker failures (torcetrapib, dalcetrapib), and that both trials enrolled people with existing disease and so do not prove Lp(a) or inflammation are not causal.

Yale: taurine loss triggers NLRP3 inflammasome; raising taurine eased inflammaging in mice

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a summary of a Yale Cell study showing macrophages release up to 80% of their taurine after inflammatory triggers, which helps activate the NLRP3 inflammasome. Restoring taurine suppressed NLRP3 activation, and raising taurine in older mice improved grip strength, coordination, some glucose measures and inflammatory markers. It is preclinical research.

Inflammaging may begin around age 26 in men and 35 in women, ATTICA analysis finds

Nov 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a summary of an ATTICA analysis of 1,825 Greek adults showing a combined IL-6, TNF-alpha and fibrinogen immune activation score crosses into the activated range by about age 26 in men and 35 in women. IL-6 and fibrinogen rose steadily with age while CRP barely changed. Earlier studies link these markers to heart disease, diabetes, dementia and death.

Statins may lower hsCRP by stabilizing plaque; fixing upstream causes beats blocking IL-6

Aug 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a clip arguing statins lower inflammation because they stabilize unstable plaque, which generates the inflammation. The clip cites JUPITER, where rosuvastatin lowered hsCRP 37%. Interventions that address upstream causes, like statins or diet, work better than blocking IL-6 downstream.
