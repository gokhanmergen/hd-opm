Meta-analysis: statins cut arterial wall inflammation on PET-CT, high-intensity most

Louis Nervegna · Oct 25, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsInflammation & CRPPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Louis highlights a 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 clinical trials (373 subjects) using 18F-FDG PET-CT, a marker of arterial wall inflammation. Statins markedly reduced uptake (target-to-background ratio), significantly so for high-intensity therapy and non-significantly for low-to-moderate intensity.

  • 10 trials, 373 subjects
  • Outcome: arterial wall 18F-FDG uptake (target-to-background ratio) fell after statins
  • High-intensity statins: significant TBR reduction; low-to-moderate: non-significant
  • Authors conclude statins suppress arterial wall inflammation

10 clinical trials have shown in aggregate that statins dramatically lower inflammation in the walls of arteries, as measured by PET-CT scans.

The effect is dose-dependent. High doses work better for suppressing arterial inflammation than low-to-moderate doses.

Paper · J Inflamm (Lond) · 2024Effect of statins on arterial wall inflammation as assessed by 18F-FDG PET CT: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis.Conclusion: Treatment with statins suppressed arterial wall inflammation as shown by using 18 F-FDG PET-CT.journal-inflammation.biomedcentral.com

Quoted:

“Pathogenesis of atherosclerosis is largely mediated by inflammatory process. Statins are lipid-lowering drugs which also have anti-inflammatory effects. 18 fluorine radiolabeled fluorodeoxyglucose (18 F-FDG) positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) is considered to be a good indicator of arterial wall inflammation. Therefore, in this meta-analysis the role of statins on inflammatory process in the artery wall was evaluated using this method since its actual validity for this purpose is not yet well established.”

“ Findings of 10 clinical trials involving 373 subjects showed a remarkable reduction of arterial wall 18 F-FDG uptake [indicative of lower inflammation] according to target-to-background ratio (TBR) index after treatment with statins.

Subgroup analysis showed a significant decrease in TBR with high-intensity and non-significant reduction of TBR with low-to-moderate-intensity statin therapy.”

“Conclusion- Treatment with statins suppressed arterial wall inflammation as shown by using 18 F-FDG PET-CT.”
