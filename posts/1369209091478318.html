Nuts several times a week linked to better brain aging; PREDIMED nut arm improved memory

Louis Nervegna · Aug 14, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Quoted video summarizes that women in the Nurses' Health Study eating nuts five or more times weekly scored higher on memory and cognition, about two years younger, and that the PREDIMED Mediterranean diet with mixed nuts improved memory and executive function over four years.

  • NHS: nuts 5+ times/week, cognition ~2 years younger
  • PREDIMED: improved memory and executive function in 4 years

Quoted:

A handful of nuts a few times a week = better brain aging 🥜 Comment ‘NEWS’ to get our newsletter, THE SYNAPSE, always featuring healthy eating insights 💌

Harvard’s Nurses’ Health Study found that women who ate nuts five or more times a week scored higher on memory and cognitive tests — about the same as being two years younger.

And in the PREDIMED trial, a Mediterranean diet enriched with mixed nuts improved memory and executive function in just four years.

Daily peanuts for 16 weeks improved brain insulin responsiveness in older adults

Oct 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A Maastricht University trial in healthy adults aged 60 to 75 gave 60 g of unsalted skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks. Functional brain imaging showed enhanced insulin responsiveness in memory regions including the hippocampus and parahippocampal gyrus. The shared post says more research is needed.

Daily peanuts for 16 weeks improved brain insulin responsiveness in older adults

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A 16-week trial in healthy adults aged 60-75 gave 60 g of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily and found improved insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions on imaging. There were no wider changes in body metabolism or liver fat, and the result helps explain earlier findings of better memory and cerebral blood flow with peanuts.
