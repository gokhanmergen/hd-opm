Non-caloric sweeteners do not cause meaningful insulin spikes, Louis says

Louis Nervegna · Nov 14, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsInsulin resistance & diabetesMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis counters a viral claim that non-sugar sweeteners mess with insulin, saying repeated human testing shows the claim is wrong for both artificial sweeteners like aspartame and natural ones like stevia. No specific studies are cited.

  • Applies to aspartame and stevia
  • Claim based on cherry-picked individual studies

The idea that non-caloric sweeteners cause significant insulin spikes is wrong.

This claim has been tested many times now in humans. The preponderance of evidence shows that it is simply not true. You can cherry pick one or two individual studies, but the evidence as a whole strongly disproves this claim.

This applies to both artificial sweeteners (e.g. aspartame) and natural non-caloric sweeteners (e.g. stevia).
