Overfeeding saturated fat raised liver fat 55%, sugar 33%, unsaturated fat 15% in an RCT

Louis Nervegna · Apr 07, 2026 · ♥ 13 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Weight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

A randomized trial overfed three groups by 1000 calories for 3 weeks from butter/dairy, olive oil/nuts or added sugar. Liver fat rose 55%, 15% and 33% respectively, showing calories are not equal for the liver.

  • +1000 kcal/day for 3 weeks
  • Saturated fat: +55% liver fat
  • Sugar: +33%
  • Unsaturated fat: +15%

This randomized trial intentionally overfed three groups of people by an extra 1000 calories for 3 weeks, in order to make them gain fat.

One group got those extra calories from butter and other high fat dairy (saturated fat). The second group got them from olive oil and nuts (unsaturated fat). The third group got them from added sugars (refined carbohydrate).

In every other way, their diets were the same.

Link to full study:

SAT induced the greatest increase in IHTG, insulin resistance, and harmful ceramides. The group consuming the extra 1000 calories from mostly saturated fat (butter, dairy) had a 55% increase in liver fat.

The group consuming the extra calories from refined sugar had a 33% increase in liver fat.

But the group that got those extra 1000 calories from mostly unsaturated fat (olive oil and nuts) had only a 15% increase in liver fat.

The bottomline here is that not all calories are the same when it comes to liver fat and insulin resistance: unsaturated fat consistently outperforms both saturated fat and refined carbohydrate when compared head to head in human randomized trials. Many trials have demonstrated this:

Quoted from the study:

“We overfed 38 overweight subjects (age 48 ± 2 years, BMI 31 ± 1 kg/m2, liver fat 4.7 ± 0.9%) 1,000 extra kcal/day of saturated (SAT) or unsaturated (UNSAT) fat or simple sugars (CARB) for 3 weeks.”

“Overfeeding SAT increased IHTG [liver fat] more (+55%) than UNSAT (+15%, P < 0.05). CARB increased IHTG [liver fat] (+33%) by stimulating DNL (+98%). SAT significantly increased while UNSAT decreased lipolysis. SAT induced insulin resistance and endotoxemia and significantly increased multiple plasma ceramides.”

“Macronutrient composition of excess energy influences pathways of IHTG [liver fat]: CARB increases DNL, while SAT increases and UNSAT decreases lipolysis. SAT induced the greatest increase in IHTG [liver fat], insulin resistance, and harmful ceramides. Decreased intakes of SAT could be beneficial in reducing IHTG [liver fat] and the associated risk of diabetes.”

Your liver likes monounsaturated fat and polyphenols more than saturated fat.

This study, which is quite famous, demonstrates this point in a very unique way.

As such, it makes very good sense for any fatty liver friendly nutrition strategy to rely much more on olive oil and nuts then it does on cheese and butter.
