Tom Dayspring: Circulating oxidized LDL in the bloodstream is a “useless” biomarker for heart disease.

He says not to waste your money on this test, and to measure ApoB instead.

Note that he is talking about the the oxidized LDL in blood circulation— not the oxidized LDL in plaques. The oxidized LDL in plaques still matters a lot. The oxidized LDL in plaques mostly happens after the LDL enters the plaque and leaves circulation. You can not measure this with a blood test unfortunately.

He is talking about LDL oxidizing in circulation before it enters the plaque. The newest studies that properly adjust for ApoB show that circulating oxidized LDL is NOT related to CVD risk. It is the ApoB that is actually causal, whether those particles are oxidized or not in circulation. The ApoB particles get oxidized anyway after they enter the plaque, which can not be measured. Whether or not they are oxidized before they enter plaque seems to make little difference.
