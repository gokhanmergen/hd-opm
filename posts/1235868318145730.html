Shingles vaccine tied to slower biological aging and less inflammation in 3,800 elders

Gokhan Can · Feb 28, 2026 · ♥ 22 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesInflammation & CRPAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Reports a large national study of more than 3,800 Americans aged 70+, in which shingles-vaccinated people showed slower biological aging, lower chronic inflammation and slower aging-related changes in gene activity. This suggests the vaccine may calm 'inflammaging', the low-grade inflammation tied to heart disease, frailty and cognitive decline.

  • More than 3,800 Americans aged 70 and older
  • Lower chronic inflammation and slower aging-linked gene-activity changes in vaccinated

"A shingles shot might do more than prevent a painful rash — it could actually help slow down the aging process. In a large national study of more than 3,800 Americans age 70 and older, those who received the shingles vaccine showed slower biological aging compared to those who didn’t. Researchers found lower levels of chronic inflammation and slower changes in gene activity linked to aging, suggesting the vaccine may calm the body’s “inflammaging” — the low-grade inflammation tied to heart disease, frailty, and cognitive decline."

Shingles vaccination linked to slower biological aging in over 3,800 older adults

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 Infections & vaccines

A US Health and Retirement Study cohort analysis of over 3,800 adults aged 70+ found those vaccinated against shingles between 2008 and 2018 had lower inflammation scores, slower genetic aging and lower overall biological aging scores. The poster links this with evidence the vaccine may lower dementia risk, with reduced inflammaging a possible mechanism.

Inflammaging may begin around age 26 in men and 35 in women, ATTICA analysis finds

Nov 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a summary of an ATTICA analysis of 1,825 Greek adults showing a combined IL-6, TNF-alpha and fibrinogen immune activation score crosses into the activated range by about age 26 in men and 35 in women. IL-6 and fibrinogen rose steadily with age while CRP barely changed. Earlier studies link these markers to heart disease, diabetes, dementia and death.

Yale: taurine loss triggers NLRP3 inflammasome; raising taurine eased inflammaging in mice

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Inflammation & CRP

Louis shares a summary of a Yale Cell study showing macrophages release up to 80% of their taurine after inflammatory triggers, which helps activate the NLRP3 inflammasome. Restoring taurine suppressed NLRP3 activation, and raising taurine in older mice improved grip strength, coordination, some glucose measures and inflammatory markers. It is preclinical research.

Taurine lowers age-related inflammation via the NLRP3 inflammasome in mouse macrophages

Oct 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 ↩ 1 Aging & longevity

A Cell paper from Yale reports that taurine levels increase with age in mice and humans, and boosting taurine further in aged mice increased healthspan and dampened inflammation by regulating the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages.
