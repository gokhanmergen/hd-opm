Metformin is being tested for CVD, cancer and Alzheimer's, but mechanisms are unclear

Louis Nervegna · Mar 08, 2024 · ♥ 14 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Insulin resistance & diabetesAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Medscape article on renewed academic interest in metformin for conditions beyond diabetes, including cardiovascular, neurodegenerative, liver, kidney disease and cancers. Many new trials are underway, and it is unclear whether benefits come from glucose lowering and insulin sensitizing or other actions like mTOR inhibition. This is emerging, not established, evidence.

  • Many new trials launched to expand metformin's indications
  • Unclear if benefits stem from insulin sensitization, mTOR inhibition or antiviral actions

Feb 27, 2024 — Metformin might protect against CVD, cancer, Alzheimer’s, and a number of other diseases.

In academia, there has been a recent resurgence of interest in metformin for these purposes, and several new trials have begun.

Quoted:

“Emerging evidence suggests the drug may be effective for a much broader range of conditions beyond managing high blood glucose, including various cancers, obesity, liver disease, cardiovascular, neurodegenerative, and renal diseases. As the evidence for diverse uses accumulates, many trials have launched, with researchers looking to expand metformin's indications and validate or explore new directions.”

“Collectively, these data raise the question: Is metformin a drug for all diseases? It remains unclear as to whether all of these putative beneficial effects are secondary to its actions as an antihyperglycemic and insulin-sensitizing drug, or result from other cellular actions, including inhibition of mTOR (mammalian target for rapamycin), or direct antiviral actions."

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.
