Statin muscle side effects may stem from HMG-CoA reductase gene variants

Louis Nervegna · May 03, 2024 · ♥ 27 reactions · 24 comments · original Facebook post
Statins
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an Atlantic article on a proposed mechanism for statin-related muscle symptoms: people at higher risk likely carry variants in genes coding for HMG-CoA reductase, which statins then further inhibit. Extreme versions of the same gene defects cause a very rare limb girdle muscular dystrophy. Louis calls it a definitive answer; the article is journalism rather than a primary paper.

  • Higher-risk people likely have variants in HMG-CoA reductase genes, which statins further inhibit
  • Extreme variants cause a rare genetic muscle disorder (limb girdle muscular dystrophy)
  • Research suggests a potential treatment may follow from the finding

There’s now a definitive answer as to why statins cause muscle-related side effects in some people.

The exact mechanism has finally been worked out.

(Edit - Try this link, if you cannot read the full article. It bypasses the paywall: Archived web page (archive.is))

People at higher risk for muscle-related side effects from statins likely have mutations to certain genes that code for the HMG-coA reductase enzyme. Statins then exacerbate these defects by further inhibiting HMG-coA reductase. An extreme version of mutations to these same genes causes a very rare genetic muscle disorder, called limb girdle muscular dystrophy.

It's a fascinating story about how studying a seemingly unrelated and incredibly rare genetic disorder helped to understand a very common problem in medicine.

Quoted:

“One of the first things you learn in medical school is association between statins and myopathy. Now the answer as to why— along with a potential treatment for it— has emerged from the DNA of just a few patients living with a seemingly unrelated genetic disease.”

Statin muscle pain is common in some, severe damage is rare; statins may aid sarcopenia

Jan 02, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 15 Statins

Louis argues statins are not toxic to muscle in most people. Myalgia occurs in some, possibly with a genetic susceptibility, while rhabdomyolysis is very rare (about 1 in 100,000, or 1 in 10,000 conservatively). Switching statins (e.g., to pravastatin or pitavastatin) or using non-statin LDL drugs can help, and low-dose statins are being studied as a treatment for sarcopenia.
