Two years of structured exercise reversed up to 20 years of heart aging at age 50

Louis Nervegna · Mar 18, 2025 · ♥ 25 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevityHeart rhythm & heart failure
AI summary & key facts

Louis reshares a FoundMyFitness description of Dr. Benjamin Levine's trial, in which sedentary 50-year-olds who trained 5-6 hours per week for two years made their hearts structurally younger, more like a 30-year-old's. The protocol combined Norwegian 4x4 intervals, recovery aerobics, long endurance sessions and twice-weekly strength training.

  • 2 years, 5-6 hours per week of training in sedentary 50-year-olds
  • Heart structure reversed up to 20 years, closer to a 30-year-old
  • Norwegian 4x4: 4 min at ~85-95% HRmax, 3 min recovery, repeat 4 times
  • Plus 20-30 min recovery aerobics, 1+ hr endurance, 30-min base pace, strength twice weekly

It was a remarkable study. We talked about it before. But she does a good job of describing it here.

Shared from FoundMyFitness · original post

A 2-year exercise regimen reversed up to 20 years of heart aging in sedentary 50-year-olds

By training 5-6 hours per week, their hearts became structurally younger—more like a 30-year-old’s

The protocol used by Dr. Benjamin Levine’s team:

  • Norwegian 4x4 Intervals: 4 minutes at the maximum intensity you can sustain (for a given pace, this is usually ~85-95% HRmax), 3 minutes of low-intensity recovery (~70% HRmax), repeat 4x
  • Recovery Aerobics: 20-30 min light exercise
  • Endurance Sessions: 1+ hr endurance training, plus separate 30-min base pace weekly
  • Strength Training: Twice weekly

Norwegian 4x4 intervals: Rhonda Patrick on reversing heart aging and raising VO2 max

Oct 04, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 27 · 💬 29 Exercise & fitness

Summarises a Rhonda Patrick video: moderate exercise is ineffective for many, so vigorous HIIT such as the Norwegian 4x4 (four 4-minute efforts at about 80-90% max heart rate with 4-minute recovery) improves VO2 max. She cites a two-year study that reversed about 20 years of cardiac aging in sedentary 50-year-olds, and exercise reducing glycation-driven heart stiffness (Asif et al., PNAS 2000).

No drug is approved for sarcopenia; strength training and protein remain the foundation

A review summary (Rolland et al., Metabolism, Dec 2023) of drug options for age-related muscle loss. No drug has regulatory approval for sarcopenia, few trials enrolled diagnosed patients, and non-drug measures remain first-line.

Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.

Exercise-trained older adults' muscle looked younger at the molecular level

Sep 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 1 Exercise & fitness

A Nature Aging multi-omics study compared skeletal muscle from younger and older adults and found that about half of the age-related molecular differences were absent in exercise-trained older adults, who looked more like young muscle. Gene expression for cellular respiration and energy metabolism was preserved.

Review of 350+ studies links protein restriction to better metabolism and healthier aging

Aug 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 ↩ 1 Protein

Dudley Lamming and colleague reviewed over 350 studies and report that lower protein intake is associated with improved metabolism and healthier aging via mechanisms like a liver hormone. Lamming says benefits of protein are clear for active people but sedentary people may overeat it.

DO-HEALTH: omega-3 slowed epigenetic aging clocks in older adults

Aug 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 Aging & longevity

A post-hoc analysis of 777 adults over 70 randomized to vitamin D 2,000 IU, omega-3 1 g, home strength exercise or combinations for three years found omega-3 slowed three of four methylation clocks. These are surrogate markers.
