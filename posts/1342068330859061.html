Dementia rates fell 13% per decade from 1988, tracking the fall in heart disease deaths

Louis Nervegna · Jul 12, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an Economist note that dementia diagnoses fell 13% per decade across six countries from 1988 to 2015, and suggests statins and falling cardiovascular disease are involved. This is an inference.

  • Dementia fell 13% per decade 1988-2015 across six countries
  • Heart disease deaths down more than 50% since statins approved (Louis)

Statins were first approved by the FDA in the Fall of 1987. Other countries quickly followed suit.

Between 1988 and 2015, dementia rates have fallen by 13% per decade, across 6 major countries.

Coincidence? I doubt it.

Dementia and atherosclerosis are intimately connected.

Death rates from heart disease have fallen by more than 50% since statins were first approved in the USA. The reduction in dementia rates has tracked this reduction in CVD very well. See these posts:

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.

Observational, randomized and genetic evidence all argue against statins causing dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Statins

Louis shares a new observational study linking statin use to lower dementia risk and lists the wider evidence in the group: observational studies as a whole suggest protection, randomized trials (including in people over 70) show no increase in dementia, trials of statins as Alzheimer's treatment are neutral, and Mendelian randomization suggests lower dementia risk with statin-pathway variants. It is a curated index of earlier group posts.

Statins started within a year of type 2 diabetes tied to 15% lower dementia risk

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Statins

Louis shares a Danish observational study of over 132,000 patients with type 2 diabetes; starting statins within a year was tied to 15% lower dementia risk over a decade, driven by vascular dementia. He notes it hints at starting early, as the STAREE trial showed no effect when starting in old age.

Statin- and ezetimibe-like gene variants linked to 76-82% lower dementia risk per 40 mg/dL

Aug 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

Louis highlights a Mendelian randomization and meta-analysis of about 1 million people showing lifelong lower non-HDL cholesterol via statin-like (HMGCR) and ezetimibe-like (NPC1L1) variants was associated with 76% and 82% lower dementia per ~40 mg/dL drop. CETP variants showed a 70% reduction.

Statin users had 14% lower dementia risk in a meta-analysis of 7 million people

Aug 05, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Statins

A systematic review of observational studies found statin use associated with 14% lower all-cause dementia and 18% lower Alzheimer's. Louis corrects that these were cohort and case-control studies, not RCTs.
