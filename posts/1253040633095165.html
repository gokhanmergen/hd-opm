Yassine: Swedish APOE4 meat study underpowered for dementia but significant for cognition

Louis Nervegna · Mar 24, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaHow to read researchMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares Hussein Yassine's analysis of the Swedish study on meat and dementia in APOE4 carriers. The genotype interaction for cognitive trajectories was significant but the dementia interaction was not, reflecting limited power, and Yassine calls for genotype-randomized nutrition trials.

  • 569 APOE 3/4 and 4/4 participants; 296 dementia cases overall
  • Cognitive trajectory interaction P = .004
  • Dementia interaction P = .10, not significant, likely limited power

Commentary on the recent Swedish observational study suggesting that unprocessed meat (poultry or red meat) and fish might lower dementia risk in APOE4 carriers.

Red Meat, APOE4, and the Limits of Observational Nutrition Science

open.substack.comhttps://www.yassinelab.org/p/red-meat-apoe4-and-the-limits-of?r=5b3vnk&triedRedirect=trueopen.substack.com

Hussein Yassine, M.D. is Director of the Center for Personalized Brain Health at the Keck School of Medicine at USC and leads a lab doing ground-breaking research on APOE 4 and lipid biology.

Below are excerpts from his Substack column on the Swedish study posted on this page last week. Dr. Yassine explains what he thinks the study does--and doesn't--say to APOE 4 carriers and emphasizes the need for "gentotype randomized nutrition trials."

ANALYSIS OF FINDINGS AND CONFIDENCE INTERVALS

➤ With only 569 APOE 3/4 and 4/4 participants and 296 total dementia cases across all genotypes, the study was underpowered to reliably detect a genotype interaction for incident dementia — this is a harder, rarer endpoint than a continuously measured cognitive score.

➤ The directionality of the dementia finding is fully consistent with the cognitive trajectory result; it simply does not clear the significance threshold for that specific secondary outcome.

TAKE-AWAYS

🔹The primary finding — a statistically significant APOE interaction for cognitive trajectories (P = .004) — is the paper’s substantive claim.

🔹 The dementia interaction did not reach conventional significance (P = .10), which reflects limited power for that secondary endpoint RATHER THAN evidence against the cognitive finding. {Emphasis added.]

🔹 The study is observational. Self-reported diet, a single Swedish cohort, and modest APOE 3/4/ and 4/4 subgroup sizes constrain interpretation

🔹 The cognitive benefit was most pronounced for unprocessed meat. Processed meat increased dementia risk regardless of genotype.

🔹There is biologically plausible support for APOE4-specific dietary fat responses.

➤ The APOE4 brain has impaired glucose metabolism and compensates by preferring fat as an energy substrate.
➤ [Oskar] Hanson and colleagues showed that an acute high-fat meal improved cognition and AD biomarkers in APOE4 carriers with MCI but worsened them in non-carriers — exactly the kind of genotype-specific response the SNAC-K data reflect at a population level.

🔹This does not mean APOE4 carriers should eat more red meat or adopt a ketogenic diet.

➤ The fats that matter most for the APOE4 brain are heart-healthy good fats, obtained from fish and seafood, but also from unprocessed red meat or plant-based foods.
➤ Processed red meat increases dementia risk regardless of genotype and should be avoided.

🔹Dietary patterns are more important than individual foods.

➤ No single food drives or prevents Alzheimer’s disease. One example of a healthy pattern is the Mediterranean-style pattern — rich in fish, vegetables, whole grains, and olive oil, with modest unprocessed animal protein — remains the best-supported framework for cognitive health, including in APOE4 carriers.

🔹 Few participants habitually followed a Mediterranean dietary pattern, so the high-meat comparison group is not eating Mediterranean — it is eating less meat within a Northern European food context.

➤ This highlights the need for personalized, culturally adapted dietary guidance [with] underlying nutritional principles — adequate high-quality fat including red meat, omega-3 sufficiency, low processed food burden — and allows people to meet them through the foods and traditions they actually live with.

❤️ The right response to this paper is to design better trials, not to change dietary recommendations.

❤️ Genotype-stratified randomized nutrition trials are urgently needed. This study makes a compelling case for them.

APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.
