A 50:50 salt/potassium blend cuts sodium and adds potassium; avoid with kidney disease

Louis Nervegna · Dec 04, 2025 · ♥ 16 reactions · 17 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassiumKidney healthSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis recommends Morton Lite Salt, a 50:50 mix of regular salt and potassium chloride that is also iodized (60 mcg iodine per 1/4 teaspoon, 40% of the RDA). He warns people with kidney problems should not use potassium salts because excess potassium can cause fatal cardiac arrest.

  • 50% sodium chloride, 50% potassium chloride
  • Each 1/4 tsp has 60 mcg iodine (40% of RDA)
  • Warning: avoid potassium salts if you have kidney problems

This tastes just like regular salt.

It’s a 50:50 mix of regular salt with potassium chloride.

This is a good way to lower your sodium intake, while increasing your potassium intake at the same time.

I use this personally, and can’t really tell the difference from regular salt. There is no sacrifice in taste imo.

It’s also worth mentioning that this is iodized. Many people in the USA don’t get enough iodine anymore, in part because they have stopped using iodized salt. See this post: ↪ Iodine deficiency is rising in the UK, US and Australia as people drop iodized salt. Each 1/4 teaspoon contains 60mcg of iodine, which is 40% of the RDA.

————-

Warning:

If you have kidney problems, do NOT take potassium salts like this.

The kidneys regulate blood levels of potassium. Too much potassium can cause a fatal cardiac arrest in people with kidney problems.
