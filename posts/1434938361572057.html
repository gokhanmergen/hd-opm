RCTs show skipping breakfast does not cause weight gain and cuts about 260 calories a day

Louis Nervegna · Oct 29, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fasting & meal timingWeight, visceral & liver fat
AI summary & key facts

Observational studies link skipping breakfast to weight gain, but a 2019 BMJ review pooling 13 randomized trials found no evidence that eating breakfast aids weight loss or that skipping it causes weight gain. Breakfast skippers ate about 260 fewer calories per day overall. The possible downside is poorer glycemic control later in the day in people with diabetes or prediabetes.

Observational studies have associated skipping breakfast with weight gain.

But when you look only at randomized controlled trials, weight gain from skipping breakfast does not occur.

In fact, people who skip breakfast in RCT’s consume 260 less calories on average than people who do not.

The downside to skipping breakfast might be a decrease in glycemic control later in the day, if you are diabetic or prediabetic.

Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

Skipping breakfast didn't make people gain weight.

A 2019 review in The BMJ pooled 13 randomized trials. It found no evidence that eating breakfast helps people lose weight, and no evidence that skipping it causes weight gain.

People who skipped breakfast did eat somewhat more at lunch and dinner, but only partly made up the difference. Their total intake for the day came out lower, by roughly 260 calories a day in those trials. Newer reviews have found the same direction.

Breakfast has its own advantage, though. In a randomized trial in people with type 2 diabetes, skipping breakfast led to higher blood sugar and a weaker insulin response after both lunch and dinner, compared with days that started with breakfast. The first meal of the day helps the body handle the meals that follow. This effect is clearest in people with type 2 diabetes.

What this means for you: if your blood sugar is normal and you want to eat less overall, skipping breakfast is a reasonable choice, and there is no evidence it makes you overeat. If you have type 2 diabetes or prediabetes, eating breakfast helps keep blood sugar steadier through the day. For most people, how much and what you eat across the whole day matters far more than when you start.

Satchin Panda: wait 2 hours after waking to eat; finish dinner 3 hours before sleep

Mar 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 8 Fasting & meal timing

Louis summarizes meal-timing advice from circadian researcher Satchin Panda for glucose control. Delay breakfast at least two hours after waking because the pancreas regulates insulin poorly then, and eat the last large meal at least 3 hours before sleep since melatonin begins rising about 3 hours before bedtime and inhibits insulin secretion. This is expert advice from a podcast clip rather than a cited trial.
