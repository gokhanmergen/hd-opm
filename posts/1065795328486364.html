Turmeric liver case report criticised: 2.25 g is a normal intake, no contamination test

Louis Nervegna · Jul 11, 2025 · ♥ 30 reactions · 19 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis criticises a media-hyped case report of liver damage in a woman taking turmeric capsules, noting average daily turmeric intake in India is about 2.5 g and she took 2.25 g. He suggests contamination is the likelier cause and argues anecdotes are not clinical evidence.

  • Average daily intake in India about 2.5 g
  • Patient took capsules totalling 2.25 g of turmeric
  • Pills reportedly not tested for contamination

I want to comment on the ridiculousness of this case report.

The average daily intake of turmeric in India is about 2.5g per day. There are 1.4 billion people in India who consume this dose every single day.

This woman was taking capsules with a total of 2.25g of whole turmeric inside.

This is no different than just eating 2.5g of whole turmeric. Putting the turmeric powder inside a gelatin capsule does not magically make it bad for your liver. And this dose is certainly not a “10x” or “megadose”. It is a perfectly healthy amount of turmeric to consume daily.

The most likely explanation for her liver damage is that her capsules were contaminated with something toxic. The physicians involved here made absolutely no attempt to test her pills for contamination before they turned this whole thing into a media spectacle.

Shame on these people. This is no different from using the VAERS database to manufacture false side effects of vaccines. Anyone who prioritizes the use of rigorous science in the practice of medicine needs to reject this kind of anecdotal garbage and condemn the people who try to portray it as a legitimate form of clinical evidence.
