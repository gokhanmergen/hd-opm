Countries with the lowest milk intake have the lowest fracture rates, Willett notes

Louis Nervegna · Nov 08, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Walter Willett clip noting that countries with low milk consumption, such as Indonesia and much of Asia, have the lowest fracture rates. His NEJM work suggests calcium needs may be lower than official recommendations, and bone health depends on diet, movement and lifestyle.

  • Lowest-milk-intake countries show the lowest fracture rates
  • Calcium needs may be lower than official recommendations

You may be over-consuming calcium.

Quoted from the original post:

Milk isn't the bone health guarantee we've been sold.

Walter Willett points to something researchers have seen in the international data: countries with the lowest milk consumption, like Indonesia and much of Asia, actually have the lowest fracture rates, not the highest. His team published this in the New England Journal of Medicine. The takeaway isn't "milk is bad," it's that our calcium needs may be lower than the official recommendations suggest, and that bone health is built through a broader ecosystem of diet, movement, and lifestyle, not one fortified glass a day.

This is the Blue Zones pattern we keep coming back to: the healthiest, longest-lived populations aren't optimizing a single nutrient. They're living in a way that makes strong bones (and a long life) a byproduct, not a project.

NEJM 2020 dairy review: a mixed bag, best for fermented forms, weak for fractures

Jan 23, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 21 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis highlights the widely cited 2020 NEJM review on dairy and disease risk. It concludes that high dairy intake is not supported for preventing fractures, is not clearly linked to weight, diabetes or CVD, and may raise prostate and endometrial cancer risk while lowering colorectal cancer risk. Effects depend on the comparison food: dairy fares better than processed red meat or sugary drinks but worse than nuts.

Bisphosphonates for bone loss: fracture reductions summarized

Oct 13, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 4 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Hormones, women's & men's health

Review of bisphosphonate evidence for osteoporosis. Alendronate cuts vertebral and non-vertebral fractures by about 50% in women with prior vertebral fractures; yearly IV zoledronic acid cuts vertebral fractures 70% and hip fractures 40%; a 2017 meta-analysis found overall fracture OR 0.62. Not a heart topic but relevant to aging.

Zoledronic acid cut fractures 35% and all-cause death 28% in hip-fracture patients

Mar 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 7 Aging & longevity

Article suggesting bisphosphonates may extend life, noting a NEJM trial of yearly zoledronic acid after hip fracture that reduced new fractures by 35% and deaths from any cause by 28%. It also mentions possible cancer, heart and aging benefits, which remain speculative.

Prune Study: 50 g of prunes daily preserved hip bone density in postmenopausal women

Jan 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 9 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

The shared summary describes a 12-month RCT of 235 postmenopausal women randomized to no prunes, 50 g or 100 g of prunes per day. The 50 g group lost significantly less hip bone density than controls; 100 g added no benefit and had lower adherence.
