Daily peanuts improved brain blood flow, memory and BP in older adults in a crossover RCT

Research cited

  • Peanut consumption and brain vascular function, Maastricht University Medical Center · RCT
Shared from Doctor ASKY · original post

A study from Maastricht University Medical Center in the Netherlands found that consuming 60 grams of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks improved brain vascular function and memory in healthy adults aged 60–75. The randomized, controlled crossover trial of 31 participants showed a 3.6% increase in global cerebral blood flow (CBF) and a 5.8% improvement in verbal memory. Blood pressure also decreased, with systolic and pulse pressure reduced by 5 mmHg and 4 mmHg, respectively.

Advanced brain imaging using arterial spin labeling MRI revealed increased blood flow in the frontal and temporal lobes, regions critical for memory and cognition. Peanuts are rich in plant protein, L-arginine, unsaturated fats, polyphenols, and fiber in the skin, which may collectively support vascular function and antioxidant activity. These nutrients likely contribute to enhanced blood delivery to the brain, offering mechanistic insight into the observed cognitive benefits.

This research highlights how a simple, plant-based dietary intervention can positively influence brain health in aging populations. Regular consumption of skin-roasted peanuts may help maintain vascular brain function, potentially reducing age-related cognitive decline and supporting memory performance in older adults.
