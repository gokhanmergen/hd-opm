Evidence on protein for muscle: most benefit near 1.2 g/kg with resistance training

AI summary & key facts

Louis says extra protein has no proven benefit without resistance training, most benefit is reached near 1.2 g/kg, and there is no proven benefit beyond 1.5-1.6 g/kg.

  • Most benefit near 1.2 g/kg
  • No proven benefit beyond 1.5-1.6 g/kg

Protein recommendations from some famous influencers are not evidence-based.

What the preponderance of evidence actually shows is this:

Consuming extra protein beyond what you get in a normal diet has no proven benefit on muscle strength in the absence of resistance training. The majority of benefit in combination with resistance exercise is achieved around a daily intake of 1.2g/kg, for normal people. This is pretty close to what the average American eats anyway. The results modestly improve at higher levels, but there is no proven benefit beyond 1.5-1.6g/kg when combined with resistance exercise, unless you are on anabolic drugs (steroids).

Harvard summary: protein source matters for diabetes; red meat raises type 2 diabetes risk

Dec 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 1 ↩ 4 Protein

Louis quotes the Harvard Nutrition Source on protein and diabetes. Red meat, especially processed, predicts higher type 2 diabetes risk while nuts, legumes and poultry predict lower risk. Replacing a daily serving of red meat with nuts, low-fat dairy or whole grains was linked to 16-35% lower risk.

Nurses' Health Study: plant but not animal protein linked to lower frailty in older women

Aug 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 10 ↩ 3 Protein

Louis cites a 2022 Nurses' Health Study analysis in older women: higher plant protein intake was associated with lower frailty risk, but animal and dairy protein were not. Substituting 5% of energy from plant protein for animal, dairy or non-dairy animal protein was linked to 38%, 32% and 42% lower frailty risk respectively.

Midlife plant protein raised odds of healthy aging 22-58% per 3% of calories (NHS)

Aug 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 15 ↩ 7 Protein

Louis summarizes a Nurses' Health Study analysis of midlife protein and later-life health. Total and animal protein were unfavorably associated with chronic disease, while plant and dairy protein were favorable. Replacing 3% of energy with plant protein (from fats, carbs, animal or dairy protein) raised odds of healthy aging by 22-58%, and the protein came mainly from food rather than supplements.

Sports science says 1 g/lb protein is excessive; extra protein needs resistance training

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 19 ↩ 4 Protein

The poster says sports-science consensus is that muscle protein synthesis maxes out around 1.6 g/kg in natural bodybuilders, so 2.2 g/kg is wasteful. Extra protein above the RDA builds strength or muscle only when combined with resistance training, per dozens of randomized trials.
