Lieberman video: exercise grows more potent for longevity with age, via hormesis

Louis Nervegna · Oct 20, 2024 · ♥ 15 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis recommends a Daniel Lieberman talk on why exercise becomes more potent for longevity as we age. It covers the active grandparent hypothesis and hormetic stress, with exercise and plant molecules as examples. Louis stresses exercise is no magic bullet, but not doing it likely shortens life.

  • Active grandparent hypothesis: humans evolved to stay active as they age
  • Hormesis: mild stress such as exercise promotes longevity
  • Plant molecules are another hormetic stressor

One of the best videos on exercise that I’ve ever seen.

Our human ancestors likely evolved to exercise as they got older. This is called the “active grandparent hypothesis”.

The second half of the video has a beautiful explanation of the concepts of hormesis and hormetic stress, and why these make you live longer. Exercise is the prototypical example of hormetic stress. Plant molecules are another one (plants produce these mildly toxic molecules to ward off microorganisms).

No, exercise is not a magic bullet. But it’s something that we likely evolved to do so we could live long enough to actively participate in raising our grandchildren. Not doing it almost certainly shortens our lives.
