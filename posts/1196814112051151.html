Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

A study is making news today that blocking the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-11 can extend lifespan in mice by about 25%.

bbc.comAnti-ageing 'supermodel granny' drug extends life in animal testsThe drug is already being tested in people, but whether it would have the same effect is unknown.bbc.com

This is an inflammatory molecule produced in excess by our immune systems as we get older. It has been linked to numerous diseases, including heart disease.

Anti-inflammatory diets (like the traditional Mediterranean diet), exercise, and certain anti-inflammatory plant molecules, supplements, and drugs, can all possibly reduce IL-11 or downstream effects of IL-11. I put a list of some of these in the comments.

IL-11 is a member of the IL-6 family of cytokines, and statins inhibit this IL-6 family (see comments for references on this). So does aspirin, metformin, ACE inhibitors, and others.

It’s also worth noting that the heart disease drug colchicine might work, in part, by inhibiting IL-11. But this is speculative. See this post: ↪ Colchicine may work partly by inhibiting IL-11, a lifespan-linked pro-fibrotic cytokine.

Pelacarsen (Lp(a)HORIZON) and ziltivekimab (ZEUS) cut their targets but not heart events

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Shared summary of two NEJM trials. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) sharply but event rates were 12.3% vs 12.1% on placebo, and ziltivekimab cut CRP and IL-6 but events were 17.6% vs 17.7%. The post notes prior biomarker failures (torcetrapib, dalcetrapib), and that both trials enrolled people with existing disease and so do not prove Lp(a) or inflammation are not causal.
