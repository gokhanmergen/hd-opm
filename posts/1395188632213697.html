Autoimmune and inflammatory diseases linked to elevated Lp(a) in ~50,000 patients

Louis Nervegna · Sep 14, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Lp(a)Inflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

A JACC Advances study of nearly 50,000 patients found people with gout, ankylosing spondylitis and IBD are more likely to have elevated Lp(a), with rates approaching those in established heart disease. Louis notes this fits Lp(a) being highly responsive to acute-phase inflammation.

  • Nearly 50,000 patients
  • Conditions: gout, ankylosing spondylitis, IBD
  • Elevated Lp(a) rates approach those in established heart disease

People with inflammatory or autoimmune diseases are more likely to have high Lp(a).

This is not surprising. Lp(a) is highly sensitive to acute-phase inflammatory responses. While Lp(a) is primarily genetically determined, anything that activates these acute-phase inflammatory responses can strongly raise it. Big swings in Lp(a) usually reflect acute inflammation, not an intervention working

Jun 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Lp(a)

Louis explains that Lp(a) is largely genetic but behaves as an acute-phase reactant, so infection, surgery, extreme exercise, vaccine reactions or inflammatory flares can shift test results temporarily. Large changes are therefore usually due to inflammation, and blaming big jumps on statins can hide an underlying problem.

Pelacarsen (Lp(a)HORIZON) and ziltivekimab (ZEUS) cut their targets but not heart events

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Lp(a)

Shared summary of two NEJM trials. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) sharply but event rates were 12.3% vs 12.1% on placebo, and ziltivekimab cut CRP and IL-6 but events were 17.6% vs 17.7%. The post notes prior biomarker failures (torcetrapib, dalcetrapib), and that both trials enrolled people with existing disease and so do not prove Lp(a) or inflammation are not causal.

Mouse and human data suggest atherosclerosis meets criteria for an autoimmune disease

Oct 03, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 8 Inflammation & CRP

The post highlights a news report of a new study in mice and patients proposing that atherosclerosis meets the criteria for an autoimmune disease. Evidence is mostly from mouse models with supporting human correlational data, so it is early; more work is needed to learn how much of the disease this process drives and whether interrupting it could slow progression.

EBV likely causes multiple sclerosis and may contribute to other autoimmune diseases

Louis says it is now fairly clear Epstein-Barr virus causes MS, and EBV might play a role in other autoimmune diseases, possibly even heart disease, though that evidence is weaker. Vitamin D 2000 IU cut autoimmune disease risk 40% in the VITAL trial, MS a key target

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Supplements & vitamins

Louis resurfaces a three-year-old post: the strongest evidence for higher-dose vitamin D is in autoimmune disease, especially MS, and he notes a new JAMA publication on vitamin D treating MS. The VITAL trial of about 25,000 people at Harvard found that 2000 IU vitamin D daily for over 2 years cut autoimmune disease risk by about 40%. CNN coverage of the same trial reports about 30% lower risk with vitamin D and fish oil supplements.
