More coffee linked to better cognition and lower inflammation in older adults with AFib

Research cited

  • Coffee consumption and cognition in older adults with atrial fibrillation (PMID 39673298) · cohort · J Am Heart Assoc
Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

According to the U.S. federal dietary guidelines, three to five 8-ounce cups of coffee per day can be part of a healthy diet, but that only refers to plain black coffee.

A new study (PMID: 39673298) published in the Journal of the American Heart Association presented evidence linking coffee consumption to enhanced cognitive performance in older individuals with arterial fibrillation, a population at heightened risk for cognitive decline. The research utilized a comprehensive neurocognitive test battery, demonstrating a clear dose-dependent benefit. The study found:

  • Overall, higher cognitive test scores were associated with higher coffee consumption.
  • Scores for processing speed, coordination and attention improved significantly by 11% among coffee consumers compared to non-consumers.
  • Cognitive age was calculated to be 6.7 years younger among those who drank the most coffee compared to those who drank the least.
  • Inflammatory markers were more than 20% lower in participants drinking five cups daily than in participants drinking less than one cup daily.

Importantly, this research suggests that regular coffee consumption, even at moderate levels, may offer protective effects against cognitive decline in vulnerable populations. These findings reinforce the potential of dietary interventions as part of holistic management strategies.

Caffeinated coffee linked to 18% lower dementia risk over 43 years in two US cohorts

Nov 13, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 Brain health & dementia

Shared summary of a JAMA cohort of 131,821 US nurses and health professionals followed up to 43 years. The highest caffeinated coffee group had 18% lower dementia risk after adjustment, decaf showed no link, and the lowest risk was at about 2-3 cups of coffee or 1-2 cups of tea. Observational, with large confounding (heavy drinkers smoked more).

Caffeinated coffee linked to 37% lower Parkinson's and 34% lower Alzheimer's risk

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A shared summary says that in one large study unsweetened caffeinated coffee was linked to a 37% lower risk of Parkinson's disease and a 34% lower risk of Alzheimer's and related dementias. Caffeine blocks adenosine A2A receptors that regulate dopamine signaling in movement circuits. The evidence described is observational, with some animal mechanistic work.
