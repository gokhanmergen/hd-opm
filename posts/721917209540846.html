Nicotinic acid (vitamin B3) may prevent or treat glaucoma, possibly via NAD

Louis Nervegna · Dec 10, 2023 · ♥ 12 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes growing evidence that nicotinic acid, the niacin form used to lower cholesterol, may prevent or treat glaucoma, a disease with no effective treatment that causes irreversible blindness. The mechanism likely involves NAD. Most studies used nicotinic acid, so it is unclear how nicotinamide, NR or NMN compare. This is an eye-health tangent, supported by a video.

  • Most studies used nicotinic acid, the flushing form
  • Unclear whether nicotinamide, NR or NMN work the same
  • Proposed mechanism: NAD

The same form of niacin used to lower cholesterol (nicotinic acid) may also prevent or treat glaucoma.

The evidence that vitamin B3 can prevent or treat glaucoma keeps getting stronger and stronger.

This is a big deal because this disease inevitably causes irreversible blindness and has no effective treatment.

It seems likely that the underlying mechanism has something to do with NAD.

So far, most of the studies have used the nicotinic acid form of niacin— the same form that causes flushing and is used to lower cholesterol. It’s unclear how other forms of vitamin B3 would compare, e.g. niacinamide (nicotinamide), NR, NMN, etc.

Vitamin B3 users with high eye pressure were about two-thirds less likely to get glaucoma

Louis shares an observational study where patients with high eye pressure taking nicotinamide were about two-thirds less likely to be diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma. He cites a small 2020 RCT and a larger ongoing trial, with an NAD mechanism.

Vitamin B3 (nicotinamide) shows early promise for protecting retinal cells in glaucoma

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Supplements & vitamins

A shared summary says replenishing NAD+ with vitamin B3 may help retinal ganglion cells keep their energy supply and function longer in glaucoma. A 2020 randomized clinical trial of 57 people with glaucoma is cited as human evidence. Evidence is early and not specific to heart health.

Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.

Niacin (nicotinic acid) showed promise in a small Parkinson's trial, likely via brain NAD

Jun 25, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis highlights a small 2021 human trial of niacin in Parkinson's disease, noting it is the form of vitamin B3 once used to lower cholesterol and that benefit may work through raising NAD in the brain. Nicotinamide riboside has similar small-trial results, including a 2023 high-dose safety trial.

Whole-blood NAD+ does not change with age or lifestyle, so it is a poor aging marker

May 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis, echoing Charles Brenner, shares a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that human whole-blood NAD+ is stable across age and not altered by exercise or lifestyle interventions. Nicotinamide riboside raised NAD+, but that does not prove clinical or longevity benefit; tissue NAD matters.
