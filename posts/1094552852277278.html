BMJ: evidence mounts that COVID-19 disrupts immunity, challenging 'immunity debt' idea

Gokhan Can · Aug 20, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

A BMJ feature reports that the immunity debt theory is increasingly challenged and that SARS-CoV-2 may cause lasting immune disruption. Evidence includes more infections after covid than after flu or RSV, viral reactivation, T-cell exhaustion and persistent inflammation.

  • Study of over 830,000 US veterans: more bacterial, viral and fungal infections after covid and higher sepsis risk vs influenza

The BMJ article (Aug 2025) reports that the theory of “immunity debt”—the idea that pandemic restrictions left people more vulnerable to routine infections—is increasingly being challenged. Instead, a growing body of research suggests that SARS-CoV-2 itself may cause subtle but lasting disruptions to the immune system. Evidence includes higher post-covid infection rates compared to influenza/RSV, rising rare infections in children too young to have experienced pandemic restrictions, reactivation of latent viruses (EBV, shingles), T cell exhaustion, and persistent inflammation—even after mild cases. Large studies, including one tracking over 830,000 U.S. veterans, found increased bacterial, viral, and fungal infections after covid, as well as higher sepsis risk versus influenza. Some data also suggest long-term immune “scarring” and viral persistence. While some experts like Ashish Jha argue covid rarely damages immunity outside long covid, others contend the weight of evidence shows immune disruption is real, though variable across individuals. Overall, the article concludes that covid’s impact on immune health may be broader and more complex than once thought.

My $0.02: Avoid it like the plague. Avoid crowded places during covid surges, which you can track via waste water. Mask up if helpful. I have no interest in mRNA (too much sides), but I'd very happily take novavax if available.
