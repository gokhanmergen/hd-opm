Palmitic acid impairs insulin action while olive oil's oleic acid counteracts it

Louis Nervegna · Jun 24, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Fox News report on a review concluding that palmitic acid, the most common saturated fat in American foods, promotes inflammation, oxidative stress and insulin resistance, while oleic acid from olive oil, nuts and avocados counteracts these effects. Researchers back swapping saturated fats for extra-virgin olive oil rather than just cutting total fat.

  • Palmitic acid promotes inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction and beta-cell dysfunction
  • Oleic acid (olive oil, nuts, avocados) appears to preserve cell function and reduce inflammation
  • Review by Manuel Vazquez-Carrera, University of Barcelona

Quoted:

Palmitic acid promotes several molecular processes that impair insulin action," study investigator Dr. Manuel Vázquez-Carrera, from the Department of Pharmacology, Toxicology and Therapeutic Chemistry at the University of Barcelona, told Fox News Digital.

Too much palmitic acid can cause harmful fat byproducts to build up in the body, which can impair the body's ability to respond to insulin, he warned. This makes it harder to control blood sugar and increases the risk of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.

"It also promotes inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction and cellular stress responses, which contribute to insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction," Vázquez-Carrera added.

By contrast, oleic acid – a hallmark of the Mediterranean diet – does not have these harmful effects, according to the researcher.

"In fact, oleic acid can counteract many of the detrimental effects triggered by palmitic acid, by promoting the storage of fatty acids in relatively inert triglycerides, preserving mitochondrial function and reducing inflammation," he said.

“This means favoring dietary patterns rich in foods such as extra-virgin olive oil, nuts, seeds, legumes, vegetables, fruits and fish, while limiting excessive consumption of foods rich in saturated fats, especially highly processed foods," he advised.

OPPOSING EFFECTS: The most common saturated fat in American foods — palmitic acid — impairs the body's ability to respond to insulin and drives up type 2 diabetes risk.

Oleic acid, found in olive oil, nuts and avocados, appears to counteract those harmful effects by preserving cell function and reducing inflammation.

Researchers say the findings support swapping saturated fats for heart-healthy alternatives like extra-virgin olive oil rather than simply cutting total fat intake.
