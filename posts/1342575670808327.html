Rosuvastatin and Kidney Function
While rare, rosuvastatin can cause kidney stress, particularly at high doses. Clinical data has associated it with a small dose-dependent risk of transient proteinuria (protein in the urine) and hematuria (blood in the urine). In very rare cases, acute kidney injury (AKI) can occur. This small risk increases with a high 40 mg dose or in patients with pre-existing chronic kidney disease.

The differences in the behavior of various statins when it comes to the kidneys was highlighted by the PLANET I and II randomized clinical trials, which evaluated patients with pre-existing proteinuria. The trials demonstrated that high-dose rosuvastatin (40 mg) significantly lowered eGFR (kidney filtration rate) and was associated with a higher incidence of renal adverse events like AKI. In contrast, high-dose atorvastatin (80 mg) significantly reduced proteinuria and preserved kidney function:

Fortunately, in cases of statin-induced renal stress, kidney function typically returns to baseline once the medication is discontinued.

For the vast majority of patients on standard doses, rosuvastatin is safe and does not cause renal issues. In fact, certain statins are recognized for potentially protective cardiovascular and renal pathways. A large post-hoc analysis of 6 double-blind randomized controlled trials published in the Journal of the American Heart Association in 2019 demonstrated that atorvastatin actually improves kidney function over time in a dose-dependent manner:

Because of these nuances, official FDA drug labeling advises caution when prescribing rosuvastatin to patients with renal impairment. For individuals with severe kidney disease (not on dialysis), the clinical guidelines state that the daily dose should not exceed 10 mg.

Atorvastatin is generally preferred over rosuvastatin in chronic kidney disease

Dec 08, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Statins

Louis shares an MRCP UK explainer on statins in CKD: atorvastatin needs no renal dose adjustment (under 2% renal excretion), while rosuvastatin is 30% renally excreted, needs dose reduction at low eGFR and can cause reversible proteinuria at high doses. KDIGO advises a statin with or without ezetimibe in CKD stages 3-5 not on dialysis.
