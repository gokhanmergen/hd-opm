Only gel-forming fibers such as psyllium and beta-glucan lower LDL

Louis Nervegna · Nov 04, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a video explaining that fibers lower LDL by forming a gel that traps bile acids, forcing the liver to pull LDL from blood. Psyllium, oat and barley beta-glucan and guar gum gel, while inulin, FOS and wheat bran do not. Psyllium lowered LDL about 13 mg/dL across 28 trials and about 9 mg/dL in a 2025 analysis of 41 trials.

  • Gel-forming: psyllium, beta-glucan (oats, barley), raw guar gum
  • Non-gelling: inulin, FOS, wheat bran
  • Gut recycles about 95% of bile acids
  • Psyllium: about -13 mg/dL LDL (28 trials); about -9 mg/dL (41 trials, 2025)

Only fibers that form a gel when mixed with water actually lower LDL.

Quoted from the original post:

Which fiber actually lowers your LDL? Psyllium vs inulin: only one gels, and the gel is the part that matters.

The bile-acid trap: your liver makes bile acids from cholesterol, and your gut recycles about 95% of them. A gel-forming fiber traps them, so more leave your body. To make new ones, your liver pulls LDL out of your blood.

Forms a gel: psyllium, oat and barley beta-glucan, raw guar gum.
Doesn't gel: inulin, FOS, wheat bran.

The gel test: if it dissolves clear, it isn't working through the bile-acid trap. (Inulin-type fibers may lower LDL a little without a gel, but that evidence is very low certainty.)

Across 28 trials, psyllium lowered LDL by about 13 mg/dL (Jovanovski et al., Am J Clin Nutr, 2018). A 2025 meta-analysis of 41 trials found about 9 mg/dL (Gholami and Paknahad, Genes Nutr, PMID 41366295).
