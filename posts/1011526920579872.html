Letter to JACC: Keto-CTA omitted pre-specified outcome, a 43% rise in non-calcified plaque

Louis shares a letter to the editor critiquing the Keto-CTA study of lean mass hyper-responders. It argues the primary outcome (non-calcified plaque volume) was not reported in the paper, but the lead author later shared a median increase of 18.8 mm3 (~43%), several-fold above normal annual progression and in contrast to regression seen in DISCO-CT. The 0.8% rise in percent atheroma volume also exceeds typical annual change in PARADIGM.

  • Primary outcome NCPV pre-registered (NCT05733325) but not reported in main text
  • Median NCPV increase 18.8 mm3 (~43%), 3.8x the 4.9 mm3 annual progression in healthy individuals
  • DISCO-CT: NCPV fell 21.3 mm3 (medical therapy) and 51.3 mm3 (plus DASH diet)

Rapid Plaque Progression Amongst Lean Mass Hyper-Responders Following a Ketogenic Diet with Elevated ApoB and LDL-Cholesterol

Letter to the Editor JACC (submitted)

———-

Quoted:

“The recent publication by Soto Mota et al. (1) raises significant concerns regarding data reporting practices particularly due to the omission of key pre-specified outcome data and the apparent bias in how the findings have been framed and interpreted.

Although the study was pre-registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05733325) with the primary outcome defined as percent change in non-calcified plaque volume (NCPV), a value for this outcome is not presented in the main body of the paper. Instead, individual level NCPV data is presented in figures presenting pre/post values for 100 participants, and the authors provide only a vague reference to “stable NCPV” across participants, omitting statistical analyses and precise values.

Since publication, the lead author has shared (Figure 1) that NCPV increased by a median 18.8 mm³, representing an approximate 43% rise from baseline, indicating that this value was calculated, available and intentionally omitted from the publication. This NCPV increase of 18.8 mm³ exceeds, by 3.8-fold, the median annualised NCPV progression rate of 4.9 mm³ observed in healthy individuals (2). For further context, this stands in stark contrast to data from DISCO-CT, where non-diabetic patients with non-obstructive coronary atherosclerosis experienced NCPV regression with either optimal medical therapy alone (mean change: -21.3 mm3) or optimal medical therapy with dietitian-led advice to adhere to the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension diet (mean change: -51.3 mm3) (3).

Furthermore, the reported secondary outcome of percent atheroma volume (PAV) is stated as a 0.8% increase. For comparison, using data from the PARADIGM study (4), this is almost 2-fold greater than the annualized rate of change of 0.45% in the low-risk strata and higher than the 0.58% increase observed in the high-risk strata (4). More notably, it approaches the 0.93% annual PAV increase previously associated with elevated risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in participants who experienced no cardiovascular events between serial coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) scans (5).
