Mendelian randomization in 1 million people: statin-like genetics cut dementia risk 76-82%

Summarises Dr Stanfield's video on a Mendelian randomization study of over a million people. Short RCTs show no cognitive harm or benefit and observational studies suggest protection; genetic variants mimicking lifelong statin and ezetimibe action were linked to 76-82% relative dementia risk reduction, implying long-term benefit missed by short trials.   • Over one million people

  • Short-term RCTs show no cognitive harm or benefit

How to Lower Your Dementia Risk?

Dr. Brad Stanfield examines the conflicting evidence on whether cholesterol-lowering drugs like statins affect dementia risk. He explains that while short-term randomized control trials (RCTs) show no cognitive harm or benefit, observational studies suggest a strong protective effect. The video's focus is a new Mendelian randomization study of over one million people, which helps resolve this paradox by simulating the effects of lifelong medication use through genetics. This new study found that genetic variants mimicking the long-term action of statins and ezetimibe were linked to a massive 76-82% relative risk reduction for dementia, suggesting the protective benefits are long-term and not captured by shorter trials. Dr. Stanfield concludes by highlighting other crucial, modifiable risk factors for dementia, including the importance of treating hearing loss, vision loss, and midlife depression, as well as staying cognitively active.

Mendelian randomization: higher ApoB shortens healthspan and may raise Alzheimer's risk

Feb 25, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 25 · 💬 4 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Summarizes Martin et al. 2024, a Mendelian randomization study finding that genetically higher ApoB shortens healthspan (time to first chronic disease), with a direct effect independent of LDL. ApoB, but not LDL, also appeared to raise Alzheimer's risk, though the replication attenuated in a larger sample.

Mendelian randomization: BMI, BP, LDL and type 2 diabetes lower odds of longevity

Jan 25, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

A Mendelian randomization study of 46 modifiable risk factors in people of European ancestry found nine linked to longevity (90+ years) and super-longevity (99+). Higher genetically predicted HDL was associated with greater odds of longevity, while higher BMI, obesity, blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, LDL and total cholesterol were associated with lower odds.
