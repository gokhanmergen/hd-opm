Moli-sani: more than 3 tablespoons of olive oil daily linked to 28% lower cancer mortality

A study published in 2024 (PMID: 38704428) in the Moli-sani Study dataset looked at the association between olive oil consumption and cancer mortality among Italian adults. The analysis, which followed 22,895 participants over 12 years, found that consuming more than 3 tablespoons of olive oil daily (1 tablespoon = 10 g) was associated with a 28% lower risk of cancer mortality compared to those consuming less than 1.5 tablespoons per day.

Findings:

  • Linear Dose-Response: Higher olive oil consumption correlated with progressively lower cancer mortality, with each 1-tablespoon increment associated with a significant reduction in risk. A threshold effect was observed at >3 tablespoons/day.
  • Biological Pathways: The study identified partial mediation of olive oil’s benefits via reductions in systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and resting heart rate. These accounted for 14.5% and 8.1% of the association with all-cause and cancer mortality, respectively.
  • Bioactive Composition: Olive oil's effects are attributed to its rich content of monounsaturated fatty acids (e.g., oleic acid) and phytochemicals like hydroxytyrosol (abundant in the fruit oil), which exhibit anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-thrombotic properties.

Its worth noting the observational nature does not allow for this study to establish a causal relationship between olive oil consumption and reduced all cause mortality. Dietary data were self-reported. The findings may be specific to an Italian Mediterranean population. Additionally, the study did not distinguish between different types of olive oil, potentially affecting the results.
