Walter Willett's daily diet: oats, nuts, salad, whole grains, chicken or fish, vegetables

Louis Nervegna · Nov 05, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Harvard Chan School piece on what Walter Willett eats: steel-cut oats with yogurt, nuts and blueberries, a large salad or whole-grain peanut butter sandwich for lunch, and chicken or fish with vegetables for dinner. His summary advice is to eat a variety of healthy, mostly plant-based foods in an enjoyable way.

  • Breakfast: steel-cut oats, yogurt, nuts, blueberries, small orange juice
  • Lunch: large salad or whole grain peanut butter sandwich
  • Dinner: chicken or fish with vegetables

#willettHD

Shared from Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health · original post

Walter Willett, professor of epidemiology and nutrition at Harvard Chan School, has played a significant role in illuminating how diet and other lifestyle choices can impact chronic disease risk. In a recent interview, he shared what he eats in a day as the world’s most highly cited nutrition expert.

Willett likes to start the day with steel-cut oats topped with yogurt, nuts, and fruit—usually blueberries, which have been shown to be protective against neurodegenerative diseases—in addition to a small glass of orange juice. He likes to have a plant-based meal for lunch, often choosing a large salad from the School’s salad bar, or a sandwich on whole grain bread (usually peanut butter with a little bit of jam or honey) and a carrot if he’s at home. For dinner, he and his wife will usually have chicken or fish with vegetables.

“If I could put 40 years of research into one answer, it would be to eat a variety of healthy, mostly plant-based foods, and find a way to do it that’s enjoyable,” said Willett.
