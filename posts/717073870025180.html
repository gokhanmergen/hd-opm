Vegan and vegetarian diets: trials showing plaque regression, compared with low carb

Gokhan Can · Nov 30, 2023 · ♥ 2 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Reviews diet trials that showed regression of coronary disease, notably the Lifestyle Heart Trial of a very low-fat vegetarian diet plus stress management and exercise. The author thinks plant-based evidence is stronger than for low carb but says he could not sustain it.

  • Lifestyle Heart Trial (n=42): stenosis 40% to 37.8% at 1 year vs progression 42.7% to 46.1% in usual care
  • Greater regression and fewer cardiac events at 5 years
  • An open 2-year trial (n=123) of a plant-based approach is also cited (Mount Abu)

Vegan/vegetarian

(Vegan/vegetarian diets have stronger evidence for reversing heart disease than low carb. However, it's a matter of palatability and sustenance -- I personally couldn't sustain it for over a few weeks.)

  • The Lifestyle Heart Trial (n=42) combined a very low-fat vegetarian diet with stress management and moderate exercise. Percent diameter stenosis regressed from 40% to 37.8% after 1 year in the intervention group, compared to progression from 42.7% to 46.1% in usual care. Greater regression was seen after 5 years along with fewer cardiac events (Ornish et al., 1990; Ornish et al., 1998). Can lifestyle changes reverse coronary heart disease? The Lifestyle Heart Trial. (Lancet, 1990)
  • An open 2-year trial (n=123) of a plant-based diet, exercise, stress management showed 6.1% average reduction in diameter stenosis. Most adherent tertile had 18.2% regression with 4-fold lower cardiac events than least adherent (38 vs 11 events) (Gupta et al., 2011).   • A 2-year vegetarian diet cohort study (n=39) showed no lesion progression in 18 patients, but no regression. Lesion growth correlated with higher cholesterol/HDL ratio (Arntzenius et al., 1985).   • The STARS diet trial (n=90) over 39 months reduced coronary narrowing progression to 15% and 12% with diet interventions compared to 46% with usual care (Watts et al., 1992). Effects on coronary artery disease of lipid-lowering diet, or diet plus… (Lancet, 1992)
