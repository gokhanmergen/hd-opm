Spinach raises blood antioxidant activity more than 1250 mg vitamin C

AI summary & key facts

Louis argues against the claim that vitamin C supplements are harmful because antioxidants can blunt exercise adaptations in short-term studies. He notes that a large serving of spinach raises blood antioxidant activity more than a 1250 mg vitamin C supplement, and that short-term biomarker studies often predict long-term outcomes poorly. He does not claim antioxidant supplements prevent disease; human RCTs have mostly failed, with exceptions like age-related macular degeneration.

  • One large serving of spinach raised blood antioxidant activity more than 1250 mg vitamin C (USDA ARS report)
  • Human RCTs of antioxidant supplements have mostly been disappointing, with exceptions such as AMD
  • Short-term studies of indirect biomarkers are poor predictors of long-term outcomes

I’d like to address the claim that vitamin C supplements are somehow harmful because they are antioxidants… and short term studies have shown that antioxidants can blunt adaptations to exercise.

I will simply point out that a single large serving of spinach raises the antioxidant activity in blood more than a 1250mg vitamin C supplement (see link below for a description of the study which showed this).

I am not making any claims that vitamin C supplements (or any other antioxidant supplement) will prevent major diseases or slow aging. Human RCT’s with antioxidant supplements have mostly failed. There are some notable exceptions, e.g. age-related macular degeneration, but for the most part the human studies have been disappointing.

I am simply pointing out that if you are really worried about vitamin C causing harm by blocking the health benefits of exercise, then you should be more worried about high ORAC fruits and vegetables like spinach, which of course is ridiculous. Biology is complex, and short term studies that look at indirect biomarkers are often very poor predictors of what really happens in the long term.
