Proposed mechanism for coffee's protection against neurodegenerative disease

Louis Nervegna · Aug 14, 2026 · ♥ 1 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Quoted podcast excerpt: lifelong coffee drinking is associated with slower cognitive decline and lower risk of Parkinson's, Alzheimer's and vascular dementia, possibly because caffeine blocks adenosine receptors and makes the brain work harder.

A proposed mechanism for why coffee seems to protect against neurological diseases like Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s, and vascular dementia.

Quoted:

Lifelong coffee consumption can help to slow cognitive decline as we age and is also protective against neurodegenerative diseases like Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s and vascular dementia.

Why is this? According to Professor Astrid Nehlig, from the French Institute of Health and Medical Research, who joined us on the Change Your Mind podcast, coffee makes your brain work harder by blocking the adenosine receptors that make us feel tired – giving it a kind of workout.

Elsewhere on the episode, she told hosts Cat de Lange and Helen Thomson how much coffee is the optimum dose and why you should never add milk or sugar.
