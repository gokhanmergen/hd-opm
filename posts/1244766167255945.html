Statins do not cause dementia or cognitive decline and may be protective in cohorts

Louis Nervegna · Mar 13, 2026 · ♥ 15 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes Katie Couric Media: in 30+ lipid-lowering studies no link with dementia was found, and a meta-analysis of 55 observational studies (7.7 million people) showed lower dementia risk with statins; many trials had cognition as a secondary outcome.

  • At least 30 studies found no association with dementia
  • 55 observational studies, 7.7 million people, lower dementia risk with statins
  • Greatest effect with longer use (3+ years) and in type 2 diabetes

Quoted:

In at least 30 studies of lipid-lowering medications, there was no association between these medications and dementia risk. “Multiple randomized trials, large cohort studies, and meta-analyses have not shown that statins or other LDL-lowering therapies increase dementia risk or cause persistent cognitive decline,” says Dr. Alon Gitig, Director of Preventive Cardiology at the Atria Health Institute, “however, many of these studies were not designed with cognition as a primary outcome. Ongoing dedicated trials will further clarify effects.”

Most of these studies followed participants for two to five years, with some extending up to 25 years. Across multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses, the findings are strikingly consistent: There is no difference in cognitive outcomes — including memory, executive function, or overall cognition — between statin users and non-users. This holds true even among older adults and those taking high doses of statins.

Recently, the largest systematic review and meta-analysis conducted to date evaluated 55 observational studies encompassing a total of 7.7 million people. This study demonstrated the neuroprotective effects of statins. Statin use was associated with a significantly reduced risk of dementia compared to nonusers, for both Alzheimer’s disease and vascular dementia. In addition, longer duration of use (>3 years), and for people with type 2 diabetes mellitus, and populations from Asia, are where the greatest protective effect was observed. Rosuvastatin (a hydrophilic statin) showed a stronger protective effect for all-cause dementia with a risk reduction of 28 percent.

Amyloid-beta drives excess astrocyte cholesterol and impairs brain waste clearance in mice

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience mouse study found amyloid-beta raises calcium activity in astrocytes, boosting cholesterol production and removing AQP4 water channels, which impairs the glymphatic waste-clearing system. Calming astrocytes, blocking cholesterol synthesis or giving atorvastatin restored clearance and memory in mice. Human trials are needed, but the mechanism may partly explain observational links between statin use and lower dementia risk.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.

Statins started within a year of type 2 diabetes tied to 15% lower dementia risk

Sep 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Statins

Louis shares a Danish observational study of over 132,000 patients with type 2 diabetes; starting statins within a year was tied to 15% lower dementia risk over a decade, driven by vascular dementia. He notes it hints at starting early, as the STAREE trial showed no effect when starting in old age.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+, not disability-free survival

A shared summary of the STAREE trial presented at ESC 2026: 9,971 adults 70+ without CVD, diabetes or dementia received atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Major cardiovascular events fell 30% (6.0% vs 8.3%) but disability-free survival did not improve significantly.

STAREE: statins reduced events 30% in healthy adults 70+, NNT 37, no dementia increase

Aug 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 ↩ 1 Statins

A Vital Human summary of STAREE: 9,971 adults averaging about 75 years, atorvastatin 40 mg versus placebo for a median 5.9 years. Major CV events fell 30% (HR 0.70) with an NNT of 37, but no significant difference in overall mortality or disability-free survival and no increase in dementia.
