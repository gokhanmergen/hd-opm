I want to comment on this “sugar diet” that has recently become popular.

This is not a healthy long-term diet.
I definitely would not recommend it for long-term use.

BUT there are some theoretical reasons why it probably works. And there are compelling experiments in mice that back those reasons up. A very old study in humans also offers some confirmation.

Isoleucine restriction extends lifespan by 33% in male HET3 mice, and dramatically improves insulin sensitivity and fascilitates weight loss.

These type of mice are the gold standard used in aging research, because they are genetically heterogenous just like mice in the wild. They are the same mice used in the Interventions Testing Program (ITP) to study aging at the NIH.

This 33% lifespan extension is comparable to the effects of 30% calorie restriction in male mice, and beats every single intervention ever tested in males in the ITP.

(In females, the lifespan extension was 7%.)

Has this diet ever been tested in humans?

Yes, it has… in the 1940’s.
But it has been forgotten.

It was called the “rice diet” at that time, and it consisted of a normal (not restricted) number of calories that came entirely from white rice, white sugar, and fruit juice. This diet had the minimum isoleucine necessary to survive.

It showed shocking results in short term controlled feeding studies in humans. People lost an enormous amount of weight, and diabetes was reversed. (We now know that clearing out the liver and pancreas of ectopic fat reverses diabetes.)

Protein restriction and aging: animal benefits, no proven human lifespan gain

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Protein

Louis shares a summary of a review (Knopf and Lamming) of protein and amino-acid restriction. Restricting methionine or the branched-chain amino acids isoleucine and valine drives many animal metabolic benefits, FGF21 is involved, but lifespan extension has not been shown in humans. The field is shifting from 'less protein' to which amino acids matter, and older or active adults may need more protein. Louis says protein is complicated and influencers oversimplify it.

Protein type matters more than restriction: whole-food plant protein beats processed meat

Nov 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 38 Protein

Louis argues against the 'protein craze': there is no good evidence protein restriction slows human aging, but long-term data show whole-food plant protein beats animal protein, especially processed and red meat. Among animal sources, seafood, poultry and fermented dairy have the lowest mortality and processed meat is worst. Plant protein's lower isoleucine, valine and methionine may explain part of it.
