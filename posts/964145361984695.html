Dr Brad Stanfield: 'normal' systolic BP near 140 raises dementia and CVD risk

Gokhan Can · Feb 09, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post

Summary of a Dr Brad Stanfield video linking high blood pressure to dementia. Citing SPRINT and a follow-up study, it says lowering systolic pressure below 120 reduces heart attacks, strokes, death and dementia, and recommends salt under 1500 mg/day, the DASH diet, exercise, weight control and medication when needed.

  • Systolic below 120 mmHg associated with fewer heart attacks, strokes, deaths and dementia (SPRINT and follow-up)
  • Salt 1500 mg/day or less
  • DASH diet and regular exercise, including short 'exercise snacks'

This video by Dr. Brad Stanfield discusses the link between high blood pressure and an increased risk of dementia, emphasizing that even blood pressure levels previously considered "healthy" (around 140 systolic) are now known to be harmful. He cites studies like the SPRINT study and a follow up study, showing that lowering systolic blood pressure to below 120 significantly reduces the risk of heart attacks, strokes, death, and dementia. The video explains how high blood pressure damages the brain's blood vessels over time, leading to cognitive decline. Dr. Stanfield recommends strategies to lower blood pressure, including reducing salt intake to 1500mg or less per day, adopting the DASH diet (rich in fruits, vegetables, and low-fat dairy), incorporating regular exercise (even short "exercise snacks"), and managing weight. He also mentions the use of medications as an additional tool when lifestyle changes are insufficient, while a salt substitute is also a good option.
