ASPC releases an upside-down food pyramid based on global cardiology guidelines

Louis Nervegna · Mar 23, 2026 · ♥ 18 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes the American Society for Preventive Cardiology's dietary guidance, drawn from major cardiology associations. It emphasizes fruits and vegetables, minimal processed food and added sugar, fish and plant proteins, whole grains, unsaturated oils and limited sodium, red and processed meat and alcohol.

  • More than 5 servings of fruits and vegetables per day
  • Sugar-sweetened beverages should be zero
  • Sodium limit 2,300 mg, lower for high-risk people
  • Favor lower-fat dairy, unsaturated plant oils such as olive oil
  • Alcohol: up to 1 drink/day for women, 2 for men

The American Society for Preventative Cardiology (ASPC) has released their own upside-down food pyramid.

It is based on guidelines from major cardiology associations around the world.

Reference:

Paper · Am J Prev Cardiol · 2026The new Dietary Guidelines for Americans: When national nutritional policy outpaces the evidence.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Here is a summary of the recommendations:

  1. Emphasize fruits and vegetables.
5 serving per day

Choose a wide variety.

  1. Minimize processed foods.
  1. Minimize added sugars

Consumption of sugar-sweetened beverages should be zero. Drink water.

  1. For protein, emphasize fish and plant proteins like legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains.

There is no set protein goal. Most people already get enough.

  1. Limit processed meat and red meat.
  1. Emphasize whole grains over refined grains.
  1. Limit sodium intake to 2300mg

Even lower for people at high risk.
Avoid salty packaged/restaurant foods.

  1. Emphasize lower fat dairy over full fat.
  1. Use unsaturated plant oils, like olive oil, in place of animal fats.

Limit saturated fat.

  1. If drinking alcohol, stick to moderate limits.

Maximum of 1 drink per day for women, and 2 for men.
