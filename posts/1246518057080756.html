About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.

Louis explains that soft tissue calcification is an actively regulated process that can sometimes be reversed, for example in calcific tendonitis, through macrophage-driven dissolving. Whether artery calcification can be reversed is poorly understood.

Louis tells group members with high CAC that the risk is high but manageable: about 30 lb weight loss clears liver and pancreas fat and can reverse soft plaque, LDL below 70 mg/dL often halts or reverses plaque, a Mediterranean diet has the most evidence, and exercise lowers risk at the same CAC.
