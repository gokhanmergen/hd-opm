Centenarians' longevity is tied to genes that lower insulin/IGF-1 signaling (Barzilai)

Louis Nervegna · May 13, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Nir Barzilai clip arguing that centenarians owe their long lives largely to genetic variants lowering insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR signaling, strong enough to offset habits like smoking and obesity. Barzilai says women with the lowest IGF-1 in old age live twice as long as those with the highest. Louis adds that diet and lifestyle can help keep insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR lower.

  • Low insulin/IGF-1 genetic variants common in centenarians
  • Per Barzilai, centenarian women with lowest IGF-1 live twice as long as those with the highest
  • Those women also had better cognitive function

The factor most responsible for the long life of centenarians appears to be genes that lower the levels of insulin/IGF1 (and consequently mTOR).

These genetic variants that lower IGF1 in centenarians appear strong enough to cancel out bad habits like heavy smoking, obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and bad genes like APOE4 and many others.
