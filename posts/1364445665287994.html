UNATI trial will test red wine versus abstaining as if alcohol were a medication

Louis Nervegna · Aug 08, 2026 · ♥ 10 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis describes UNATI, a large randomized trial of about 10,000 people testing a set daily wine dose against reducing alcohol, with cardiovascular disease as the primary question and cancer and all-cause mortality as secondary endpoints.

  • ~10,000 participants
  • Cardiovascular disease primary; cancer and mortality secondary
  • Registered as NCT06338215

The UNATI Trial - Alcohol & CVD

(University of Navarra Alumni Trialist Initiative)

We are finally seeing a large long-running randomized controlled trial that is directly testing alcohol (red wine) verses cardiovascular disease.

The official website for the trial can be found in this post:
↪ UNATI: 10,000-person randomized trial of moderate red wine vs reducing alcohol

The study involves 10,000 people, and is running for 5 years. It will also report on secondary endpoints of cancer and all-cause mortality.

They are testing alcohol in this trial as if it were a medication.

One group is randomized to consume a set dose of wine each day, and the other group is randomized to consume an alcohol-free alternative. Everything else is the same between the two groups. Wine is the only variable.

The trial is being run in the same style as the famous PREDIMED trial that tested a traditional Mediterranean diet vs CVD. In fact, it is being run by the same principle investigator as PREDIMED.

Given the people involved, this should be a very high quality trial.

It will finally give us the truth on mild-to-moderate alcohol consumption and health, whatever that may be.

Quoted:

“Randomized controlled trials are needed to ensure that potential benefits or harms from alcohol consumption are adequately assessed, Martínez-González said. He and his colleagues at the University of Navarra received funding for such a study earlier this year. The study is recruiting physicians ages 50 to 75, who will be randomized to moderate drinking or abstention groups. Both groups will receive free beverages—red wine or an alcohol-free alternative—during the five-year study.”

“So, should we remove wine from the Mediterranean diet?” he asked. “Yes, definitely for those under 35 years.” But for older adults, it’s more complicated. He and his colleagues, and other researchers, have found that the Mediterranean diet loses up to 23.5% of its protective effect if wine is removed.”

UNATI, led by Martinez-Gonzalez, will randomize about 10,000 drinkers (men 50-70, women 55-75, 3-40 drinks per week) to reduce alcohol or drink moderately (under 7/week women, under 14/week men, red wine with meals) for about 4 years.
