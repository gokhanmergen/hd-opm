AHA recommends 3.5-5 g of potassium daily, preferably from food, for blood pressure

Louis Nervegna · Sep 04, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassiumGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes the AHA explanation that potassium blunts the effect of sodium, increases sodium loss in urine and eases tension in blood vessel walls. Adults with elevated or high blood pressure who are otherwise healthy may benefit from more dietary potassium.

  • AHA: 3.5 g to 5 g potassium per day, preferably from diet
  • More potassium means more sodium lost in urine
  • Applies to BP 120/80 or higher in otherwise healthy adults

To help prevent or treat high blood pressure, the American Heart Association (AHA) recommends 3.5g to 5g of potassium per day, preferably from diet.

Quoted:

“Foods rich in potassium are important in managing high blood pressure, also known as hypertension. Potassium can reduce the effects of sodium. Too much sodium has been shown to increase blood pressure. The more potassium you eat, the more sodium you lose through urine. Potassium also helps to ease tension in your blood vessel walls, which also helps lower blood pressure.

Adults with elevated blood pressure (120/80 mm Hg or higher) or high blood pressure (130/80 mm Hg or higher) who are otherwise healthy may benefit from increasing potassium in their diet.”
