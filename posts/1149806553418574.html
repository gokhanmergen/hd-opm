Exercise mortality benefit plateaus near 40% at 5-10 h/week moderate plus 2.5-5 h vigorous

Louis Nervegna · Nov 01, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis cites pooled Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals data (over 116,000 people) in which 5-10 hours of moderate and 2.5-5 hours of vigorous weekly exercise gave the largest mortality reduction, about 40%. He cautions that part of this may reflect reverse causality.

  • 116,000+ participants followed for decades
  • Maximum mortality reduction about 40%
  • Part of the effect may be reverse causality

In higher quality long-term studies, the mortality benefit from frequent exercise maxes out at about a 40% reduction. That’s probably the best you can do in the long term from exercise alone.

(A major caveat here is that a portion of this mortality benefit is likely due to reverse causality: ↪ Finnish twin study suggests exercise's mortality benefit is partly reverse causality.)

What’s the optimal amount of exercise for reducing mortality?

According to recently published pooled results from both the Harvard Nurse’s Study and the Harvard Physician’s Health Study (over 116,000 people), 5-10 hours of moderate exercise and 2.5 to 5 hours of vigorous exercise weekly resulted in the greatest mortality reductions. Combining those amounts of moderate and vigorous exercise led to a maximum mortality reduction of about 40%.

The advantages of high quality prospective cohort studies like these is that they show long term effects. The exercise habits of these nurses and physicians were tracked for decades before they developed diseases and died (hence the word, prospective).
