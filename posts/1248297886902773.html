Fuhrman: vegans should supplement algae-based DHA and EPA for brain protection

Louis Nervegna · Mar 18, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsFats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)
AI summary & key facts

Joel Fuhrman argues that vegans converting plant ALA poorly may have an omega-3 index below 4%, linked to higher stroke, dementia and Parkinson's risk in older vegans, and recommends algae-based DHA/EPA to reach 6-9%. Claims come from a promotional blog without cited studies.

  • Omega-3 index below 4% called the danger zone
  • Target omega-3 index 6-9%

#omega3HD #dhaHD #epaHD #veganHD #brainHD

Shared from Joel Fuhrman, M.D. · original post

If you're following a plant-based diet and not supplementing with DHA and EPA, you're likely putting your brain at risk. While vegans excel at avoiding heart disease and diabetes, the data reveals a troubling pattern: low omega-3 status correlates with higher rates of stroke, dementia, and Parkinson's in older vegans.

The problem? Most people cannot efficiently convert plant-based ALA from flax and walnuts into the DHA your brain desperately needs. Studies show most vegans who don’t supplemented have an omega-3 index below 4% - the danger zone for cardiovascular disease and cognitive decline. Don't let low omega-3s be your one modifiable risk factor for neurodegeneration.

Supplement with algae-based DHA and EPA to reach a protective omega-3 index of 6-9% and give your brain the building blocks it needs for a lifetime of sharp cognition. Why Vegans Need DHA and EPA (drfuhrman.com)

People with the highest EPA had a 36% lower risk of bleeding stroke, DHA was neutral, and ALA was linked to 32% higher risk (needs confirmation). Pooled RCTs found no extra brain bleeding, except small risk with high-dose prescription EPA. The study is observational.

High-dose DHA did not slow cognitive decline in 2-year APOE4 trial, but reached the brain

Jun 27, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Brain health & dementia

Louis comments on a Medscape report of a trial of high-dose omega-3 in older adults at high dementia risk. It found no improvement in memory, cognition or hippocampal volume, although DHA reached the cerebrospinal fluid equally in APOE4 carriers and non-carriers. He notes the trial was short at two years, and APOE4 carriers with the highest DHA to arachidonic acid ratio declined less.
