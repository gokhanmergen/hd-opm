Gokhan recommends an NYT Opinion video on Russian disinformation campaigns and adds a short note contrasting reproducible, evidence-based science with anecdote and isolated studies. The post is mainly about politics and disinformation rather than heart health.

I highly recommend watching this video to understand what's going on with the western societies-- why are we fighting so much internally? Where's so much disinformation coming from? Operation InfeKtion: How Russia Perfected the Art of War | NYT Opinion (New York Times Opinion)

In such an environment, it is crucial to distinguish between scientific knowledge and pseudoscience:

  1. Scientific, evidence-based knowledge – Derived from rigorous experimentation. You test hypotheses on humans, mice, monkeys..., obtain reproducible results, and establish conclusions beyond a reasonable doubt. When findings are consistently verified, they become part of the scientific body of knowledge.
  2. Non-scientific or pseudo-scientific claims – Often anecdotal. "My cousin told me this," "It happened to my neighbor," or "Scientist X wrote an opinion piece" without any substantial evidence. It can also stem from isolated studies that contradict a vast body of research—random noise or experimental error rather than legitimate science.

In times like these, critical thinking and a commitment to evidence are more important than ever. Any extreme positions are likely encouraged by the adversaries, and are suspect.
