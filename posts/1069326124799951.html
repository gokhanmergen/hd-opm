Cane-sugar Coke is no healthier: sucrose splits into glucose and fructose just like HFCS

Louis Nervegna · Jul 16, 2025 · ♥ 15 reactions · 18 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

In response to news that Coca-Cola would use cane sugar in US cola, Louis explains that sucrose is exactly 50% glucose and 50% fructose and is split by sucrase almost immediately, much like HFCS. He warns members not to think it makes soda healthier.

  • HFCS is about 50% glucose and 50% fructose
  • Sucrose is exactly 50% glucose and 50% fructose
  • Sucrase breaks sucrose down in saliva and digestion

I hope people in the group are not foolish enough to believe that this makes soda any healthier.

The evidence clearly shows the opposite:

↪ Walter Willett: refined sugar and starch, especially sugary drinks, threaten heart health

High fructose corn syrup contains primarily two ingredients, glucose and fructose. They each make up about 50% of the syrup.

“Real cane sugar” contains primarily sucrose, which is composed of exactly 50% glucose and 50% fructose.

The moment that the “real cane sugar” touches your mouth, it immediately starts getting broken down into glucose and fructose by the enzyme sucrase in your saliva and in your digestive tract. This process rapidly continues as the sucrose passes into your stomach. In a short period of time, all of the sucrose is converted to glucose and fructose.

For all intents and purposes, high fructose corn syrup and “real cane sugar” are identical. They are both equally bad for you.
