US labels can show 0 g trans fat when under 0.5 g per serving; check for hydrogenated oils

Louis Nervegna · Dec 08, 2025 · ♥ 10 reactions · 19 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Guidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that the USA lets manufacturers list 0 g trans fat when a serving has under 0.5 g, which can add up over several servings, so the ingredient list ('partially hydrogenated') is the reliable check. He notes natural ruminant trans fat in dairy and beef does not appear to share the harms. A WHO figure in the share says each 2% of energy from trans fat raises heart disease risk 25% and all-cause death 14%.

  • Under 0.5 g per serving can be labeled 0 g trans fat
  • Search ingredients for 'partially hydrogenated'
  • Every 2% energy from trans fat: heart disease risk +25%, all-cause death +14% (WHO)
  • Natural trans fat in dairy and beef appears less harmful per AHA

The USA still allows < 0.5g per serving of trans fat in foods.

Manufacturers are allowed to list this as 0g trans fat on the label, which can be misleading. If you eat several servings in one sitting, the amount of trans fat can add up quickly.

The only way to know for sure if a product contains artificial trans fats is to search the ingredients list for the words “partially hydrogenated”.

Note that both dairy and beef naturally contain some trans fat. (This trans fat is produced naturally in the stomachs of ruminant animals during the process of digestion.) The AHA notes that these naturally occurring trans fats do not seem to have the same adverse health effects as the artificially created ones. See comments for more on this.
