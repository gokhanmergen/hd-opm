Lifelong lower LDL adds about 1.2 years of life per SD, and benefits compound over time

Gokhan Can · Sep 27, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelStatins
AI summary & key facts

Mendelian randomization suggests each standard deviation of lifelong lower LDL-C adds about 1.2 years of expected lifespan, with proportionally more for larger reductions. A meta-analysis shows the relative risk reduction per mmol/L of LDL-C lowering grows with treatment duration, so earlier and longer lowering matters.

  • 1 SD lifelong lower LDL-C: about +1.2 years expected lifespan (2 SD: 2.4 y, 3 SD: 3.6 y)
  • Per mmol/L LDL-C lowered, relative reduction in major vascular events: 12% at year 1, 20% at year 3, 23% at year 5, 29% at year 7

How Much Longer Do You Live on LDL Lowering Therapy?

The best answer to this question comes from mendelian randomization studies. Genetically lowered LDL cholesterol by one standard deviation lifelong adds 1.2 years to one's expected lifespan. If you lower it by 2 or 3 standard deviations, you can expect a proportional gain of 2.4 yrs, 3.6 years etc. If you start lowering cholesterol in middle age, you can probably still get somewhat comparable benefits because the worst (most visible) damage happens in later years.

MR:

Paper · Br J Clin Pharmacol · 2021Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and lifespan: A Mendelian randomization study.Conclusion: This genetic evidence supports that higher LDL-c levels reduce lifespan and longevity. In a general population that is not selected for increased cardiovascular risk, there is likely to be a net lifespan benefit of LDL-c lowering therapies, particularly for PCSK9 inhibitors, although randomized controlled trials are necessary before modification of clinical practice.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

The benefits of LDL-C lowering do not seem to be fixed but increase steadily with longer durations of treatment. Each mmol/L LDL-C lowered was associated with a relative risk reduction in major vascular events of 12% for year 1, 20% for year 3, 23% for year 5, and 29% for year 7.
