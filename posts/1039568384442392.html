Adenoma detection rates support lowering colorectal cancer screening age to 45

Louis Nervegna · Jun 03, 2025 · ♥ 5 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A shared JAMA post from ASCO 2025 notes that adenoma detection rates were similar in people aged 45-49 and 50-54, with slightly lower prevalence in the younger group, supporting screening from age 45.

  • Similar adenoma detection rates at 45-49 vs 50-54
  • Slightly lower prevalence in the younger group

Lowering the colorectal cancer screening age to 45 is supported by similar adenoma detection rates between those aged 45 to 49 and those aged 50 to 54, with slightly lower prevalence in the younger group.

High vitamin B12 in a multivitamin did not raise disease risk in a 14-year Harvard trial

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 9 Supplements & vitamins

The poster argues it is unlikely high B12 intake causes cancer or clonal disease. The Physicians' Health Study multivitamin trial, which contained 10 times the RDA of B12, found no increase in major disease over 14 years and an 8% lower overall cancer risk. Nurses' and Physicians' cohorts also found no B12-disease link, except combined B12 and B6 with hip fracture, which vanished for B12 alone.
