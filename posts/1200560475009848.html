Only about 9% of statin users see a measurable rise in blood sugar, per UCLA Health

Louis Nervegna · Jan 13, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes UCLA Health saying statins raise blood glucose in about 9% of users, mostly with moderate-to-high doses and in people already trending toward prediabetes. He notes he is among the 91% with no effect.

  • About 9% of people on statins develop elevated blood sugar (analysis of existing studies, per UCLA Health)
  • More often seen with moderate-to-high doses
  • More common in people whose blood sugar is already high or in the prediabetes range

Only about 9% of people who take statins actually experience a measurable increase in blood sugar as a result of doing so:

(I am one of the 91% who has seen no effect.)

Quoted from UCLA Health:

“It is important to note that statins don't cause elevated blood sugar levels in everyone who takes them. An analysis of existing studies into the issue found this occurs in about 9% of people using the drug. Developing insulin resistance has been associated with moderate-to-high doses of the drug and is seen more often in people whose blood sugar levels are already high. This includes those who have been trending into the prediabetes range when they started taking the drug, or who already had been diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes.”

In JUPITER, rosuvastatin brought diabetes diagnosis forward by only 5.4 weeks

Feb 13, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 14 ↩ 1 Statins

Louis uses the JUPITER trial (rosuvastatin 20 mg vs placebo, 17,600 people, up to 5 years) to size the statin-diabetes link. Statin allocation advanced the time to diabetes diagnosis by an average of 5.4 weeks and raised HbA1c about 0.1 point at 24 months. His reading is that no one got diabetes who was not going to anyway, and that 20 mg is a high dose versus the 5 mg many take. The Lancet analysis concluded benefits exceeded the diabetes hazard.

WOSCOPS 20-year follow-up: 5 years of pravastatin gave lasting mortality and event benefit

Apr 07, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 1 Statins

Describes the 20-year follow-up of the West of Scotland Coronary Prevention Study: 5 years of pravastatin in middle-aged men with high cholesterol and no prior CHD produced a lasting legacy effect, with reduced all-cause, CV and coronary mortality and fewer heart failure and MI hospitalizations. No excess cancer or non-CV mortality was seen.
