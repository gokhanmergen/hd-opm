One egg a day does not raise heart attack or stroke risk for most people, Harvard says

Louis Nervegna · Feb 24, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 13 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes Harvard Health: large long-term cohort studies find no higher rates of heart attack, stroke or other CVD in people eating up to one egg daily. He adds that a neutral food is fine but the overall pattern should aim to lower risk, as the Mediterranean diet does.

  • Large cohorts following hundreds of thousands of people over decades
  • No higher CVD rates with up to one egg per day

Eggs and Heart Health

Quoted:

“For most people, an egg a day does not increase your risk of a heart attack, a stroke, or any other type of cardiovascular disease.”

“The evidence that cholesterol in one egg a day is safe for most people comes from huge studies — many conducted here at Harvard Medical School — that have followed hundreds of thousands of people over decades. They regularly report what they eat and all of the medical conditions that they develop. It is those studies that do not find higher rates of heart attacks, strokes, or other cardiovascular diseases in people who eat up to one egg per day.”

Just keep this caveat in mind: It’s good that a particular food does not increase risk of CVD. But the overall goal is to lower risk. It’s fine to include some neutral foods if you enjoy them, but it makes sense to focus your overall dietary pattern on lowering risk.

For example, the traditional Mediterranean diet allows some egg consumption in moderation, but focuses mainly on foods that likely lower risk.
