Low-carb high-fat diet linked to higher LDL-C, ApoB and 2.18-fold MACE risk at 11.8 years

Louis Nervegna · May 04, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 24 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreLDL, ApoB & lipid panelHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a JACC: Advances cohort analysis comparing low-carbohydrate high-fat (LCHF) eaters with standard-diet controls. LCHF had significantly higher LDL-C and ApoB, more severe hypercholesterolemia, and more major adverse cardiovascular events at 11.8 years; the association remained after adjustment. People with a high LDL polygenic risk score had the highest LDL-C on LCHF.

  • Severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C >5 mmol/L): 11.1% LCHF vs 6.2% standard diet
  • MACE after 11.8 years: 9.8% vs 4.3%; adjusted HR 2.18 (95% CI 1.39-3.43)
  • Highest LDL-C in LCHF eaters with elevated LDL polygenic risk score

Association of a Low-Carbohydrate High-Fat Diet With Plasma Lipid Levels and Cardiovascular Risk

JACC - April 4, 2024

“In this cohort, low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C) and apolipoprotein B levels were significantly increased in the LCHF vs SD group (P < 0.001). 11.1% of LCHF and 6.2% of SD individuals demonstrated severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C >5 mmol/L, P < 0.001). After 11.8 years, 9.8% of LCHF vs 4.3% of SD participants experienced a MACE (P < 0.001). This difference remained significant after adjustment for cardiovascular risk factors (HR: 2.18, 95% CI: 1.39-3.43, P < 0.001). Individuals with an elevated LDL-C polygenic risk score had the highest concentrations of LDL-C on a LCHF diet. Similar significant changes in lipid levels and MACE associations were confirmed in the entire cohort and in ≥2 dietary surveys.”

“Conclusions:
Consumption of a LCHF diet was associated with increased LDL-C and apolipoprotein B levels, and an increased risk of incident MACE.”

Louis shares a post arguing that normal insulin spikes from healthy carbohydrate do not drive hunger or overeating. In a controlled NIH study, 20 adults lived in a research facility on a high-carb low-fat diet (about 75% carbohydrate) and a ketogenic diet (about 10% carbohydrate) for two weeks each. Despite higher glucose and insulin, they ate an average of 689 fewer calories per day on the high-carb diet, contradicting the carbohydrate-insulin model's prediction, although the diets differed in other ways.
