Mendelian randomization finds no causal effect of VO2-max on longevity or type 2 diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Jun 22, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A 2024 two-sample Mendelian randomization study found that genetically predicted VO2-max had no association with longevity or type 2 diabetes, despite strong observational links to mortality. The poster concludes the observational link is likely reverse causality or confounding, though exercise itself remains beneficial.

  • Genetically predicted VO2-max: no association with longevity or T2D
  • VO2-max is ~55-60% heritable
  • Authors: VO2-max is not causally associated with T2D or longevity
  • Poster stresses exercise remains good for you

The idea that a decline in VO2-max is directly causal in aging (and diseases like diabetes) is likely wrong.

We now have a very well done Mendellian randomization study— recently published— that elegantly demonstrates this.

A 2024 Mendelian randomization study published in The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism used genetic variants to explore whether VO2-max causally affects longevity and diabetes.

The findings were striking: while VO2-max is allegedly a strong predictor of mortality in observational studies, genetically predicted VO2-max showed NO association whatsoever with longevity or age-related diseases like diabetes.

This suggests that the correlation seen in observational studies stems entirely from reverse causality or confounding factors.

(I say allegedly above because studies claiming to associate VO2-max with lower mortality mainly used a treadmill test as a crude proxy for VO2-max, which has many problems. See these posts: ↪ VO2max as a causal longevity metric is doubtful; Mendelian randomization shows no effect, ↪ VO2-max longevity claims rest on weak, over-extrapolated evidence, argues a critique.)

The implication of this Mendelian randomization study is that VO2-max per se does not extend lifespan in humans or reduce risk of diabetes. If it did, then people who have genetically high levels of VO2-max for their entire lives would live longer and have less diabetes than people who have genetically low levels. They do not. Both groups live the same length of time and have the same risk for diabetes.

Quoted:

“Conclusion- Cardiorespiratory fitness can be improved by favorable body composition, physical activity, and performance enhancers. Despite being a strong predictor of mortality, VO2-max is not causally associated with T2D or longevity.”

Note:

Mendelian randomization (MR) is exceptionally well-suited for studying VO2-max because it overcomes the limitations of traditional observational studies— specifically confounding factors and reverse causation— by using genetic variants as proxies for aerobic capacity to determine causality.

Because V02-max is a highly heritable trait (estimated around 55-60%), genetic markers serve as valid instrumental variables to investigate its true impact on health outcomes like longevity or disease risk, independent of reverse causality or confounding lifestyle factors like smoking or diet. There are nearly 100 genes influencing initial levels of VO2-max and trainability. Inherited factors include heart size, lung capacity, and slow-twitch muscle fiber compisition. (See comments for more on this.)

——

Based on this study, it is instead likely that aging causes decreased VO2-max— not the other way around.

It is a classic example of reverse causality.

The truth is that the evidence for the idea that a decline in VO2-max directly causes aging (or age related diseases) was always very weak, and came purely from low-quality observational studies. There was never a good causal mechanism to explain it in the first place.

Exercise is good for you. Improving cardiorespiratory fitness is good for you. But “biohacking” VO2-max will likely do nothing to make you live longer, aside from the already well-established benefits of exercise in general. It is not the magical longevity elixir that it is made out to be. People claiming that it is are scamming you.
