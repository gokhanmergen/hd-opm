Coffee polyphenols bind NR4A1, an exercise-linked receptor, in cell models

Louis Nervegna · Mar 30, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

SuppVersity reports a Nutrients 2026 study in which brewed coffee components such as chlorogenic acid and kahweol bound the nuclear receptor NR4A1 as inverse agonists in cell models. NR4A1 is linked to glucose and fat handling and is raised by exercise, suggesting a possible mechanism for coffee's epidemiological benefits. This is cell-model evidence only.

  • Binding at low micromolar concentrations in cell models
  • NR4A1 regulates metabolism, inflammation and cellular stress genes
  • Suggests non-caffeine coffee components may be biologically active

Research cited

  • Hailemariam et al., Brewed coffee and its components act through NR4A1 · in-vitro · Nutrients · 2026
Shared from SuppVersity · original post

Our daily dose of #coffee (news) give us today: Study identifies coffee is like 🏋🏃🏽exercise!

Yes, you read that right, your morning joe with its plethora of coffee polyphenols (chlorogenic, caffeic, ferulic, p‑coumaric acids, kahweol, cafestol, etc.) binds the nuclear receptor NR4A1 at low micromolar concentrations and act as inverse agonists in cell models.

🤔 WHUT?

NR4A1 is already known to regulate genes involved in metabolism, inflammation, and cellular stress responses; it is upregulated by exercise and muscle contraction and links to improved glucose and fat handling.

The work thus offers a plausible molecular target (NR4A1) through which habitual coffee intake could contribute to lower all‑cause mortality and cardiometabolic risk seen in epidemiology.

It supports the idea that non‑caffeine components of coffee (polyphenols, diterpenes) are biologically active and may be at least as important as caffeine for long‑term health effects.
