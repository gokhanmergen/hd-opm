Transplanted hearts take on the biological age of their recipients, preprint finds

Louis Nervegna · Oct 05, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

A bioRxiv preprint reports that donor hearts assimilate the biological age of the recipient: older hearts in younger bodies showed signs of rejuvenation, and young hearts in older bodies showed accelerated aging, in mice and humans. The poster relates it to parabiosis experiments and notes implications for donor organ allocation.

  • Older donor hearts in younger recipients showed signs of rejuvenation
  • Young hearts in older bodies showed accelerated aging, in mice and humans
  • Preprint, not yet peer-reviewed

It’s long been known that attaching the bloodstream of a young mouse to an old mouse (parabiosis) can partially rejuvenate the tissues and organs of the old mouse.

The same appears to be true when old organs (hearts) are transplanted into the bodies of young mice. The transplanted organs get younger. It seems to be true in humans, as well.

Link to journal paper (preprint):

To meet the increasing demand, organs are frequently transplanted across an age discrepancy between donor and recipients.
