Untreated hearing loss in middle age is directly causal of dementia. It is responsible for 7% of dementia cases according to the 2024 Lancet Commission Report, which makes it the biggest modifiable risk factor for dementia (tied with LDL):

↪ Treating midlife hearing loss may reduce dementia risk; up to 32% of cases attributable

↪ Lancet Commission 2024: high LDL and hearing loss each account for 7% of dementia cases

↪ 2024 Lancet Commission: high LDL is the top modifiable dementia risk factor (7% of cases)

If you have hearing loss, you should treat it ASAP to protect your brain.

CHOICE trial: hearing aids gave nearly six-fold cognitive improvement in MCI

A secondary outcome of the CHOICE randomized trial found cognitive improvement in 15.34% of older adults with mild cognitive impairment given hearing aids versus 2.36% of controls. The primary outcome was null and the authors say the surprisingly large effect needs replication.

Louis says midlife hearing loss is, along with LDL, one of the two largest modifiable dementia risk factors and that treating it may reduce dementia risk. A shared JAMA Otolaryngology item estimates up to 32% of dementia cases are attributable to audiometric hearing loss.

Louis recommends treating hearing loss with a hearing aid before age 70, citing news of a study of nearly 3,000 participants over up to two decades. Those with hearing loss who used hearing aids had 61% lower dementia risk among people younger than 70. He says multiple studies now show this.
