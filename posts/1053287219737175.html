Higher blood linoleic acid tied to lower inflammation in about 1,900 people

AI summary & key facts

A study of blood markers in nearly 1,900 people found that higher membrane omega-6 (linoleic acid, which comes only from food) was associated with lower inflammation and better metabolic markers, challenging the claim that seed oils are inflammatory.

  • About 1,900 participants
  • Higher membrane linoleic acid correlated with lower inflammation and improved metabolic markers

Myth-busting study shows controversial seed oils are associated with reduced inflammation

Because lineolic acid only comes from food, its measurement in blood cell membranes is expected to reflect the dietary intake. The conclusion? The highest Omega 6 in membranes is correlated with reduced inflammation and other improved metabolic markers.

ScienceDailyMyth-busting study shows controversial seed oils reduce inflammationA new study is turning heads by challenging the popular belief that seed oils are harmful to health. Researchers analyzed blood markers from nearly 1,900 people and found that higher levels of linoleic acid — an omega-6 fat commonly found in seed oils — were linked to lower inflammation and better cardiometabolic health.
