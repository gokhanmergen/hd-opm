Blood microplastics reflect recent diet and inhalation, so a blood test is not useful

Louis Nervegna · Dec 29, 2024 · ♥ 4 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)Risk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites the group that first measured microplastics in human blood (2022): the main entry routes are diet/gut and inhalation, plus surgery. Even a contamination-free measurement would only reflect what was recently eaten, drunk or inhaled, so he calls the Bryan Johnson test a scam.

  • Entry routes: diet/gut, inhalation, direct exposure in surgery
  • Claimed detection is on the order of 10 single particles per 100 uL blood
  • Blood level changes as soon as diet or environment changes

The primary entry point for microplastics into the human bloodstream is the diet/gut and inhalation. What you eat and inhale is quickly affecting the concentration in the blood.

This comes directly from the group that was first to measure microplastics in human blood in 2022. These people are the real experts on this.

Quoted from their paper:

“The most likely route to enter the blood is via diet/gut but there are certainly two additional routes of exposure: inhaled MPs crossing the lung to the bloodstream (Jenner et al., 2022) or during surgery directly into the bloodstream as airborne fallout or from spallation of medical equipment (Field et al., 2022).”

—-

Even if a commercial lab could accurately measure the concentration of microplastic particles in your blood without contamination (on the order of 10 single particles per 100uL blood sample)— the test would still not be useful. All it would tell you is the amount of plastic in whatever you most recently ate, drank, or inhaled. The blood level would significantly change as soon as you ate something different or changed environments.

I fully stand by my claim that the microplastics blood test that Bryan Johnson is selling is a scam.
