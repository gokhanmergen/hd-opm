Framingham data link a high omega-3 index to about 5 more years of life expectancy

Louis Nervegna · Dec 03, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsNuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a FoundMyFitness claim that people with a high omega-3 index (about 8%) have roughly 5 more years of life expectancy than those with a low index (about 4%) in cohort data from the Framingham Heart Study. It also says very low omega-3 may carry mortality risk similar to smoking and that nearly 90% of Americans fall short of EPA and DHA targets.

  • High omega-3 index (about 8%) vs low (about 4%): about 5-year longer life expectancy
  • Omega-3 index below 4% claimed similar mortality risk to smoking
  • Nearly 90% of Americans fall short of daily EPA+DHA
  • Observational data

People with high blood levels of omega 3 have about a 5 year higher life expectancy than people with low levels.

This data comes from the famous Framingham Heart Study.

Optimizing your omega-3 intake might be one of the simplest ways to extend your lifespan

Robust cohort studies demonstrate a clear ~5-year lifespan increase in individuals with a high omega-3 index (around 8%) compared to those with low levels (~4%)

Astonishingly, research shows low omega-3 levels (an omega-3 index below 4%) may carry the same mortality risk as regular smoking

Yet nearly 90% of Americans fall short of daily EPA & DHA requirements, easily correctable by eating fish or taking a high-quality supplement
