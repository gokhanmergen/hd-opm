News: mouth bacteria biofilms in artery plaque may trigger some heart attacks

Gokhan Can · Sep 10, 2025 · ♥ 2 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

A news report on the same study: oral bacteria can form dormant biofilms within arterial plaque and be woken by stressors such as viral infection, causing inflammation and rupture. The takeaway is that good oral hygiene may matter for heart health.

A recent study has uncovered a potential link between oral bacteria and heart attacks. Scientists have found evidence of bacteria, commonly found in the mouth, forming dormant communities called biofilms within the plaque of arteries. These biofilms can be "woken up" by stressors such as a viral infection, leading to an inflammatory response that can cause the plaque to rupture and form a blood clot, which may trigger a heart attack. This finding suggests that good oral hygiene could be an important factor in maintaining cardiovascular health, as it may help prevent these bacteria from colonizing in arterial plaques.
