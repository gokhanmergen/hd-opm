Metformin slowed brain aging in monkeys, with neurons resembling those 6 years younger

Louis Nervegna · Sep 12, 2024 · ♥ 15 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityBrain health & dementiaInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis flags a Cell study, covered by Nature, in which male monkeys on daily metformin for over three years showed slower age-related brain decline, enhanced cognition and preserved liver function. Their neuronal activity resembled monkeys about six years younger (around 18 human years). The study was small, and human benefit is unproven.

  • Small study, tracked over 3 years in male monkeys
  • Neuronal activity like monkeys ~6 years younger (about 18 human years)
  • Better cognition and preserved liver function
  • Louis has taken metformin for nearly 15 years

This is going to be in the health and medical news during the next few days, and it’s warranted.

It is an important study that is probably going to have an impact in aging research.

I’ve taken metformin for nearly 15 years in hopes this would be proven in primates, so it’s wonderful to see it finally happening.

Quoted:

“A low-cost diabetes drug slows ageing in male monkeys and is particularly effective at delaying the effects of ageing on the brain, finds a small study that tracked the animals for more than three years. The results raise the possibility that the widely used medication, metformin, could one day be used to postpone ageing in humans.

Monkeys that received metformin daily showed slower age-associated brain decline than did those not given the drug. Furthermore, their neuronal activity resembled that of monkeys about six years younger (equivalent to around 18 human years) and the animals had enhanced cognition and preserved liver function.

This study, published in Cell on 12 September, helps to suggest that, although dying is inevitable, “ageing, the way we know it, is not”, says Nir Barzilai, a geroscientist at the Albert Einstein College of Medicine in New York City, who was not involved in the study.”

Louis calculates that the cynomolgus monkey aging study used 20 mg/kg metformin daily for 40 months. Using the FDA allometric scaling factor of 3.1, this is about 6.45 mg/kg, or roughly 450 mg for a 70 kg person, about a quarter of the 2 g typical diabetic dose.

40-month Cell study: metformin slowed aging clocks in male monkeys, brain age down ~6 yrs

Louis reposts the Cell paper abstract on a 40-month trial of metformin in adult male cynomolgus monkeys. Metformin slowed pan-tissue aging clocks, notably about a 6-year regression in brain aging, preserved brain structure and improved cognition, partly through Nrf2 activation in neurons. Only males were studied.
