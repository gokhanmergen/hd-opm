Blood NAD+ is a useless aging biomarker: stable across seven cohorts (Nature Metabolism)

Louis Nervegna · May 14, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that Charles Brenner is vindicated by a Nature Metabolism study quantifying NAD+ in seven human cohorts by mass spectrometry. Whole-blood NAD+ stayed stable with age and lifestyle and changed only with nicotinamide riboside supplementation, so blood NAD+ tests do not reflect tissue levels.

  • Seven independent human cohorts
  • Whole-blood NAD+ stable with age and lifestyle interventions
  • Rose with nicotinamide riboside supplementation

Prominent NAD researcher Charles Brenner has argued for years that blood levels of NAD are a useless biomarker. They don’t reflect tissue levels (which is what really matters), and they likely don’t change with age or lifestyle. All they tell you is how much vitamin B3 that you recently ingested, which you already know.

Now we have this new study, which is the nail in the coffin. Brenner was right.

Proud to say that I never wasted a penny on this test.

But I do take my vitamin B3, just like Brenner.

Quoted:

“Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) levels in blood and tissues are widely proposed to decline with age, yet evidence in human blood is inconsistent. Using a rigorously validated ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry system that accounts for real-world analytical variability, we quantify NAD+ across seven independent human cohorts. We find that whole-blood NAD+ levels remain remarkably stable with age and across lifestyle interventions, but change in response to nicotinamide riboside [vitamin b3] supplementation, as expected. Our results challenge the utility of blood NAD+ levels as a biomarker of ageing or lifestyle factors.”

Whole-blood NAD+ does not change with age or lifestyle, so it is a poor aging marker

May 22, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 4 Aging & longevity

Louis, echoing Charles Brenner, shares a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that human whole-blood NAD+ is stable across age and not altered by exercise or lifestyle interventions. Nicotinamide riboside raised NAD+, but that does not prove clinical or longevity benefit; tissue NAD matters.

Blood NAD levels do not fall with age, so commercial NAD blood tests are useless

Oct 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 5 Aging & longevity

A new Nature Metabolism study found whole-blood NAD+ levels stay stable with age across seven independent human cohorts. Louis explains blood NAD mainly reflects recent vitamin B3 intake, so commercial NAD blood tests are not meaningful, while tissue NAD levels may matter but cannot be commercially measured. There is still no convincing clinical evidence NR or NMN supplements slow human aging.
