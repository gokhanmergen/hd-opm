Ovarian aging may track lifespan; early menopause raises heart disease risk in women

Louis Nervegna · Oct 14, 2025 · ♥ 12 reactions · 16 comments · original Facebook post
Hormones, women's & men's healthAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Pharmacy Times item suggesting ovarian function is tied to lifespan, with anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH) a possible marker. He adds that ovaries are the first organ to age in mammals, slower ovarian aging relates to longer life, and early menopause is a heart disease risk factor; rodent studies show interventions slowing ovarian aging extend life.

  • Early menopause is a risk factor for heart disease in women
  • AMH proposed as a marker of ovarian function
  • Rodent studies: slowing ovarian aging tends to extend lifespan (per Louis)

Did you know that the ovaries are the very first organ to show signs of aging in mammals?

They can serve as a benchmark for how well a person is aging. Women whose ovaries age at slower rate tend to live longer.

Early menopause is a risk factor for heart disease in women.

Interventions that slow down the rate at which ovaries age in mammals tend to make the animals live longer. Multiple studies in rodents have proven this.

Shared from Dr. Kara Fitzgerald · original post

New research suggests ovarian function may be closely tied to overall lifespan. From a functional medicine perspective, this reinforces what we’ve long understood: ovarian health is not just about fertility, but about systemic aging and overall healthspan.

Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) is emerging as a potential marker to track and preserve ovarian function, with implications for both longevity and women’s health. Supporting ovarian health means addressing inflammation, nutrient status, hormonal balance, and lifestyle factors early and consistently.

This is an important frontier in women’s health and one where functional medicine can lead the way in advancing strategies that extend both reproductive and overall healthspan.
