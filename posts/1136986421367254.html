AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that negative anecdotes about statins or vaccines from conspiracy-prone people should be discounted because up to 90% of such reported side effects are due to the nocebo effect, where expecting harm creates real symptoms. He also notes mind-body effects can be harnessed in treatment. The 90% figure refers to the statin N-of-1 trial discussed in a linked post.

  • Up to 90% of negative anecdotes attributed to nocebo (per Louis' referenced post)
  • Nocebo symptoms are real but generated by expectation, not the drug

Never trust negative anecdotes about standard medical treatments from people who are prone to health-related conspiracy theories.

Studies have shown that up to 90% of those negative anecdotes are due to nocebo effect.

(Reference on that 90% number:
↪ N-of-1 trial: about 90% of statin side effects were nocebo)

The nocebo effect is extremely powerful.

This is when your mind creates physical symptoms in response to the thought that you are taking a something (e.g. a pill or shot) that is somehow harming you. These physical symptoms are very real, but in the case of the nocebo effect, they are generated entirely by the mind. Not by the thing you are taking.

The nocebo effect is an example of a mind body intervention. These mind body interventions can be exploited as effective treatments, as well.

People who are prone to health-related conspiracy theories are much more likely to suffer from nocebo effect.
