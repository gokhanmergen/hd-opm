Twin study: exercise benefits are real but smaller after controlling genetics and bias

Louis Nervegna · Apr 11, 2026 · ♥ 14 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

A 30-year Finnish twin study of ~22,000 participants found exercise improves healthy aging but offers a maximum of about 7% lower mortality, with both sedentary and highly active people showing faster biological aging. Louis argues observational exercise-longevity literature is confounded.

  • ~22,000 twins, 30 years
  • Max ~7% lower mortality
  • U-shaped biological aging curve

The observational literature on exercise and longevity is fraught with genetic confounding and reverse causality.

When you actually isolate these sources of error in large twin studies that track people for decades, the benefits of exercise are still very real— but the magnitude is attenuated.

Quoted from the article:

“Genetic Confounding and Reverse Causality: The Real Game Changers

One of the most powerful aspects of the study was its twin-based design, allowing researchers to separate genetic influences from lifestyle factors.

When they compared monozygotic (identical) twins who differed in physical activity levels, the benefits of exercise were much less pronounced. This points to genetic confounding—where people who are genetically predisposed to good health are also more likely to be active.

Another issue: reverse causality. People who are unwell are often less active, making it hard to determine if inactivity causes poor health, or poor health causes inactivity. Once researchers excluded those with early cardiovascular disease, the longevity benefits of exercise all but disappeared.”

“U-Shaped Curve: Can Too Much Exercise Backfire?

One of the study’s most eyebrow-raising findings was that high levels of physical activity were associated with faster biological aging.

Specifically, markers like cystatin C and beta-2-microglobulin—linked to kidney function and cardiovascular risk—were elevated in both sedentary and highly active individuals.

This echoes previous studies that found a U-shaped curve, where both too little and too much exercise might increase mortality risk. Overtraining has been linked to heart rhythm disorders and sudden cardiac events, although those cases are rare and usually involve extreme endurance athletes.”

“Biological aging was assessed using cutting-edge “epigenetic clocks” that estimate how fast a person is aging at the cellular level. Researchers also used polygenic scoring to evaluate each individual’s genetic predisposition toward traits like fitness and disease.”

Shared from BoxLife Magazine · original post

A 30-year Finnish twin study of 22,000 participants found that while regular exercise improves quality of life and healthy aging, it offers only modest longevity benefits—with a maximum 7% lower mortality risk.
The research revealed a surprising U-shaped curve: both sedentary and highly active individuals showed faster biological aging, suggesting moderate activity aligns best with WHO guidelines.
Though exercise may not add years to your life, it dramatically enhances your years of living.
