Louis retracts a sugar-consumption graph after the underlying data was shown to be wrong

Louis Nervegna · Mar 28, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis deleted a graph on sugar consumption and obesity after a group member pointed out the data was wrong and possibly distorted by the sugar industry. Stephan Guyenet, who shared it, corrected it quickly and the post links Guyenet's review of Taubes's The Case Against Sugar.

I deleted the graph that I posted on sugar consumption and obesity.

It looks like the data was wrong (and quite possibly manipulated by the sugar industry), as pointed out to me by Landrey Ann. See this article:

MediumNo, Sugar Consumption Hasn’t FallenAnti-sugar journalist Gary Taubes recently kicked off a web debate concerning the role of sugar in the American epidemic of obesity and…medium.com

However, the researcher that posted the graph, Stephan Guyenat, is a well-respected academic. He certainly recognizes the harm that excess refined sugar causes. It looks like it was an honest mistake, which he quickly corrected.

Stephan responded with the following post, which replaces his original post involving the graph:

stephanguyenet.comBad sugar or bad journalism? An expert review of “The Case Against Sugar”.stephanguyenet.com

Stephan fully recognizes that excess consumption of refined sugar is a major contributor to obesity. He is not challenging that. However, what he is challenging is the idea that this is the main root cause of obesity (as promoted by Gary Taubes). Instead, what he is arguing is that there are multiple causes, of which sugar is only one.

Note that all of this took place back in 2017. None of this is new information.
