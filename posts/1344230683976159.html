Vitamin B3 users with high eye pressure were about two-thirds less likely to get glaucoma

Louis Nervegna · Jul 15, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares an observational study where patients with high eye pressure taking nicotinamide were about two-thirds less likely to be diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma. He cites a small 2020 RCT and a larger ongoing trial, with an NAD mechanism.

  • About two-thirds lower POAG diagnosis
  • Needed fewer eye drops and laser procedures
  • Small 2020 RCT of nicotinamide promising; larger trial underway

This new observational study is part of a broader literature suggesting that vitamin B3 (niacin & niacinamide) might prevent and treat glaucoma.

The mechanism almost certainly involves NAD.

A small 2020 randomized trial in humans testing the niacinamide form of vitamin B3 produced promising results in patients with glaucoma:

Research cited

  • Vitamin B3 (nicotinamide) and glaucoma, observational study · cohort
  • CERA nicotinamide glaucoma trial · RCT · 2020
Shared from Medscape · original post

Among patients with high eye pressure, those who took vitamin B3 (nicotinamide) supplements were about two thirds less likely to be diagnosed with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and needed fewer treatments, including pressure-lowering eye drops and laser procedures, than those who did not take the supplements. High Eye Pressure? Vitamin B3 May Shield Against Glaucoma (Medscape)

A randomized Columbia University trial tested high-dose nicotinamide (vitamin B3) with pyruvate in patients being treated for open-angle glaucoma. After about two months the supplement group had significantly more improving visual-field locations than placebo, and Louis notes the mechanism likely involves NAD.

Nicotinamide riboside failed in phase 3 early Parkinson's trial, symptoms modestly worse

Nov 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a JAMA-reported phase 3 trial of oral nicotinamide riboside (NR), a popular NAD+ booster, in early Parkinson's disease. It did not improve outcomes versus placebo at 52 weeks and patients on NR had modestly worse MDS-UPDRS scores. Louis says it shows the supplement may have downsides in some conditions.
