Higher midlife and late-life physical activity linked to 41-45% lower dementia risk

Louis Nervegna · Nov 19, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

A JAMA Network Open Framingham analysis found the most active adults had 41% (midlife) and 45% (late life) lower all-cause dementia risk than the least active.

  • Midlife: 41% lower risk
  • Late life: 45% lower risk

Adults with the highest levels of physical activity at midlife and late life had 41% and 45% lower risk of all-cause #dementia, compared with those with the lowest levels of physical activity. JAMA Network Open article (JAMA Network Open)

Higher midlife vitamin D linked to lower brain tau 16 years later in Framingham

Nov 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Brain health & dementia

A Framingham Heart Study analysis followed 793 adults for 16 years, starting at average age 39. Vitamin D was measured in 2002-2005 and brains were scanned in 2016-2019, and higher midlife vitamin D was linked to lower tau tangles but not to amyloid. The post notes vitamin D acts like a steroid hormone with receptors in memory regions.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality.
