LDL-C is cumulative exposure: lowering it earlier and longer matters most

Gokhan Can · Jul 02, 2026 · ♥ 16 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Reposts an infographic arguing that LDL-C should be seen as plaque-years: every year of elevated LDL adds arterial burden that cannot be undone, so starting treatment at 30 beats starting at 45, and early intervention may matter more than drug strength.

  • Starting at 30 changes the trajectory more than starting at 45
  • Years of elevated LDL-C cannot be erased
  • Lower LDL-C earlier and for longer

LDL-C is not just a number—it’s cumulative exposure over time.

  • Think beyond LDL-C levels. Think plaque-years.

The earlier you lower LDL-C, the greater the lifetime benefit.

  • Starting treatment at 45 is better than never—but starting at 30 changes the trajectory.
  • Every year with elevated LDL-C adds to the arterial burden. Those years cannot be erased.
  • Prevention is not about treating cholesterol. It’s about preventing decades of plaque accumulation.
  • The most powerful lipid-lowering therapy isn’t necessarily the strongest drug—it’s early intervention.
  • Lower LDL-C. Earlier. Longer.

2025 ACC statement: inflammation is actionable in CVD, with universal hsCRP screening

Oct 09, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 15 · 💬 13 Inflammation & CRP

Summarises the 2025 ACC Scientific Statement (JACC) concluding the inflammation-ASCVD link is now clinically actionable. It recommends universal hsCRP screening in primary and secondary prevention alongside cholesterol, notes IL-6 and fibrinogen also predict risk, and says CT perivascular fat attenuation is promising but not routine. It contrasts this with a more cautious 2002 CDC/AHA workshop.

Meta-analysis: lowering LDL with statins had no effect on cancer incidence or death

Summarizes an individual-participant meta-analysis from the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists showing that about five years of statin therapy did not change new cancer diagnoses or cancer deaths. This is reassuring evidence against fears that lowering LDL promotes cancer.
