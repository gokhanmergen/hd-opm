Full-fat cheese and cream linked to lower dementia risk over 25 years (observational)

Louis comments on a news-covered observational study in which regular higher-fat cheese and cream, but not low-fat versions, butter or milk, were linked to lower dementia risk over 25 years. Most omnivores already eat enough fish or poultry for the APOE4 benefit in a Swedish study

Mar 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 5 Brain health & dementia

Louis comments that participants with an APOE4 gene variant saw the maximum benefit at about 2.5 oz per day of unprocessed meat or fish, which people eating Mediterranean or MIND-style diets typically already reach. He notes the study is observational, benefit was seen only in APOE 3/4 and 4/4, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.

APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Mar 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 22 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.

Full-fat yogurt lowered ApoB while low-fat yogurt plus butter raised it in a crossover RCT

Jun 07, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 15 ↩ 1 Meat, eggs & dairy

Louis summarizes a randomized crossover trial matching saturated fat between full-fat yogurt and low-fat yogurt with added butter. Despite equal saturated fat, the butter arm raised ApoB while full-fat yogurt lowered it, which the study attributes to the milk fat globule membrane (MFGM) that is intact in yogurt but lost in butter. He suggests this may partly explain why yogurt looks better than other dairy in epidemiology.
