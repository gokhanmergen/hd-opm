Nicola Guess: Keto-CTA plaque rose ~20 mm3 in 1 year vs ~13 mm3 over 3 years in PARADIGM

Louis Nervegna · Apr 12, 2025 · ♥ 7 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares Dr. Nicola Guess's critique of the Keto-CTA study: the abstract omitted the registered primary outcome (non-calcified plaque volume), but the figure suggests a rise of ~20 mm3 in one year in lean keto dieters. In PARADIGM, high-risk people on Western diets rose ~13 mm3 over three years (about 17 mm3 with metabolic syndrome). With no control group, the comparison suggests accelerated soft plaque growth.

  • Primary outcome NCPV not mentioned in abstract
  • Estimated average rise ~20 mm3 in 12 months (about 45 to just over 65 mm3)
  • PARADIGM (1,200 people with known or suspected CAD): ~13 mm3 over ~3 years; ~17 mm3 with metabolic syndrome
  • Study had no control group

Commentary on the recent LMHR prospective cohort study (Keto-CTA) by Oxford diabetes researcher Dr. Nicola Guess:

She notes that typical progression of NCPV (Non Calcified Plaque Volume) in high risk patients with heart disease on standard western diets is 13mm^3 over three years. This data comes from the PARADIGM study.

Lean metabolically healthy people on a carnivore-style ketogenic diet in the LMHR study saw progression of 20mm^3 in only one year!

She explains that this suggests substantial accelerated growth of unstable soft plaque, in otherwise healthy induviduals on this keto diet.

Quoted:

“Well, this is bonkers because you look at the abstract which is supposed to succinctly summarise what the study did and found, and the investigators….. don’t mention their primary outcome at all.

THE ABSTRACT DOES NOT EVEN MENTION THE PRIMARY OUTCOME.

[NCPV is the official primary outcome registered at clinicaltrials.gov for this LMHR trial.]

But luckily, the investigators do show a figure in the paper which displays the change in NCPV [Non Calcified Plaque Volume] over the one year. And it looks like it goes up…….

If I blow up the image on my iPad and use a ruler (!!!!), it looks like on average people are starting at about 45mm3 and heading up to just over 65mm3? So maybe close to an average 20 mm3 rise…….over the course of 12 months?

Okay, so how does this compare with what might be expected in a group not following a ketogenic diet? Or a group with low LDL cholesterol? Well, this study doesn’t have a control group so we can’t tell for sure. But what we can do is look at other observational cohorts and see what kind of rise would be expected overtime.

I couldn’t find any data on change in NCPV on a healthy diet like a Mediterranean diet as a comparison. But I did find data from the PARADIGM (Progression of Atherosclerotic Plaque Determined by Computed Tomographic Angiography Imaging) cohort, comprising 1200 people with known or suspected stable coronary artery disease (CAD), who are being followed up and monitored over time.

And if we look at the change in NCPV in this cohort, we can see that there seems to be an average increase of about 13mm3 over ~3 years. In folks with MetS, progression is about 17mm3 over ~3 years. (Albeit with a ton of variability).

And yet in lean individuals following a ketogenic diet and with elevated baseline LDL-C, they have an increase of ~20mm3* in ONE YEAR.

This looks bad.

It looks bad because the data from this KETO-CTA Trial therefore seems to suggest accelerated growth in unstable plaque in otherwise healthy individuals.”
