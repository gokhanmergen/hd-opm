Age is a far stronger heart disease risk factor than cholesterol, per the geroscience view

Louis shares Eric Verdin's argument that age predicts heart disease much more strongly than cholesterol, and that aging biology drives heart disease, stroke, cancers and dementia together. The framing is geroscience: target aging itself rather than managing downstream markers one by one.

  • Claim: cholesterol is about seven times less important than age as a predictor
  • Aging as a shared upstream driver of many chronic diseases

Age is by far the biggest risk factor for heart disease.

Much bigger than any other risk factor.

The main cause of heart disease is the aging process itself.

#riskfactorsHD #agingHD #ageHD #cvdHD #geroscienceHD

Shared from Eric Verdin · original post

Your cholesterol level is seven times less important as a predictor of heart disease than your age.

This came up in my recent conversation with @drmarkhyman , and we both paused.

Because it reframes everything about how we approach prevention.

We have built a system of medicine around downstream markers like blood glucose and cholesterol.

But they all sit downstream of the same upstream driver. Aging itself.

Heart disease, stroke, many cancers, type two diabetes, Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s.

These are not separate problems that happen to cluster in older people. They share a common biology.

And that biology is aging.

Here is what that means in simple terms:

If you spend decades managing individual risk factors and never address the underlying aging process, you are treating symptoms while the cause keeps advancing.

Targeting aging biology directly means the chance to reduce risk across multiple diseases at once.

That is the geroscience hypothesis. The alternative is what I call “whack-a-mole medicine,” where we treat these individual conditions as independent occurrences without realizing their shared origin in the biology of aging. The geroscience hypothesis is changing how the most serious physicians and scientists think about what prevention actually means.

Age raises CVD death risk about 19.5x from 65 to 85, far above diabetes or smoking

Dec 18, 2023 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 20 ↩ 5 Cardiovascular disease: the big picture

Louis compares hazard ratios from the SCORE O.P. risk model for people over 65. A hazard ratio of 1.16 per year of age compounds to about 19.5x higher CVD death risk between 65 and 85, far larger than diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure or LDL. The numbers used are for women; men are similar. (The post also writes this as 19,500%, which is a mislabel of 19.5x.)
