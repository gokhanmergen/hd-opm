Could a gut-derived metabolite help flip the switch on one of cardiology’s toughest puzzles? 🫀🔬

In Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF), the heart can still pump—but it struggles to relax. Because standard pump tests often look normal, this "stiff heart" condition is notoriously difficult to manage, with few targeted therapeutic options available.

Enter Urolithin A—a compound generated when our gut microbiome metabolizes ellagitannins found in foods like pomegranates and walnuts. 🧬✨

Recent research points to a fascinating mechanism: how cellular cleanup and mitochondrial health might directly influence myocardial relaxation and tissue stiffness.

Can targeting cellular quality control offer a new pathway for diastolic dysfunction?

Urolithin A, a pomegranate gut metabolite, improved HFpEF in mice and human heart tissue

Aug 20, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 20 · 💬 9 Heart rhythm & heart failure

A Science Advances study from King's College London found that urolithin A activates the heart-protective protein PKGIα at cysteine 42, improving heart relaxation, fibrosis and exercise activity in mice with HFpEF and relaxing engineered human heart tissue. About half of people produce urolithin A from pomegranate; human trials are lacking, so benefit for patients is unproven.

Luigi Fontana's summary of an NEJM trial of lepodisiran, an siRNA that blocks hepatic Lp(a) production. At the 400 mg dose Lp(a) fell by 93.9% placebo-adjusted, lasting up to a year, with only mild injection-site reactions.
