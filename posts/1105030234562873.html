Louis argues clinicians should not give public health advice on multivitamins

Louis Nervegna · Sep 04, 2025 · ♥ 18 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsBrain health & dementiaHow to read research
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that epidemiology is a specialized field and that clinicians dismissing multivitamins ignored high-quality cohort data and COSMOS RCTs. The shared FoundMyFitness text claims multivitamins improved processing speed, cognition and episodic memory in older adults.

  • Prospective cohorts link multivitamin use to lower cognitive decline (per post)
  • COSMOS RCT cited as showing slower cognitive decline

Sadly, this story illustrates why clinicians should not be giving public health advice.

Public health is fundamentally epidemiological in nature. Studies of this kind require a deep understanding of mathematics and statistics, at the PhD level— in addition to medical expertise.

Most clinicians lack the required mathematical background.

The truth is that we’ve known for decades that multivitamin use is associated with lower risk for cognitive decline in high quality prospective cohort studies. Given the strength of the evidence and the very low risk, the best epidemiologists at the Harvard School of Public Health have been recommending a basic RDA multivitamin for over 25 years.

It’s a tragedy that clinicians told people for years that a basic RDA multivitamin does nothing or causes harm. There was never credible evidence to support that.

Today, we know from large long-running RCT’s at Harvard (COSMOS) that basic RDA multivitamin use dramatically slows cognitive decline in older people.

A daily multivitamin could significantly reverse cognitive aging by up to 5 years

Far from being "expensive urine," a multivitamin can actually make your brain functionally younger—restoring memory to levels seen five years earlier
