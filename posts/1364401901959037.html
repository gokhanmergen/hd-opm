hsCRP needs averaging; GlycA may be a better inflammation marker than Lp-PLA2 or MPO

Louis Nervegna · Aug 08, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Inflammation & CRPRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis discusses inflammation markers: hsCRP is noisy and not artery-specific but predicts risk when averaged over time; Lp-PLA2 and MPO are more artery-specific but poor predictors; GlycA predicts well without needing averaging.

  • hsCRP: average repeated measures
  • Lp-PLA2 and MPO: weak risk predictors
  • GlycA: predicts risk without averaging

I’ve written about Lp-Pla2 and MPO before, and about issues with using hsCRP to measure inflammation.

See these posts:

↪ Eight facts about CRP and hs-CRP: systemic inflammation, repeat testing needed

The problem with hsCRP is that it is very noisy, and it is not specific to arteries alone. Even so, it is a good predictor of risk if you take multiple measurements over a longer period of time and average them. This averages out the noise.

In principle, Lp-Pla2 and MPO give an indication of inflammation specifically inside arteries. The problem (as Dayspring notes below) is that they are not very good at predicting risk.

GlycA might end up being the most useful inflammation marker of them all… because it predicts risk very well, but does not require averaging like hsCRP.

For those who are unfamiliar:

GlycA stands for Glycoprotein Acetylation. When your body has ongoing inflammation, your immune system produces more of these specific sugar-coated proteins.

GlycA is an inflammatory blood marker that acts like a dashboard, summarizing multiple inflammatory signals in the body at once, rather than just looking at a single marker like hsCRP. It uses nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) technology to measure a composite signal of inflammatory proteins in the bloodstream. Unlike hsCRP, which fluctuates wildly from minor things like a hard workout or a cold, GlycA is very stable over time. This means a single GlycA test provides an accurate, low-noise picture of your chronic inflammatory-driven cardiovascular risk without requiring you to repeat and average multiple blood draws.
