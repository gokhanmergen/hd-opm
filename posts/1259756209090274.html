Blood cholesterol levels have no bearing on brain cholesterol, a shared FYI says

A simple FYI for anyone who was not aware. What this then means is that blood cholesterol levels have zero bearing on what brain cholesterol levels are. Zero.

Tissues make their own cholesterol: even very low LDL-C does not impair hormone production

Sep 17, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 11 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that the blood holds only a small fraction of the body's cholesterol and that tissues, including the brain, mostly synthesize what they need. The steroid hormone demand supplied by blood is only tens of milligrams a day, easily met at very low LDL-C. He cites FOURIER and ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, in which LDL-C below 15-20 mg/dL did not impair adrenal or gonadal hormone production or vitamin D.

Blood carries only 2-5% of body cholesterol; cells and the brain make their own

May 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 22 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Shared breakdown of why blood LDL cholesterol is not a measure of what cells need. The body holds roughly 120-150 g of cholesterol but only 3-7 g is in blood, and the brain makes all of its own. One cited model suggests only about 50-90 mg/dL total blood cholesterol is needed to keep transport functional, versus a US average near 200.

PCSK9 inhibitors lower circulating cholesterol only, not brain cholesterol

Mar 09, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 16 · 💬 24 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

Louis states that PCSK9 inhibitors lower cholesterol only in circulation and not in the brain, because circulating cholesterol cannot cross the blood-brain barrier and the brain produces its own. He frames this as basic physiology countering claims about brain harm from lower LDL.

The brain makes its own cholesterol and does not use cholesterol from the blood

Jul 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 3 Brain health & dementia

Louis corrects a common online claim: the blood-brain barrier prevents the brain from using circulating cholesterol, so the brain synthesizes what it needs. The shared post adds that diet affects brain health mainly through blood vessels, and that saturated and trans fats raise LDL while unsaturated fats lower it.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).

Nearly every cell makes its own cholesterol, especially brain cells

Oct 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 2 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis notes that nearly every nucleated cell synthesizes its own cholesterol rather than relying on blood cholesterol, particularly cells in the brain. The shared explainer describes cholesterol biosynthesis through the mevalonate pathway with HMG-CoA reductase as the rate-limiting step. It frames why circulating cholesterol and tissue cholesterol are largely separate pools.
