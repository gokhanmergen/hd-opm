What cardiologists say about statins: dementia, muscle pain, diabetes, primary prevention

Louis Nervegna · Jan 13, 2026 · ♥ 20 reactions · 18 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementiaInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

A long point-by-point rebuttal of anti-statin claims. It cites a meta-analysis linking statins to lower dementia risk, the SAMSON trial on nocebo muscle pain, JUPITER's 44% reduction in major events, and a pooled analysis showing about 5 cardiac events prevented per extra diabetes case.

  • Meta-analysis of 55 studies in over 7 million people: statin use associated with lower dementia risk
  • PREVENTABLE: ~20,000-person RCT testing atorvastatin for dementia prevention is ongoing
  • SAMSON trial: about 90% of statin muscle pain attributed to nocebo effect
  • JUPITER: 44% lower risk of heart attacks and strokes vs placebo in 1.9 years
  • 13 trials (91,140 patients): 255 treated for 4 years caused 1 extra diabetes case while preventing 5.4 CV events

If you go to a cardiologist at a major research hospital and ask about statins, this is what they will likely tell you…

This is what the data from many rigorously conducted studies in humans actually shows.

  1. Statins likely protect against dementia, or are at least neutral.

A recent meta-analysis of 55 studies in over 7 milllion people demonstrates that statin use is associated with lower risk of dementia, suggesting a protective long term effect:

Paper · A&D Transl Res & Clin Interv · 2025Statin use and dementia risk: A systematic review and updated meta‐analysisDementia affects 55 million people globally, with the number projected to triple by 2050. Statins, widely prescribed for cardiovascular benefits, may also have neuroprotective effects, although studies on their impact on dementia risk have shown contradictory results. In this systematic review and meta‐analysis, we searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and…alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com

There is a huge 20,000 person randomized trial (PREVENTABLE) now testing to see if statins can protect against Alzheimer’s in older people. They are using atorvastatin. Results should be published in the next few years. Then we will know for certain whether or not it really works:

↪ PREVENTABLE: 20,000-person RCT testing atorvastatin to prevent dementia in people over 75

If you’re worried about statins causing short term memory loss (which you shouldn’t be) then just take rosuvastatin, which doesn’t cross the blood brain barrier very well to begin with.

  1. Muscle pain is a real side effect of statins, but most cases are due to nocebo effect.

The SAMSON randomized controlled trial proved that 90% of cases of muscle pain from statins are actually caused by nocebo effect. Only 10% of cases are actually real:

↪ SAMSON N-of-1 trial: statin and placebo caused equal symptoms, pointing to nocebo

↪ Nocebo effect explained about 90% of symptoms in patients who quit statins (SAMSON trial)

Muscle damage from statins is extremely rare.

This same trial also demonstrated that complaints of short term memory loss were nocebo effect.

  1. Gold standard randomized trials have proven that statins dramatically lower risks of heart attack and stroke in primary prevention.

↪ 25 statin RCTs: about 75 mg/dL LDL lowering cut heart attack and stroke roughly 40%

For example, the JUPITER trial proved that statins lower the risk of heart attacks and strokes by 44% compared to placebo, in just 1.9 years. This is massive. (If your absolute risk of having a heart attack in the next 2 years is 3%, this trial proved that statins will cut that in half to 1.5%.)

  1. Statins are associated with a slightly increased risk of diabetes in those already at risk for diabetes, but the benefit in reducing heart attacks and strokes in this higher risk population far outweighs the risk.

An analysis of 13 randomized trials demonstrates that for every 1 extra person diagnosed with diabetes as a result of statins, 5 major cardiac events are prevented, including heart attacks and strokes:

Paper · The LancetThe Lancet articlethelancet.com Paper · Circulation · 2012Statins and Risk of New-Onset Diabetes Mellitusahajournals.org

Quoted from AHA reference above: “Combining information from 13 individual studies (involving a total of 91,140 patients) showed that treating 255 patients with statins for 4 years led to 1 extra case of diabetes mellitus, whereas 5.4 cardiovascular events were prevented.”

The same JUPITER trial which showed a 44% reduction in MACE events from statins also showed an average increase in A1C of 0.1%:

↪ In JUPITER, rosuvastatin brought diabetes diagnosis forward by only 5.4 weeks

Most of the small increase in A1C from statins can be canceled-out by just taking a tablespoon of psylium powder twice a day.

(BTW he means a 0.05% increase in A1C in the video, not a 0.5% increase. He made a mistake. This 0.05% increase is true for low to moderate doses of statins. High doses tend to produce a larger increase.)

They are off-patent and generic. A 1 month supply of 5mg rosuvastatin tablets costs $4 on Amazon pharmacy.

Louis uses the JUPITER trial (rosuvastatin 20 mg vs placebo, 17,600 people, up to 5 years) to size the statin-diabetes link. Statin allocation advanced the time to diabetes diagnosis by an average of 5.4 weeks and raised HbA1c about 0.1 point at 24 months. His reading is that no one got diabetes who was not going to anyway, and that 20 mg is a high dose versus the 5 mg many take. The Lancet analysis concluded benefits exceeded the diabetes hazard.
