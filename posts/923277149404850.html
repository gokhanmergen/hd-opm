Statins block an IL-33 inflammation pathway that may drive inflammation-linked cancers

Research cited

  • Statin prevents cancer development in chronic inflammation by blocking interleukin 33 expression · animal · Nature Communications · 2024
Shared from Integrative Oncology-Essentials Fan Page · original post

A new study has discovered that statins, commonly used to lower cholesterol, might also help prevent certain types of cancer by reducing chronic inflammation, which accounts for 20% of cancer cases worldwide. Conditions like pancreatitis and inflammatory bowel disease have increased, highlighting the need for better prevention strategies.

Statins such as pitavastatin, atorvastatin, simvastatin, and lovastatin work by blocking the TLR3/4-TBK1-IRF3 pathway, which links environmental triggers to IL-33 production—a key protein in chronic inflammation and cancer development. Pitavastatin was found to prevent TBK1 activation, thereby reducing IL-33 levels and lowering the risk of chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer.

This pathway's suppression could help prevent various cancers, including skin and pancreatic cancers, and improve existing treatments. Statins are safe for long-term use, making them a promising option for cancer prevention.

Statins neither cause nor cure cancer: meta-analyses of randomized trials show no effect

Dec 31, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 4 Statins

Reviews the evidence on statins and cancer. A 2012 meta-analysis of 134,537 participants in 22 RCTs found identical cancer incidence (1.4% per year) and mortality (0.5% per year) in statin users and non-users, and a 2023 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found the same. Anticancer effects in specific contexts remain preclinical or suggestive.

Statin use linked to 35% lower odds of liver cancer in UK database study

Sep 15, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 Statins

A UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink case-control study of 3,719 liver cancer cases and 14,876 controls found statin users had 35% lower odds of liver cancer, and cholesterol absorption inhibitors (ezetimibe class) 31% lower odds. This is an association, not proof, and non-statin findings need replication.
