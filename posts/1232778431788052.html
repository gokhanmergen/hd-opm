Harvard cohorts: DASH diet adherence in midlife linked to lowest later cognitive decline

Louis Nervegna · Feb 24, 2026 · ♥ 7 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post
Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsBrain health & dementiaBlood pressure, salt & potassium
AI summary & key facts

A prospective study of Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals follow-up participants found six healthy eating patterns were tied to less cognitive decline, with DASH best, and plant-based and Mediterranean patterns also performing well.

  • Six healthy eating patterns assessed
  • DASH had lowest subjective decline risk and strongest link to objective cognition

In the latest published paper from the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicians Followup Study, participants who adhered most closely to a DASH diet in midlife and onward had the lowest risk of ultimately developing dementia later on. Other diets based around a plant-based or traditional Mediterranean pattern of eating also did well.

Quoted:

“The DASH dietary plan, which had the best outcomes in the current study, promotes eating vegetables, fruits, whole grains, fat-free or low-fat dairy products, fish, poultry, beans, nuts, and vegetable oils. It recommends limiting foods high in saturated fat, sugary beverages and sweets, and sodium intake.”

Healthcare professionals who followed six healthy eating patterns in midlife had less risk of long-term cognitive decline and better cognitive function, a large prospective study showed.

The DASH diet had the lowest subjective decline risk and the strongest relationship with objective cognitive function.
