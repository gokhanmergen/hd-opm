Tim Spector had a mini stroke at 53 and now takes statins

Louis shares Tim Spector's account of avoiding statins for nearly a decade despite family history, then a mini stroke at 53. Spector says statin muscle cramps were nocebo, and reports hs-CRP fell 40%.

  • Mini stroke at 53; father's heart attack at 57
  • hs-CRP fell 40% on statins
  • One major event prevented per 30 people (per article)

He thought that he could prevent heart disease just by diet and lifestyle alone, despite his strong family history.

He had a mini stroke at 53yo, and now admits that he was wrong.

(This guy is a highly-cited academic nutrition scientist who does research on the human gut microbiome. He has eaten an extremely healthy diet his whole life. He is the cofounder of ZOE, the personalized nutrition and health-sciences company which is very active on social media.)

He also admits that his muscle cramps were due to nocebo effect, and not the statin he tried taking. He is smart enough to realize that he was fooled (as up to 90% of people are).

Quoted:

“Why I’m taking statins after avoiding them for nearly a decade

My concerns about side effects from the medication dissipated once I experienced their health benefits first-hand.

Not only did the majority of the worries about statin side effects turn out to be rubbish, I saw first-hand how they can actually benefit your health.

My inflammation levels – measured by a high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) test – have fallen by 40 per cent since I started taking the drug. All the latest studies show that inflammation (not cholesterol) is the best predictor of future health problems.

The research is also conclusive on how statins can save lives, preventing one major cardiovascular event – like a heart attack or stroke – in every 30 people who take them. New research has even shown that obese adults can have the same risk of heart-related conditions as people with a healthy weight, because the majority of them are on statins.

Then there’s the observational research that suggests statins reduce dementia and have anti-depressive effects which, while not concrete yet, are very promising.

What’s more, there are signs that, by lowering inflammation in our gut, statins are changing the composition of the microbes that live in our intestines, reducing levels that are thought to harm our health.

It’s all so encouraging that I’ve decided to stay on statins – I’ve been taking them since last February, with just a few months’ break when I thought they were behind my muscle cramps – and monitor my progress as time goes on.

I’m 67 and will likely be on the pill for life – though the type and strength of statin may change. The risk of having a heart attack or stroke increases with age, so the benefit of staying on the drug increases as we get older.

Statins are a good example of a drug that we take for one reason – preventing heart disease – but seems to have much wider effects across our whole body, including our brains.”

My note:

A major reason why statins lower hsCRP is because high LDL itself is inflammatory. It gets inside the artery walls and attracts immune cells which secrete inflammatory molecules. We now know that most drugs that lower LDL also lower hsCRP. This is also true for bempedoic acid, ezetimibe, and fibrates. Not just statins.
