Why eating protein raises insulin: amino acids need insulin to enter muscle

Louis Nervegna · Feb 23, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis gives a simple physiological explanation: insulin drives amino acids into cells, mostly skeletal muscle, just like glucose, so the pancreas releases insulin when it senses excess amino acids after eating protein.

Why does consuming protein raise insulin?

A simple way to think about this is the following:

You need insulin to drive amino acids into cells— the same way that you need insulin to drive glucose into cells.

Without insulin, all the amino acids that you eat will build up in your blood to the point that they become toxic and cause harm. This is analogous to the way that glucose will built up in your blood without insulin.

So the moment that your pancreas senses excess amino acids in your blood (from eating protein), it secretes insulin to drive those amino acids into cells where they are needed. Most of them end up being driven into your skeletal muscle, just like with glucose.
