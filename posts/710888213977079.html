Centella asiatica and pine bark extract: small trials on arterial plaque stability

Gokhan Can · Nov 17, 2023 · ♥ 8 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Summarizes small trials of Centella asiatica and French maritime pine bark for arterial plaque, including a 1-year study of 60 patients where soft plaque did not grow compared with a 23% increase on placebo. A 3-year study of over 300 patients reported plaque progression in 5.3% with the combination versus over 20% in controls.

  • 1-year, 60 patients: soft plaque stable vs 23% increase with placebo; firm plaque up 63% vs placebo
  • 3-year, over 300 patients: plaque progression 5.3% vs over 20% in controls
  • Major events under 4% with extracts vs 22% without

Centella asiatica (Gotu Kola) and French maritime pine bark extracts for arterial plaque (link)

  • In a 1-year study of 60 patients with soft plaque taking Centella asiatica extract, plaque size did not increase compared to a 23% increase in the placebo group. Centella also increased firm plaque by 63% vs placebo. (2001)
  • In a 3-year study of over 300 patients, the combination of pine bark and Centella extracts led to plaque progression in only 5.3% of patients versus over 20% in the control groups not receiving the extracts. Major cardiovascular events occurred in less than 4% with the extracts versus 22% without. (2020)
  • In a 4-year study of over 200 patients with mild atherosclerosis (class IV plaques), progression to worse plaque (class V-VI) was 1.1% with pine bark and Centella extracts versus 21-48% with lifestyle counseling alone. (2015)
  • In a 1-year study of 84 patients, the number of arterial calcifications decreased by 10% with pine bark and Centella extracts while increasing by 35% with aspirin alone. (2020)
  • In a 6-month study of 40 patients, plaque stability index doubled with the pine bark and Centella extracts while remaining unchanged in the lifestyle counseling only group. Plaque size and number also decreased. (2017)

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.
