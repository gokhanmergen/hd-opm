Intensive BP control in SPRINT: 2.9 more years of survival and less white matter damage

Gokhan Can · Nov 17, 2023 · ♥ 2 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassiumBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Quotes follow-up analyses of the SPRINT trial comparing a systolic target under 120 with under 140 mm Hg. Intensive treatment was projected to add 2.9 years of survival from age 50 and was linked to fewer white matter lesions and better blood flow, markers of better brain health.

  • Residual survival at age 50: 37.3 vs 34.4 years (difference 2.9 years, 95% CI 0.9-5.0)
  • SPRINT enrolled hypertensive adults aged 50+ without diabetes or prior stroke
  • Intensive treatment: reduced frontal and posterior deep white matter lesions

Blood Pressure

"The study.. is a follow-up analysis of the Systolic Blood Pressure Intervention Trial (SPRINT), a multicenter clinical trial that compared intensive systolic blood pressure control (target less than 120 mm Hg) versus standard control (target less than 140 mm Hg). SPRINT enrolled participants aged 50 or older with hypertension and without diabetes or a history of stroke."

"At age 50 years, the estimated residual survival was 37.3 years with intensive [BP lowering] treatment and 34.4 years with standard treatment (difference, 2.9 years [95% CI, 0.9-5.0 years];"

"..Patients receiving intensive blood pressure treatment showed reduced white matter lesions in frontal and posterior deep white matter, and improved blood flow, indicating better overall brain health.. White matter lesions are among the changes that can be associated with Alzheimer's disease, non–Alzheimer's disease cognitive impairment and advanced brain aging."
