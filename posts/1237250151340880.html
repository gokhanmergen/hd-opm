Repatha for 18 months reduced non-calcified plaque on CT angiography in a 47-patient study

Louis Nervegna · Mar 02, 2026 · ♥ 24 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugsLDL, ApoB & lipid panel
AI summary & key facts

In a small imaging study, intensive LDL lowering with the PCSK9 inhibitor evolocumab (average LDL about 30 mg/dL) shrank non-calcified plaque while total plaque volume was unchanged. Louis compares this with the roughly 5 uL/year soft plaque growth in people untreated at risk.

  • 47 patients, mean follow-up 18 months, 196 lesions
  • Non-calcified plaque 607.3 to 562.1 uL (p<0.001)
  • Low-attenuation plaque 37.1 to 20.4 uL
  • Total plaque unchanged (716.2 to 710.8 uL)
  • 37 of 47 had some soft plaque reduction

Aggressive cholesterol lowering with the PCSK9 inhibitor Repatha (evolcumab) induced a major reversal of non-calcified plaque (NCPV) on CT-angiography after 1.5 years.

This was a small 47 person clinical trial. Mathew Budoff was one of the investigators.

Out of all 47 participants, 37 saw some degree of soft plaque (NCPV) reversal. Many of the reversals were large.

The average LDL of the participants on Repatha was around 30 mg/dL.

To put this in perspective… A typical person at risk for CVD, following the standard American diet and sedentary lifestyle, grows about 5uL of soft plaque (NCPV) per year:

.

Note that NCPV can be measured in either units of uL or units of mm^3. These are the same thing. One cubic millimeter (1 mm^3) is exactly the same volume as one microliter (1 uL). Keep this in mind when comparing different studies to one another.

Quoted:

“A total of 47 patients (age 61.8 ± 10.1 years, 87% male) and 196 lesions were studied. Twenty-three (48.9%) patients were asymptomatic, 16 (34%) presented with chest pain, and 8 (17%) presented with dyspnea. Four (8.5%) patients had a prior coronary artery disease history. At a mean follow-up of 18 months, there was no significant change in total plaque volume (716.2 ± 431.4 µL to 710.8 ± 456.2 µL, difference: 5.4 ± 97.4 µL; P = 0.705). Changes in plaque composition were observed, with a significant reduction in noncalcified plaque (607.3 ± 346.8 µL to 562.1 ± 337.3 µL, difference: 45.2 ± 63.8 µL; P < 0.001) and low-attenuation noncalcified plaque (37.1 ± 28.9 µL to 20.4 ± 15.4 µL, difference: 16.6 ± 23.5 µL; P < 0.001). In contrast, there was an increase in calcified plaque (108.9 ± 133.7 µL to 148.7 ± 175.3 µL, difference: 39.8 ± 56.1 µL; P < 0.001). There was a significant reduction in coronary microcalcification activity (1.35 ± 1.68 to 1.08 ± 1.37; P = 0.004) and lesion target-to-background ratio (1.73 ± 0.85 to 1.62 ± 0.83; P = 0.005).”

“Conclusion - In stable patients with extensive noncalcified plaque volume at baseline, 18 months of evolocumab treatment was associated with a shift toward a lower risk quantitative plaque phenotype and reduction in microcalcification activity. (Effect of Evolocumab on Coronary Atherosclerosis; NCT03689946).”
