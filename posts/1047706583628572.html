Low vitamin D tied to statin myalgia; correcting it let 64% retry statins in a pilot

Louis Nervegna · Jun 15, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis suggests statin users with low vitamin D consider supplementing, citing figures that about 22% of statin users have muscle pain, that 25-OH vitamin D below 20 ng/ml is independently associated with myalgia, and a 6-month pilot where 64% of vitamin D-deficient, statin-intolerant patients tolerated statin re-challenge after supplementation.

  • About 22% of statin patients have some musculoskeletal pain
  • 25-OH vitamin D < 20 ng/ml independently associated with myalgia
  • Pilot (6 months): 64% tolerated statin re-challenge after normalizing vitamin D
  • Small pilot, not a definitive trial

Anyone taking statins should consider supplementing with vitamin D, if blood levels are low.

This might help to prevent muscle-related side effects (myalgia).

This is interesting.

Approximately 22% of patients on statins have some degree of musculoskeletal pain (myalgia)
Low serum concentrations of 25-hydroxyvitamin D (< 20 ng/ml) are independently associated with myalgia and there is a possible connection between statin intolerance and vitamin D deficiency
Data from a 6-month pilot study found that the number of vitamin D-deficient and -intolerant patients who – after achieving normal vitamin D levels with supplementation – were able to tolerate the re-challenge of a statin for at least six months was 64%.
