Moderate caffeinated coffee and tea linked to lower dementia risk in 130,000 US adults

AI summary & key facts

Shared JAMA summary of the Harvard Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study. Moderate intake of caffeinated coffee or tea was associated with lower dementia risk and modestly better cognition, while decaf showed no benefit.

  • More than 130,000 US adults followed up to 43 years
  • Best associations at 2-3 cups/day caffeinated coffee or 1-2 cups/day tea
  • Decaffeinated coffee did not show the benefit
  • Higher intakes gave no additional benefit

This data on coffee and tea consumption came from the Harvard Nurses Study and the Harvard Physicians Followup Study.

Shared from JAMA · original post

In a large prospective cohort study including more than 130 000 US adults followed for up to 43 years, greater consumption of caffeinated coffee and tea was linked to a reduced risk of developing #dementia and modest improvement in #cognitive function.

The strongest associations were observed at moderate consumption levels: about 2 to 3 cups per day of caffeinated coffee or 1 to 2 cups per day of tea. Decaffeinated coffee did not show these benefits, suggesting the protective effects may be attributable to caffeine or related bioactive compounds. Notably, higher intakes did not confer additional benefit, emphasizing an optimal threshold for cognitive health.
