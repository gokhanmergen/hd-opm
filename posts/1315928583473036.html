STOMP: atorvastatin 80 mg for 6 months did not impair muscle strength (n=420)

Louis Nervegna · Jun 11, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites the STOMP randomized trial in which high-dose atorvastatin had no effect on strength, VO2 max, endurance or physical activity across 14 measures. Muscle pain was about twice as common on atorvastatin, but did not measurably change performance.

  • 420 healthy subjects, atorvastatin 80 mg vs placebo, 6 months, double-blind
  • No significant difference in any of 14 muscle strength, endurance or aerobic measures
  • Muscle pain doubled (9.4% vs 4.6%) on atorvastatin, without measurable performance loss
  • Only 6 months, healthy adults, atorvastatin only; may not generalize

Compared to placebo, 6 months of maximum dose (80mg) atorvastatin had no effect on hand grip strength, elbow strength, knee strength, VO2 max, endurance, or amount of physical activity.

There was no statistically-significant difference between atorvastatin and placebo in any single measure of muscle strength, endurance, or aerobic performance, across 14 variables.

These are the results of the STOMP randomized trial.

It is important to point out that there was double the incidence of muscle pain (myalgia) in the 80mg atorvastatin group compared to the placebo group, but this had no measurable impact on athletic performance in any of the 14 tests.

Shared from Dr Paddy Barrett · original post

If you’re active and your doctor has told you that you need a statin — and your first concern was what it would do to your performance — the STOMP trial is the study you need to know about.
420 healthy subjects. Randomised. Double-blind. Placebo-controlled. Six months of atorvastatin 80mg versus placebo. Measuring hand grip, elbow and knee strength, VO2 max, endurance, and physical activity by accelerometer.
Result: no significant difference between atorvastatin and placebo in any single measure of muscle strength, endurance, or aerobic performance across 14 variables.
Important caveats: atorvastatin doubled reproducible muscle pain rates (9.4% vs 4.6%). But myalgic subjects on atorvastatin did not lose significantly more muscle strength than myalgic subjects on placebo. The pain was real. The performance loss was not statistically significant. STOMP also lasted only six months in healthy adults on atorvastatin specifically — it may not generalise to all statins, longer durations, or sicker patients.
For a healthy active adult in their 40s or 50s starting a statin — this is the most rigorous evidence available. Your cardiovascular risk is real. Your performance concern is understandable. The data says you don’t have to choose between them.
Save this. Share it with someone avoiding a statin because they’re worried about their training.
