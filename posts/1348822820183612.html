Mechanical stress on bones made mice live nearly 3 months longer via anti-aging signals

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 Exercise & fitness

A news report on a mouse study found that mechanical strain on the skeleton, similar to what occurs in exercise and resistance training, makes osteocytes secrete anti-aging chemicals. Gently squeezing leg bones extended mouse lifespan by nearly 3 months, roughly nine human years. A related study found bone stress improved memory and motor function after traumatic brain injury in mice and pigs.

Fatty liver can scar the liver years before ALT rises; 10% weight loss resolves it in 90%

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Weight, visceral & liver fat

Routine ALT testing can miss advanced fatty liver disease: a Hepatology study biopsied 51 patients with normal ALT and found 12 with bridging fibrosis and 6 with cirrhosis. The post notes selection bias in that sample but says fibrosis behind a normal ALT is well established. Ten percent body weight loss resolved fatty liver in 90 percent and regressed fibrosis in 45 percent, and Mediterranean diet, resistance training and insulin-resistance management reduce liver fat independent of weight.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.

Shin bone loading may speed brain injury recovery via PIEZO1 in mice and pigs

Oct 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 1 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience paper found that compressing the tibia for a few minutes a day after traumatic brain injury improved movement and memory in mice and pigs. Bone cells sensed the force through the PIEZO1 sensor, and turning it off removed the benefit. The poster calls it the most interesting bone study he has read in a long time.
