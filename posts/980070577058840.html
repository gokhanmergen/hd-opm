Omega-6 to omega-3 ratio does not predict heart or diabetes risk, experts say

Louis shares a podcast episode where Rhonda Patrick and fatty-acid scientist Bill Harris argue the omega-6:omega-3 ratio is not useful for predicting cardiovascular or diabetes risk. Harris co-authored two large pooled biomarker studies finding higher blood omega-6 linked to lower heart disease and diabetes, and they say avoiding nuts over this ratio makes no sense.

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

Rhonda Patrick and Bill Harris explain why the omega 6 to omega 3 ratio is not useful in predicting heart disease risk or diabetes risk.

Harris is a well known fatty acid scientist who has published numerous papers on the health benefits of omega 3’s. He is also a co-author on two influential papers showing that higher blood levels of omega 6 are associated with lower rates of both heart disease and diabetes (referenced in the video). These studies were very large (70,000 and 40,000 people) and were based on direct measurements of omega 6 in the blood, not food questionnaires. See comments for links to both studies.

They both agree in the video that avoiding eating heart healthy foods like nuts because of unfounded claims about this ratio makes no scientific sense.
