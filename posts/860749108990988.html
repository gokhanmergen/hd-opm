Vitamin C 1-2 g/day raises urinary oxalate, a concern for calcium oxalate stone-formers

Louis Nervegna · Aug 29, 2024 · ♥ 5 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsKidney health
AI summary & key facts

Louis warns that vitamin C supplementation increases oxalate production, with 1-2 g daily producing a detectable rise in urinary oxalate excretion. Quoting a Reviews in Urology paper, he notes it does not change urinary pH and might promote stone activity in calcium oxalate stone-formers.

  • 1-2 g ascorbic acid daily increased urinary oxalate excretion
  • No change in urinary pH
  • May promote stone activity in calcium oxalate stone-formers

Vitamin C supplementation increases the body’s natural production of oxalates.

A dose of 1-2g is enough to produce a clearly detectable increase.

Something to be aware of.

Quoted:

“Both of these articles clearly demonstrate that 1 to 2 grams of ascorbic acid administered daily to both normal subjects and calcium oxalate stone-formers result in no urinary pH changes but in increased oxalate excretion. Therefore, this practice should not be used in managing patients with struvite calculi, because it does not reduce urinary pH, or in calcium oxalate stone-formers, as it might promote stone activity.”

EVITA trial: vitamin C lowered deaths in clonal blood conditions (exploratory result)

Sep 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 15 · 💬 8 Supplements & vitamins

In the small phase 2 EVITA randomized trial of oral vitamin C in clonal cytopenia and lower-risk myeloid cancers, the vitamin C group had 65% lower risk of death (11 vs 24 deaths) and fewer serious adverse events. Survival was exploratory and the primary endpoint of clonal growth showed no difference. The poster speculates relevance to CHIP and cardiovascular disease but calls it a big unproven leap.
