Video: David Sinclair reportedly takes high-dose Lipitor daily as prevention

Louis Nervegna · May 02, 2026 · ♥ 10 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Quoted claim that David Sinclair has taken a high-dose statin since his late 20s due to family history and high cholesterol. No data are presented.

“David Sinclair has openly shared that he has taken a statin daily since his late 20s as a preventative measure against cardiovascular disease, largely due to a strong family history of heart problems and genetically high cholesterol levels. He has specifically mentioned using Lipitor at a relatively high dose, viewing it as part of a long-term strategy to reduce risk rather than treat an existing condition.

From a longevity perspective, he has noted that statins may have broader benefits beyond cholesterol control, potentially lowering the risk of age-related diseases such as cancer and dementia.

At the same time, Sinclair acknowledges the importance of managing side effects, particularly the depletion of Coenzyme Q10, which is why he has discussed supplementing it. While he recognizes that statins remain controversial in some health discussions, he emphasizes that their use should always be guided by a healthcare professional and tailored to individual risk profiles.”

Amyloid-beta drives excess astrocyte cholesterol and impairs brain waste clearance in mice

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience mouse study found amyloid-beta raises calcium activity in astrocytes, boosting cholesterol production and removing AQP4 water channels, which impairs the glymphatic waste-clearing system. Calming astrocytes, blocking cholesterol synthesis or giving atorvastatin restored clearance and memory in mice. Human trials are needed, but the mechanism may partly explain observational links between statin use and lower dementia risk.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
