Master@Heart: higher wearable-measured training load linked to more coronary plaque

Louis Nervegna · Mar 06, 2026 · ♥ 28 reactions · 27 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessPlaque, CAC & CT angiography
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues intense exercise, especially high intensity plus high volume, carries risks as well as benefits, citing a Circulation 2026 Master@Heart analysis of wearable training load and coronary atherosclerosis in middle-aged and older athletes versus active controls. He notes the net benefit of exercise remains large but the plaque signal can no longer be ignored.

  • Wearable-derived training load vs CCTA-measured coronary atherosclerosis
  • Athletes and physically active controls, middle-aged and older

It’s time to start acknowledging the fact that intense exercise has risks, as well as benefits— just like any other intervention.

This is particularly true when high intensity is combined with high volume.

The case for this has been slowly building for years in the cardiology literature. The new study described in this video strengthens the case to the point that it is impossible to ignore anymore.

Reference:

Paper · Circulation · 2026Wearable-Derived Training Load and Coronary Atherosclerosis in Middle-Aged and Older Athletes and Physically Active Controls: A New Perspective From the Master@Heart StudyBACKGROUND: Middle-aged and older endurance athletes have increased prevalence of coronary artery disease (CAD) on coronary computed tomography angiography compared with healthy controls, despite similarly low cardiovascular risk. Previous studies relied on self-reported data to quantify training load (TL), which poorly correlates with objective wearable-derived TL and may bias outcomes. The effect of objective TL…ahajournals.org

While the net benefit of high intensity + high volume training seems to be positive for the average person, there are clear risks that might be serious for certain groups of people.

There is a lot of bad information on this subject from influencers (e.g. all the VO2-max nonsense). They make it seem like large amounts of high intensity exercise is a panacea. This is not what the cardiology literature actually shows. There is a benefit, but it likely comes with downsides when the volume is high.

Anyone interested in longevity should familiarize themselves with the cardiology literature on this subject, and consider the risk vs benefit analysis in their own particular situation. This video is a good starting point.

This is a complicated subject that I plan to discuss a lot more in the group.

Please note that we are mainly talking about extremes here, where intense exercise is performed in high volume. The typical person who exercises for general health does not need to be concerned about this. The problem in the vast majority of people is getting too little exercise.

Louis agrees with a critique of influencers who treat VO2 max as the key heart-health metric. VO2 max is oxygen consumption divided by body weight, so it can rise through weight loss without real fitness gain. Mendelian randomization: high VO2max is a health proxy, not a causal driver of longevity

May 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Exercise & fitness

Louis shares a 2-sample Mendelian randomization of about 70,000 people: genetic predisposition to high VO2max did not predict longevity, but genetic predisposition to low body fat, high muscle mass and high activity did. The same logic applies to grip strength.

Sauna works the heart like light cardio and lowered blood pressure after one session

Nov 07, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 4 Blood pressure, salt & potassium

Louis shares a summary of sauna physiology. Heat opens skin vessels, so the heart works harder to hold blood pressure, matching light to moderate exercise. One session lowered arterial stiffness and systolic pressure from 137 to 130 in 102 adults with risk factors, and a Finnish cohort of 2,315 men followed about 20 years showed lower mortality with more frequent sauna use.

After a marathon, troponin and NT-proBNP spike but look like normal physiology

Jun 10, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 2 Exercise & fitness

Louis shares a 2026 meta-analysis of 49 studies and 3,274 runners showing cardiac strain markers surged after marathons, often past diagnostic thresholds, while heart chambers changed shape. Changes were modest, MRI showed no clear structural injury, and the authors consider them normal responses, though long-term effects of repeated marathons are unknown.
