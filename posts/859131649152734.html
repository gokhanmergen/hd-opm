Exercise activates AS160 and GLUT4 for muscle glucose uptake; Zierath wins 2024 EASD prize

Louis Nervegna · Aug 26, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Exercise & fitnessInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares news that Prof. Juleen Zierath won the 2024 EASD Diabetes Prize for Excellence for 30 years of work on exercise and muscle insulin signalling. Her group found exercise activates AS160, which boosts GLUT4 to move glucose into muscle and fat cells, a pathway still working when insulin's effect is impaired in type 2 diabetes. This supports exercise for lowering blood sugar.

  • Exercise activates AS160, boosting GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake
  • Insulin's effect on AS160 is impaired in type 2 diabetes
  • Zierath: 2024 EASD/Novo Nordisk Foundation Diabetes Prize for Excellence

Exercise protects against diabetes and cardiometabolic diseases including CVD.

Quoted:

“Professor Zierath’s groundbreaking research on the plasticity of skeletal muscle has unveiled critical insights into how muscle adapts and maladapts to exercise, and the mechanisms behind insulin resistance in obesity and type 2 diabetes,” said Chantal Mathieu, President of EASD and chair of the committee that awards the prize.

“Her work on the physiological regulation of insulin signalling pathways in skeletal muscle has significantly advanced our understanding of diabetes, making her a distinguished addition to the list of previous luminary recipients,” she adds.

“The group found that exercise activates a protein called AS160 that in turn boosts the activity of a second protein, GLUT4, which moves glucose from the blood into muscle and fat cells.

While insulin can also trigger this process, the effect of insulin on AS160 is impaired in people with type 2 diabetes. Her discovery thereby offered hope for people with type 2 diabetes, who could improve glucose uptake in their muscles and lower blood sugar levels, simply through exercise.”

Shared from Novo Nordisk Foundation · original post

Professor Juleen R. Zierath has spent more than 30 years exploring the therapeutical potential of exercise, with profound implications for people living with cardiometabolic diseases. In recognition of her work, she is the 2024 recipient of the Diabetes Prize for Excellence, awarded by the European Association for the Study of Diabetes and the Novo Nordisk Foundation.
