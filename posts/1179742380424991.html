Lyon Diet Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut cardiac events and deaths about 70%

Louis Nervegna · Dec 12, 2025 · ♥ 20 reactions · 16 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis comments on a video clip about the Lyon Heart Study, a randomized secondary-prevention trial in people with a prior heart attack. The traditional Mediterranean diet cut cardiovascular events about 70% and all-cause death about 70% over 2.5 years, which he calls the largest total mortality reduction reported in a CVD prevention trial and about twice the benefit of statins in secondary prevention.

  • Secondary prevention: participants had a prior heart attack
  • About 70% fewer heart attack, stroke and CV death at 2.5 years
  • About 70% lower all-cause death
  • Diet emphasized canola oil and olive oil, with ALA-fortified canola margarine instead of butter

He is talking about the Lyon Heart Study.

You can read more about this study here:
↪ Lyon Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut heart attacks and CV deaths 73% vs an AHA diet

As he explains in the video clip, this randomized trial demonstrated that a traditional Mediterranean diet reduced chances of heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular-related death by about 70% after 2.5 years. This was in very high risk people who had already had a heart attack prior to the start of the trial.

As he notes, this is roughly twice the benefit shown in secondary prevention trials with statins.

Most shockingly, this diet also reduced chances of death from any cause by about 70% during this 2.5 year timeframe. This is the largest reduction in total mortality ever reported in a CVD prevention trial. In terms of total mortality, it beats every drug trial and other diet trial ever conducted for primary or secondary prevention in the history of cardiology.

As mentioned in the video clip, the Lyon Heart Study emphasized the use of canola oil in addition to olive oil. Participants in the intervention group were encouraged to use both. A trans-fat free margarine made with canola oil and fortified with the omega 3 fat ALA was used in place of butter.

Lyon Diet Heart Study: Mediterranean diet cut heart attacks and cardiac death 73%

Mar 30, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 24 · 💬 7 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

Louis summarizes the Lyon Heart Study (1988-1992), which randomized over 600 heart attack survivors to a Mediterranean-style diet or an AHA diet. After a mean of 27 months, the Mediterranean group had far fewer cardiac events and deaths, and the trial was stopped early. The diet was not low-fat: it was rich in olive and canola oil, nuts, seeds and fatty fish, and low in butter, cream and red meat.
