🧠 Is your brain aging faster than it should? It might start in your belly.

New research in PNAS reveals that brain network destabilization—a precursor to cognitive decline—starts in midlife and is strongly linked to insulin resistance and elevates systolic blood pressure in the brain.

Here’s the twist:
👉 Insulin resistance and increased blood pressure is often driven by excess visceral fat—the “hidden” fat around your organs that releases inflammatory signals and disrupts glucose metabolism and blood pressure regulation via norepinephrine activation.

But there’s good news:
✅ Targeting visceral fat through diet and exercise may be one of the most powerful ways to protect your brain.

🔍 How it works:

  • Excess Visceral fat reduces adiponectin and increases systemic inflammation, impairing insulin signaling.
  • This leads to reduced glucose uptake systemically and in the brain, causing metabolic stress, network instability, and eventually cognitive decline.
  • But reducing visceral fat improves insulin sensitivity—and can restore brain network function.

💪 The prescription?

  • Daily endurance exercise: Reduces visceral fat, insulin resistance, and improves glucose utilization also via acute GLUT4 translocation.
  • Whole-food primarely plant-based mildly calorie-restricted diets: markedly improve insulin sensitivity, reduce systolic blood pressure and inflammation, supportint metabolic flexibility.
  • Healthy weight maintenance: Even small reductions in visceral fat have big effects on insulin function.

🧠✨ Your brain isn’t just in your head—it’s deeply connected to your body’s metabolism.

By targeting visceral fat and diet quality along with moderate daily ‘cardio’ exercise, we can delay or even prevent age-related brain changes and cognitive decline.

Amyloid-beta drives excess astrocyte cholesterol and impairs brain waste clearance in mice

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience mouse study found amyloid-beta raises calcium activity in astrocytes, boosting cholesterol production and removing AQP4 water channels, which impairs the glymphatic waste-clearing system. Calming astrocytes, blocking cholesterol synthesis or giving atorvastatin restored clearance and memory in mice. Human trials are needed, but the mechanism may partly explain observational links between statin use and lower dementia risk.
