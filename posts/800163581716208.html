Why Louis takes a low-dose statin: known side effects beat unknown ones of newer drugs

Louis Nervegna · May 09, 2024 · ♥ 15 reactions · 20 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsPCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains his personal reasoning for choosing a low-dose statin over newer cholesterol drugs: statin side effects are well characterized after decades, while newer drugs may have undiscovered ones. He adds that this is probably why statins remain standard of care for most people. This is personal opinion, not a study.

  • Statin side effects studied for decades ('known unknowns')
  • Newer drugs have shorter track records ('unknown unknowns')

This is the main reason why I personally take a statin (low dose), instead of any of the newer drugs to lower cholesterol.

The side effects of statins are a known unknown.

The side effects of the newer drugs are unknown unknowns.

What this basically means is that we know exactly what the side effects of statins are. They’ve been studied for decades now. It’s unknown if we’ll actually get the side effects, but we do know exactly what we’re dealing with if we do get them. These side effects are a “known unknown”.

The newer drugs have not been around as long. They sound great on paper. But there are probably side effects that have not been discovered yet. These side effects are “unknown unknowns”.

I would rather deal with the potential for side effects that I properly understand— as opposed to the potential for undiscovered side effects that no one understands.

That’s my rationale.

And this is probably also the rationale why statins alone still remain the standard of care for most people, according to the guidance. They’ve been studied the longest.

Limiting diabetes risk on LDL-lowering drugs: statin risk is modest, alternatives exist

Review explaining that statins modestly increase new-onset diabetes in a dose-dependent way, mostly in people with existing metabolic risk factors, and that cardiovascular benefit clearly outweighs this. It suggests statins mainly bring forward diagnosis in predisposed people, outlines mechanisms (beta-cell dysfunction, insulin resistance) and proposes ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors and pitavastatin as options that limit glycemic harm.

Review: aggressive LDL lowering outweighs theoretical risks, yet few reach <55 mg/dL

Oct 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Summarises a July 2025 review: guidelines now target LDL-C <55 mg/dL (<40 after a recurrent event), PCSK9 inhibitors add 59-62% LDL reduction, and evidence (FOURIER, CTT meta-analysis, PCSK9 genetics) supports benefit to very low levels. It says safety concerns such as hemorrhagic stroke, cognitive decline (EBBINGHAUS) and hormone disruption are unsupported, while fewer than one in four high-risk patients reach target.

Lipoprotein(a) explained: 80-90% genetic, stable for life, a risk factor beyond LDL

Oct 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 8 Lp(a)

Explainer on Lp(a): an LDL-like particle with apo(a) attached, 80-90% genetically determined, fully expressed by 1-2 years of age, and elevated in about 1 in 5 US individuals (1.4 billion globally). Levels above 50 mg/dL or 125 nmol/L raise ASCVD risk, possibly via impaired fibrinolysis and cholesterol deposition, and may occur with normal lipid panels.
