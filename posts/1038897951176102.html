Higher plasma linoleic acid tied to lower glucose, insulin and inflammation (n=1,894)

Louis Nervegna · Jun 02, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetesInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

A cross-sectional analysis of 1,894 people found higher plasma linoleic acid was consistently associated with lower glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR and inflammation markers (CRP, GlycA, SAA). Louis adds that dozens of prospective cohorts show higher omega-6 levels go with lower CVD and diabetes risk.

  • n = 1,894 in a Covid-19 observational cohort
  • Lower glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR, CRP, glycoprotein acetyls and serum amyloid A with higher linoleic acid
  • Cross-sectional, so causation is not shown

Yet another study (just published) shows that higher blood levels of the omega 6 fat linoleic acid are associated with lower risk factors for heart disease and diabetes.

This is the primary type of fat in most nuts and seeds.

News-MedicalLinoleic acid may help to lower risks for heart disease and type 2 diabetesNew research that used blood markers to measure linoleic acid levels and their relation to cardiometabolic risk adds evidence that this omega-6 fatty acid may help to lower risks for heart disease and type 2 diabetes.news-medical.net

Quoted:

“For the new study, the investigators performed a cross-sectional analysis on data from 1,894 people in an observational cohort focused on Covid-19. They found that higher levels of linoleic acid in plasma-indicative of dietary intake-were consistently associated with lower levels of risk factors for cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes.

Specifically, study participants with higher linoleic acid showed lower levels of glucose and insulin as well as HOMA-IR, a biomarker of insulin resistance. They also had lower levels of inflammation biomarkers, including C-reactive protein, glycoprotein acetyls, and serum amyloid A.

"We saw consistent results across the different biomarkers measured," Maki said. "People with higher levels of linoleic acid in their blood tended to have a healthier overall risk profile for heart disease and diabetes."

There are now dozens of prospective cohort studies that have shown that people with the highest blood and tissue levels of omega 6 have lower risk of developing CVD and diabetes.

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
