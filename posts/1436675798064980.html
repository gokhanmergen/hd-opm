Lewy body dementia is common and often missed; ApoE4 overlap with Alzheimer's

Louis Nervegna · Oct 31, 2026 · ♥ 9 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a MedPage Today article on Lewy body dementia following news about Patrick Stewart and adds that it is the second most common dementia, shares risk factors such as ApoE4 with Alzheimer's and often co-occurs with Alzheimer's pathology. News and commentary without cited research.

  • Second most common form of dementia
  • Shares ApoE4 risk with Alzheimer's; often overlaps with Alzheimer's co-pathology

"Lewy body dementia is more common than many people realize; however, it is often overlooked or mistaken for Alzheimer's or Parkinson's disease. Awareness matters because recognizing the diagnosis early can change care, safety, and quality of life for families."

My note:

Lewy body dementia is the second most common form of dementia. It has many of the same underlying risk factors as Alzheimer’s, including the ApoE4 gene. But like Parkinson’s, it is thought to have a stronger environmental component than Alzheimer’s. It often occurs in combination with Alzheimer’s co-pathology, as appears to have been the case with Patrick Stewart.

APOE4 drives cholesterol buildup in brain astrocytes, promoting toxic protein deposits

Oct 06, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 17 · 💬 4 Brain health & dementia

Mount Sinai researchers using human stem-cell-derived miBrain tissue found APOE4 causes cholesterol to accumulate in astrocytes, impairing lysosomal clearance of alpha-synuclein and promoting amyloid and alpha-synuclein deposits. A second study showed APOE4 damages brain blood vessels through mechanisms unrelated to cholesterol. The poster reads this as cholesterol being potentially causal in APOE4 carriers.
