Methylene blue is a toxic industrial dye with unknown long-term risk as an anti-aging pill

Louis Nervegna · Dec 28, 2024 · ♥ 6 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis warns that methylene blue is an industrial textile dye that fails the Ames test, is non-biodegradable and is considered a water pollutant. In medicine it is used only short term or for serious diseases, so long-term anti-aging use has unknown harms.

  • Fails the Ames test (carcinogenic and toxic), per Louis
  • Non-biodegradable; excreted in blue form
  • Medical use is short term or for serious disease

Methylene blue is a synthetic industrial chemical, originally invented for use as an artificial bright blue textile dye.

It is used to disinfect and clean aquariums.

It fails the Ames test, meaning that it is carcinogenic and toxic.

Like other industrial dyes, it is considered a dangerous pollutant that is a threat to the public water supply. It is not biodegradable. Your body cannot break it down. You pee it out in its pure blue form (and it will literally dye your toilet seat blue).

In medicine, methylene blue is only used short term, or to treat serious diseases where uncertainty about longer term risk is warranted.

We have absolutely no idea whatsoever how much harm it might cause when taken long term as a supposed “anti-aging” treatment.

Quoted:

“Industrial dyes are one of the dominant chemicals that make water unfit for drinking. Among these dyes, methylene blue (MB) is toxic, carcinogenic, and non-biodegradable and can cause a severe threat to human health and environmental safety. It is usually released in natural water sources, which becomes a health threat to human beings and living organisms. Hence, there is a need to develop an environmentally friendly, efficient technology for removing MB from wastewater.”
