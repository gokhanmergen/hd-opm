Post-hospitalization COVID-19 cognitive deficits at 1 year matched aging from 50 to 70

Louis Nervegna · Sep 25, 2024 · ♥ 5 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Shared from Stuart Phillips: a Nature Medicine study assessed one-year brain injury after COVID hospitalization with MRI, serum biomarkers and cognitive tests. It found reduced gray matter and cognitive deficits equivalent in magnitude to aging from 50 to 70 years. This concerns the brain, not heart disease.

  • Cognitive deficits at 1 year after hospitalization, equivalent to aging 50 to 70
  • Reduced gray matter volume and elevated brain injury markers

Daily multivitamin slowed cognitive aging by about two years in a COSMOS meta-analysis

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 10 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a summary of COSMOS cognition results: in the main analysis of 573 adults 60+, a daily multivitamin modestly improved overall cognition and episodic memory over two years. Pooling with two earlier COSMOS cognition studies (more than 5,000 older adults) gave clear benefits, estimated at about two years of slower cognitive aging. Effects were modest, it is unclear which nutrient matters, dementia prevention was not tested, and the manufacturer donated tablets. He adds that this was an RDA-level multivitamin (Centrum Silver).
