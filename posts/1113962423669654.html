Meet the first man who is able to live without a heartbeat or a pulse

Surgeons at the Texas Heart Institute, Dr. Billy Cohn and Dr. Bud Frazier, developed an innovative artificial heart using two centrifugal pumps with whirling rotors, creating a continuous blood flow without a pulse.

Tested first on a calf named Abigail, the device was later implanted in Craig Lewis, a 55-year-old dying from amyloidosis.

Lewis lived for over a month with the pulseless heart, but ultimately succumbed to his underlying disease, not device failure.

The doctors believe this continuous-flow approach could outlast traditional pulsing artificial hearts, potentially revolutionizing heart replacement despite the absence of a heartbeat.

Further development and FDA approval are needed.
