My note:

Basically what they showed here is that loading people up on a large bolus dose of oatmeal (300g) for just 2 days seems to produce lasting changes in the microbiome that reduce LDL and improve other health metrics. These changes persisted for a significant period of time (6 weeks) after they stopped giving them the high dose oatmeal for 2 days, which is rather remarkable.

It’s always best to eat whole grains as intact as possible. The most intact form of oatmeal is slow-cook steel-cut oats. See this post: ↪ Glycemic index of oats: steel-cut 42, rolled 55, instant 83.

Research cited

  • Cholesterol-lowering effects of oats induced by microbially produced phenolic metabolites in metabolic syndrome · RCT · Nat Commun · 2026
Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

A randomized controlled trial published in Nature Communications (2026) by researchers at the University of Bonn studied adults with metabolic syndrome—a condition marked by high LDL cholesterol, excess weight, high blood pressure, and impaired glucose control. Participants followed a two-day, calorie-restricted diet in which they ate 300 g per day of whole-grain rolled oats, cooked in water, with only small amounts of fruits or vegetables allowed. Compared with a calorie-restricted control group that did not eat oats, the oatmeal group showed a ~10% reduction in LDL (“bad”) cholesterol after just two days, along with modest weight and blood-pressure improvements.

The researchers found that this rapid cholesterol drop was linked to changes in the gut microbiome. Oats increased beneficial gut bacteria that produced phenolic metabolites (including derivatives of ferulic acid), which are known to support healthier cholesterol metabolism. Notably, the LDL-lowering effect persisted for up to six weeks, suggesting the short intervention triggered longer-lasting metabolic changes. Smaller daily oat intakes over six weeks produced weaker effects, indicating that a short, high-dose whole-grain oat intervention was key to the result.
