Another large cohort: higher plant protein linked to 35% lower all-cause mortality

Louis Nervegna · Sep 10, 2026 · ♥ 18 reactions · 3 comments · original Facebook post

Louis notes that another large prospective cohort confirms Harvard findings on protein. Higher plant protein intake was associated with 35% lower all-cause mortality, while animal protein showed no significant association. The shared summary adds that the effect likely reflects whole plant foods such as grains, legumes and vegetables.

  • Plant protein highest vs lowest intake: 35% lower all-cause mortality
  • Animal protein: no statistically significant association

Another large prospective cohort study confirms the central findings on protein from the Harvard Nurses Study and Harvard Physicians Followup Study. Increased plant protein is linked with lower mortality.

Shared from Vital Human- Dr. Daniel Chong · original post

In this recent, large, prospective cohort study, animal protein intake showed no statistically significant association with all-cause mortality, BUT higher plant protein intake showed a 35% LOWER RISK of all-cause mortality compared to those with a lower intake.

To be clear, the effect seen here likely reflects consumption of whole plant foods like grains, legumes, and vegetables rather than any independent benefit of plant protein itself.

Take home message: Eat more plant foods.

Midlife plant protein raised odds of healthy aging 22-58% per 3% of calories (NHS)

Aug 31, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 15 ↩ 7 Protein

Louis summarizes a Nurses' Health Study analysis of midlife protein and later-life health. Total and animal protein were unfavorably associated with chronic disease, while plant and dairy protein were favorable. Replacing 3% of energy with plant protein (from fats, carbs, animal or dairy protein) raised odds of healthy aging by 22-58%, and the protein came mainly from food rather than supplements.
