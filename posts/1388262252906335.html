Choline deficiency can cause fat to accumulate in the liver

🧬🫀 Did you know? One reason choline is so important may be hiding inside your liver: this essential nutrient helps your body package and move fat out of liver cells.

Choline is needed to produce phosphatidylcholine, a major component of cell membranes and an important part of the lipoprotein particles that transport fats through the bloodstream.

When choline intake is severely inadequate, the liver can have a harder time assembling and exporting these fat-carrying particles. Fat can then begin accumulating inside liver cells.

This connection has been demonstrated particularly clearly in controlled feeding studies, where deliberately restricting dietary choline can cause liver fat to accumulate in otherwise healthy people. When adequate choline is restored, the liver can often reverse this process.

And you don't need to drink alcohol for fatty liver to develop. Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is strongly linked with factors such as insulin resistance, excess body fat, and metabolic health—and inadequate choline may be another factor affecting how the liver handles fat.

Choline is also involved in acetylcholine production, nerve signaling, cell-membrane structure, and normal brain function, making it much more than a “liver nutrient.”

Good food sources include eggs, liver, meat, fish, soybeans, and some legumes. One large egg, for example, provides a meaningful amount of choline.

💡 Try this: Include several choline-rich foods throughout your week rather than relying on a single source. Eggs are one of the simplest options, while fish, lean meats, soy, and legumes can add variety.

🧬 The fascinating part: Your liver doesn't simply store fat—it is constantly deciding how to package, transport, and use it. Choline is one of the nutrients involved in that process.

Could something as simple as getting enough choline be an overlooked piece of liver health? 👀
