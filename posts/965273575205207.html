Potassium-enriched salt cut recurrent stroke 14% and stroke deaths 21% in stroke survivors

Gokhan Can · Feb 11, 2025 · ♥ 17 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Blood pressure, salt & potassiumHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

A cluster randomized trial in rural northern China (a subgroup analysis of a larger trial) gave stroke survivors a salt substitute containing 25% potassium chloride. It reduced recurrent stroke, hemorrhagic stroke and stroke-related death compared with regular salt, suggesting a cheap, widely usable strategy where sodium intake is high.

  • Salt substitute with 25% potassium chloride vs regular salt
  • 14% fewer recurrent strokes
  • 30% fewer hemorrhagic strokes
  • 21% fewer stroke-related deaths

A large-scale clinical trial in China found that using a salt substitute enriched with potassium (25% potassium chloride) significantly reduced the risk of recurrent stroke and death in individuals with a history of stroke. The study, analyzing a subgroup of a larger trial, showed a 14% reduction in recurrent stroke, a 30% reduction in hemorrhagic stroke, and a 21% decrease in stroke-related deaths in the group using the potassium-enriched salt compared to those using regular salt. The findings suggest that this simple, low-cost dietary change could be a widely applicable strategy, especially in regions with high sodium intake.
