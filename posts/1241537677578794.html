Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that urgently requires effective treatments. A 2024 study published in the Journal of Alzheimer’s Disease investigated the potential of sildenafil, a phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitor commonly used for other medical conditions, as a candidate drug for AD. Researchers analyzed real-world patient data from large healthcare databases, including MarketScan® Medicare Supplemental and Clinformatics®. After adjusting for factors such as age, sex, race, and comorbidities, the study found that individuals using sildenafil had a significantly lower likelihood of developing Alzheimer’s disease compared to patients using other medications such as bumetanide, furosemide, spironolactone, and nifedipine. For example, sildenafil use was associated with a 54% reduced incidence of AD in the MarketScan® database and a 30% reduced prevalence in the Clinformatics® database.

To further explore how sildenafil might affect Alzheimer’s disease, the researchers conducted laboratory experiments using neurons derived from patient-induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), including cells from both familial and sporadic AD cases. The results showed that sildenafil reduced tau hyperphosphorylation—an important pathological feature of Alzheimer’s disease—in a dose-dependent manner. Additionally, RNA-sequencing analysis revealed that sildenafil influences genes and biological pathways associated with AD progression. These findings suggest that sildenafil may have protective effects against Alzheimer’s disease and could potentially be repurposed as a treatment. However, the authors emphasize that randomized clinical trials are still needed to confirm its safety and effectiveness for treating AD.
