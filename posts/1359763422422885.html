An opinion piece is not a study: judge excess-death claims by the evidence hierarchy

Louis Nervegna · Aug 03, 2026 · ♥ 18 reactions · 74 comments · original Facebook post
How to read researchMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis responds to a post citing a Japanese paper on excess deaths after COVID vaccination, saying the publication is a speculative perspective piece and not empirical evidence.

THIS IS NOT A STUDY.
IT IS AN OPINION PIECE.

———-

The publication is a speculative opinion/perspective piece rather than an empirical scientific study.

When reading journal publications like this, you need to understand where they fall on the hierarchy of evidence in medicine:

↪ Evidence hierarchy: RCTs and long prospective cohorts sit at the top

Opinion pieces like this aren’t even on the hierarchy at all. They are not studies. They serve to communicate hypotheses that in turn need to be tested in actual studies.

Everyone is welcome to their own opinions, including the authors of this publication. But when it comes to making statements of fact, we use the evidence hierarchy in medicine as our guide to judging whether or not something is true. That’s how this group works.

Please remember that there are over 1.5 million new papers added to pubmed each year. Many of these are opinion pieces like this publication, and not actual empirical studies. Just because something is indexed on pubmed does not mean that it is factually true. You have to use the evidence hierarchy to make that judgement.

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.
