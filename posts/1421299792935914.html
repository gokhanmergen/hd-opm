Losartan may stimulate brown fat, preclinical Rockefeller study suggests

Rockefeller researchers report that the blood pressure drug losartan, an angiotensin receptor blocker, may stimulate brown fat. Brown fat is linked to better insulin sensitivity and lower diabetes and cardiovascular risk. The link between ARBs and brown fat was first found in an analysis of over 100,000 patients, and this study is preclinical.

Shared from The Rockefeller University · original post

Findings from Rockefeller's Paul Cohen suggest that repurposing losartan—a commonly prescribed blood-pressure drug—may be a clinically viable method of stimulating #brownfat, which has been linked to improved #cardiovascular health and, reported for the first time in this study, #psychiatric health.

The Cohen lab, along with colleagues at Weill Cornell Medicine and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, initially discovered the connection between brown fat and the drug class to which losartan belongs, known as angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), through an analysis of over 100,000 patients using next-generation analytical tools.

Previous work had suggested a link between brown fat and reduced prevalence of cardiovascular disease. In this work, the team now demonstrates that in addition to the having brown fat or not, a higher degree of brown fat activity is connected to enhanced cardiometabolic benefits.

They also explored these associations across the body’s six brown fat depots, with increased activity in the armpit (axillary) depot being most strongly tied to cardiometabolic health. They uncovered some new connections as well, including a lower incidence of anxiety and depression in patients with the presence of brown fat.
