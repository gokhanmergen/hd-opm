Ketogenic diets may increase AGE’s.

Research cited

  • Ketosis Leads to Increased Methylglyoxal Production on the Atkins Diet · other · Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences · 2005
Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

Ketogenic diets significantly increase methylglyoxal production, which directly causes glycation.

Methylglyoxal is a precursor to formation of advanced glycation end products (AGE’s), which is implicated in the pathogenesis of diabetes, heart disease, alzheimer’s, and aging. Methylglyoxal is a main cause of glycation in humans and other animals.

Blocking production of methylglyoxal significantly extends lifespan in mice.

Claims that ketogenic diets protect against glycation should be viewed with extreme suspicion. They may be doing the exact opposite.

Quoted:

“Since methylglyoxal and its byproducts are recognized as a significant cause of blood vessel and tissue damage, we measured methylglyoxal, acetone, and acetol in subjects on the Atkins diet. We found that by 14-28 days, methylghyoxal levels rose 1.67-fold (P= 0.039) and acetol and acetone levels increased 2.7- and 6.12-fold, respectively (P= 0.012 and 0.028). Samples from subjects with ketosis showed even greater increases in methylglyoxal (2.12-fold), as well as acetol and acetone, which increased 4.19- and 7.9-fold, respectively; while no changes were seen in samples from noncompliant, nonketotic subjects.”
