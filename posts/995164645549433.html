Scurvy can occur on a carnivore diet, as a case report of canned-beef eating shows

Louis Nervegna · Mar 29, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites a case report showing that a man eating only canned beef and other cooked foods with minimal fresh produce developed classic scurvy. He recovered within weeks on vitamin C 500 mg twice daily. Louis argues that vitamin C needs vary and that fresh meat's vitamin C may not suffice for everyone.

  • Patient ate mainly canned beef, almost no fresh fruit or vegetables
  • Treated with ascorbic acid 500 mg twice daily, complete recovery within weeks
  • Canned beef would have little vitamin C compared with fresh meat

Yes, you can develop scurvy on a pure carnivore diet.

It is documented in the medical literature. See reference below.

Clearly there are some people on a carnivore diet who do actually develop scurvy, even if most do not. Biological requirements for dietary vitamin C vary from person to person. Fresh meat does contain some vitamin C, but this might not be enough for everyone.

The patient in this case report ate a great deal of canned beef, which would have little if any vitamin C compared to fresh meat.

Quoted:

“On further questioning, the patient admitted to a diet consisting only of Bunker Hill canned beef and other cooked foods, with very minimal fresh fruits or vegetables. His constellation of findings was believed to be classic for scurvy and he was discharged on vitamin C supplements (ascorbic acid 500 mg twice a day) with instructions to continue this therapy for life. Within weeks he had made a complete recovery.”

EVITA trial: vitamin C lowered deaths in clonal blood conditions (exploratory result)

In the small phase 2 EVITA randomized trial of oral vitamin C in clonal cytopenia and lower-risk myeloid cancers, the vitamin C group had 65% lower risk of death (11 vs 24 deaths) and fewer serious adverse events. Survival was exploratory and the primary endpoint of clonal growth showed no difference. The poster speculates relevance to CHIP and cardiovascular disease but calls it a big unproven leap.

Vitamin C blood levels follow an S-curve and plateau, with saturation near 1000 mg/day

Jun 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Louis shares a pharmacokinetics explanation: blood vitamin C rises steeply from 30 to 100 mg/day, then flattens, so higher doses do not proportionally raise levels. These figures are for healthy people; infection and stress raise the saturation point.

Neutrophil vitamin C saturates near 200 mg twice daily; infection raises the need

May 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 11 Supplements & vitamins

William Wallace's shared explanation: neutrophils use vitamin C as an internal antioxidant pool to survive the respiratory burst they use to kill microbes. In healthy people neutrophil levels saturate at around 200 mg twice per day, and the dose needed to saturate them rises during infections.
