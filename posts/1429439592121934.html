Statin use linked to lower open-angle glaucoma risk in a large UC San Diego database study

Louis Nervegna · Oct 23, 2026 · ♥ 2 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A retrospective TriNetX cohort study reported in the British Journal of Ophthalmology found statin use was associated with lower open-angle glaucoma incidence and less need for treatment escalation. It is observational, so it shows association and not proof of protection.

👁️ Statins, the common cholesterol-lowering pills, may also be linked to a lower risk of glaucoma, a large study suggests.

Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that damage the optic nerve, which carries signals from the eye to the brain. It is one of the leading causes of permanent blindness worldwide.

It is often linked to high pressure inside the eye. Treatment usually focuses on lowering that pressure with eye drops, laser treatment or surgery.

Researchers at the University of California San Diego and other universities looked at health records from a large global database.

They compared about 210,000 adults over 40 who took statins with a similar group who had never used them.

After five years, 2.3 percent of statin users had developed open-angle glaucoma, the most common type. Among non-users, it was 2.6 percent.

That works out to about 15 percent lower risk. After 10 years, the risk was about 10 percent lower.

The team also studied people who already had glaucoma. Those taking statins were less likely to need stronger treatment.

At five years, they were 36 percent less likely to need surgery, and 60 percent less likely to need a laser procedure.

It is not clear why. Statins may protect nerves, reduce inflammation or improve the flow of fluid out of the eye.

Senior author Robert Weinreb said that, if confirmed, statins could be used alongside standard treatments to lower eye pressure.

But there are important caveats. This was an observational study, so it shows a link, not proof that statins prevent glaucoma.

The records did not include eye pressure or vision test results, and people who take statins may differ in other ways.

Statins should not be taken just to prevent glaucoma. Anyone with concerns should talk to their doctor, and regular eye checks remain important.
