Can K2 + D3 slow calcium buildup in your heart’s arteries? A new trial found a measurable benefit

In 398 adults with severe coronary calcification, two years of daily supplementation produced approximately 21% less progression in calcium score than placebo: a rise of 196 versus 248 points.

The dose: 720 µg vitamin K2 as MK-7 + 1,000 IU vitamin D3 daily.

But calcium is only one component of plaque. In the 143 participants assessed for plaque composition, total plaque volume increased similarly in both groups.

These findings support slower calcium accumulation in people with substantial existing calcification. They don’t establish plaque reversal, fewer heart attacks, or whether K2 alone would deliver the same result.

Hasific et al. | Circulation, 2026 | PMID: 42661461.

A 2021 narrative review in Open Heart describes vitamin K2 as a regulator of calcium in the cardiovascular system by activating matrix Gla protein (MGP). Low vitamin K status leaves more inactive MGP, which has been linked to arterial stiffness and vascular calcification. It is a narrative review, not outcome trial evidence.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

Vitamin D trials in heart disease mostly disappointing despite mechanistic promise

Louis shares a 2026 review describing a 'vitamin D paradox': promising cell, animal and observational data, but supplementation has not consistently reduced cardiovascular events in unselected populations unless people are truly deficient. Aside from bone health, he says the best RCT evidence is for autoimmune disease.
