CARDIA: low-carb diets in young adults tied to more coronary calcium if animal-based

Low-Carbohydrate Diet Score and Coronary Artery Calcium Progression: Results From the CARDIA Study

Abstract

Objective: To investigate whether low-carbohydrate diets (LCDs) were associated with coronary artery calcium (CAC) progression. Approach and Results: We included the participants who completed computed tomography assessment of baseline CAC in 2000 to 2001 (year 15) and follow-up (year 20 or 25) and food frequency questionnaire (years 0, 7, and 20) in the CARDIA study (Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults). CAC progression was defined as CAC >0 at follow-up among participants with baseline CAC of 0 and an annualized change of 10 or percent change of ≥10% for those with 0<baseline CAC<100 or baseline CAC≥100, respectively. Among 2226 included participants (age, 40.4±3.5 years; 45.4% men), the carbohydrate intake accounted for 47.8±6.5% of total energy, and 204 (9.2%) had CAC at baseline (year 15). Over a mean follow-up of 8.3 years, 591 (26.5%) participants had CAC progression. After adjustment for traditional cardiovascular risk factors and other dietary factors, carbohydrate intake as a percentage of total energy was inversely associated with the risk of CAC progression (hazard ratio, 0.731 [95% CI, 0.552-0.968]; P=0.029). Furthermore, the animal-based but not plant-based LCD score was significantly associated with a higher risk of CAC progression (animal-based LCD score: hazard ratio, 1.456 [95% CI, 1.015-2.089]; P=0.041; plant-based LCD score: hazard ratio, 1.016 [95% CI, 0.821-1.257]; P=0.884; both comparing extreme groups).

Conclusions: LCDs starting at a young age are associated with an increased risk of subsequent CAC progression, particularly when animal protein or fat are chosen to replace carbohydrates.

Urinary metals linked to coronary calcium comparable to smoking and diabetes in MESA

Sep 18, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 8 · 💬 5 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

A JACC study in 6,418 MESA participants followed over 10 years found higher urinary levels of non-essential (cadmium, tungsten, uranium) and essential (cobalt, copper, zinc) metals corresponded to more coronary artery calcification, with associations similar in size to traditional risk factors like smoking and diabetes.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.

No long-lived population ate a carbohydrate-free diet; long-term effects unknown

Jun 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 21 · 💬 4 Low-carb, keto & carnivore

Louis argues that every long-lived population studied includes carbohydrate from vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains or fermented dairy, and that the few carbohydrate-free-eating groups, such as certain Eskimo populations, were not notably long-lived. He says low-carb diets may help short-term but long-term heart-disease effects are speculative, so he suggests including low-GI carbohydrates.
