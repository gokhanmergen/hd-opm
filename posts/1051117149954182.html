Inflammation is a causal risk factor; CRP predicts heart disease as well as LDL (Ridker)

Louis Nervegna · Jun 20, 2025 · ♥ 17 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes Harvard Health on Paul Ridker's view that people with low LDL on statins can still carry inflammatory risk, and that a CRP blood test predicts heart disease about as well as LDL. He frames inflammation as an independent causal risk factor.

  • Residual inflammatory risk persists with low LDL on a statin
  • CRP is described as as good as LDL for predicting heart disease

Inflammation is now recognized as an independent causal risk factor for heart disease.

Quoted:

“Even if you're on a statin and your LDL cholesterol is quite low, you're not home free. You may still have inflammatory risk," says Dr. Paul Ridker, director of the Center for Cardiovascular Disease Prevention at Harvard-affiliated Brigham and Women's Hospital. A blood test that detects C-reactive protein (CRP), a byproduct of inflammation, is just as good at predicting heart disease as an LDL measurement, he says.

At least six strategies have reversed plaque in randomized trials

Louis lists six strategies shown to reverse plaque in human RCTs: major weight loss, Mediterranean/DASH diet, LDL-lowering drugs (statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, ezetimibe), colchicine, exercise and high-dose EPA fish oil. He would like to see a trial combining all of them.
