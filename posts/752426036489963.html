Quality CPR may preserve brain function up to an hour into arrest, early evidence says

Louis Nervegna · Feb 07, 2024 · ♥ 11 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Heart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis urges never giving up on CPR, quoting reports that the brain shows signs of electrical recovery for as long as an hour into ongoing CPR and that the old teaching that the brain dies after 3-5 minutes is not accurate. The claim of surviving with no severe brain damage is stronger than the quoted evidence, which is described as emerging.

  • Brain shows signs of electrical recovery up to an hour into ongoing CPR (as quoted)
  • Quoted researcher: 3-5 minutes of oxygen deprivation does not necessarily mean brain death

Never give up on CPR !

You can keep someone alive (with NO severe brain damage) for up to an HOUR with well done CPR.

——

“If someone has been in cardiac arrest for 10 minutes, the brain is permanently damaged and there's nothing to do, right?

Not so according to emerging evidence that suggests that the brain shows signs of electrical recovery for as long as an hour into ongoing cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR).”

“One of the big implications of the study, he said, is that a lot of physicians are taught that somehow after, say, 3-5 minutes of oxygen deprivation, the brain dies. Our study showed this is not true. It showed that the brain may not be functioning, which is why they flatline. But if you're able to resuscitate them appropriately, you can restore activity up to an hour later."
