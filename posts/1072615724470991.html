Krill oil preserved muscle and strength during weight loss in an 8-week RCT

Louis Nervegna · Jul 21, 2025 · ♥ 17 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post

Louis notes that omega-3 protection against muscle loss has been accumulating for years. The shared summary describes a 2025 placebo-controlled trial in adults with overweight or obesity doing alternate-day fasting, where krill oil preserved fat-free mass and function despite equal weight loss.

  • Weight loss ~4.5 kg in both groups over 8 weeks
  • Fat-free mass change -0.2 kg with krill oil vs -1.2 kg with placebo
  • Grip strength -0.2 kg vs -0.9 kg
  • Daily dose: EPA 764 mg, DHA 376 mg
  • Limitations: short duration (8 weeks)

This is not the first study that has shown omega 3 fats protect against muscle loss.

Shared from William Wallace, Ph.D. · original post

A 2025 randomized, placebo-controlled trial published (PMID: 40671417) found that krill oil supplementation during alternate-day fasting preserved fat-free mass and muscle strength in adults with overweight or obesity undergoing ~4.5 kg of weight loss over 8 weeks.

Findings:
Despite equivalent weight loss between groups (−4.5 to −4.6 kg), krill oil prevented functional and structural losses:

  • FFM loss was −0.2 kg with krill oil vs. −1.2 kg with placebo
  • Grip strength decreased −0.2 kg with krill oil vs. −0.9 kg with placebo
  • Chair rise time improved by −1.8 sec in the krill group vs. no change in placebo
  • Greater reductions in CRP and TNF-α (markers of inflammation)

🧬 Active compound profile (daily dose):

  • EPA: 764 mg
  • DHA: 376 mg
  • Astaxanthin: 400 mcg
  • Choline: 312 mg

📊 Comparison to fish oil:
This EPA+DHA dose is roughly equivalent to ~2.5–3 g/day of concentrated fish oil. However, krill oil delivers omega-3s in phospholipid form (potentially enhancing bioavailability) and uniquely includes astaxanthin and choline, which may support antioxidant activity and neuromuscular function.

Mechanistic insight:
Krill oil may preserve muscle via:

  • Reducing inflammation (↓ CRP, TNF-α)
  • Supporting muscle protein synthesis
  • Enhancing mitochondrial function
  • Providing choline for acetylcholine production (necessary for muscle contraction)
  • Delivering astaxanthin, an antioxidant shown to reduce muscle degradation

⚠️ Limitations:

  • Short duration (8 weeks)
  • FFM measured via D₂O dilution; hydration shifts may confound lean mass estimates
  • Muscle function was assessed only by grip strength and chair rise
  • The dose may not be practical for all consumers due to cost and capsule burden
  • No exercise training was included, limiting generalizability to active populations

Krill oil may be a valuable adjunct to support muscle preservation during calorie restriction, especially in sedentary individuals with overweight.
