TG/HDL ratio tracks visceral fat; clearing liver and pancreas fat improves it

Louis Nervegna · Jul 27, 2024 · ♥ 37 reactions · 50 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

The poster argues TG/HDL is really a marker of metabolic health driven by visceral fat, especially in the liver and pancreas. Any diet that removes that fat should improve TG/HDL, insulin sensitivity and glycemic control. This is opinion without a cited study.

  • TG/HDL ratio tracks visceral fat
  • Liver and pancreas fat are the key determinants
  • Fat loss there also improves insulin sensitivity and glycemic control

A general comment for anyone trying to improve their triglycerides and HDL:

The ratio of TG/HDL tracks visceral fat. Any diet that gets rid of visceral fat will dramatically improve TG/HDL. This ratio is really a marker of metabolic health, and the biggest determinant of that is visceral fat— particularly the visceral fat that gets stored in the liver and pancreas. Clear out the fat in your liver and pancreas— and in and around your abdominal organs in general— and your TG/HDL ratio is almost guaranteed to improve. Most people will also see a big improvement in insulin sensitivity and glycemic control when clearing out this visceral fat, as well, which is what the TG/HDL ratio is really reflecting.
