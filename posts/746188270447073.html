Willett: refined starch and sugar (~40% of US calories) drive heart disease and diabetes

Louis Nervegna · Jan 26, 2024 · ♥ 14 reactions · 31 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues that mainstream nutrition science now sees refined sugar and refined starch, possibly including white potatoes, as direct causes of heart disease and diabetes, citing Harvard's Walter Willett in a ZOE interview. He notes about 40% of US calories come from refined sugar/starch and white potatoes, and says this should be replaced with healthier carbohydrate or healthy fat and protein sources. The post is opinion backed by an expert interview, not a specific study.

  • ~40% of US calories come from refined sugar/starch and white potatoes (per the post)
  • Trans fats have largely been removed from the food supply
  • Suggested swap: healthier carbs, or healthy fat/protein sources

Even in traditional academic evidence-based nutrition, it has become very clear that refined sugar and refined starch are directly causal in multiple diseases including heart disease and diabetes. This may also include the starch in white potatoes.

The scientific evidence for this has become overwhelming. Listen to Harvard professor Walter Willett explain it in this video. (This guy is about as rigorous and evidence-based as it gets in nutrition.)

In the USA, we get 40% of our calories from refined sugar/starch and white potatoes. Now that trans fats have been removed from the food supply (largely because of past lobbying efforts from Willett), this enormous intake of refined carbohydrate may represent the biggest single cause of disease and suffering in western diets.

To eliminate this increase in disease risk, the 40% of calories from refined sugar/starch and white potatoes has to be transitioned to healthier forms of carbohydrate or to healthy sources of fat or protein.

Swapping 10% of carb calories for vegetable fats cut lethal prostate cancer 29%

Dec 20, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 2 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis quotes a UCSF-led study of nearly 4,600 men with non-metastatic prostate cancer. Substituting healthy vegetable fats (olive and canola oil, nuts, seeds, avocado) for carbohydrates or animal fats was associated with lower lethal prostate cancer and all-cause mortality. The study adjusted for age, treatment, BMI, smoking, exercise and other factors.
