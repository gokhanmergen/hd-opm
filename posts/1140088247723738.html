2025 ESC/EAS update: where ezetimibe, bempedoic acid, PCSK9 drugs and inclisiran fit

AI summary & key facts

A shared graphic summarizes the 2025 ESC/EAS dyslipidaemia guideline update on add-on LDL-lowering after statins. Ezetimibe is the first oral add-on, bempedoic acid suits statin intolerance, PCSK9 antibodies give the biggest reductions in very-high-risk patients, and inclisiran offers twice-yearly dosing for adherence.

  • Ezetimibe: about 20% LDL-C reduction, first-line oral add-on
  • Bempedoic acid: about 23% alone, about 38% with ezetimibe
  • PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies: about 50-60% reduction
  • Inclisiran: siRNA injection given twice yearly
  • Stepwise approach from oral combinations to injectables

Not at LDL-C Goal? Here’s Where the New Agents Fit in 2025.

Despite statin therapy, many patients require additional LDL-C reduction. The 2025 ESC/EAS update refines the role of newer agents:

· Ezetimibe: First-line oral add-on; ~20% reduction.
· Bempedoic Acid: Oral option for statin intolerance; ~23% alone, ~38% with ezetimibe.
· PCSK9 mAbs: High-efficacy injectables (~50-60% reduction) for very high-risk patients.
· Inclisiran (siRNA): Novel, twice-yearly injection for long-term adherence.

Takeaway: A stratified, stepwise approach—from oral combinations to advanced injectables—ensures more patients achieve and maintain their LDL-C goals.
References: Mach F, et al. 2025 ESC/EAS Guidelines for the management of dyslipidaemias. Eur Heart J. 2025.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).
