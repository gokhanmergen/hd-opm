Louis argues that the Roy Taylor randomized trial, 8 weeks of very low calorie shakes followed by a Mediterranean maintenance diet, led to regeneration of pancreatic tissue on imaging and normal beta cell function after two years. He defends calling this a cure and stresses it was not low carb or keto.

  • Then normal-calorie traditional Mediterranean diet for maintenance
  • After 2 years: pancreas regrown on imaging and beta cell function normal

Regeneration of the pancreas (as proven on imaging) and restoral of normal beta cell function is a cure for type 2 diabetes, not just a reversal or a remission.

It would be inaccurate to not use the word “cure” in a case where the pancreas is literally regrown on imaging and restored to normal function again, and where all clinical signs of diabetes have disappeared.

There is only one randomized controlled trial in the history of medicine that has ever accomplished this. It used extreme calorie restriction to rapidly clear the liver and pancreas of fat, and then a traditional Mediterranean diet with normal calories for long-term maintenance:

It was not low carb or keto.

The liver and pancreas were completely cleared of ectopic fat after 8 weeks, with this approach. After 2 years of keeping the ectopic fat in the pancreas very low (using a normal calorie traditional Mediterranean diet), the tissue in the pancreas literally regrew back to (near) normal again. This is proven on imaging. Beta cell function was restored to normal. All clinical signs of the diabetes disappeared.

I absolutely stand by the use of the word “cure” in this case.

Here are the details of the diet:

For 8 weeks, it was an extreme calorie restriction diet using meal replacement shakes. The shakes contained normal proportions of carbs, protein, fat. It was not low carb. But it was only 800 calories. People were also allowed unlimited amounts of low calorie salad vegetables.

After 8 weeks on this diet, people lost around 30lbs, and the liver and pancreas were completely cleared of fat.

Then people were transitioned to a normal calorie traditional Mediterranean diet for maintenance. They stayed on the Mediterranean diet for the following 2 years when the pancreas regenerated.

The key to the regeneration was keeping the liver and pancreas free of fat for the entire 2 years.

If they regained any significant weight during the 2 years, they were immediately put back on the 800 calorie diet for a few weeks, until the weight was lost again. Then back on the normal calorie Mediterranean diet again for maintenance.

Exercise was encouraged, but not mandatory.
