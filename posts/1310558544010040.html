Fewer ultra-processed foods linked to 41% lower dementia risk; processed meat highest risk

Louis Nervegna · Jun 05, 2026 · ♥ 0 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaMediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward dietsMeat, eggs & dairy
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a 2026 American Journal of Public Health analysis of the Health and Retirement Study, in which lower ultra-processed food intake was linked to 41% lower dementia risk and processed meats such as bacon, hot dogs and ham showed the highest risk. He adds that processed meats have among the worst hazard ratios in nutrition epidemiology.

  • Lower ultra-processed food intake: 41% lower dementia risk (per quoted news report)
  • Processed meats linked to the highest risk
  • Health and Retirement Study of older US adults, 2013-2020

“Tonight, new research finding that ultraprocessed foods might increase the risk for dementia. The study published in the American Journal of Public Health, showing that people who ate fewer [ultra] processed foods had a 41% lower dementia risk. Scientists linked processed meats, like bacon, hot dogs, and ham, to the highest risk.”

Two slices of deli meat a day linked to 44% higher dementia risk in a new review

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 15 Meat, eggs & dairy

A review in the Annual Review of Nutrition reports that about two slices of processed meat daily is linked to 44% higher dementia risk and 52% higher Alzheimer's risk, drawing on dozens of studies including nearly 500,000 UK participants. Replacing 10% of ultra-processed food calories with unprocessed food was linked to 19% lower dementia risk. The poster says processed meat has the worst hazard ratios of any food category apart from trans fats.

Most omnivores already eat enough fish or poultry for the APOE4 benefit in a Swedish study

Louis comments that participants with an APOE4 gene variant saw the maximum benefit at about 2.5 oz per day of unprocessed meat or fish, which people eating Mediterranean or MIND-style diets typically already reach. Yassine: Swedish APOE4 meat study underpowered for dementia but significant for cognition

Louis shares Hussein Yassine's analysis of the Swedish study on meat and dementia in APOE4 carriers. The genotype interaction for cognitive trajectories was significant but the dementia interaction was not, reflecting limited power, and Yassine calls for genotype-randomized nutrition trials.

APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Mar 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 22 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.

Swapping processed red meat for nuts, legumes or fish linked to 19-28% lower dementia risk

Louis shares news of a Harvard prospective cohort linking processed red meat to dementia. Replacing a daily serving with nuts or legumes was associated with 19% lower dementia risk, and with fish up to 28%. It is the second large cohort in six months linking processed meat to dementia.
