Lp(a) risk starts above the median, well below the 125 nmol/L guideline cutoff

Louis Nervegna · May 09, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post

Louis shares analysis of the Mass General Brigham Lp(a) Registry suggesting guideline thresholds for elevated Lp(a) are too high. MACE risk was already higher for people from the 51st percentile upward, even though guidelines flag only 125 nmol/L or above (the 80th percentile).

  • Guideline cutoff 125 nmol/L = 80th percentile (20% of population above)
  • 51st-70th percentile (42-111 nmol/L): adjusted HR 1.14 (95% CI 1.05-1.24) for MACE over more than a decade
  • 71st-90th percentile (112-215 nmol/L): adjusted HR 1.21, leveling off thereafter

Thresholds for elevated Lipoprotein(a) are too low, new research suggests.

Guidelines currently identify individuals with Lp(a) values of 125 nmol/L or above as being at higher risk for major adverse cardiovascular events. That cutoff is the 80th percentile, meaning 20% of the population has levels above 125.

However, new data analysis from the Mass General Brigham Lp(a) Registry indicates that anyone over the median also has a significantly and meaningfully increased risk for major adverse cardiovascular events.

“MACE risk over more than a decade was 14% higher among those in the 51st to 70th percentiles 42-111 nmol/L (adjusted HR 1.14, 95% CI 1.05-1.24) compared with average or lower Lp(a). The adjusted hazard ratio rose to 1.21 for the 71st to 90th percentile (112-215 nmol/L) and leveled off thereafter.”
