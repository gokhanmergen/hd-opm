Arterial stiffness may drive CVD, caused mainly by aging elastin rather than plaque

Louis Nervegna · Jul 08, 2024 · ♥ 12 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

The poster says arterial stiffness likely contributes substantially to CVD, driven mainly by aging of elastin in the artery wall rather than by plaque or calcification. He says diet, exercise and some drugs and supplements may prevent or reverse it, referencing a Mike Hill clip.

  • Primary driver: aging of elastic tissue (elastin)
  • Diet, exercise, drugs and supplements may help prevent or reverse stiffening

Arterial stiffness is likely a major contributing factor in CVD.

The primary driver of this is not plaque or calcification. It is the aging of the elastic tissue itself in the artery (elastin proteins), which becomes less flexible as you get older.

Diet, exercise, and certain drugs and supplements can all help to prevent and reverse this stiffening of the arterial tissue.
