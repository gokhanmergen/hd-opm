Shingles vaccination linked to slower biological aging in over 3,800 older adults

Louis Nervegna · Oct 03, 2026 · ♥ 8 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A US Health and Retirement Study cohort analysis of over 3,800 adults aged 70+ found those vaccinated against shingles between 2008 and 2018 had lower inflammation scores, slower genetic aging and lower overall biological aging scores. The poster links this with evidence the vaccine may lower dementia risk, with reduced inflammaging a possible mechanism.

  • More than 3,800 US adults aged 70+
  • Vaccinated 2008-2018: lower inflammation scores, slower genetic aging, lower overall biological aging score
  • Proposed mechanism: reduced chronic inflammation

The evidence has been building for some time now that the shingles vaccine might lower risk for dementia.

The benefit might be even broader. It might be modestly slowing the aging process itself. A proposed mechanism is reduced inflammation (“inflammaging”).

Shared from Discover Magazine · original post

A shingles vaccine may offer benefits beyond preventing the painful rash — a study of more than 3,800 U.S. adults age 70 and older found that vaccinated participants showed signs of slower biological aging.

Researchers analyzed blood samples, physical assessments, and vaccination histories, focusing on markers tied to inflammation, immune function, circulation, and neurodegenerative and genetic aging. People who had received a shingles vaccine between 2008 and 2018 had lower inflammation scores, slower genetic aging, and a lower overall biological aging score.

The researchers think reduced chronic inflammation may help explain the association. Preventing reactivation of the virus that causes shingles could lessen the kind of persistent low-grade inflammation sometimes called “inflammaging,” which is linked to conditions such as heart disease, frailty, and cognitive decline.
