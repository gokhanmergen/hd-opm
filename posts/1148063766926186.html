Meta-analysis of 155 studies: flu, shingles and COVID raise heart attack and stroke risk

Louis Nervegna · Oct 29, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 8 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a report on a meta-analysis of 155 studies finding that common viral infections raise heart attack and stroke risk, both soon after infection and long term. Flu carried up to six times higher heart attack risk the month after infection, and COVID-19 nearly doubled risk.

  • 155 studies pooled
  • Flu: up to 6x heart attack risk in the following month
  • COVID-19: nearly double risk of heart disease or stroke
  • Severity of illness correlates with complication risk

A comprehensive meta-analysis of 155 studies has found that common viral infections, including influenza, shingles, and COVID-19, significantly increase the risk of heart attacks and strokes both immediately following infection and in the long term.

NBC Palm SpringsViral Infections Linked to Elevated Heart Attack and Stroke Risk, Meta-Analysis FindsThe elevated risk period extends beyond immediate post-infection recovery, suggesting lasting effects on cardiovascular health.nbcpalmsprings.com

Quoted:

“The research revealed that individuals who contracted the flu faced up to six times higher risk of heart attack within the month after infection. COVID-19 patients showed nearly double the risk of developing heart disease or stroke compared to those who were never infected.

According to the study's lead author, the severity of viral illness correlates directly with cardiovascular complication risk. More serious infections pose greater danger to heart health. The findings also indicate that viral infections can exacerbate pre-existing cardiac conditions.

The meta-analysis, which examined research spanning multiple decades, highlights a previously underappreciated connection between routine viral illnesses and cardiovascular events. The elevated risk period extends beyond immediate post-infection recovery, suggesting lasting effects on cardiovascular health.”

Platelet reactivity score (PRESS) predicts cardiovascular events in PAD patients

Aug 20, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 6 · 💬 19 Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinners

NYU researchers developed PRESS, combining platelet aggregation responses and RNA sequencing, to detect platelet hyperreactivity. In PAD patients, hyperreactive platelets predicted more major adverse cardiac or limb events within 30 days of revascularization, and PRESS above the median carried a 90% higher risk of major events over 18 months. It might help target aspirin.
