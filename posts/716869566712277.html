Review of 884 supplement RCTs: omega-3, folic acid and CoQ10 helped; beta-carotene harmed

Louis Nervegna · Nov 30, 2023 · ♥ 12 reactions · 7 comments · original Facebook post
Supplements & vitaminsCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a JACC systematic review of 884 randomized trials (883,627 participants) on 27 micronutrients. Omega-3 reduced CVD mortality, heart attack and CHD events; folic acid reduced stroke; CoQ10 reduced all-cause mortality; vitamins C, D, E and selenium had no effect on CVD or type 2 diabetes; beta-carotene increased mortality and stroke. Several others improved risk factors with moderate-to-high quality evidence.

  • 884 RCTs, 27 micronutrients, 883,627 participants
  • Omega-3: CVD mortality RR 0.93, MI RR 0.85, CHD events RR 0.86
  • Folic acid: stroke RR 0.84; CoQ10: all-cause mortality RR 0.68 (CI 0.49-0.94)
  • Vitamin C, D, E and selenium: no effect on CVD or T2D
  • Beta-carotene: all-cause mortality RR 1.10, CVD mortality RR 1.12, stroke RR 1.09

Results were compiled from 884 RCTs on the effect of various dietary supplements on cardiovascular disease risk:

“A total of 884 randomized controlled intervention trials evaluating 27 types of micronutrients among 883,627 participants (4,895,544 person-years) were identified. Supplementation with n-3 fatty acid, n-6 fatty acid, l-arginine, l-citrulline, folic acid, vitamin D, magnesium, zinc, α-lipoic acid, coenzyme Q10, melatonin, catechin, curcumin, flavanol, genistein, and quercetin showed moderate- to high-quality evidence for reducing CVD risk factors. Specifically, n-3 fatty acid supplementation decreased CVD mortality (relative risk [RR]: 0.93; 95% CI: 0.88-0.97), myocardial infarction (RR: 0.85; 95% CI: 0.78-0.92), and coronary heart disease events (RR: 0.86; 95% CI: 0.80-0.93). Folic acid supplementation decreased stroke risk (RR: 0.84; 95% CI: 0.72-0.97), and coenzyme Q10 supplementation decreased all-cause mortality events (RR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.49-0.94). Vitamin C, vitamin D, vitamin E, and selenium showed no effect on CVD or type 2 diabetes risk. β-carotene supplementation increased all-cause mortality (RR: 1.10; 95% CI: 1.05-1.15), CVD mortality events (RR: 1.12; 95% CI: 1.06-1.18), and stroke risk (RR: 1.09; 95% CI: 1.01-1.17).”
