Coffee polyphenols bind NR4A1, an exercise-linked receptor, in cell models

Mar 30, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

SuppVersity reports a Nutrients 2026 study in which brewed coffee components such as chlorogenic acid and kahweol bound the nuclear receptor NR4A1 as inverse agonists in cell models. NR4A1 is linked to glucose and fat handling and is raised by exercise, suggesting a possible mechanism for coffee's epidemiological benefits. This is cell-model evidence only.

Plant phytochemicals act as hormetic signals linked to resilience and healthy aging

Eric Verdin's post, shared by Louis, describes phytochemicals such as sulforaphane, EGCG, quercetin, curcumin, fisetin and resveratrol as protective compounds that interact with pathways for antioxidant defense, autophagy, mitochondria and inflammation. It frames this as one reason diverse plant-rich diets associate with healthier aging.

Green Mediterranean diet slowed brain aging in a randomized trial of 300 adults

Sep 16, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

In a randomized worksite trial in Israel, a calorie-controlled green-Mediterranean diet with exercise support shaped the brain age gap on MRI. Earlier reports said both Mediterranean diets slowed brain atrophy by about 50% over 18 months, and the green-MED diet lowered two proteins linked to brain aging.

Caffeine activates AMPK in yeast cells, which may explain coffee's longevity links

Jun 26, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 11 · 💬 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A Queen Mary University of London study in Microbial Cell found that caffeine acts via AMPK, the cellular fuel gauge, influencing growth, DNA repair and stress response. It was done in fission yeast, so human relevance is unproven.

Coffee linked to younger epigenetic age and up to 27% lower mortality, per FoundMyFitness

Sep 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 8 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A FoundMyFitness solocast summary says each extra daily cup is associated with a roughly 0.7-1 year younger epigenetic age, that 3+ cups daily links to 34-41% lower odds of accelerated biological aging, and coffee drinkers show up to 27% lower all-cause mortality. Decaf shows similar benefits.
