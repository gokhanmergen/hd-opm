Louis comments on a Nick Norwitz video in which a ketovore diet, whose LDL reached 500 mg/dL, is paired with isoleucine restriction. He explains Dudley Lamming's research showing isoleucine restriction extends HET3 mouse lifespan 33% and promotes weight loss, noting animal protein is high in isoleucine and whole plant protein is naturally low. The attachment mentions human data on protein restriction raising energy expenditure via FGF21 (Nature Metabolism).

I had a good laugh after watching this one.

Keto bro influencer who promoted high animal protein diets for years discovers the metabolic benefits of modest isoleucine restriction.

(His LDL hit 500mg/dL on the high animal protein ketovore diet that he was promoting.)

I’ve posted about isoleucine lots of times before in the rapamycin group.

Isoleucine restriction dramatically extends lifespan in HET3 mice (the same mice used in the ITP). They lose significant weight even in the absence of calorie restriction.

This is Dudley Lamming’s research at the University of Wisconsin, including the stuff about FGF21 in the video. Lamming is the one who discovered all this.

Simply cutting isoleucine intake by 2/3 extends male HET3 mouse lifespan by 33%. This beats rapamycin in males:

↪ Isoleucine restriction extends mouse lifespan 33%, and may explain the old rice diet

Search the rapamycin group for “isoleucine” to see all the posts on this topic.

We’re just starting to see the transition of this isoleucine research into humans. It looks promising.

Animal protein is very high in isoleucine.

Guess what kind of protein is naturally low in isoleucine? Yep, plant protein from whole food. Nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains, etc.

An easy way to practice modest isoleucine restriction is to try to get most of your protein from whole plant foods.

Cutting dietary isoleucine by two-thirds extended male mouse lifespan 33%

Louis shares a Cell Metabolism mouse study in which restricting a single essential amino acid, isoleucine, lengthened life (33% in males, 7% in females), improved leanness, blood sugar, strength and frailty, and reduced age-related cancer. Isoleucine restriction extended male HET3 mouse lifespan 33% despite higher calorie intake

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 · 💬 2 ↩ 4 Protein

Louis reposts a summary of the HET3 (Interventions Testing Program) mouse study from Dudley Lamming's group. Mice fed a diet with isoleucine cut by two-thirds from 6 months of age lived 33% longer in males and 7% in females with better health scores, leaner bodies and less cancer, despite higher calorie intake. Louis says this beats rapamycin in males and recommends whole plant protein as a way to lower isoleucine intake.

Protein restriction and aging: animal benefits, no proven human lifespan gain

Nov 14, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 Protein

Louis shares a summary of a review (Knopf and Lamming) of protein and amino-acid restriction. Restricting methionine or the branched-chain amino acids isoleucine and valine drives many animal metabolic benefits, FGF21 is involved, but lifespan extension has not been shown in humans. The field is shifting from 'less protein' to which amino acids matter, and older or active adults may need more protein. Louis says protein is complicated and influencers oversimplify it.

Protein type matters more than restriction: whole-food plant protein beats processed meat

Nov 12, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 18 · 💬 38 Protein

Louis argues against the 'protein craze': there is no good evidence protein restriction slows human aging, but long-term data show whole-food plant protein beats animal protein, especially processed and red meat. Among animal sources, seafood, poultry and fermented dairy have the lowest mortality and processed meat is worst. Plant protein's lower isoleucine, valine and methionine may explain part of it.
