Conspiracy believers overrate their abilities and overestimate public agreement (Cornell)

Louis Nervegna · Sep 04, 2026 · ♥ 15 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
Myths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a Cornell study of over 4,000 US adults finding that conspiracy believers overrate their cognitive abilities and believe 93% of the public agrees with them when true majority support was about 12%. It is an observation about behavior in health groups, not about heart health.

  • Over 4,000 US adults studied
  • Believers thought 93% of public agreed; true majority 12% of the time

I see this behavior all the time in these health groups…

Quoted:

“Led by researchers at Cornell University, the study examined over 4,000 U.S. adults and found that conspiracy believers consistently overrate their own cognitive abilities, such as numeracy and perception, while possessing a massive blind spot regarding their actual performance.

Even more striking is how these individuals misread public opinion. The research revealed that while the fringe conspiracies they supported were only backed by a true majority 12% of the time, the believers genuinely thought an astounding 93% of the public agreed with them. This massive false consensus effect means that conspiracy theorists are completely unaware of how far out on the fringe their views actually are.”

Statins neither cause nor cure cancer: meta-analyses of randomized trials show no effect

Dec 31, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 9 · 💬 4 Statins

Reviews the evidence on statins and cancer. A 2012 meta-analysis of 134,537 participants in 22 RCTs found identical cancer incidence (1.4% per year) and mortality (0.5% per year) in statin users and non-users, and a 2023 meta-analysis of 35 RCTs found the same. Anticancer effects in specific contexts remain preclinical or suggestive.

Olive oil protected against cardiac hypertrophy in aging-model rats

Nov 23, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 11 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

In rats given D-galactose to mimic cardiac aging, eight weeks of olive oil reduced cardiac hypertrophy and oxidative stress (MDA), increased antioxidant activity (SOD) and improved expression of heart-health genes. It is an animal study, so human benefit is not shown.
