Blood clot warning signs: DVT and pulmonary embolism

Louis Nervegna · Aug 13, 2025 · ♥ 3 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

A shared Cleveland Clinic item explains that venous clots can show no symptoms or severe ones; the most common are DVT in the legs and pulmonary embolism when a clot travels to the lungs.

  • DVT usually forms in leg deep veins
  • PE occurs when a DVT travels to the lungs

#dvtHD #pulmonaryembolismHD

Shared from Cleveland Clinic · original post

Early signs of a blood clot can be hard to identify. Everyone has different symptoms that can range from none to severe.

The most common kinds of venous blood clots are:

Deep vein thrombosis (DVT). A DVT most often forms in your legs in the deep veins of your body. Thrombosis is when clots stay put and block blood flow.

Pulmonary embolism (PE). If a DVT breaks off and travels to your lungs, it can create a pulmonary embolism. Embolisms are blood clots that break free and travel to other parts of your body.

DVTs can occur in your legs or arms. The most common signs of a blood clot in your leg include:

🔴Swelling
🔴Leg pain
🔴Varicose vein changes

Some common signs of a blood clot in your lungs include:

🔴Shortness of breath
🔴Chest pain
🔴Coughing up blood

Some pulmonary embolisms can be life-threatening, so go to the emergency room for a doctor’s evaluation if you notice any signs of a blood clot in your lung. Your primary care doctor may send you to the ER so you can get a same-day lung scan.

If you think you have a DVT and your symptoms remain for more than a day or two and worsen, reach out to your primary care doctor. Your doctor can help you decide your next steps.
