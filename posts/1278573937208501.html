Up to 90% of statin side effects occur on placebo too (SAMSON and ASCOT)

Louis Nervegna · Apr 28, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a video on the nocebo effect explaining SAMSON and ASCOT, stating up to 90% of statin side effects stem from nocebo, while genuine side effects exist and shouldn't be dismissed.

  • SAMSON: 90% of symptom burden also on placebo
  • ASCOT: side effect reports jumped 41% once patients knew they were on statins

Nocebo effect is extremely powerful.

The side effects that you experience with nocebo effect are very real— but they are not caused by the actual drug itself that you are taking.

Studies have shown that up to 90% of statin side effects are due to nocebo effect.

This is absolutely shocking, but true.

There are two very well-designed randomized trials that have elegantly demonstrated this, the SAMSON trial & the ASCOT trial. The video clip explains them.

Yes, statins have real side effects. These should not be minimized when they are genuine. But we now know that reports of statin side effects are dramatically inflated due to nocebo effect.

It’s very important to definitively rule out nocebo effect, if you are experiencing side effects from a statin. Anybody can be fooled by this. I have been fooled by it myself. It was a humbling experience, but now I know better.

Pooled blinded trials: statins cause only about 1 in 15 reported muscle aches

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 12 Statins

A pooled analysis of 19 blinded trials with 123,940 people found that over about four years, 27.1 of 100 on a statin reported muscle pain or weakness versus 26.6 of 100 on placebo. This implies that the vast majority of muscle complaints on statins are not caused by the drug, consistent with a nocebo effect.
