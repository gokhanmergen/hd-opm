Swapping processed red meat for nuts, legumes or fish linked to 19-28% lower dementia risk

Louis Nervegna · Jan 22, 2025 · ♥ 5 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Meat, eggs & dairyBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares news of a Harvard prospective cohort linking processed red meat to dementia. Replacing a daily serving with nuts or legumes was associated with 19% lower dementia risk, and with fish up to 28%. It is the second large cohort in six months linking processed meat to dementia.

  • Replacing a serving/day of processed red meat with nuts or legumes: 19% lower dementia risk
  • Replacing it with fish: up to 28% lower
  • Second large prospective cohort in 6 months linking processed meat and dementia

Yet another study showing harm from processed meat.

(See comments for a link that bypasses the paywall.)

Quoted:

“The new study concluded that replacing a serving of processed red meat with a serving of nuts or legumes, which include beans, peas and lentils, per day might lower the risk of dementia by 19 percent and by as much as 28 percent if the serving is replaced with fish.”

This is the second large prospective cohort study in the last 6 months to link processed meat with dementia. Here is the previous one:

Louis shares Hussein Yassine's analysis of the Swedish study on meat and dementia in APOE4 carriers. The genotype interaction for cognitive trajectories was significant but the dementia interaction was not, reflecting limited power, and Yassine calls for genotype-randomized nutrition trials.

APOE4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk in Swedish study

Mar 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 14 · 💬 22 ↩ 1 Brain health & dementia

Louis summarizes a widely shared observational study: APOE 3/4 and 4/4 carriers eating unprocessed meat or fish had lower dementia risk and possibly slower progression, with fish and poultry equal to red meat. No benefit was seen in non-APOE4 carriers, and processed meat raised dementia risk in everyone.

Walter Willett: unsaturated fats, Mediterranean eating and daily habits at 81

Oct 11, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 12 · 💬 4 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A Washington Post interview with Harvard nutrition researcher Walter Willett. He says his group's data showed total fat was irrelevant, trans fats were bad, saturated fat versus refined carbohydrate was only weakly related to heart disease, and unsaturated fats were linked to lower heart disease and mortality. He also describes his own diet, including oats, nuts, blueberries and a plant-based lunch.
