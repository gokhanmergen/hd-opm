Shared from Blue Zones · original post

One of the pillars of the Okinawan diet, Okinawan imo is a supercharged purple sweet potato, a cousin of the common yellow-orange varieties that has been an island staple since the 17th century. Despite its saccharine flavor, it does not spike blood sugar as much as a regular white potato. Like other sweet potatoes, it contains an antioxidant called sporamin, which possesses a variety of potent antiaging properties. The purple version contains higher levels than its orange and yellow cousins. This superfood is high in complex carbs, has a surprisingly low glycemic load, and packs an antioxidant punch with anthocyanin (the compound that makes blueberries blue). (Info from Dan Buettner's best-selling cookbook, #BlueZonesKtichen.)
