Shingles vaccine linked to 28% fewer heart attack and 42.5% fewer stroke hospitalizations

Gokhan Can · Aug 29, 2025 · ♥ 28 reactions · 14 comments · original Facebook post
Infections & vaccinesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

A Kaiser Permanente Southern California cohort matched 102,766 Shingrix recipients to 411,064 unvaccinated patients. Vaccinated people had lower shingles complications and fewer hospitalizations for heart attack and stroke. Gokhan notes the effect sizes are striking and that flu vaccine has similar heart-protective effects.

  • 102,766 vaccinated vs 411,064 matched unvaccinated; mean follow-up 2.5 years
  • Herpes zoster ophthalmicus risk 72.9% lower; postherpetic neuralgia 83.7% lower
  • Hospitalization for MI 28% lower; for stroke 42.5% lower

"In a study published on 9 August 2025.. Emily Rayens, PhD, MPH, of Kaiser Permanente Southern California in Pasadena, and colleagues, matched 102,766 vaccinated patients (recombinant herpes zoster vaccine, Shingrix) with 411,064 unvaccinated patients based on age, sex, race/ethnicity, and index date. Patients received two doses of the vaccine 4 weeks to 6 months apart from April 2018 to December 2020. The average follow-up time was 2.5 years.

The researchers adjusted for factors such as history of herpes zoster, healthcare utilization patterns, and prior vaccination with the live shingles virus.

  • Vaccinated individuals had a 72.9% lower risk of herpes zoster ophthalmicus.
  • Vaccinated individuals had an 83.7% lower risk of postherpetic neuralgia.
  • Vaccinated individuals had a 28% lower risk of hospitalization for acute myocardial infarction.
  • Vaccinated individuals had a 42.5% lower risk of hospitalization for stroke."

My thoughts: I'm blown away by the effect sizes here. Flu vaccine has similar heart-protective effects.
