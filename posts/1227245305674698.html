ASPREE: aspirin did not lower cancer risk in adults 70+ and may raise cancer deaths

Louis Nervegna · Feb 17, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Aspirin, antiplatelets & blood thinnersCancer
AI summary & key facts

Long-term ASPREE data in JAMA Oncology show daily 100 mg aspirin did not lower cancer risk in adults 70 and older and may increase cancer-related deaths.

  • 100 mg aspirin daily
  • Age 70+
  • No cancer risk reduction; possibly more cancer deaths

Aspirin does not appear to lower cancer risk in people over 70, like it does in younger people.

Research cited

  • ASPREE long-term cancer outcomes · RCT · JAMA Oncology
Shared from Medscape · original post

Long-term ASPREE trial data show that daily low-dose aspirin (100 mg) does not lower cancer risk and may increase cancer-related deaths in adults aged 70 and older. Published in JAMA Oncology, the findings suggest aspirin’s cancer protection seen in younger adults doesn’t extend to this age group. Louis shares a 2025 Nature study showing that platelet-derived thromboxane A2 binds CD8+ T cells and, via ARHGEF1, weakens their anti-tumor activity. Aspirin or COX-1 inhibitors lifted this and reduced metastatic burden in mouse melanoma and lung models, only in animals with intact T-cell immunity, suggesting a mechanism for aspirin's apparent cancer protection.
