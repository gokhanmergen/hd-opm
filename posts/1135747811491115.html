Prolonged very low carb diets can suppress thyroid hormones while TSH looks normal

Louis Nervegna · Oct 13, 2025 · ♥ 17 reactions · 20 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreHormones, women's & men's health
AI summary & key facts

Louis says long-term very low carbohydrate diets can suppress thyroid function, with a pattern of normal or low TSH but low T3 and/or T4 that can mislead doctors. He explains this as an adaptive response to perceived low calories, the same pattern found with prolonged calorie restriction. No study is cited.

  • Pattern: normal or low TSH with suppressed T3 and/or T4
  • Same pattern as prolonged calorie restriction
  • Body lowers thyroid function to conserve calories
  • Louis suggests mild suppression might partly explain lifespan extension from calorie restriction in animals

This kind of eyebrow thinning is a classic sign of hypothyroidism.

There are lots of potential causes for hypothyroidism.

Long term adherence to a very low carbohydrate diet can sometimes suppress thyroid function. It is something to watch out for, if you are on this kind of diet for a prolonged period of time.

On very low carb diets, you can see a pattern where TSH is normal or even low, but T3 and/or T4 are suppressed.

This pattern can confuse doctors who are not used to seeing it. They look at the TSH and think everything is fine, even though the patient is exhibiting clinical signs of hypothyroidism. Further testing reveals low T3 and/or T4.

The reason this can happen on a very low carb diet was explained in this post: ↪ Why the LMHR phenotype happens: starvation-like physiology in lean keto dieters

In a nutshell, the prolonged lack of carbohydrate tricks the body into thinking that calories are low. (This is not necessarily a bad thing, if not taken to extreme. Calorie restriction extends lifespan in animals. It might be a good thing.)

The body responds by lowering thyroid function in order to slow metabolism and conserve calories as much as possible. It does this by producing less hormone to stimulate the thyroid (TSH) and by reducing T3 and/or T4.

This is the same pattern that occurs in people on prolonged calorie restriction diets, where calories are kept very low. In fact, the phenomenon was first discovered in studies on calorie restriction. The slowing of metabolism by the thyroid that occurs in response to low calories is an ancient survival mechanism that evolved in animals to help keep them alive longer when food is scarce. It reduces the number of calories necessary for the organism to survive. (Mildly suppressed thyroid function like this may actually play a role in why calorie restriction extends lifespan in animals. The slowing of overall metabolism that it causes might contribute to extending lifespan.)
