A CAC score of zero does not exclude enough soft plaque to cause a serious blockage

Louis Nervegna · Mar 23, 2025 · ♥ 9 reactions · 15 comments · original Facebook post
Plaque, CAC & CT angiographyRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis comments that it happens often that someone has enough soft plaque for a serious blockage despite a CAC score of zero, and that calcified plaque is a sign of late-stage disease.

  • CAC of zero can coexist with significant soft plaque
  • Calcified plaque marks late-stage disease

Sadly, this happens quite often.

You can still have enough soft plaque to cause a serious blockage, even with a CAC score of zero.

Calcified plaque is a sign of late stage disease.

About 95% of acute-thrombosis heart attacks involve soft plaque, not calcified plaque

Oct 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 9 · 💬 9 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

The post says about 64% of heart attacks from acute thrombosis come from rupture of soft lipid-rich plaque and about 25% from erosion of soft plaque, while only about 5% involve calcified nodules. Because statins and other lipid-lowering drugs stabilize and reverse soft plaque, the poster argues they address the plaque type most responsible for heart attacks.
