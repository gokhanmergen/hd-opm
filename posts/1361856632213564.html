Statin users had 14% lower dementia risk in a meta-analysis of 7 million people

Louis Nervegna · Aug 05, 2026 · ♥ 20 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementia
AI summary & key facts

A systematic review of observational studies found statin use associated with 14% lower all-cause dementia and 18% lower Alzheimer's. Louis corrects that these were cohort and case-control studies, not RCTs.

  • 55 studies, ~7 million people
  • -14% all-cause dementia; -18% Alzheimer's
  • Observational

One correction to what he says: These were cohort and case-control studies. Not clinical trials per se. So this is a meta-analysis of observational studies, not a meta-analysis of randomized trials.

Still, the massive size of the pooled results make the result compelling. 7 million people is enormous.

I have done a video on statins and dementia and another one about statins don’t deplete your brain of cholesterol, but I feel compelled to show this picture again.

If anybody wants to look at this study, there’s the title. It is a meta-analysis of 55 human clinical trials involving 7 million people. And statin users compared to non-statin users had a 14% reduction of all cause dementia. It was even higher at 18% in Alzheimer’s dementia. 7 million people don’t lie. So when you hear from your friends, family, or social media charlatans that statins deplete your brain of cholesterol and cause dementia, nothing could be further from the truth.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).

Observational, randomized and genetic evidence all argue against statins causing dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 4 · 💬 1 Statins

Louis shares a new observational study linking statin use to lower dementia risk and lists the wider evidence in the group: observational studies as a whole suggest protection, randomized trials (including in people over 70) show no increase in dementia, trials of statins as Alzheimer's treatment are neutral, and Mendelian randomization suggests lower dementia risk with statin-pathway variants. Amyloid-beta drives excess astrocyte cholesterol and impairs brain waste clearance in mice

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 2 Brain health & dementia

A Nature Neuroscience mouse study found amyloid-beta raises calcium activity in astrocytes, boosting cholesterol production and removing AQP4 water channels, which impairs the glymphatic waste-clearing system. Calming astrocytes, blocking cholesterol synthesis or giving atorvastatin restored clearance and memory in mice. Human trials are needed, but the mechanism may partly explain observational links between statin use and lower dementia risk.

STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Nov 01, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 · 💬 5 Statins

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.
