STAREE: atorvastatin cut major CV events 30% in adults 70+ with no rise in dementia

Louis Nervegna · Nov 01, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsBrain health & dementiaHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

A quoted summary of the STAREE trial in NEJM: 9,971 adults aged 70 or older without cardiovascular disease, diabetes or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo for a median 5.9 years. Atorvastatin cut major cardiovascular events 30% and MI and revascularization 43% each, with no significant increase in dementia or all-cause mortality and equal serious adverse events.

  • 9,971 adults 70+, median follow-up 5.9 years, investigator-initiated
  • 30% lower major cardiovascular events; 43% lower MI; 43% lower coronary revascularization
  • Dementia HR 1.03 (not significant)
  • Serious adverse events 2.7% in both groups

Statins & Dementia - The STAREE trial

Quoted:

Statins and Dementia? Well let’s have a look see in a new primary prevention trial of older adults that looked at Dementia risk in addition to cardiac risk.

The new STAREE trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, randomized 9,971 adults age 70+ with no known cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or dementia to atorvastatin 40 mg or placebo and followed them for a median of 5.9 years.

This was an investigator-initiated study with no commercial or industry involvement.

The results:

  • 30% lower risk of major cardiovascular events with atorvastatin
  • 43% lower risk of MI
  • 43% lower risk of coronary revascularization
  • No significant increase in dementia: HR 1.03
  • No significant increase in all-cause mortality
  • Serious adverse events: 2.7% in BOTH groups

You’re allowed to dislike statins.

You’re allowed to discuss their limitations and adverse effects.

But you don’t get to invent your own data.

And the claim that statins cause dementia and don’t prevent heart disease is simply not an evidence-based position.

Only lipophilic statins like atorvastatin enter the brain; PCSK9 drugs and ezetimibe don't

Oct 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 25 · 💬 10 Brain health & dementia

Louis explains the blood-brain barrier and cholesterol: the brain makes its own cholesterol separately from blood, so non-statin LDL drugs and hydrophilic statins like rosuvastatin have little brain effect. Lipophilic statins do enter the brain, with promising observational data against Alzheimer's, and a 20,000-person atorvastatin trial is under way. Two prior trials showed statins do not worsen existing Alzheimer's, and midlife high LDL is a leading modifiable dementia risk (2024 Lancet Commission, about 7% of US cases per Louis).
