Nitric oxide and cardiovascular health: Ignarro's Nobel work and lifestyle support

Louis Nervegna · Jul 27, 2026 · ♥ 6 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis Ignarro won the 1998 Nobel Prize for showing nitric oxide relaxes vascular smooth muscle and regulates blood pressure. Exercise, healthy weight, blood sugar control and certain foods support production.

  • 1998 Nobel Prize
  • NO widens blood vessels

Louis Ignarro won the 1998 Nobel Prize in Medicine for discovering that nitric oxide acts as a crucial signaling molecule in the cardiovascular system. His research proved that nitric oxide causes vascular smooth muscle to relax, widening blood vessels and regulating blood pressure and flow. This breakthrough introduced a completely new principle for how gases function as signaling agents in the human body, paving the way for modern cardiovascular therapies and drugs like sildenafil (Viagra).

Quoted:

Your body’s ability to produce nitric oxide is closely connected to how you live.

Regular exercise, maintaining a healthy weight, managing blood sugar, and eating foods such as fish, spinach, blueberries, pomegranate, and Brussels sprouts can all support nitric oxide production.

That matters because nitric oxide helps relax and widen blood vessels, improve circulation, regulate blood pressure, and support blood flow throughout the body.
