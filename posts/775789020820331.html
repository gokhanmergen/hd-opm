LDL is necessary but not always sufficient for atherosclerosis, among 25+ risk factors

Louis Nervegna · Mar 25, 2024 · ♥ 14 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelCardiovascular disease: the big picture
AI summary & key facts

Louis summarizes a Joel Kahn video: there are at least 25 major heart disease risk factors, all of which should be addressed, but LDL is special because it appears necessary for plaque to form. It is not always sufficient, since some people with very high LDL never develop plaque. No other risk factor has been shown to be necessary, which is why cardiologists focus on LDL.

  • At least 25 major risk factors (per Kahn)
  • LDL appears necessary but not always sufficient for atherosclerosis

Does high cholesterol cause heart disease? And if so, why?

——

As Joel Kahn explains in the video, there are at least 25 major risk factors that cause heart disease. We should be trying to address all of them to lower risk as much as possible.

LDL is only one of them.

But what makes LDL special is that it appears to be necessary for atherosclerosis to occur. In other words, with zero LDL, it would be near impossible for plaque to form.

However, we know that LDL by itself is not always sufficient to cause atherosclerosis, because there are many examples of lucky people with very high LDL that never form plaque. So clearly high LDL by itself is not always sufficient to cause plaque, even if it is always necessary.

In a nutshell, LDL is likely necessary but not always sufficient to cause heart disease. The “necessary” part of that statement is why cardiologists are obsessed with LDL, more so than other risk factors. So far, no other risk factor has been identified as necessary, except for LDL.
