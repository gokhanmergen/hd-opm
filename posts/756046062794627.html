CSL112 ApoA-I infusion failed in phase 3 AEGIS-II, supporting HDL as marker not cause

Louis Nervegna · Feb 13, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelInsulin resistance & diabetesHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis argues the failure of AEGIS-II, in which patients received native ApoA-I (CSL112), shows ApoA-I is not directly causal in heart disease. Every large pharmaceutical trial raising HDL or ApoA-I has failed to prevent heart attacks and strokes. HDL and ApoA-I remain good risk markers, probably standing in for insulin resistance, so raising them by means other than improving insulin sensitivity may not lower risk.

  • AEGIS-II: phase 3 trial of CSL112 (human ApoA-I) failed in top-line results
  • Louis: every large RCT of HDL/ApoA-I-raising drugs has failed
  • Most researchers suspect HDL/ApoA-I are markers of insulin resistance

Another very large randomized controlled trial testing an intervention to raise HDL (in this case ApoA1) has failed.

They literally gave patients native ApoA1 isolated from human plasma (CSL112) and it still failed in this large phase 3 trial. This proves definitively that ApoA1 by itself is not directly causal in heart disease.

So far, every single large RCT that has ever tested pharmaceutical interventions to raise HDL and/or ApoA1 has failed to prevent heart attacks and strokes.

This does NOT mean that HDL or ApoA1 are bad markers for risk— they are good markers— but it does provide clear evidence that they are not causal by themselves.

If not directly causal by themselves, then the really interesting question becomes:
What are they actually markers for? Most researchers suspect insulin resistance.

An important corollary here is the following: If you are trying to raise your HDL or ApoA1 by any method besides improving insulin sensitivity, then there is a good chance that this will not actually lower your risk— because HDL by itself does not appear to be directly causal. However, insulin resistance is causal.

HDL and ApoA-I raising therapies repeatedly failed, so HDL is probably not directly causal

Louis reviews why HDL or ApoA-I is probably not causal for heart disease. CETP inhibitor trials failed (ILLUMINATE with torcetrapib was stopped for harm; dal-OUTCOMES with dalcetrapib and ACCELERATE with evacetrapib showed no benefit), as did fibrate and niacin trials and CSL112 infusions, and Mendelian randomization agrees. He proposes HDL and ApoA-I mainly act as a proxy for insulin sensitivity, which is the real causal factor.

Why insulin resistance lowers HDL, and why improving insulin sensitivity beats chasing HDL

Mar 21, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 5 ↩ 10 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis lists six mechanisms by which insulin resistance lowers HDL: impaired HDL maturation (lower LCAT), reduced apoA-I production, faster HDL catabolism (hepatic lipase, CETP), altered HDL composition and function, higher triglycerides making small dense HDL, and inflammation and oxidative stress. He concludes the best way to raise HDL is improving insulin sensitivity (lose visceral fat, exercise, cut refined starch and sugar), since insulin resistance is causal in heart disease while large RCTs suggest HDL itself is probably not.
