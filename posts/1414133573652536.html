APOE4 drives cholesterol buildup in brain astrocytes, promoting toxic protein deposits

Louis Nervegna · Oct 06, 2026 · ♥ 17 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Brain health & dementiaLDL, ApoB & lipid panelBlood vessels, circulation & endothelium
AI summary & key facts

Mount Sinai researchers using human stem-cell-derived miBrain tissue found APOE4 causes cholesterol to accumulate in astrocytes, impairing lysosomal clearance of alpha-synuclein and promoting amyloid and alpha-synuclein deposits. A second study showed APOE4 damages brain blood vessels through mechanisms unrelated to cholesterol. The poster reads this as cholesterol being potentially causal in APOE4 carriers.

  • APOE4 causes cholesterol accumulation in astrocytes, impairing lysosomal waste disposal
  • Alpha-synuclein aggregates and spreads to neurons
  • Second study: APOE4 turns vessel support cells into scar-forming cells; blocking a signaling pathway reversed damage in preclinical models
  • Experiments used stem-cell-derived miBrain 3D human brain tissue

In APOE4 carriers, cholesterol may be directly causal in both Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease. A new study demonstrates mechanistically that the over-accumulation of cholesterol in astrocytes (support cells in the brain) ultimately leads to the generation of toxic proteins like alpha-synuclein and amyloid, which are hallmarks of these diseases.

Quoted:

Experiments showed that APOE4 causes cholesterol to accumulate in astrocytes, support cells with essential roles in maintaining brain health. This excess cholesterol impairs the astrocytes' lysosomal waste-disposal system, reducing their ability to break down alpha-synuclein. The protein instead aggregates and spreads to neurons, leading to harmful protein deposits. The findings identify cholesterol metabolism in astrocytes and lysosomal function as promising therapeutic targets for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.

The miBrain model allowed researchers to study, in complex human brain-like tissue, how genetic risk factors such as APOE4 promote the buildup of harmful proteins associated with neurodegenerative disease. The researchers identified a chain of events linking APOE4 to lipid [cholesterol] accumulation in astrocytes, impaired clearance of alpha-synuclein, and toxic protein deposits, pointing to lipid metabolism and cellular waste-removal pathways as potential therapeutic targets.

Note:

In a second study, the researchers also discovered how APOE4 damages the microvasculature supplying blood to white matter, via mechanisms unrelated to cholesterol.

Shared from Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai · original post

Mount Sinai researchers have identified how APOE4, the strongest genetic risk factor for Alzheimer’s disease, damages the brain's blood vessels and promotes the buildup of abnormal proteins linked to neurodegenerative disease. The findings, published in Cell and Cell Stem Cell, identify potentially reversible disease mechanisms and point to new therapeutic targets.

The Cell study found that APOE4 causes blood-vessel support cells to transform into scar-forming cells, promoting vascular damage and amyloid accumulation around the brain's vessels. Importantly, blocking a key cellular signaling pathway reversed this damage in preclinical models. The Cell Stem Cell study revealed that APOE4 causes cholesterol to accumulate in brain support cells called astrocytes, impairing their ability to clear abnormal proteins — findings with implications for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.

Both studies made use of miBrains, a three-dimensional stem cell-derived human brain tissue platform developed at Mount Sinai that enabled researchers to model disease processes in complex human brain-like tissue and rapidly test potential therapeutics.
