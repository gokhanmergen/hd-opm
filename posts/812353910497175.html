Tongxinluo, a traditional Chinese medicine, cut post-heart attack events in a JAMA trial

Louis Nervegna · May 31, 2024 · ♥ 8 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Heart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Newsweek report on a large JAMA clinical trial of the traditional Chinese medicine tongxinluo in people after a first heart attack. The report says it reduced heart attacks, deaths and other major cardiovascular complications for at least a year. No effect sizes are given in the post.

  • Published in JAMA; one of the first large Western-style trials of a traditional Chinese medicine
  • Reported reduction in MI, death and major cardiovascular complications for at least a year after a first heart attack
  • Made from plants and animals including scorpion and leech extracts

“A traditional Chinese medicine has been shown to be effective at reducing complications following a heart attack after a large-scale, clinical trial.”

“The latest study, which has been published in JAMA (formerly The Journal of the American Medical Association), represents one of the first times that a traditional Chinese medicine has been tested in a large-scale, Western-style clinical trial.

The authors of the study, who include researchers from several Chinese universities and hospitals, in collaboration with experts from the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center (UTSW)—found that tongxinluo reduced the risk of heart attacks (known as myocardial infarctions), deaths and other major cardiovascular complications for at least a year after a first heart attack.”

Summarises a 2019 JACC review arguing plaque progression is a necessary, modifiable step between subclinical atherosclerosis and MI, and that preventive strategy should shift toward imaging and aggressively treating subclinical disease. Dr Carvalho: evidence for vitamin K2 supplements and arterial calcium is weak

Nov 16, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 27 Supplements & vitamins

Summarises a Carvalho video: arterial calcium is a marker of long-standing plaque, not the cause of risk, so lowering a calcium score can mislead, just as statins raise calcium while lowering risk. No large properly powered trials show K2 reduces heart attacks or strokes, and he warns of false reassurance. The admin adds that supplement use is fine but expectations should stay grounded.
