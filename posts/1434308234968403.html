CoQ10 improved tinnitus severity and sleep in presbycusis patients in a small 2025 RCT

Shared from Healthy Harbor · original post

Persistent ringing, buzzing, or humming in the ears can make everyday activities difficult, particularly when it interferes with sleep, concentration, and quality of life. This condition, known as tinnitus, frequently accompanies age-related hearing loss (presbycusis), and effective treatments remain limited. A 2025 double-blind, randomized controlled trial published in the Iranian Journal of Otorhinolaryngology investigated whether Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), a naturally occurring compound involved in mitochondrial energy production and antioxidant protection, could help reduce tinnitus symptoms. Researchers enrolled 50 patients with tinnitus associated with age-related hearing loss, randomly assigning 25 participants to receive 100 mg of CoQ10 daily alongside routine treatment and 25 to receive a placebo alongside routine treatment for six weeks.

The results showed that participants receiving CoQ10 experienced significantly greater improvements in several tinnitus-related outcomes compared with placebo. Sleep disturbance scores decreased by an average of 7.6 points in the CoQ10 group versus 1 point in the placebo group (P < 0.001). Tinnitus-related disability scores also improved, with reported average reductions of 17.2 points versus 4.56 points (P < 0.001). Left-ear tinnitus loudness scores decreased by 2.2 points in the CoQ10 group compared with 0.54 points in the placebo group (P < 0.001). However, improvements in right-ear tinnitus loudness and overall quality of life were not statistically significant between groups. Researchers concluded that CoQ10 supplementation may be a promising complementary approach for managing tinnitus associated with age-related hearing loss, although larger and longer clinical trials are needed to confirm its effectiveness and determine whether the benefits persist after treatment.

This implies that the vast majority of muscle complaints on statins are not caused by the drug, consistent with a nocebo effect.

CoQ10 rejuvenated aging platelet-producing cells in mice via COPS3 and autophagy

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 Supplements & vitamins

A mouse study in Aging Cell found dietary CoQ10 restored function of aged megakaryocytes, the bone marrow cells that make platelets, through a COPS3-autophagy pathway. Aging makes platelets stickier and more clot-prone, raising heart attack and stroke risk. The poster calls it promising but it is animal-only.
