Coffee: observational data link 1-3 cups a day to about 30% lower all-cause mortality

Louis Nervegna · Jul 24, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foodsAging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis notes the coffee research is mostly observational but huge, and shares a video quote that 1-3 cups a day is associated with about 30% lower all-cause mortality.

  • 1-3 cups daily: about 30% lower all-cause mortality vs non-drinkers (per quoted source)

The research on coffee is mostly observational, but there is an enormous amount of it.

I organized many of the past posts on the health benefits of coffee under the tag #coffeeHD.

Quoted:

Drinking caffeinated coffee daily might be one of the simplest ways to extend your life

People who drink 1–3 cups daily experience around 30% lower all-cause mortality compared to non-coffee drinkers, thanks to powerful polyphenols like chlorogenic acids—potent antioxidants with anti-inflammatory effects.

Coffee drinkers even show signs of delayed biological aging (about one year younger based on epigenetic markers)

Regular coffee intake is also strongly linked to reduced risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, dementia, and Alzheimer's (but caffeine is key for brain benefits).

Review: moderate coffee intake linked to lower mortality and age-related disease

Nov 29, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 17 · 💬 1 ↩ 2 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A review of coffee and human aging reports that regular moderate coffee consumption is associated with lower all-cause mortality and fewer cardiovascular, stroke and cancer cases. Coffee and its components (caffeine, chlorogenic acids) appear to support stress adaptation and aging mechanisms.

Coffee linked to lower type 2 diabetes risk: 29% lower at 5 cups a day, decaf included

Nov 04, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis shares a SuppVersity summary of a Journal of Functional Foods review. A meta-analysis found 5 cups a day versus none was associated with a 29% lower type 2 diabetes risk, with each extra cup about 6% lower, and decaf showed a similar benefit to caffeinated coffee. Data beyond 6 cups are murky.

How coffee roasting changes chlorogenic acid and polyphenol content

Sep 28, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 10 · 💬 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

An infographic explaining that coffee beans contain chlorogenic acids that help regulate blood sugar, protect blood vessels and act as antioxidants, and that roasting changes how much reaches the cup. Light roast has the highest total polyphenols.

Darker coffee roasts contain fewer polyphenols such as chlorogenic acid

Jan 25, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 13 · 💬 12 ↩ 1 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

Louis notes that more roasting lowers polyphenols like chlorogenic acid, sharing a post on a Spanish randomized crossover study. In it, lightly roasted, phenolic-rich coffee produced a small improvement in body composition compared with regular roast. Body weight and metabolic syndrome markers did not change.

Caffeine activates AMPK in yeast cells, which may explain coffee's longevity links

Jun 26, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 11 · 💬 4 Nuts, fish, coffee, tea, cocoa & other foods

A Queen Mary University of London study in Microbial Cell found that caffeine acts via AMPK, the cellular fuel gauge, influencing growth, DNA repair and stress response. It was done in fission yeast, so human relevance is unproven.

Plant phytochemicals act as hormetic signals linked to resilience and healthy aging

Eric Verdin's post, shared by Louis, describes phytochemicals such as sulforaphane, EGCG, quercetin, curcumin, fisetin and resveratrol as protective compounds that interact with pathways for antioxidant defense, autophagy, mitochondria and inflammation.
