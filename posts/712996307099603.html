Strict keto can suppress thyroid function (low T3, T4 and TSH); try adding back carbs

Louis Nervegna · Nov 21, 2023 · ♥ 11 reactions · 5 comments · original Facebook post
Low-carb, keto & carnivoreHormones, women's & men's health
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that very low carb diets, especially combined with fasting and heavy exercise, can lower T3, T4 and TSH together by mimicking starvation. Mild suppression resembles healthy calorie restriction, but full hypothyroidism is a major risk factor for disease including CVD. He suggests gradually adding small amounts of whole grains, sweet potatoes or fruit to see whether thyroid function recovers. These are his mechanistic opinions without cited studies.

  • Low T3/T4 with low TSH together can confuse doctors
  • Risk is greatest when keto is combined with fasting, intense exercise, protein restriction or rapamycin
  • Hypothyroidism is a risk factor for multiple diseases including CVD
  • Self-experiment: gradually add whole-food higher-GI carbs

If you’re on a keto or very low carb diet and have persistently low T3, T4 and TSH, then the diet may be the cause. These extremely low carb diets can lower thyroid function, especially if you combine them with fasting and lots of exercise.

By eliminating all carbs, you can trick your body into thinking that food is scarce (these diets mimic calorie restriction), and it responds by signaling your thyroid to slow down your metabolism to conserve calories. That’s the basic reason why thyroid function can slow down on these diets. It’s an ancient defense mechanism to protect against starvation, which mammals evolved millions of years ago.

Often what you see is a combination of low T3/T4 and low TSH at the same time, which confuses doctors. Your body is directly reducing T3/T4 to slow metabolism and conserve calories, and it is also lowering TSH at the same time to signal to your thyroid to stop producing more hormones.

Note that this is not necessarily a bad thing. Mild drops in thyroid function happen on calorie restriction (CR), and this CR extends the lifespan of almost every animal species in which it has been tested. It’s when you become full blown hypothyroid that it becomes a big issue. Hypothyroidism is a major risk factor for multiple diseases, including CVD.

If you do the low carb diet right, then you get mild thyroid suppression and you mimic healthy calorie restriction. But if you overdo the low carb diet and combine it with too many other things that also mimic calorie restriction (like intense exercise, fasting, actual CR, protein restriction, drugs like rapamycin, etc.), you start to mimic unhealthy starvation instead of healthy CR.

If you believe that you may have made yourself full blown hypothyroid on a very low carb diet, then I suggest that you do an experiment where you gradually add in small amounts of healthy high GI carbs, like whole grains, sweet potatoes, or whole fruit. If your thyroid function begins to perk up, then this is likely the cause. Now you can continue the experiment until you find the bare minimum amount of high GI carbs that you need to correct the underlying hypothyroidism. Then stick with that. You will still get benefits of keeping carbs lower, but you will also get rid of the very dangerous hypothyroidism.
