Layne Norton: no hard-outcome data show saturated fat beats seed-oil omega-6 1:1

Louis Nervegna · Apr 02, 2025 · ♥ 4 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Insulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes Layne Norton, who says human studies of omega-6 fatty acids from seed oils replacing saturated fat 1:1 show neutral or positive effects on metabolic health, insulin sensitivity, liver fat and CVD, and that he knows of no hard-outcome data showing saturated fat is healthier. The linked video covers a study of over 200,000 people in which replacing butter with plant oils lowered mortality.

  • Per 10 g of butter replaced with plant oils: 17% lower mortality and cancer mortality
  • Results held when olive oil was excluded from the plant oil total
  • Study included over 200,000 participants

Quoted:

“The studies in humans looking at omega 6 derived fatty acids from seed oils show either neutral or positive effects when replacing saturated fat in a one to one ratio on metabolic health, insulin sensitivity, liver fat, cardiovascular disease, cancer possibly. And again, not all studies show benefits to those compounds but they’re at least neutral.

There are NO studies that I am aware of looking at actual hard outcome data demonstrating that in 1-to-1 ratios that saturated fat is healthier for things like insulin sensitivity, metabolic health, liver fat, cardiovascular disease, cancer, compared to polyunsaturated fats from seed oils.”

Layne Norton rebuts seed-oil claims with human evidence on inflammation, LDL and oxidation

Nov 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 3 · 💬 5 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.

Higher blood linoleic acid predicts lower CVD risk in 68,000-person pooled analysis

Jul 18, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 7 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Louis shares a Johns Hopkins piece citing the 2019 Marklund pooled analysis of over 68,000 participants in 30 cohorts across 13 countries. People with the highest blood linoleic acid had lower CVD risk, especially for CV mortality and stroke; controlled trials also show LA lowers LDL and may improve glucose metabolism. The association is observational, which the author notes.
