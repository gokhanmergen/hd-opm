Skepticism of a startup's claimed drug combo that mimics young blood for aging

Louis Nervegna · Sep 09, 2026 · ♥ 12 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevityMyths, misinformation & controversies
AI summary & key facts

Louis comments on a startup claiming a two-drug combination replicates the effect of young blood. He says the underlying parabiosis science is real but the commercial rollout, based on anecdotes and a pay-to-play trial, endangers the field's reputation. He speculates the drugs are anti-inflammatory agents targeting cytokines such as IL-11 and TGF-beta.

  • Underlying science (blood dilution rejuvenating old mice) is real
  • Evidence for the drug combo is anecdotal reports and a pay-to-play trial
  • Louis's guess at mechanism: lowering cytokines like IL-11 and TGF-beta

The science here is real and exciting, but the way they are attempting to cash in on it for a quick profit is sad to watch (and puts the delicate reputation of the whole field in jeopardy).

Based on what they’ve published or stated on the topic to date, these 2 mystery drugs are likely agents with anti-inflammatory properties that lower the blood levels of certain problematic cytokines which accumulate in the blood of older people (e.g. IL-11, TGF-beta, etc.) or mitigate their damaging effects. Their past experiments demonstrated that diluting the blood of old mice (with saline and albumin) to reduce the concentration of these harmful cytokines had a major rejuvenative effect on the mice. The Conboys have stated multiple times in the past that they believe cytokines are the major damaging component of old blood.

Shared from Singularity Hub · original post

A Berkeley scientist who spent her career proving that young blood makes old animals act younger says she's found a drug combination that produces the same effect, without any blood exchange required.

The claim is significant. Irina Conboy's parabiosis research is real, widely cited, and genuinely influential in longevity science. What she says she's now discovered is a combination of two existing generic drugs that keeps human cells young even when bathed in old blood serum.

The evidence so far: a handful of insiders who say they feel better. A spreadsheet of anecdotal reports. A pay-to-play clinical trial led by alternative medicine doctors, including one who previously recommended COVID treatments the FDA designated as fraudulent. The company won't disclose what the two drugs are.

The journalist who reported this story was offered the treatment as part of a marketing campaign targeting longevity influencers. He declined. The science behind the approach is real enough to take seriously. The way this particular company is pursuing it is worth reading carefully before drawing any conclusions.
