Whole grains linked to 23% lower coronary heart disease risk in three US cohorts

Louis Nervegna · Jun 17, 2025 · ♥ 5 reactions · 4 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsHeart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes a summary of a BMC Medicine analysis from the Nurses' Health Studies I and II and Health Professionals Follow-Up Study, averaging 25.8 years of follow-up. People eating the most whole grains had lower CHD risk, with specific foods such as whole grain cereal, bread, oatmeal and brown rice associated with benefit.

  • Followed for an average of 25.8 years, diet updated every 2-4 years
  • Highest vs lowest whole grain intake: 23% lower CHD risk
  • Each serving linked to 7% lower risk
  • Whole grain cereal or bread 1+/day associated with lower CHD risk vs less than monthly

Quoted:

Here we have yet more high-quality research displaying the benefits of consuming whole grains for heart health.

This publication included data from the Nurses Health Studies I & II, as well as the Health Professional Follow-Up Study, which each followed several tens of thousands of health professionals for an average of 25.8 years. Diet, lifestyle, and health information was updated every 2 to 4 years, so the researchers knew if people had changed their dietary or lifestyle practices throughout the follow-up period, which is one of the reasons these cohorts are amongst the best we have for studying nutrition. 🤓

After adjusting for potential confounding variables, those who consumed the most whole grains had a 23% lower risk of developing coronary heart disease (CHD) than those consuming the least. Further, each serving of whole grains was associated with a 7% lower risk.

They also went further and looked at different types of whole grains and found that consuming at least a serving per day of whole grain breakfast cereals or bread were associated with a lower risk of developing CHD compared to those eating them less than once per month. In addition, consuming oatmeal, brown rice, added bran, or wheat germ at least twice per week was associated with a lower risk compared to those consuming them less than once per month, and each daily serving of oatmeal, brown rice, or added bran may lower risk by 16%, 24%, and 9% respectively. 🍞

So what does this tell us? Whole grains, including breakfast cereals, can be heart healthy additions to one’s diet. ❤️
