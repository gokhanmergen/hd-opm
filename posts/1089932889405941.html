Sardinia's Blue Zone diet of beans, sourdough and olive oil: 10x the US centenarian rate

Louis Nervegna · Aug 14, 2025 · ♥ 17 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis cites the classic Sardinian diet as a real-world example backing up trial evidence for the traditional Mediterranean diet. Blue Zones reports Sardinia has 10 times the US rate of centenarians.

  • Sardinia: 10x US centenarian rate
  • Whole-grain sourdough, beans, vegetables, fruits, olive oil

As we’ve talked about many times in the group, there is rigorous data from large long-running randomized controlled trials and prospective cohort studies that backs up the health benefits of the traditional Mediterranean diet:

↪ Four long-term RCTs show the Mediterranean diet cuts CVD death by 46% in aggregate

We also have practical examples of groups of people who eat this way in the real world and live a long time.

Quoted:

“The classic Sardinian diet consists of cheap
"peasant foods" like whole-grain sourdough bread, beans, garden vegetables, fruits, and, in some parts of the island, olive oil. Sardinians also traditionally eat pecorino cheese made from grass-fed sheep, whose cheese is high in omega-3 fatty acids. Goat's milk is the dairy of choice, and meat is largely reserved for Sundays and special occasions.”

“Sardinians drink wine moderately. Cannonau wine has two or three times the level of artery-scrubbing flavonoids as other wines. Moderate wine consumption may help explain the lower levels of stress among men.”

Mediterranean-like macronutrient mix lowered estimated biological age in 4 weeks

Sep 18, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 3 Aging & longevity

A University of Sydney study in 104 adults aged 65-75 found three diet groups reduced estimated biological age after four weeks, with the strongest evidence for an omnivorous diet of 28-29% fat, 14% protein and 53% carbohydrate. Biological age was estimated from 20 biomarkers including cholesterol, insulin and C-reactive protein.

Mediterranean diet linked to higher mitochondrial microproteins humanin and SHMOOSE

Mar 24, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 Mediterranean, DASH, MIND & plant-forward diets

A USC study reported by Medical Xpress found that people closely following a Mediterranean diet had higher levels of the mitochondrial microproteins humanin and SHMOOSE, both linked to protection against cardiovascular disease and neurodegeneration. Foods like olive oil, fish and legumes may influence aging at a cellular level.
