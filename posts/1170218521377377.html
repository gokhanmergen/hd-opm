A lifelong vegan's heart attack: plant-based diets lower but do not eliminate risk

Louis Nervegna · Nov 28, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a commentary by a former Vegetarian Society (Singapore) president who had a heart attack at 70 after decades of veganism. Louis stresses that healthy vegan diets reduce risk but are not a magic cure.

  • Author vegetarian/vegan for 43 years, heart attack needing two stents
  • Genetics, age and other factors also matter

Vegan diets can be very healthy if done correctly, but they are not a magic cure for heart disease.

Quoted:

“My one year of vegetarianism became 43 years. During that time, I went vegan and became an activist for plant-based diets and alternative protein, heading Vegetarian Society (Singapore) for more than 10 years.

Then, on Mar 18 this year, I had a heart attack, needing two stents and a lot of medication.

It wasn’t supposed to happen. Aren’t vegans “heart attack-proof”? I felt a bit like the fitness guru, Jack LaLanne, who until his death at age 96 in 2011, used to say: “I can’t die, it would ruin my image”.

My diet was good. I wasn’t a “junk food vegan”. Even with all the vegan ice cream, cakes, and other sweets newly arrived on the market, I stuck mostly to less processed plant foods.

I didn’t eat fried foods or refined grains. I resisted the siren song of potato chips and other salty snacks, and stayed far away from sugary drinks. Overall, I knew to avoid foods high in SOS (Salt, Oil and Sugar).

Also, I exercised regularly: Walking 10,000 steps on most days, plus swimming and playing pickleball and tennis.

Despite this exercise and my diet, my weight rose slowly but surely as I reached 70 years of age in 2022, with a body mass index of 24.2. My LDL cholesterol level - the “bad” cholesterol - was higher than it should have been.”

#veganHD

Shared from Robin Ong · original post

The article, written by George Jacobs, a former president of the Vegetarian Society (Singapore), recounts his personal experience of adopting a vegan diet for over 40 years, initially motivated by concerns about meat production's impact on world hunger. Despite his commitment to veganism and a relatively healthy lifestyle, Jacobs suffered a heart attack at age 70 in 2022. He acknowledges that while a plant-based diet can reduce health risks—such as lowering the chances of heart disease, diabetes, and obesity—it does not eliminate them entirely. Factors like genetics, age, and other lifestyle elements also play significant roles in health outcomes. Jacobs notes his rising weight and elevated LDL cholesterol levels as potential contributors to his condition. He remains dedicated to veganism for ethical and environmental reasons, emphasizing that no diet is a complete shield against health issues, and encourages ongoing health monitoring and nutritional awareness, such as ensuring adequate vitamin B12 intake for vegans.
