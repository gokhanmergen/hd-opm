Shared from Genuinely Healthy · original post

A 2026 study published in the peer-reviewed journal Cancer Research investigated how PDE5 inhibitors, including sildenafil (Viagra), affect cancer metastasis. Researchers found that sildenafil caused cholesterol to accumulate inside lysosomes in both mouse and human cancer models, reducing the cholesterol available to cancer cells. This disrupted cancer cell migration, mitochondrial energy production, and the ability of cancer cells to spread (metastasize).

The researchers also found that combining sildenafil with statins produced stronger anti-metastatic effects than either treatment alone by blocking both cholesterol transport and cholesterol production. In addition, an analysis of digital health records showed that cancer patients taking sildenafil had significantly improved survival, with a dose-dependent additional survival benefit observed among those taking both sildenafil and statins. The authors concluded that PDE5 inhibition may be a promising strategy to help restrict cancer metastasis, but further clinical studies are needed to confirm these findings.
