AHA statement: low alcohol shows no risk to possible benefit, heavy drinking harms

Louis Nervegna · Jun 10, 2025 · ♥ 11 reactions · 9 comments · original Facebook post
Alcohol, smoking & vapingGuidelines, policy & public health
AI summary & key facts

Louis quotes the 2025 AHA scientific statement on alcohol and cardiovascular disease. Low intake (up to 1 to 2 drinks per day) is associated with no risk to possible risk reduction for CAD, stroke, sudden death and possibly heart failure, whereas binge or 3+ drinks per day is consistently linked with worse outcomes across every CVD entity.

  • Low intake (no more than 1-2 drinks/day): no risk to possible risk reduction
  • Binge or 3+ drinks/day: worse outcomes in every CVD entity studied
Paper · Circulation · 2025Alcohol Use and Cardiovascular Disease: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart AssociationAlcohol is one of the most commonly consumed substances in the world, exhibiting complex relationships with multiple aspects of cardiovascular health and disease. The majority of the research on the topic is observational and therefore prone to bias and confounding. The available evidence suggests no risk to possible risk reduction when alcohol is consumed in low amounts (such as no more than 1 to 2 drinks a day) in…ahajournals.org

Quoted:

“The available evidence suggests no risk to possible risk reduction when alcohol is consumed in low amounts (such as no more than 1 to 2 drinks a day) in regard to coronary artery disease, stroke, sudden death, and possibly heart failure.”

“In stark contrast, heavier alcohol consumption such as binge drinking or consuming on average ≥3 drinks/d is consistently associated with worse outcomes in every cardiovascular disease entity studied.”
