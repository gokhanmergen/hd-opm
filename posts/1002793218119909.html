Keto-CTA plaque progression vs plaque regression on Repatha in Budoff's other study

Louis Nervegna · Apr 09, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 2 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

Louis compares non-calcified plaque volume (NCPV) changes in the Keto-CTA (LMHR) study with a second Matthew Budoff study in higher-risk people whose LDL was lowered to about 30 mg/dL with the PCSK9 inhibitor Repatha. The lean, healthy keto group with LDL 254 mg/dL progressed, while the hypertensive, higher-risk Repatha group mostly showed major regression. He also notes that uL and mm^3 are the same unit when comparing NCPV across studies.

  • Keto group: average LDL 254 mg/dL; all participants showed progression or neutral NCPV
  • Repatha study: 53% hypertension, 21% diabetes, high baseline NCPV, LDL lowered to about 30 mg/dL; most showed major regression
  • 1 mm^3 equals 1 uL, so NCPV units are interchangeable

Let's look at the changes in non-calcified plaque volume (NCPV) in the recent Keto-CTA (LMHR) trial.

In the first image, we see the changes in NCPV in the keto trial among people with an average LDL of 254 mg/dL. Overall, we see significant increases. The higher the baseline plaque volume level, the greater the increase in non-calcified plaque volume. All participants saw plaque progression or neutral.

For comparison, we have a recent study by the same principal investigator Matthew Budoff. In the second image we see changes in the same parameter (non-calcified plaque volume) in people with additional risk factors for atherosclerosis such as hypertension (53%) and diabetes (21%) and with a high baseline level of non-calcified plaque volume.

These people had their LDL levels intensively lowered with the PCSK9 inhibitor Repatha to around 30 mg/dl. Most participants saw major plaque regression.

In this comparison, the keto trial results look bad.

The lean metabolically-healthy group on keto with very high LDL shows significant plaque progression, despite having low plaque volume to begin with.

The hypertensive overweight metabolically-unhealthy group with very low LDL shows significant plaque regression, despite having high plaque volume to begin with.

Note that NCPV can be measured in either units of uL or units of mm^3. These are the same thing. One cubic millimeter (1 mm^3) is exactly the same volume as one microliter (1 uL). Keep this in mind when comparing different studies to one another.
