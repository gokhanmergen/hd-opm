Swapping saturated fat for polyunsaturated seed oils cut CVD events 29% in strict RCTs

Louis Nervegna · Jun 27, 2025 · ♥ 6 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)Heart attack, stroke & procedures
AI summary & key facts

Louis states adipose omega-6 is linked to lower mortality in cohorts, and that RCTs meeting strict criteria (no trans fats, supplied food, 2+ years, biomarker-verified adherence, validated events) show a 29% reduction in heart disease events from replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat. He explains why the Minnesota Coronary Experiment, Sydney Heart Study and Rose Corn Oil Trial fail those criteria.

  • 29% fewer CVD events in aggregate from qualifying RCTs
  • Minnesota Coronary Experiment: 75% dropout in year 1, mean duration 384 days
  • Minnesota and Sydney trials used trans-fat-containing partially hydrogenated oils

Adipose tissue levels of omega 6 (linoleic acid) are linked to lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in prospective cohort studies.

In addition, if you look at tightly-controlled randomized controlled trials that met the following criteria listed below, then you find that replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat from seed oils reduces heart disease events by 29% in aggregate:

  1. Partially hydrogenated oils (trans fats) were not included in the diet
  2. Participants were given their food and observed
  3. There was at least 2 years of sustained intake
  4. Adherence was validated by objective biomarkers
  5. Information was validated on cardiovascular events

The Minnesota Coronary Experiment, Sydney Heart Study, and Rose Corn Oil Trial are very old trials from the 1960’s and 1970’s that all failed to meet multiple of these criteria. For example, the first two of these trials used partially hydrogenated oils (trans fats) in the intervention group. In the Minnesota Coronary Experiment, 75% of participants dropped out during the first year, and the average duration was only 384 days.

Dozens of newer short term randomized controlled trials have shown that replacing saturated fats like butter with seed oils leads to beneficial cardio-metabolic effects like reducing liver fat, improving metabolic status and reducing inflammation. A shared post argues that human trial evidence does not support the claim that seed oils are harmful. Systematic reviews of randomized trials find that raising linoleic acid does not consistently increase inflammatory markers, replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat lowers LDL and cuts coronary events, and trials show no significant increase in lipid peroxidation. It adds that LDL oxidation mostly occurs after retention in the artery wall, so LDL concentration still matters.
