Centenarians' higher LDL reflects survivorship bias, not a benefit of high LDL

Oct 15, 2025 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 36 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains that centenarians tend to have higher LDL simply because higher LDL is common in the general population; they reach 100 because of protective genes that let them survive despite high LDL. Low LDL is uncommon in Western countries, genetics is the main determinant of reaching 100, and low LDL may delay cardiovascular death but will not by itself get someone to 100.

NLA consensus: apoB testing helps when LDL-C and risk are discordant

Oct 15, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 6 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Summary of a National Lipid Association expert consensus on apoB testing. ApoB counts all atherogenic particles and may predict risk better than LDL-C, especially after statin treatment when the two diverge, and testing is suggested for borderline-risk patients deciding on a statin and in other discordant cases.

Each ~40 mg/dL LDL drop cuts heart attack and stroke risk about 20% (relative)

Nov 09, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 1 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Louis explains the rule of thumb from dozens of randomized trials that every roughly 40 mg/dL LDL reduction lowers relative risk of heart attack and stroke by about 20%. He shows how to convert to absolute risk: a 20% 10-year risk becomes 16% after a 40 mg/dL drop. People with prior events get more absolute benefit, hence LDL goals under 55 or even 40.
