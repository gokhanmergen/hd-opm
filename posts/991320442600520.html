Louis highlights a prospective cohort of about 5,700 asymptomatic participants in which carotid ultrasound plaque burden (cPB) predicted all-cause mortality better than coronary artery calcium score. He recommends cPB over carotid intima-media thickness when choosing a carotid ultrasound, since it is cheap and radiation-free. He later noted two companies that offer it.

  • Median follow-up 12.4 years; 901 (16%) of participants died
  • Fully adjusted HR for all-cause mortality: cPB 1.23 (1.16-1.32), CAC 1.15 (1.08-1.23)
  • Median cPB progressed from 29.2 to 91.3 mm3 in those rescanned; progression associated with mortality
  • cPB performed better than CAC

When doing ultrasound of the carotid artery, carotid plaque burden (cPB) is a much better test than carotid intermediary thickness (CIMT). If you have a choice, pick cPB.

Shared from Louis Nervegna · original post

This study was just published.

It demonstrates the importance of tracking carotid artery atherosclerosis progression by ultrasound (carotid plaque burden, cPB) for prediction of all-cause mortality. It was a 12 year long prospective cohort study of 5,700 participants without symptoms.

Carotid artery plaque burden (cPB) measured by ultrasound was more accurate in predicting risk than coronary artery calcium (CAC) score.

If you have never had an ultrasound of the carotid artery, you may want to show this study to your doctor. This test is cheap and safe (it uses sound, not radiation).

Edit 4/27/26 - People keep asking where they can get this test. It is done nationwide wide by a company called CardioRisk. They offer it as part of their CIMT test. There is another company, VasoLabs, which offers it as well. There are more, but these are the two that I know of.

Quoted:

“Over a median 12.4 years’ follow-up, 901 (16%) participants died. After adjustment for cardiovascular risk factors and background medication, baseline cPB and CAC score were both significantly associated with all-cause mortality (fully adjusted trend HR: 1.23; 95% CI: 1.16-1.32; and HR: 1.15; 95% CI: 1.08-1.23), respectively (both P < 0.001), thus providing additional prognostic value. cPB performed better than CAC score. In participants with a second vascular ultrasound evaluation, median cPB progressed from 29.2 to 91.3 mm3. cPB progression was significantly associated with all-cause mortality after adjusting for cardiovascular risk factors and baseline cPB (HR: 1.03; 95% CI: 1.01-1.04 per absolute 10-mm3 change; P = 0.01).”

The ultrasound test used in this paper is called Carotid Plaque Burden (cPB).

This cPB test is more accurate than cIMT, according to the authors.

If you’re going to get an ultrasound of your carotid, try to get cPB.

Quoted from paper:

“Two key considerations may underlie our findings. First, volumetric measurement of cPB is a more accurate than other atherosclerosis measures such as cIMT, which is no longer recommended for cardiovascular risk assessment because it instead reflects hypertensive or age-related changes.”

There are several different carotid vascular disease metrics such as carotid intima-media thickening (cIMT), maximal plaque thickness, or carotid plaque burden (cPB). Please don’t mix them up.
