Shared from Genuinely Healthy · original post

A review in Open Heart highlighted vitamin K2 as an important regulator of calcium in the cardiovascular system. Vitamin K2 activates matrix Gla protein (MGP), a protein made by vascular smooth muscle cells that helps stop calcium crystals from depositing in artery walls.

When vitamin K status is low, more MGP remains inactive, and higher levels of inactive MGP have been linked with greater arterial stiffness, vascular calcification, aortic valve calcification, heart failure severity, and cardiovascular mortality.

One of the strongest human trials included in the review followed 244 healthy postmenopausal women for 3 years. Women who took 180 mcg of vitamin K2 as MK-7 daily had significant improvements in measures of arterial stiffness compared with placebo, including carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity and stiffness index.

Other studies in the review also found that higher vitamin K2 intake was associated with less coronary calcification and lower cardiovascular risk, although trials on directly slowing calcium buildup have produced mixed results, so the clearest clinical finding is that K2 can improve arterial stiffness while supporting activation of the body’s anti-calcification proteins.

Grape seed extract lowered PWV 1.70 m/s versus placebo and folate 1.46 m/s. Louis notes grape seed extract is likely safer than megadosing folate.

Vitamin K2 slows coronary calcium mainly in severe calcification; event benefit unproven

Dec 02, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 16 ↩ 1 Supplements & vitamins

Review of vitamin K2 (with or without vitamin D) and CAC. A 2023 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=1,533) found a -17.3 Agatston units/year pooled difference. The largest RCT (389 men, 720 ug MK-7 + 1000 IU D vs placebo for 2 years) missed significance overall but slowed CAC at baseline CAC >=400; some CKD trials were null. Not yet a standard therapy.
