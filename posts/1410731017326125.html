ASPREE: aspirin tied to 70% lower dementia with high platelet-count genetic scores

AI summary & key facts

Monash researchers analysed genetic data from 13,541 ASPREE participants and found that among those in the top 20% of a platelet-count polygenic score, 1% on 100 mg aspirin developed dementia versus 3.3% on placebo. Aspirin is not recommended for dementia prevention, and this is a genetic subgroup finding that needs confirmation, with bleeding risk to weigh.

  • 13,541 ASPREE participants, daily 100 mg aspirin vs placebo
  • Top quintile platelet-count polygenic score: dementia 1% with aspirin vs 3.3% with placebo
  • About 70% lower relative risk in that subgroup
  • Screened 5,182 polygenic scores; authors note aspirin is not recommended for dementia prevention

Daily low-dose aspirin might protect a subset of older adults against dementia.

These results are from the ASPREE randomized trial.

Low-dose daily aspirin is linked to a 70% lower relative risk of dementia in older adults who carry a specific genetic profile tied to platelet count.

Study Overview

  • Institution: Monash University researchers analyzed data from the ASPREE Trial (Aspirin in Reducing Events in the Elderly).
  • Publication: The findings were published in Alzheimer's & Dementia: The Journal of the Alzheimer's Association.
  • Participant Scope: Genetic data from over 13,500 healthy older adults was examined.

Key Findings

  • Genetic Link: The strongest protective benefit of aspirin against dementia appeared in participants in the highest 20% (top quintile) of a platelet-count polygenic risk score.
  • Risk Reduction: In this high-scoring genetic group, 1% of participants taking daily low-dose (100-milligram) aspirin developed dementia, compared to 3.3% of those taking a placebo.
  • No Effect Elsewhere: Participants outside of this top genetic tier showed no cognitive benefit from taking aspirin.

Important Caveats and Risks

  • Bleeding Risk: Researchers noted that aspirin doubled the risk of major bleeding events in this high genetic score subgroup (4.4% on aspirin versus 2.1% on placebo).
  • Need for Validation: These exploratory results are hypothesis-generating and require formal clinical validation before doctors can use genetic screening to prescribe personalized aspirin for dementia prevention.
  • Consult a Doctor: Experts warn that individuals should not start taking aspirin to prevent memory loss or cognitive decline without first speaking with a healthcare provider.

Shared from Science Acumen · original post

A daily low dose of aspirin may offer protection against dementia for a specific group of older adults, according to a new analysis of one of the largest aspirin trials ever conducted. The findings suggest genetics could determine who receives cognitive benefits from the drug.

Researchers examined genetic data from more than 13,500 participants in the ASPREE trial, which originally tested daily 100-milligram aspirin against a placebo in healthy older adults. The new analysis focused on genetic variants associated with platelet count.

Among participants in the highest 20 percent for a platelet-related genetic score, about 1 percent of aspirin users developed dementia, compared with 3.3 percent receiving placebo, representing roughly a 70 percent lower risk.

However, this finding does not mean older adults should start taking aspirin to prevent dementia. The original ASPREE trial, involving more than 19,000 people, found no significant overall reduction in dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, mild cognitive impairment or cognitive decline among people taking aspirin.

Aspirin also increases bleeding risk. In ASPREE, major hemorrhage occurred more frequently among aspirin users than among participants receiving placebo. The new results therefore point toward personalized prevention rather than universal aspirin use.

Researchers need additional studies to confirm whether genetic testing can reliably identify people whose potential neurological benefits outweigh aspirin’s risks.

Until then, older adults should not begin daily aspirin specifically for dementia prevention without discussing their individual cardiovascular and bleeding risks with a healthcare professional.

Source: Monash University, ASPREE study; Alzheimer’s & Dementia, 2026.
