Bone marrow stem cells are limited by telomere shortening, not immortal

Louis Nervegna · Jul 22, 2026 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Aging & longevity
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains that bone marrow stem cells, which renew blood and help repair tissue including the heart, cannot divide indefinitely because of telomere shortening. He shares a study showing telomeric DNA damage signaling drives hematopoietic aging, and inhibiting it restored function in mice and human stem cells.

  • Hematopoietic stem cells replace blood; mesenchymal stem cells help repair organs
  • Telomere shortening limits their divisions despite higher telomerase
  • Telomeric damage signaling inhibition restored hematopoiesis in mice and improved human stem cell function

There is a mistaken idea that the stem cells in your bone marrow can divide forever.

These bone marrow stem cells perform important functions: Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) in your bone marrow produce new cells that replace your blood. And mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) in your bone marrow can even stimulate repair of organ and tissue damage all over your body, including your heart.

In reality, bone marrow stem cells are limited by telomere shortening. They do express higher levels of telomerase than other cells, but they ultimately face this same limitation.

This means that your bone marrow stem cells can only divide so many times (to do their jobs mentioned above) before they stop functioning properly. This contributes to age-related diseases and dysfunction.

Vitamin C slowed bone marrow aging in monkeys in a 3-year trial

Jul 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 6 · 💬 2 Aging & longevity

A 3+ year trial in aged cynomolgus monkeys found oral vitamin C partly offset bone marrow aging, expanding common lymphoid progenitors and rebalancing lineage output, with about a 4-year reduction in transcriptomic age estimates. Louis notes bone marrow stem cell decline contributes to aging and heart disease.

CoQ10 rejuvenated aging platelet-producing cells in mice via COPS3 and autophagy

Oct 02, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 Supplements & vitamins

A mouse study in Aging Cell found dietary CoQ10 restored function of aged megakaryocytes, the bone marrow cells that make platelets, through a COPS3-autophagy pathway. Aging makes platelets stickier and more clot-prone, raising heart attack and stroke risk. The poster calls it promising but it is animal-only.

Cholesterol as a master regulator of blood stem cells, driving inflammatory hematopoiesis

Oct 19, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 4 Inflammation & CRP

Review of preclinical evidence that high LDL pulls hematopoietic stem cells out of dormancy, causing proliferation, oxidative stress, telomere shortening, impaired repopulation and lasting epigenetic changes that program blood and immune cells toward chronic inflammation. This offers a molecular basis for residual inflammatory risk after cholesterol is treated.

Aging stem cells open a direct platelet pathway that raises thrombosis risk (Cell 2024)

Jun 09, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 12 Heart attack, stroke & procedures

Shares the abstract of a Cell paper showing that with age, hematopoietic stem cells increasingly make platelets through a direct route that bypasses the usual progenitor cascade. The platelets from this pathway are more prone to thrombosis and cause higher platelet counts. It offers a possible cellular explanation for why clotting-related cardiovascular disease rises so steeply in older people.

Boosting VEGF signaling to improve circulation extended lifespan 45% in old mice

Dec 19, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 16 · 💬 36 Blood vessels, circulation & endothelium

Gokhan highlights a mouse study in which restoring youthful VEGF signaling kept blood vessels healthier with age, improving metabolism, muscle, bone and inflammation and extending lifespan by 45%. He speculates about human applications such as walking and daily activity, but the finding is in mice.

Alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG): longevity data in worms and mice, plus anti-thrombotic effects

Mar 02, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 13 Supplements & vitamins

Summarizes the case for the supplement AKG, which extended lifespan in worms by about 50% and healthspan in aged mice, apparently by mildly suppressing mitochondrial function and mimicking calorie restriction. The linked EBioMedicine paper suggests AKG inhibits thrombosis and inflammation. The human evidence for cardiovascular benefit is limited and the author notes he personally uses it.
