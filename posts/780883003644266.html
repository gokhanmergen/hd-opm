HCM, athlete's heart and LVH look alike on imaging but differ greatly in risk

Louis Nervegna · Apr 03, 2024 · ♥ 4 reactions · 1 comments · original Facebook post
Heart rhythm & heart failureExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains HCM, athlete's heart and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) can look alike on EKG or ultrasound. HCM is usually genetic and serious, potentially causing sudden cardiac arrest; athlete's heart follows long-term intense exercise and is usually benign; LVH is usually due to uncontrolled hypertension or aortic stenosis. A person can have both HCM and athlete's heart.

  • HCM: usually genetic, can cause cardiac arrest
  • Athlete's heart: from prolonged intense exercise, usually benign
  • LVH: usually from uncontrolled high blood pressure or aortic stenosis; also CKD, obesity, genetics, anabolic steroids

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) is often confused with Athlete’s Heart, but they are different. They often present the same way on EKG or ultrasound.

HCM is usually genetic, and is far more serious. It can lead to sudden cardiac events, including cardiac arrest. Athlete’s heart is caused by intense exercise over a long period of time, and is usually considered benign. Both involve a thickening of the heart muscle.

It is possible to have both HCM and athlete’s heart at the same time.

Another closely related condition which is often confused with HCM and Athlete’s Heart is Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH):

heart.orgWhat is left ventricular hypertrophy lvhheart.org

LVH is usually caused by uncontrolled high blood pressure or aortic stenosis, but there are less common causes, as well, including chronic kidney disease, obesity, genetic factors, and use of anabolic steroids. HCM and Athlete’s Heart can also cause LVH. Certain congenital heart diseases, cardiomyopathies, and valve disorders can also cause LVH.
