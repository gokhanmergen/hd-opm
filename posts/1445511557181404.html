Each ~40 mg/dL LDL drop cuts heart attack and stroke risk about 20% (relative)

Louis Nervegna · Nov 09, 2026 · ♥ 1 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
LDL, ApoB & lipid panelStatinsRisk assessment & lab tests
AI summary & key facts

Louis explains the rule of thumb from dozens of randomized trials that every roughly 40 mg/dL LDL reduction lowers relative risk of heart attack and stroke by about 20%. He shows how to convert to absolute risk: a 20% 10-year risk becomes 16% after a 40 mg/dL drop. People with prior events get more absolute benefit, hence LDL goals under 55 or even 40.

  • Secondary prevention goals often under 55, sometimes under 40

Every ~40mg/dL point drop in LDL cuts risk of heart attack and stroke risk by roughly 20%.

This is a good rule of thumb to remember.

It is in comparison (relative) to not having the drop. For example, someone with LDL of 100mg/dL is 20% less likely to have a heart attack or stroke compared (relative) to having an LDL of 140mg/dL.

This rule of thumb is an average drop in relative risk, extracted from analysis of dozens of randomized trials.

You can easily translate this to an absolute risk for your particular situation by using simple arithmetic:

Say your absolute risk of a heart attack or stroke in the next 10 years is 20%. This means you have a 1 in 5 chance of having a heart attack or stroke during this 10 year time period, which puts you at high risk. If you drop your LDL by 40mg/dL, this reduces to an absolute risk of 16% over this same 10 year period, which represents a 20% relative decline.

Hit your LDL goal? 🎉 Celebrate. Then ask the next question.

Every ~40-point drop in LDL cuts heart attack and stroke risk by roughly 20%. But what that 20% means for you depends on where you started.

Already had a heart attack or stroke? You're standing closer to the edge, so the same drop protects you more. That's why the goal is often under 55, and sometimes under 40.

Two questions for your doctor: What's my personal goal? And how close to the edge am I standing?
