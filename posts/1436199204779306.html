Could adding green tea and duckweed to a Mediterranean diet help reduce visceral fat?

Visceral fat accumulates around internal organs and can contribute to inflammation and metabolic dysfunction.

In an 18-month randomized controlled trial, researchers compared a standard healthy diet, a Mediterranean diet, and a Green Mediterranean diet enriched with green tea and Wolffia globosa (duckweed).

The results were impressive: the Green Mediterranean diet reduced visceral fat by approximately 14%, compared with roughly 6% for the standard Mediterranean diet.

The intervention included approximately 100 grams of duckweed and 2–3 cups of green tea daily, alongside the broader Mediterranean dietary pattern.

Importantly, these findings don’t establish that green tea or duckweed alone would produce the same benefit.

How to Use This: A Mediterranean-style diet is a reasonable starting point for improving metabolic health. Adding polyphenol-rich foods and beverages may provide additional benefits.
