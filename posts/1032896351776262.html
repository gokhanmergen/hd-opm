Men (from Mars) and Women (from Venus) Lose Weight (on Earth) in a Similar Manner With Energy Restriction (and GLP-1RA)

Science misinformation (disinformation) has become endemic these days. Social Media is now the playground to craft niche narratives, convincing followers that things happen in contrast to science (or even the laws of physics). The latest is that males and females lose weight differently in response to energy restriction. The upshot? Special weight loss programs for women! So, let’s take a look at what the evidence shows!

  1. Energy-restriction diets yield equivalent weight loss across sexes

A meta-analysis of 13 long-term randomized controlled trials (≥12 months of calorie-restricted diets) found no significant difference between men’s and women’s weight loss—neither in absolute kilograms lost (p = 0.90) nor in percent body-weight reduction (p = 0.78). LinkedIn (lnkd.in)
.

  1. Low-carbohydrate regimens show the same pattern

Systematic reviews of low-carbohydrate interventions report that most studies observe no gender gap in weight loss; when differences occur, men more often edge out women by a small margin, but overall responses are overwhelmingly similar. LinkedIn (lnkd.in)
.

  1. GLP-1 receptor agonists drive robust weight loss in both sexes

Pooling data from 14 GLP-1RA trials (dulaglutide, exenatide, liraglutide, semaglutide, retatrutide), women lost on average ~1.0 kg more than men (MD 1.04 kg, 95% CI 0.70–1.38; 1.69% greater percent loss), a modest sex difference relative to total weight change.

Bottom line:
Across both dietary and pharmacological weight-loss strategies in healthy adults, men and women respond in much the same way to energy restriction. While meta-analytic data occasionally reveal small sex-based differences—slightly greater losses in males on some diets and slightly greater female losses with GLP-1RAs—these gaps are minor. They do not justify entirely separate weight-loss prescriptions by sex; rather, they reinforce that individualized, evidence-based approaches should guide program design for everyone. Physiology (and physics) haven't changed... All weight loss runs through thermodynamics; Ein and Eout rule the day! Don't be fooled by bespoke (rodent study-based) claims on weight loss.
