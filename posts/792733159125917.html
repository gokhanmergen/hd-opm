Urinary metals linked to coronary calcium comparable to smoking and diabetes in MESA

A JACC study in 6,418 MESA participants followed over 10 years found higher urinary levels of non-essential (cadmium, tungsten, uranium) and essential (cobalt, copper, zinc) metals corresponded to more coronary artery calcification, with associations similar in size to traditional risk factors like smoking and diabetes.

CNIC researchers report in Nature Medicine, using the PESA-CNIC-Santander cohort, that people with clonal hematopoiesis mutations at baseline were more likely to develop atherosclerosis later, supporting it as a cause rather than a consequence. A related European Heart Journal study is also noted.

Over a third of 'low-risk' adults had coronary plaque; low LDL and ApoB were protective

Aug 18, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 12 · 💬 5 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

A JACC Advances study of 1,033 asymptomatic adults aged 40-65 classified as low risk found 35.9% had coronary plaque and 25.3% early calcification, increasing with higher LDL, non-HDL cholesterol and apoB, even below guideline levels. Gokhan reads it as showing that keeping LDL under 70 mg/dL greatly reduces risk.
