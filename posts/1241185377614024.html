Louis on the nocebo effect: real symptoms generated by expecting harm from a treatment

Louis Nervegna · Mar 08, 2026 · ♥ 3 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
StatinsHow to read researchSleep, stress & social health
AI summary & key facts

Explains the nocebo effect, where expecting a pill or shot to harm you produces genuine physical symptoms, and argues this partly explains negative anecdotes about statins and vaccines. Cites unspecified studies suggesting up to 90% of symptoms may be nocebo-driven.

  • Up to 90% of symptoms may be nocebo effect (poster's claim, studies not named here)

Nocebo effect is very powerful.

This occurs when your mind creates symptoms in response to the thought that you are taking something (e.g. a pill or shot) that is somehow harming you. These symptoms are physical and very real. They are not just “in your head”. But in the case of nocebo effect, they are generated entirely by the mind. Not by the thing you are taking. This is what makes it so easy to be fooled.

We all like to think that we are too smart to be tricked by our own minds, but it’s just not true. I have been fooled by this myself, and it was a real wake-up call to experience it firsthand.

The nocebo effect is an example of a mind body intervention. These mind body interventions can be exploited as effective treatments, as well.

People who are prone to health conspiracy theories are far more likely to experience nocebo effect, although it can happen to anyone. Many of these people truly are experiencing legitimate symptoms from nocebo effect. They are not making them up, which is why they are often so adamant and believable.

Studies have shown that up to 90% of symptoms may be due to nocebo effect:

↪ N-of-1 trial: about 90% of statin side effects were nocebo

↪ Nocebo effect explained about 90% of symptoms in patients who quit statins (SAMSON trial)

This is why I never trust negative anecdotes about standard medical treatments from people who are prone to health-related conspiracy theories.

Pooled blinded trials: statins cause only about 1 in 15 reported muscle aches

Oct 23, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 7 · 💬 12 Statins

A pooled analysis of 19 blinded trials with 123,940 people found that over about four years, 27.1 of 100 on a statin reported muscle pain or weakness versus 26.6 of 100 on placebo. This implies that the vast majority of muscle complaints on statins are not caused by the drug, consistent with a nocebo effect.
