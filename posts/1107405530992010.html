Friend's heart attack at 48 prompts reconsideration of LDL 70 and low-dose ezetimibe

Gokhan Can · Sep 07, 2025 · ♥ 26 reactions · 31 comments · original Facebook post

AI summary & key facts

A personal reflection after a friend who ignored a prescribed statin had a heart attack at 48 and needed bypass surgery. Gokhan considers retrying low-dose ezetimibe to approach an LDL of 50 mg/dL with his cardiologist's support.

  • Current LDL 70 mg/dL; target around 50 mg/dL

A good friend of mine had a heart attack recently at age 48. And now undergoing an emergency bypass. This was a shocking reminder that things we are doing in this group aren't just "theoretical" (e.g. a statin was prescribed to him, but he was ignoring it). This kind of made me panic a little... "oh man, is my LDL too high at 70mg/dL? How about my mom?" Anyways I was hesitant to take the low dose ezetimibe, but I might give it another try to approach 50mg/dL, which my cardiologist is very supportive.

Lp(a) explained: an LDL particle with a tail, genetic, unaffected by standard drugs

Sep 03, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 2 Lp(a)

Louis shares graphics explaining Lp(a). It is an LDL-like particle that is genetically set, affects about one in five people, and is not lowered by statins, ezetimibe, fibrates or fish oil. New drugs lower it by 80-99%, but benefit on outcomes was not yet shown.

Admin asks cardiologist about rosuvastatin raising insulin, and pitavastatin plus PCSK9i

Dec 30, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 39 Statins

Personal message to his cardiologist: on rosuvastatin 5 mg plus ezetimibe 10 mg his LDL was 90 then 63 mg/dL, not very different from low-dose rosuvastatin alone. He is concerned that fasting insulin rose roughly 50% on any rosuvastatin dose and wants to explore pitavastatin and a PCSK9 inhibitor. Anecdotal, n-of-1.

Limiting diabetes risk on LDL-lowering drugs: statin risk is modest, alternatives exist

Nov 26, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 17 Statins

Review explaining that statins modestly increase new-onset diabetes in a dose-dependent way, mostly in people with existing metabolic risk factors, and that cardiovascular benefit clearly outweighs this. It suggests statins mainly bring forward diagnosis in predisposed people, outlines mechanisms (beta-cell dysfunction, insulin resistance) and proposes ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors and pitavastatin as options that limit glycemic harm.

Mendelian randomization in 1 million people: statin-like genetics cut dementia risk 76-82%

Nov 06, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 11 · 💬 17 Brain health & dementia

Summarises Dr Stanfield's video on a Mendelian randomization study of over a million people. Short RCTs show no cognitive harm or benefit and observational studies suggest protection; genetic variants mimicking lifelong statin and ezetimibe action were linked to 76-82% relative dementia risk reduction, implying long-term benefit missed by short trials. The video also notes treating hearing loss, vision loss and midlife depression.

Ezetimibe lowers LDL-C about 18-20% alone and a further 21-30% on top of a statin

Sep 24, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 16 · 💬 19 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

A quick reference on ezetimibe: it blocks cholesterol absorption in the small intestine and is most useful added to a statin. It also modestly lowers apoB and triglycerides and slightly raises HDL.

Thrice-weekly ezetimibe cut LDL-C 20% in 33 statin-intolerant patients

Sep 22, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 24 PCSK9, ezetimibe & newer drugs

A small study of 33 patients, mostly intolerant of other lipid drugs, found that dosing ezetimibe only three times a week still lowered LDL substantially, consistent with its 22-hour half-life. Most patients tolerated it.
