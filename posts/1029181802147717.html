Hysterectomy before 50 linked to higher CVD risk in Nurses' Health Studies

Shared from Medscape · original post

A study of 239,907 women from the Nurses’ Health Study (NHS) and NHS II examined cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk after hysterectomy with or without oophorectomy. Results showed increased CVD risk among participants who underwent hysterectomy before age 50 years, with a 21% higher risk in those younger than 46 years who did not use estrogen. Louis shares the REACT study in NEJM of more than 16,000 adults aged 18-70 without known CVD, imaged by vascular ultrasound and CT. About 57% had plaque, rising from 1 in 13 in their 20s to about 9 in 10 in their 60s. Men developed silent disease 5-10 years earlier than women. He concludes age is the biggest risk factor.
