Waist size beats BMI for obesity-related cancer risk in men, likely via visceral fat

Louis Nervegna · Mar 26, 2025 · ♥ 8 reactions · 11 comments · original Facebook post
Weight, visceral & liver fatCancer
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Swedish study showing that waist circumference was a stronger marker than BMI for obesity-related cancers in men but not women. He suggests the reason is that waist size in men mostly reflects visceral fat, whereas women store more waist fat subcutaneously, and visceral fat is the dangerous kind.

  • Waist circumference stronger than BMI for obesity-related cancers in men, not women (Swedish study)
  • Louis' explanation: waist reflects visceral fat in men; women store more subcutaneous fat

The likely reason is because waist circumference in men almost always reflects visceral fat.

In women, however, a lot of the fat around the waist tends to be stored subcutaneously.

It’s the visceral fat that is dangerous.

Waist size predicted future disability better than 9 other aging and fitness measures
