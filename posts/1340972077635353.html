Protein benefits for muscle top out around 1.6 g/kg with resistance training

Louis Nervegna · Jul 11, 2026 · ♥ 5 reactions · 10 comments · original Facebook post
ProteinExercise & fitness
AI summary & key facts

Louis says the evidence ceiling for protein in muscle building is about 1.6 g/kg/day, with most benefit by 1.3 g/kg, and no strength benefit without resistance training.

  • Ceiling about 1.6 g/kg; most benefit by 1.3 g/kg
  • 1 g/lb is 2.2 g/kg
  • No proven strength benefit without resistance training

You certainly do not need more than 1.6g/kg of protein to build muscle.
That is a myth.

There is a consensus on this topic in academic sports science.

The ceiling is around 1.6g/kg. This is where benefits when combined with resistance training max out. Most of the benefit is actually achieved at around 1.3g/kg, but the actual ceiling is at 1.6g/kg:

(For reference, 1g/lb is 2.2g/kg.)

This all assumes you are doing resistance training. In the absence of resistance training, there is no proven benefit to muscle strength from higher protein intake.

Sports science says 1 g/lb protein is excessive; extra protein needs resistance training

Jul 26, 2024 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 19 ↩ 4 Protein

The poster says sports-science consensus is that muscle protein synthesis maxes out around 1.6 g/kg in natural bodybuilders, so 2.2 g/kg is wasteful. Extra protein above the RDA builds strength or muscle only when combined with resistance training, per dozens of randomized trials.

Stuart Phillips preprint: muscle benefit of protein still caps at 1.6 g/kg

Oct 08, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 1 Protein

Phillips posted a new, not peer-reviewed preprint reviewing the evidence and concluding that the muscle-related benefit of protein still tops out at about 1.6 g/kg per day, and only in people doing resistance training. He argues that most trials report lean mass, about half of which is not muscle, and that women's needs are not different from men's.

Stanford experts: the new 1.2-1.6 g/kg protein guideline has no new science behind it

Louis quotes Stanford Medicine on the newly revised US guidelines, which moved protein from 0.8 to 1.2-1.6 g/kg. Christopher Gardner says no new evidence justified it, Americans already eat enough, and strength training does far more than extra protein to prevent muscle loss.

Stuart Phillips: the 1.6 g/kg protein ceiling holds for older adults; women may need less

Mar 29, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 11 · 💬 6 ↩ 5 Protein

Louis shares part 2 of Stuart Phillips's protein rebuttal. In older adults the meta-analysis found lean mass gains at 1.2-1.59 g/kg/day and no trials above 1.6 g/kg; in older men, raising protein from 0.8 to about 1.3 g/kg raised muscle protein synthesis about 9%, while collagen did nothing.

Phillips: 74 trials show no extra muscle gain above 1.6 g/kg/day protein with lifting

Mar 28, 2026 · Louis Nervegna ♥ 8 · 💬 12 ↩ 5 Protein

Louis shares Phillips's own summary: a pooled analysis of 74 RCTs (nearly 2,700 people) found that beyond about 1.6 g/kg/day with resistance training, more protein adds nothing. In a 40% calorie deficit, 2.4 g/kg helped a little with lean mass, but 1.2 g/kg still preserved muscle.
