Quoted view: sky-high LDL on low-carb diets shows rapid plaque progression within a year

AI summary & key facts

Gokhan quotes a newsletter, 'Scientific Integrity and the Lean Mass Hyper-Responder', stating that people with very high LDL-C on low-carb or keto diets show rapid atherosclerosis progression in one year and should consider lipid-lowering treatment.

"Individuals with sky-high LDL-C on low-carb/ketogenic diets exhibit rapid atherosclerosis progression in just 1 year.

They need to drop their ideology and seriously consider lipid-lowering treatment.

Or their ideology will probably kill them one day."

JACC review: rapid plaque progression precedes rupture and LDL below 70 mg/dL halts it

Jan 02, 2026 · Gokhan Can ♥ 19 · 💬 5 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

Summarises a 2019 JACC review arguing plaque progression is a necessary, modifiable step between subclinical atherosclerosis and MI, and that preventive strategy should shift toward imaging and aggressively treating subclinical disease. It reports data from PROSPECT, PARADIGM, GLAGOV and PESA showing progression stops at LDL <70 mg/dL and regression needs lower.

Microplastics raised plaque 63% in male mice, with no change in cholesterol or weight

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

Summarises a UC Riverside mouse study: atherosclerosis-prone mice given 10 mg/kg/day microplastics for nine weeks. Males had 63% more plaque in the aortic root and 624% more in the brachiocephalic artery; females had no significant increase. Weight and cholesterol were unchanged, suggesting direct endothelial damage and inflammation. Admin suggests high fiber and Finnish sauna as defenses (not from the study).

Engineered regulatory T cells targeting oxidized LDL cut plaque 70% in mice

Nov 22, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 35 · 💬 7 Inflammation & CRP

Summarises a University of Pennsylvania preclinical study adapting CAR T therapy: Tregs engineered to recognise oxidized LDL dampened arterial inflammation and reduced plaque 70% in cholesterol-prone mice. Unlike cancer CAR-T, the cells suppress rather than destroy; it is a possible add-on for patients at risk despite cholesterol drugs.

Why elevated cholesterol harms: ApoB particles drive plaque and inflammatory aging

Explains that cholesterol is essential, but too many ApoB-containing particles like LDL get trapped in artery walls and drive plaque, and high circulating cholesterol may push bone-marrow stem cells toward inflammatory myeloid lineages. Keeping ApoB/LDL low is described as a powerful lever for arterial health.

Metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance and visceral fat drive plaque growth beyond LDL

An annotated evidence summary (reshared from Ken Gursky, with a long analysis) argues that residual plaque growth despite low LDL is linked to metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance and visceral fat. It cites imaging studies in which these factors predicted greater plaque progression, even on statins.
