Microplastics raised plaque 63% in male mice, with no change in cholesterol or weight

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

Summarises a UC Riverside mouse study: atherosclerosis-prone mice given 10 mg/kg/day microplastics for nine weeks. Males had 63% more plaque in the aortic root and 624% more in the brachiocephalic artery; females had no significant increase. Weight and cholesterol were unchanged, suggesting direct endothelial damage and inflammation. Admin suggests high fiber and Finnish sauna as defenses (not from the study).

How to reduce microplastic exposure: less plastic, glass, natural fibers, HEPA filters

Aug 31, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 15 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

Practical tips from a Bloomberg article: use less single-use plastic, avoid microwaving in plastic, choose glass or stainless steel, wear natural-fiber clothing, use HEPA filters and remove shoes indoors. Complete avoidance is impossible, and links to heart disease are potential.

Non-traditional heart disease risk factors converge on inflammation and oxidative stress

Aug 29, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 18 · 💬 11 ↩ 1 Inflammation & CRP

An overview of residual-risk drivers beyond cholesterol, blood pressure, diabetes and smoking: gum disease, chronic immune activation, clonal hematopoiesis, gut microbiome metabolites and pollutants. The common thread is chronic inflammation and oxidative stress.

Imidazole propionate, a gut-bacterial metabolite, drives atherosclerosis in mice

Jul 24, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 14 · 💬 4 ↩ 1 Gut microbiome

A Nature paper reports that imidazole propionate (ImP) is independently associated with cardiovascular disease and causes atherosclerosis in mice through inflammation via the I1 imidazoline receptor. Blocking that receptor could become a cholesterol-independent therapy.

Examine: saturated fat raises LDL-C and apoB in WHO meta-analysis of 84 RCTs

Sep 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 5 · 💬 3 Fats & oils (saturated fat, seed oils, omega-6, olive oil)

Summary of Examine's article on saturated fat. A 2016 WHO meta-analysis of 84 RCTs (2,353 healthy adults) examined replacing 1% of calories from carbohydrates or unsaturated fat with saturated fat. Saturated fat raised LDL-C and apoB (suggesting higher risk), and HDL rose only about one-tenth as much as LDL-C, raising the LDL/HDL ratio.

Clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) mutations precede and raise atherosclerosis risk in PESA study

Sep 05, 2024 · Gokhan Can ♥ 7 · 💬 12 Plaque, CAC & CT angiography

CNIC researchers report in Nature Medicine, using the PESA-CNIC-Santander cohort, that people with clonal hematopoiesis mutations at baseline were more likely to develop atherosclerosis later, supporting it as a cause rather than a consequence. A related European Heart Journal study is also noted.
