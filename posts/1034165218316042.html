Eating an apple before rice lowered blood sugar and insulin peaks

Louis Nervegna · May 26, 2025 · ♥ 34 reactions · 6 comments · original Facebook post
Carbs, fiber, sugar & whole grainsInsulin resistance & diabetes
AI summary & key facts

Louis cites a 2022 study showing apple eaten before a rice meal blunted glucose and insulin peaks, most in impaired glucose tolerance. He attributes this to fiber and polyphenols.

  • Normal tolerance: 14% lower glucose peak, 19% lower insulin peak
  • Impaired tolerance: 16.5% lower glucose peak

There is so much nonsense about fruit and blood sugar on social media and in pop nutrition books.

Here is the truth:

Eating certain fruits before a starch-containing meal helps moderate changes in blood sugar and insulin.

For people with normal blood sugar:

  • Eating apple first led to:
  • A 14% lower peak in blood sugar:
  • A 19% lower peak in insulin:
  • The total insulin response over time was lower.

For people with impaired glucose tolerance:

  • Eating apple first led to:
  • A 16.5% lower peak in blood sugar:
  • A lower total blood sugar response (iAUC).

Eating apple before rice significantly reduces both the peak and total rise in blood sugar and insulin levels—especially in people with impaired glucose tolerance.

It’s likely the fiber and polyphenols in fruit that are responsible.

Quoted:

“We previously reported on the effect of gold kiwifruit intake timing on blood glucose levels [21]. This fruit contains glucose and fructose as carbohydrates. Unlike glucose, fructose is thought to be less likely to raise blood glucose. It would, therefore, be interesting to investigate the main carbohydrates in apples. Therefore, the consumption of apples at the beginning of a meal may effectively reduce the increase in postprandial glucose levels due to the fiber and polyphenols, which are abundant in apples, and the carbohydrate composition, fructose.”

“Eating an apple before a meal may be a simple and effective strategy for managing the glycaemic response in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance.”
