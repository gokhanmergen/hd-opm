Admin rule: telling heart disease patients that LDL is not causal will get you removed

Louis Nervegna · Jul 27, 2025 · ♥ 119 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Group news & community

If you tell sick people in this group that LDL is not causal in heart disease, I am going to remove you.

This is not up for debate. You will be removed. I am not going to allow anyone in this group with heart disease to be harmed by comments like that.

What you do with your own health is your business.

But do not tell people in this group to ignore the medical advice of their cardiologist regarding managing their LDL cholesterol. This is a violation of the rules.

There are plenty of other places on social media where you can spread as much nonsense as you want about cholesterol and heart disease. Please don’t do it here.

Microplastics raised plaque 63% in male mice, with no change in cholesterol or weight

Dec 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 12 Environment (microplastics, pollution, heat)

Summarises a UC Riverside mouse study: atherosclerosis-prone mice given 10 mg/kg/day microplastics for nine weeks. Males had 63% more plaque in the aortic root and 624% more in the brachiocephalic artery; females had no significant increase. Weight and cholesterol were unchanged, suggesting direct endothelial damage and inflammation. Admin suggests high fiber and Finnish sauna as defenses (not from the study).

Engineered regulatory T cells targeting oxidized LDL cut plaque 70% in mice

Nov 22, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 35 · 💬 7 Inflammation & CRP

Summarises a University of Pennsylvania preclinical study adapting CAR T therapy: Tregs engineered to recognise oxidized LDL dampened arterial inflammation and reduced plaque 70% in cholesterol-prone mice. Unlike cancer CAR-T, the cells suppress rather than destroy; it is a possible add-on for patients at risk despite cholesterol drugs.

Review: aggressive LDL lowering outweighs theoretical risks, yet few reach <55 mg/dL

Oct 28, 2025 · Gokhan Can ♥ 13 · 💬 5 LDL, ApoB & lipid panel

Summarises a July 2025 review: guidelines now target LDL-C <55 mg/dL (<40 after a recurrent event), PCSK9 inhibitors add 59-62% LDL reduction, and evidence (FOURIER, CTT meta-analysis, PCSK9 genetics) supports benefit to very low levels. It says safety concerns such as hemorrhagic stroke, cognitive decline (EBBINGHAUS) and hormone disruption are unsupported, while fewer than one in four high-risk patients reach target.
