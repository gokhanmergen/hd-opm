Summary of how micronutrients such as vitamin C and CoQ10 affect endothelial function

Louis Nervegna · Jul 16, 2025 · ♥ 13 reactions · 0 comments · original Facebook post
Blood vessels, circulation & endotheliumSupplements & vitamins
AI summary & key facts

Louis shares a Linus Pauling Institute review summarising studies on micronutrients and endothelial dysfunction. Quoted examples include pooled RCTs showing vitamin C improved endothelial markers in heart failure, atherosclerosis or diabetes but not hypertension, and CoQ10 150-300 mg/day modestly improving function in five small trials.

  • Vitamin C improved endothelial markers in heart failure, atherosclerosis, diabetes but not hypertension
  • CoQ10 150-300 mg/day for 4-12 weeks modestly improved endothelial function (5 small RCTs)

A nice summary of studies showing how various micronutrients affect endothelial function.

Quoted:

“A pooled analysis of randomized controlled trials found that supplementation with vitamin C improved markers of endothelial function in individuals with heart failure, atherosclerosis, or diabetes mellitus, but not in those with high blood pressure.”

“Combining the results from five, small, randomized controlled trials, supplemental coenzyme Q10 (150 to 300 milligrams/day for 4 to 12 weeks) modestly improved endothelial function in individuals with and without cardiovascular disease.”

“Several randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that consumption of certain flavonoid-rich beverages (black tea, purple grape juice, and high-flavanol cocoa powder) improves endothelial function in patients with CHD, elevated blood cholesterol, or type 2 diabetes.”

“Only a few, small randomized controlled trials have tested the effect of LA [Lipoic Acid] on endothelial function in individuals with diabetes or metabolic syndrome. Intravenous infusion plus oral supplementation of LA improved endothelial function in these high-risk patient populations.”

“Several small, randomized controlled trials have tested the effect of supplemental resveratrol on endothelial function and biomarkers of endothelial inflammation. These preliminary trials suggest that high-doses of supplemental resveratrol (>150 milligrams/day) may improve measures of endothelial function and inflammation.”
