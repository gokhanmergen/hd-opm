Muscle loss on GLP-1 drugs may partly reflect autophagy, the author argues (opinion)

AI summary & key facts

Louis explains his opinion that muscle shrinkage on GLP-1 agonists is part of how calorie restriction works, with organs shrinking too and autophagy clearing damaged tissue. He labels it opinion; no study is cited in the text.

  • Labeled as the author's opinion
  • Argues brief catabolic periods are beneficial

In case you’re wondering why people lose muscle mass on GLP-1 agonists.

This is how calorie restriction is supposed to work. It’s not just the muscles that shrink; it’s the organs too. Brief periods of catabolism are not a bad thing.

We need catabolic processes like autophagy to clear out damaged tissue and stimulate repair.

The standard American diet and lifestyle puts us in anabolism nearly 100% of the time. This is not a good thing.

My opinion is that the muscle loss on GLP-1 agonists is due largely to autophagy. It’s the old damaged tissue being cleared out. That’s what calorie restriction does. This is part of why it extends lifespan in nearly every animal species tested. The same thing happens in mice. They lose muscle and their organs shrink, but they live much longer.

The people prescribed these drugs have been in a huge calorie surplus for years. They’ve had autophagy mostly shut off the whole time. They have lots and lots of old damaged muscle that needs clearing out.

Once all that old damaged muscle tissue is gone and they hit their target weight, then they can work on building back young healthy muscle to replace it, with resistance exercise. This process of cleaning out old damaged tissue by autophagy, and then replacing it with healthy new tissue later on via anabolism is a good thing.

Worrying about this autophagy driven muscle loss is counterproductive and harmful. It’s not just the muscle that’s shrinking. So are the other organs. That’s how autophagy works. Let it happen naturally. Then build it back again fresh and new.

Your Paleolithic ancestors went through periods of food deprivation like this on a regular basis. This is perfectly natural and healthy. We evolved to deal with this. Your body prioritizes breaking down the old damaged tissue first when calories are scarce.

We did not evolve to be constantly shoving food in our face 24/7 to the point that we are in anabolism all the time, with catabolic processes like autophagy nearly shut off. That’s not how nature designed us to operate. We need catabolism to be healthy in the long term.
