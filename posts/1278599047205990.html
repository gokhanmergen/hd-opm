Ginger 1,600 mg/day for 8 weeks lowered Lp(a), hsCRP and fasting glucose in a small RCT

Louis Nervegna · Apr 28, 2026 · ♥ 28 reactions · 12 comments · original Facebook post
Lp(a)Supplements & vitaminsInflammation & CRP
AI summary & key facts

Louis discusses a double-blind RCT in 57 men with atherosclerosis: ginger cut Lp(a) by about 5 ng/mL (from a low baseline of 27), hsCRP by 0.66 ug/dL and fasting glucose 12.5%.

  • 57 male patients, 1,600 mg ginger daily for 8 weeks
  • Lp(a) fell about 5 ng/mL from a mean of 27
  • hsCRP down 0.66 ug/dL; fasting glucose down 12.5%

Link to full study:

Paper · ARYA Atheroscler · 2020Decreased lipoprotein (a) and serum high-sensitivity C-reactive protein levels in male patients with atherosclerosis after supplementation with ginger: A randomized controlled trial.Conclusion: This study showed that ginger had anti-atherosclerosis and anti-glycemic properties associated through a significant decreased Lp(a) and FBS in patients with atherosclerosis supplemented with ginger.pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

This was a small randomized trial involving 57 male patients with atherosclerosis.

The roughly 5 ng/mL drop in Lp(a) was small, but statistically significant. It’s worth noting that the average Lp(a) in the study was already low to begin with, 27 ng/mL. (Perhaps people with high Lp(a) would see a larger effect?)

There was also a 0.66 ug/dL drop in hsCRP, which is more impressive. Ginger is known to have anti-inflammatory properties.

Additionally, the ginger lowered fasting blood sugar by 12.5% compared to controls.

Quoted from the full study;

“This study was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial conducted on [male] patients with atherosclerosis. Participants in the ginger and control groups received 1600 mg of powdered ginger or placebo (wheat flour) in capsules daily for 8 weeks. Weight, body mass index (BMI), fasting blood sugar (FBS), cholesterol, triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), lipoprotein (a) [Lp(a)], high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), and total anti-oxidant capacity (TAC) were assessed before and after the intervention.”

“Ginger consumption in the intervention group significantly reduced serum Lp(a) level (27.25 ± 1.30 ng/ml vs. 23.57 ± 0.97 ng/ml) (P = 0.040) and also the level of hs-CRP in the intervention group was 1.90 ± 0.33 µg/ml and 1.24 ± 0.15 µg/ml (P = 0.010) before and after intervention, respectively, but the levels of Lp(a) and hs-CRP were not decreased significantly in the placebo group. The level of TAC in the ginger group was 0.71 ± 0.05 mM and after the trial was 0.57 ± 0.04 mM (P = 0.050); no significant differences were seen in TAC when ginger was administered at 1600 mg/daily for 60 days. Also the level of Lp(a) and hs-CRP but not TAC reduced significantly in ginger group compared to placebo group after intervention.”

“This study showed that ginger had anti-atherosclerosis and anti-glycemic properties associated through a significant decreased Lp(a) and FBS in patients with atherosclerosis supplemented with ginger.”

“It is well known that ginger can act as a hypoglycemic agent in patients with type 2 diabetes [18,25]. Based on our findings, there was a 12.5% decrease in FBS in patients who consumed ginger supplements for 8 weeks compared to control subjects.”

Shared from Genuinely Healthy · original post

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial published in 2020 found that taking 1,600 mg of ginger daily for 8 weeks significantly reduced lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] levels in men with established atherosclerosis, along with lowering inflammation markers like hs-CRP and fasting blood sugar.

Since Lp(a) is largely genetically determined and typically hard to modify through diet or standard medications, this finding is notable. However, the study was relatively small and focused only on men with existing cardiovascular disease, and it did not significantly change traditional cholesterol markers like LDL or HDL.

MESA: high Lp(a) raised CVD risk only when hsCRP was 2 mg/L or higher

In MESA (mean follow-up 13.6 years, 684 CVD events), high Lp(a) conferred no significant risk when hsCRP was below 2 mg/L. With hsCRP of at least 2, Lp(a) of 50-99.9 mg/dL had HR 1.36 and 100 or more had HR 2.09. Participants were lower-risk, averaged age 62, so applicability to others is unclear.
